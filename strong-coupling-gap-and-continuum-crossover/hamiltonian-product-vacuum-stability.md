# Product-Vacuum Stability for the Lattice Gauge Hamiltonian

At fixed spatial spacing, the continuous-time compact-group lattice Hamiltonian has a noncircular volume-uniform strong-coupling gap by a published stability theorem for weakly interacting product vacua. The theorem permits infinite-dimensional link Hilbert spaces and unbounded electric operators. What must be small is the local magnetic interaction relative to the one-site electric gap, not its extensive total norm. Gauge restriction preserves the bound. This supplies a Hamiltonian strong-coupling anchor, not the four-dimensional continuum crossover.

## Normalize the local interaction, not the entire volume

Let \(G\) be connected and compact, with a specified bi-invariant metric and positive Laplacian \(L_G\). Its constants form the simple zero eigenspace and
\[
c_G:=\inf\bigl(\sigma(L_G)\setminus\{0\}\bigr)>0.
\]
On a \(d_s\)-dimensional spatial lattice, group the positively oriented outgoing links at vertex \(x\):
\[
\mathcal H_x=L^2(G^{d_s}),\qquad
h_x=c_G^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{d_s}L_{(x,i)},\qquad \Omega_x=1.
\tag{HV1}
\]
Then \(h_x\) is self-adjoint, may be unbounded, and has a simple product vacuum and gap at least one.

Consider the declared Kogut--Susskind-type Hamiltonian
\[
H=A\sum_eL_e+B\sum_p[1-\phi(U_p)],
\qquad A>0,\quad
\phi(g)=\frac{\operatorname{ReTr}_{R}g}{\dim R}.
\tag{HV2}
\]
The unitary representation \(R\) is fixed, and \(|\phi|\le1\). The coefficients are energy-valued and belong to this Hamiltonian; (HV2) is not an identity for a finite-spacing Wilson logarithm.

A plaquette based at \(x\), in directions \(i,j\), acts only on grouped sites \(x,x+e_i,x+e_j\). Use the fixed range \(\Lambda_0=\{0,e_1,\ldots,e_{d_s}\}\). Subtract the scalar plaquette constants, which changes neither ground vectors nor excitation gaps, and group the \(m=\binom{d_s}{2}\) plaquettes at \(x\). In units \(Ac_G\), their bounded perturbation satisfies
\[
\boxed{\varepsilon:=\sup_x\|\varphi_x\|
\le\frac{m|B|}{Ac_G}.}
\tag{HV3}
\]
Without the scalar subtraction, \(2m|B|/(Ac_G)\) is a valid weaker estimate. The norm of the sum over the entire volume is not the hypothesis.

## A theorem on the actual interacting vacuum

[[library/quasi-particles-in-weak-perturbations-of-non-interacting-quantum-lattice-systems/inq|Yarotsky, Section 1, Theorems 1--3]] applies to this on-site gap and fixed interaction range, including infinite-dimensional \(\mathcal H_x\). There are range-dependent positive constants \(c_1,c_2\) such that, for \(\varepsilon<c_1\) and \(c_2\varepsilon<1\), the finite-volume ground state is nondegenerate and
\[
\boxed{H_\Lambda-E_\Lambda
\ge Ac_G(1-c_2\varepsilon)
\bigl(I-|\psi_\Lambda\rangle\langle\psi_\Lambda|\bigr).}
\tag{HV4}
\]
The theorem also supplies the thermodynamic ground-state limit and the same lower bound in its GNS representation. The constants are existence constants, not explicit numerical critical couplings.

For literal application use the paper's empty-boundary convention: include \(\varphi_x\) only when \(\Lambda_0+x\subset\Lambda\). This retains whole plaquettes and omits an outer collar of interactions. No periodic-box or arbitrary boundary-sector constant is inferred without a separate adaptation.

The finite operator is elliptic on a compact connected product with a bounded real potential. Its heat semigroup is positivity improving, so its ground state can be chosen strictly positive. Gauge transformations commute with \(H_\Lambda\) and preserve positivity; uniqueness and normalization force them to fix \(\psi_\Lambda\). Restriction to the Gauss-law invariant subspace therefore retains (HV4). In the thermodynamic construction, the closed sector generated by invariant local observables likewise retains the vacuum and the lower bound.

The raw one-site constant \(c_G\), not the larger gauge-invariant girth constant, is needed in (HV1). Charged one-link states exist in the raw tensor product used by the theorem. Removing them later cannot justify changing the theorem's earlier normalization.

## What this controls, and what it does not

At finite volume normalize \(\|\psi_\Lambda\|_2=1\), set \(\nu_\Lambda=\psi_\Lambda^2\,d\mathrm{Haar}\), and let \(Q_0=I-\Pi_1\) on \(L^2(\nu_\Lambda)\), or its gauge-invariant subspace. For the actual positive ground-state-transformed Hamiltonian semigroup, suppressing \(\Lambda\) in the formula,
\[
P_\ell=\psi^{-1}e^{-\ell(H-E_0)/(\hbar c)}\psi,
\]
(HV4) gives the complete centered transfer estimate
\[
\|P_\ell Q_0\|
\le e^{-\ell Ac_G(1-c_2\varepsilon)/(\hbar c)}.
\tag{HV5}
\]
In infinite volume, state (HV5) directly for \(e^{-\ell H_\infty/(\hbar c)}\) on the vacuum complement of the constructed GNS representation. No density relative to infinite product Haar measure is asserted. This is physical Euclidean time, not an auxiliary sampling clock. A Hamiltonian gap alone does not imply a universal two-endpoint midpoint bridge floor; [[bridge-score-fusion-geometry/inq|the bridge counterexamples]] keep the direction of implication explicit. The [[wilson-slab-conditional-fisher-certificate|finite-spacing Wilson bridge theorem]] is a separate estimate in a different parameter regime.

With the conventional coupling dependence, \(A\) is proportional to \(g_0^2\hbar c/a_s\) and \(B\) to \(\hbar c/(g_0^2a_s)\); numerical proportionality factors depend on the fixed trace and plaquette conventions. Thus
\[
\varepsilon\ \text{is proportional to}\ g_0^{-4}.
\tag{HV6}
\]
The small-perturbation theorem applies at sufficiently large bare \(g_0\), whereas the asymptotically free continuum trajectory has \(g_0(a_s)\to0\). Taking continuous time at a fixed spatial lattice does not cross this obstruction. Nor does strong finite-volume convergence of anisotropic transfer kernels automatically carry a volume-uniform spectral estimate through simultaneous limits.

The quasiparticle theorems in the source impose additional assumptions on isolated excited levels. This application asserts neither those assumptions for physical glueballs nor a predicted glueball spectrum. Its contribution is a proved interacting strong-coupling anchor that does not need a global Haar/vacuum density ratio.

[[wilson-temporal-column-coercivity|The column-response route]] supplies a separate explicit sufficient vacuum bound before time refinement. [[wilson-to-hamiltonian-vacuum-limit|Norm convergence at fixed spatial volume]] passes that already uniform inequality to the Hamiltonian. It does not use the present theorem to infer uniform convergence or a finite-step bridge estimate.
