# Vacuum-Balance Fisher Geometry

The vacuum-balance sector of an orthogonal channel decomposition has an exact information-geometric face. Its real amplitude directions are the Fisher--Hellinger tangent space of the vacuum's block-weight law, while its imaginary directions are relative phases invisible to that law. A physical transfer can turn this geometry into a rate only under additional reduction, positivity, and clock hypotheses; without them, the complete balance-plus-internal transfer matrix remains the operator target. This separates ambiguity, coherence, attenuation, entropy loss, and mass instead of naming them all “collapse.”

**Status: [EXACT] for the Fisher--Hellinger identification, phase kernel, transfer block majorant, shorted balance defect, compression-semigroup obstruction, and static-rate no-go; [CONDITIONAL] for the Markov/lumpability interpretation and physical ground-state transform; [OPEN] for a continuum-uniform Yang--Mills estimate and Poincare reconstruction.**

## A pointed block law

Use the orthogonal resolution and pointed vector from
[[vacuum-aligned-innovation-completion/inq|Vacuum-Aligned Innovation
Completion]]. Put

$$
\Omega_\alpha:=E_\alpha\Omega,
\qquad
p_\alpha:=\|\Omega_\alpha\|^2,
\qquad
J:=\{\alpha:p_\alpha>0\},
\qquad
u_\alpha:=\frac{\Omega_\alpha}{\sqrt{p_\alpha}}.
\tag{BF1}
$$

The map

$$
U_p:L^2(J,p)\longrightarrow\mathcal V_\Omega,
\qquad
U_pf:=\sum_{\alpha\in J}f_\alpha\Omega_\alpha
\tag{BF2}
$$

is unitary and sends \(1\) to \(\Omega\). Hence

$$
\boxed{
\mathcal B_\Omega
=
U_pL^2_0(J,p),
\qquad
L^2_0(J,p):=
\left\{f:\sum_\alpha p_\alpha f_\alpha=0\right\}.}
\tag{BF3}
$$

The commutative algebra generated by the \(E_\alpha\) sees the restriction of
the pointed state as the probability law \(p\). Equation (BF3) is stronger
than a dimension count: it gives the balance sector its canonical norm.
For finite \(J\), it is nonzero exactly when at least two block components
of the vacuum are nonzero. This is one exact, scoped meaning of
“distinction requires ambiguity.”

## The real balance space is the Fisher tangent

The real Fisher tangent of the fixed-support probability simplex at \(p\) is

$$
T_p^F\Delta_J
:=
\left\{
v:\sum_\alpha v_\alpha=0,\quad
\sum_\alpha\frac{v_\alpha^2}{p_\alpha}<\infty
\right\},
\tag{BF4}
$$

with score \(s_\alpha=v_\alpha/p_\alpha\) and metric

$$
g_p^F(v,w)
=
\sum_\alpha\frac{v_\alpha w_\alpha}{p_\alpha}
=
\langle s,t\rangle_{L^2(p)}.
\tag{BF5}
$$

Therefore

$$
\boxed{
v\longmapsto U_p(v/p)}
\tag{BF6}
$$

is an isometry from \(T_p^F\Delta_J\) onto the real amplitude-aligned space

$$
\mathcal B_\Omega^{\mathbb R}
:=
U_pL^2_0(J,p;\mathbb R).
\tag{BF7}
$$

The factor convention matters. The normalized Born-amplitude embedding is

$$
A(q):=\sum_\alpha\sqrt{q_\alpha}\,u_\alpha,
\tag{BF8}
$$

so

$$
\mathrm dA_p(v)
=
\frac12U_p(v/p),
\qquad
\|\mathrm dA_p(v)\|^2
=
\frac14g_p^F(v,v).
\tag{BF9}
$$

Thus \(2A\), not \(A\), is the isometric Hellinger embedding used elsewhere
in the workspace.

For an explicit normalized amplitude path, let
\(f\in L^2_0(J,p;\mathbb R)\) be bounded and define

$$
Z_f(t):=\sum_\alpha p_\alpha e^{2tf_\alpha},
\qquad
p_\alpha(t):=\frac{p_\alpha e^{2tf_\alpha}}{Z_f(t)},
\tag{BF10}
$$

$$
\Omega_f(t)
:=
\sum_\alpha\sqrt{p_\alpha(t)}\,u_\alpha
=
\frac{1}{\sqrt{Z_f(t)}}
\sum_\alpha e^{tf_\alpha}\Omega_\alpha.
\tag{BF11}
$$

At \(t=0\),

$$
\dot\Omega_f(0)=U_pf,
\qquad
\dot p_\alpha(0)=2p_\alpha f_\alpha,
\tag{BF12}
$$

and hence

$$
\boxed{
g_p^F(\dot p,\dot p)
=
4\|U_pf\|^2.}
\tag{BF13}
$$

The relative entropy has the coincidence expansion

$$
D(p(t)\Vert p)
=
\frac{t^2}{2}g_p^F(\dot p,\dot p)+o(t^2),
\tag{BF14}
$$

so its Hessian on an actual real amplitude velocity \(b=U_pf\) is

$$
\left.\frac{\mathrm d^2}{\mathrm dt^2}
D(p(t)\Vert p)\right|_{t=0}
=
4\|b\|^2.
\tag{BF15}
$$

This is the relative-entropy Hessian of the chosen block readout. It is not
automatically the BKM metric of the underlying noncommutative state space.
For countably infinite \(J\), (BF4)--(BF7) describe the Hilbert completion of
regular score tangents; an exponential path additionally needs local
exponential integrability.

## The imaginary balance space is phase, not Fisher information

As a real Hilbert space,

$$
\mathcal B_\Omega
=
\mathcal B_\Omega^{\mathbb R}
\oplus
i\mathcal B_\Omega^{\mathbb R}.
\tag{BF16}
$$

For real centered \(h\), the path

$$
\widetilde\Omega_h(t)
:=
\sum_\alpha e^{ith_\alpha}\Omega_\alpha
\tag{BF17}
$$

has velocity \(iU_ph\), but every block weight remains exactly
\(p_\alpha\). Classical Fisher geometry therefore sees the real summand and
annihilates the phase summand. Cross-block observables may still see those
phases.

If the \(E_\alpha\) are genuinely central superselection projections,
relative phases are operationally absent and a pure vacuum normally belongs
to one global sector. The boundary-charge labels in the gauge construction
must instead be shown to remain internal cut channels of one globally
neutral sector. Otherwise the balance space must not be advertised as a
physical coherent excitation.

## The complete transfer defect

Let \(T\) be a normalized positive self-adjoint physical transfer,
\(0\leq T\leq I\), with \(T\Omega=\Omega\). Write

$$
\Omega^\perp
=
\mathcal B_\Omega\oplus\mathcal I_\Omega,
\qquad
\mathcal I_\Omega
:=
\widehat\bigoplus_\alpha\mathcal H_\alpha^\circ,
\tag{BF18}
$$

and represent the centered transfer as

$$
T_0
:=
T|_{\Omega^\perp}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
A&X\\
X^*&D
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{BF19}
$$

Put \(a=\|A\|\), \(d=\|D\|\), and \(\kappa=\|X\|\). Comparing its quadratic
form with the scalar matrix gives

$$
\boxed{
\|T_0\|
\leq
\frac{
a+d+\sqrt{(a-d)^2+4\kappa^2}
}{2}.}
\tag{BF20}
$$

Consequently,

$$
a<1,
\qquad
d<1,
\qquad
\kappa^2<(1-a)(1-d)
\tag{BF21}
$$

is a sufficient two-sector stopping condition. A balance contraction by
itself cannot certify the full vacuum-complement gap.

There is a sharper geometric reading of the cross term. If \(d<1\), the
short of the full defect \(I-T_0\) to the balance carrier is

$$
\boxed{
S_{\mathcal B}
:=
(I-A)-X(I-D)^{-1}X^*.}
\tag{BF22}
$$

It satisfies

$$
\langle b,S_{\mathcal B}b\rangle
=
\inf_{i\in\mathcal I_\Omega}
\langle b+i,(I-T_0)(b+i)\rangle.
\tag{BF23}
$$

The effective operator therefore acts on a fixed between-block distinction
after minimizing over every compatible internally centered extension.
Leaving the balance carrier and returning through internal modes subtracts
coercivity:

$$
S_{\mathcal B}
\geq
\left[
(1-a)-\frac{\kappa^2}{1-d}
\right]I_{\mathcal B}.
\tag{BF24}
$$

This is a genuine reversal tactic: one does not assign a mass to the block
label. One asks for the least full physical transfer defect compatible with
that visible balance distinction. Even this short is a dimensionless
one-slab defect, not yet energy or mass.

## A compressed balance motion need not be a process

Suppose the physical transfer semigroup is

$$
T_\ell=e^{-\ell H/(\hbar c)}.
\tag{BF25}
$$

If \(\mathcal B_\Omega\) reduces every \(T_\ell\), then

$$
T_\ell|_{\mathcal B_\Omega}
=
e^{-\ell H_{\mathcal B}/(\hbar c)},
\qquad
\gamma_{\mathcal B}
:=
-\frac1\ell\log\|T_\ell|_{\mathcal B_\Omega}\|
=
\frac{\inf\sigma(H_{\mathcal B})}{\hbar c}.
\tag{BF26}
$$

Without reduction, the compression
\(A_\ell=Q_{\mathrm{bal}}T_\ell Q_{\mathrm{bal}}\) is not generally a
semigroup. With \(Q_{\mathrm{int}}\) projecting onto
\(\mathcal I_\Omega\),

$$
\boxed{
A_{2\ell}
=
A_\ell^2
+
(Q_{\mathrm{bal}}T_\ell Q_{\mathrm{int}})
(Q_{\mathrm{int}}T_\ell Q_{\mathrm{bal}}).}
\tag{BF27}
$$

The second term is the positive leave-and-return contribution. Therefore
\(-\ell^{-1}\log\|A_\ell\|\) is not generally the edge of a balance
generator. Even when balance reduces the semigroup, its edge proves nothing
about a soft internal mode; the complete matrix remains necessary.

## When the Fisher face is genuinely Markov

The compression

$$
K:=U_p^*TU_p
\tag{BF28}
$$

fixes \(1\) and preserves the \(p\)-mean. It becomes a reversible Markov
operator on the block labels only if it also preserves the real positive
cone. In the discrete case this means that

$$
(Kf)_\alpha
=
\sum_\beta
\frac{\langle\Omega_\alpha,T\Omega_\beta\rangle}{p_\alpha}
f_\beta
\tag{BF29}
$$

has nonnegative kernel entries. Hilbert positivity \(T\geq0\) does not imply
this order positivity. Exact label dynamics further requires invariance of
\(\mathcal V_\Omega\); otherwise
\((U_p^*TU_p)^n\neq U_p^*T^nU_p\). In a classical partition, this is the
lumpability condition.

When these hypotheses hold, a centered score \(f\) is sent to \(Kf\), so

$$
g_p^F(pKf,pKf)
=
\|Kf\|_{L^2(p)}^2
\leq
\rho_K^2\|f\|_{L^2(p)}^2
=
\rho_K^2 g_p^F(pf,pf),
\qquad
\rho_K:=\|K|_{L^2_0(p)}\|.
\tag{BF30}
$$

The amplitude attenuation exponent is
\(-\ell^{-1}\log\rho_K\), while the quadratic Fisher contraction exponent
is twice that value. The GNS defect \(I-K\), the Fisher data-processing
defect \(I-K^*K\), and the logarithmic generator \(-\log K\) are three
different operators, as the binary counterexample in
[[binary-information-geometry/binary-channel-carrier|The Binary Channel as
a Carrier Test]] already shows.

There is one legitimate physical Markov presentation. If the actual OS
transfer is positivity preserving on \(L^2(\mu)\), has a strictly positive
vacuum \(\Omega\), and is self-adjoint, then the ground-state transform

$$
G_\Omega f:=\Omega f,
\qquad
\mathsf K_\ell:=G_\Omega^{-1}T_\ell G_\Omega
\tag{BF31}
$$

is a reversible Markov semigroup on
\(\mathrm d\nu=\Omega^2\,\mathrm d\mu\), unitarily equivalent to the
physical transfer and with the same spectrum. An arbitrary sampler sharing
the same stationary law is not such a presentation. A further lumpability
theorem is still needed before the block labels alone form a Markov process.

## Static ambiguity does not select a rate

The block law \(p\), its Fisher metric, and the whole vacuum-aligned
decomposition contain no clock normalization. Indeed, for every
\(\gamma\geq0\),

$$
T_\ell^{(\gamma)}
:=
P_\Omega
+
e^{-\gamma\ell}(I-P_\Omega)
\tag{BF32}
$$

is a positive self-adjoint semigroup with the same vacuum, block weights,
balance carrier, and Fisher geometry, but an arbitrarily chosen centered
rate \(\gamma\). Entropy geometry supplies a metric on distinctions; it
does not choose how quickly a physical dynamics traverses them.

The Yang--Mills stopping condition therefore remains

$$
\limsup_{a\downarrow0}\sup_{L,\mathsf s}
\|C_{a,L,\mathsf s}^{(\ell_*)}\|_{2\to2}
\leq q_*<1,
\tag{BF33}
$$

where \(C\) is the complete vacuum-aligned physical transfer matrix,
including balance-to-internal blocks. It gives

$$
\Delta_E
\geq
-\frac{\hbar c}{\ell_*}\log q_*.
\tag{BF34}
$$

This becomes a mass statement only after OS/Poincare reconstruction
identifies the carrier, translations, vacuum, and invariant Casimir. The
changing-carrier version is
[[vacuum-aligned-transfer-cocycle/inq|the ordered complete-matrix product]]:
complementary stages may each have norm one while their joint product
contracts the whole vacuum complement. Its logarithm per fixed physical slab
is an attenuation rate, not yet a Hamiltonian generator.
