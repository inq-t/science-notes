# Two Species of Reason

A necessitating reason fixes a contextual value, whereas a sufficing reason fixes a probability assignment without fixing the value realized. Gleason constrains the form of the latter; Bell and Kochen--Specker constrain attempts to replace it by predetermined values. These theorems support the distinction only under explicit hypotheses and do not by themselves prove that two metaphysical species of ground exist.

## Definitions

Fix a noncommutative algebra $\mathcal M$, a commutative readout context $\mathcal D\subseteq\mathcal M$, and its spectrum $X_{\mathcal D}$.

> **Necessitating reason.** A ground sufficient for which contextual fact obtains: formally, enough structure to determine a character $\chi\in X_{\mathcal D}$.

> **Sufficing reason.** A ground sufficient to determine a coherent probability law $\mu$ on $X_{\mathcal D}$, but insufficient to determine a character from that law alone.

The names specify explanatory termini, not causal mechanisms. A group action can transport a determined point reversibly, but group structure is not part of the bare definition of a necessitating reason. Likewise, a probability measure is not itself a semigroup. The dynamical distinction between groups and one-sided semigroups is an additional thesis developed in [[algebraic-arrow-of-time]].

## What Gleason fixes

Let $\mathcal H$ be a Hilbert space with $\dim\mathcal H\ge3$. Under the usual additivity and normalization assumptions, Gleason's theorem says that a probability assignment $m$ on projections has the form

$$
m(P)=\operatorname{Tr}(\rho P)
$$

for a unique density operator $\rho$.

The theorem fixes the *form* of every admissible projection-probability assignment. It does not select a particular $\rho$, prove that probability is ontologically fundamental, or select an outcome. Rigidity of form is therefore compatible with insufficiency for content.

The projection-valued theorem has a genuine two-dimensional exception. Qubit versions can recover Born form by enlarging the measurement domain, for example to suitable POVMs, but that uses stronger hypotheses. Any foundational claim must state which version it needs.

## What the no-go theorems exclude

Kochen--Specker excludes a global noncontextual valuation satisfying the functional relations among observables in dimension at least three. In presheaf language, the compatible global section does not exist.

Bell excludes a locally causal hidden-variable completion reproducing the relevant quantum correlations, given the other assumptions entering a Bell experiment. It does not exclude every deterministic completion: Bohmian mechanics retains determination by accepting nonlocality.

The disciplined conclusion is conditional:

> A probability assignment cannot be upgraded to a single global value assignment while retaining the locality or noncontextuality conditions ruled out by Bell and Kochen--Specker.

The stronger conclusion—there are exactly two irreducible species of reason—is the proposed philosophical reading of these constraints. It is not the literal statement of either theorem.

## Ignorance and structure

An ordinary ignorance distribution presupposes a space of determinate underlying possibilities. A quantum state also yields classical probability measures after restriction to each commutative context, but Kochen--Specker obstructs combining those contextual measures with one global noncontextual valuation. The difference is therefore not that quantum probabilities lack all possible hidden-variable interpretations; it is that a classical ignorance interpretation cannot be imposed globally while preserving all the targeted structural conditions.
