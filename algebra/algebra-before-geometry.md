# Algebra Before Geometry

Algebra can ground geometry only after the admissible operations, morphisms, probes, covers, and realization functors have been fixed or derived. The first-principles object is therefore not one privileged algebra but an algebraic doctrine whose generalized points determine what counts as distinction and whose topology determines what counts as local. A uniqueness theorem inside such a doctrine can establish conditional mathematical necessity; it cannot silently turn an unselected doctrine into physically instantiated nature.

## Geometry is algebra seen by probes

For a commutative ring $A$, the affine scheme

$$
X=\operatorname{Spec}A
$$

is not exhausted by its ordinary points. Its functor of points is

$$
h_X(R)
=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathsf{CAlg}}(A,R).
$$

The Yoneda lemma says that the full functor $h_X$ determines $X$ up to unique isomorphism. Generalized probes can detect structure that a coarse point set misses. For example, $\operatorname{Spec}k[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2)$ has one underlying topological point but a nonzero infinitesimal direction. Likewise, a stack such as $BG$ may have one coarse point while retaining the whole stabilizer group $G$.

This corrects the idea that difference must begin as a multiplicity of ordinary points. The more invariant question is whether the represented object is terminal or contractible under a declared separating family of generalized probes.

## The algebra-selection ledger

Before an algebra can be called fundamental, the following choices must be exposed:

| Datum | Question that must be answered |
|---|---|
| scalars | field, ring, semiring, spectrum, or internal scalar object? |
| multiplication | associative, alternative, Jordan, operadic, higher, or partial? |
| unit and grading | required, emergent, or absent? |
| involution and order | what makes adjoints, positivity, and states meaningful? |
| enrichment and completion | algebraic, topological, bornological, $C^*$, $W^*$, or derived? |
| tensor product | which notion expresses independent composition? |
| morphisms | homomorphisms, correspondences, bimodules, channels, or all with distinct roles? |
| equivalences | isomorphism, Morita equivalence, $KK$-equivalence, quasi-isomorphism, or physical equivalence? |
| probes | characters, prime ideals, modules, representations, states, contexts, or test algebras? |
| covers | Zariski, étale, fppf, operator-algebraic, causal, or another topology? |
| realization | which functor produces metric, causal, spectral, or factual structure? |

Different answers define different geometries. They cannot be mixed merely because the same group or integer appears in each.

The current [[program-core/entry|program core]] begins well inside one branch of this ledger: complex associative operator algebras, states, completely positive maps, relative entropy, differentiability, and causal area are already available. The octonions belong to an alternative nonassociative branch. Connes' ordinary spectral triples use associative involutive algebras. A map between those branches is an additional construction, not a harmless change of notation.

## No most general algebra

The octonions are the largest-dimensional and final listed member of the finite-dimensional real normed division algebras in the Hurwitz classification. This is not categorical terminality: unital embeddings into \(\mathbb O\) are nonunique. It also does not make the octonions “the most general algebra.” Removing finite dimensionality, a norm, division, alternativity, or the real scalar field produces vastly broader classes. Even within nonassociative algebra, Jordan, Lie, operadic, and higher-categorical structures are not specializations of one octonionic universal object in the required sense.

There is nevertheless a precise and striking core to the chat intuition. After choices of compatible imaginary units, one obtains noncanonical embedded chains

$$
\mathbb R\subset\mathbb C_u\subset\mathbb H_{u,v}\subset\mathbb O.
$$

Thus the octonions contain representatives of the earlier real normed division algebras, but the embeddings and preferred subalgebras are additional choices. The exact octonionic facts also include

$$
S^6=\{u\in\operatorname{Im}\mathbb O:\|u\|=1\}
\cong G_2/SU(3),
$$

and the long roots of $G_2$ form an $A_2$ subsystem. But $A_2$ does not define all of $G_2$, and the octonionic almost-complex structure on $S^6$ is not integrable. These facts motivate a search for a functor; they do not supply it.

## Locality is additional structure

The word *local* has no content until a class of covers has been declared. A Grothendieck topology $J$ on a category of tests says which families

$$
\{U_i\to U\}
$$

are jointly sufficient presentations of $U$. A stack then asks for descent relative to $J$. Changing $J$ changes which objects become locally trivial and which obstructions remain global. [[basic-concepts/descent/entry|Strict descent]] therefore cannot be inferred from a diagram that merely looks layered or from a map that discards information.

For a physical doctrine, the site itself is part of the derivation burden. If its objects are causal regions, the theory has already imported causal localization. If they are metric balls, it has imported metric geometry. If they are commutative readout contexts, it has imported an observational type. Such choices may be legitimate constitutive starting points, but they are not derivations of those structures from pure algebra.

## What necessity can mean

Mathematics can prove necessity only relative to a selected doctrine and explicit axioms. A universal property can force an object up to the appropriate equivalence, but only after its category, variance, and mapping condition have been stated. It does not by itself prove that the axioms are uniquely unavoidable, that the category is physically instantiated, or that a dimensionful calibration agrees with nature. [[algebra/theorem-programme|The algebra-to-cosmos theorem programme]] owns the precise representability schema and recovery gates.

Choosing an algebra because it reproduces the desired output is the freedom-displacement failure described in [[program-core/explanatory-economy|explanatory economy]]. The first-principles task is to show why the algebraic doctrine is forced before asking what physics it can imitate; the theorem programme then tests noncircular realization and empirical return values separately.
