The "stacky $\mathbb{P}^1$ thing" refers to an algebraic stack or orbifold topologically shaped like a Riemann sphere ($\mathbb{P}^1$), but containing special points with fractional symmetries (isotropy). [1, 2]

In Levent Alpöge’s proof, this geometry acts as the base engine for building the complex structure on the 6-sphere ($S^6$). [1]

## 1. What is a Stacky $\mathbb{P}^1$?

A normal complex projective line ($\mathbb{P}^1$) is just a smooth sphere. A stacky $\mathbb{P}^1$ is a sphere with "memory" of group actions at specific points: [2]

- The Points: It typically features three special points that act like the vertices of a triangle.
    
- The Symmetries: At these points, the geometric space behaves as if it has been divided by a finite symmetry group (like $\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$, $\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}$, or $\mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$).
    
- The Modular Connection: The most famous classical example is the moduli stack of elliptic curves, $\mathcal{M}_{1,1}$. Its base is a stacky $\mathbb{P}^1$ governed by the modular group—a triangle group. [1, 2, 3]
    

## 2. The Universal Family of Tori

Over this stacky $\mathbb{P}^1$ base, Alpöge erects a universal family of 2-dimensional tori ($T^2$). [1]

- The Bundle: For every point on the sphere, there sits a distinct torus. This creates a 4-dimensional geometric space (2D base + 2D fiber). [1]
    
- The Degeneration: As you slide across the base sphere toward the three stacky "triangle corners," the tori fibers do not remain uniform. They twist, rotate, and experience extra automorphisms dictated precisely by those fractional symmetries. [2]
    

## 3. How This Solves the 6-Sphere ($S^6$)

To turn this 4-dimensional family into a 6-dimensional complex space, Alpöge utilizes a specific compactification strategy: [1]

- The Gluing: He punctures three neighborhoods on this space and glues them back together using explicit holomorphic transition maps. [3]
    
- The Topology Check: By calculating the fundamental group and integral homology, he proves that the resulting smooth complex 3-fold ($X$) is simply connected and has the exact homology of a 6-sphere. [3]
    
- The Poincaré Finale: Using Stephen Smale's high-dimensional Poincaré Conjecture, he proves that this complex space $X$ is smoothly diffeomorphic to the standard $S^6$, successfully transferring a complex structure to it. [3]
    

## The Non-Trivial Monodromy Link

Crucially, previous attempts to solve the $S^6$ problem failed because they assumed the geometry required trivial monodromy. Alpöge’s family explicitly incorporates non-trivial monodromy. As you loop around the punctures on his stacky $\mathbb{P}^1$, the tori fibers switch places and braid. [3]

This braiding is governed by the exact same order-6 $S_3$ Weyl monodromy found in Ruble’s July 2026 Jacobian counterexample. The universal sheets of the Jacobian cubic and the universal tori of Alpöge's $S^6$ are two sides of the exact same geometric coin: they use the fractional, triangular symmetries of a stacky base to cleanly stitch higher-dimensional spaces together. [1, 3]
