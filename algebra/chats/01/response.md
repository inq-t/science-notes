
Yes, there is a well-defined monodromy around an A₂ singularity, and it behaves as a fundamental building block in algebraic geometry, singularity theory, and integrable physics systems. [1, 2]

Because the A₂ singularity is a simple isolated hypersurface singularity (defined locally by f(x,y) = x³ + y² = 0), you can look at its monodromy through two main lenses: algebraic/geometric topology and Hamiltonian physics. [1, 3]

## 1. The Monodromy Group and Operators (Topology & Geometry)

When you smoothly deform (perturb) the A₂ singularity into a smooth Milnor fiber, you can track how the homology cycles of that fiber transform as you loop around the singular values in the parameter space. [2]

- The Milnor Fiber: For A₂, the Milnor fiber has a rank-2 homology group (H₁ or H₂ depending on the ambient dimensions) spanned by two vanishing cycles that intersect exactly once. [2, 3]
    
- The Monodromy Group: The full local monodromy group is isomorphic to the Weyl group of A₂, which is the symmetric group S₃ (the isometry group of an equilateral triangle). [2, 4]
    
- The Monodromy Operator (Picard-Lefschetz): Looping around individual simple critical values acts as a Picard-Lefschetz reflection (a Dehn twist) on the vanishing cycles. [2, 5]
    
- The Algebraic Monodromy Matrix: The total monodromy operator T acting on the cycles has an order of 6 (T⁶ = I). Its eigenvalues are the primitive 6th roots of unity, $e^{2\pi i/6}$ and $e^{4\pi i/6}$. [3]
    

## 2. Hamiltonian Monodromy (Physics)

In physics, particularly in classical and quantum integrable systems (like multi-spin systems or the [Tavis-Cummings model](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.14835)), A₂ singularities show up in the energy-momentum bifurcation diagrams of singular Lagrangian fibrations. [1]

- What it means physically: If you prepare a system in a regular torus action and continuously change the control parameters in a closed loop around the A₂ singularity, the action variables fail to return to their original values. [1, 6]
    
- The Effect: The action coordinates undergo a global twist dictated by the S₃ Weyl action, meaning the geometric lattice of quantum states shifts systematically when completing the parameter cycle. [2]
