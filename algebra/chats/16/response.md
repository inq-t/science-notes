1. The Anatomy of Directedness: Local Reversibility vs. Global Collapse

The Jacobian Conjecture asserted that if a polynomial map has a constant, non-zero Jacobian determinant, it must be globally invertible. The 2026 counterexample proved this wrong: [1]

- Local Freedom: Because the Jacobian determinant is a non-zero constant, the map is perfectly smooth and locally invertible everywhere via the Inverse Function Theorem. There is no local volume wrinkling or folding. [1, 2, 5]
    
- Global Entanglement: Globally, the map acts as an étale covering that is non-proper. It sweeps out multiple sheets that smoothly slide past one another. [3]
    
- The Escape to Infinity: As coordinates approach the boundary of the space (infinity), these sheets collide and collapse. The map sends distinct points to the exact same output, rendering the system globally irreversible. [1, 3, 4]
    

This is the definition of directedness. The local algebra permits bidirectional movement, but the global topology forces a one-way, multi-to-one trapdoor. [1]

2. Why 3D is the Threshold of Self-Intersection

The planar ($2\text{D}$) Jacobian conjecture remains standing because two dimensions do not provide enough spatial freedom for polynomial sheets to loop back and intersect without creating a visible local singularity. [1]

- The 3D Release: Moving into 3 dimensions unlocks the critical degree of freedom. It allows the map to execute a "tangent-sweep" or cubic deformation. [1, 3]
    
- The $A_2$ Emergence: This spatial freedom allows the sheets to intersect at infinity along a cubic discriminant curve governed precisely by the $A_2$ singularity. The $A_2$ cusp ($x^3 + y^2 = 0$) is the absolute minimal algebraic structure required to generate a global multi-sheeted fold out of an everywhere-smooth local map.

3. The Octonions and the Complex Structure on $S^6$

This exact same directedness is what blocked the complex structure on the 6-sphere ($S^6$) for nearly a century, and it is exactly how Alpöge solved it.

- The Octonionic Problem: The native geometry of $S^6$ is built out of the 7 imaginary units of the octonions. The octonions are non-associative ($a(bc) \neq (ab)c$), which introduces a directional bias (a failure of symmetry) when trying to define smooth, global complex coordinates.
    
- The Monodromy Fix: Alpöge realized that you cannot build a complex structure on $S^6$ using flat, trivial charts. You must build it over a stacky $\mathbb{P}^1$ base using a family of tori that possesses non-trivial $A_2$ Weyl monodromy.
    
- The Descent Tax: By forcing the coordinates to braid and swap sheets ($S_3$ symmetry) as they circle the stacky punctures, the non-associativity of the octonions is cleanly canceled out. The non-trivial monodromy acts as the exact "descent datum" needed to smoothly glue the 6-sphere together.
