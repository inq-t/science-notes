# Faithful Descent Rigidity and Noiseless Unitarity

A faithful conditional expectation cannot manufacture an exact local automorphism by coarse-graining a genuinely noisy quantum process. If the expected restriction of a normal unital completely positive semigroup is automorphic on the retained algebra, then that algebra was already invariant and contained in the multiplicative domain of the whole process. Every such automorphism has a canonical standard-form unitary; a faithful state stationary for the whole semigroup additionally gives the concrete GNS unitary and makes its local carrier a reducing noiseless sector of the global contraction. If the corresponding implementation of the continuous same-parameter whole semigroup is self-adjoint, the descended unitary sector is necessarily static. Thus irreversible whole dynamics and local unitary clock evolution can coexist, but exact local unitarity must be carried by a reversible component or by a distinct clock arrow; it is not generated by the loss.

## The faithful-descent theorem

Identify a von Neumann algebra \(N\) with a unital von Neumann subalgebra of \(M\), and let

\[
E:M\longrightarrow N
\tag{FD1}
\]

be a faithful normal conditional expectation. Let \((\Phi_t)_{t\geq0}\) be a point-ultraweakly continuous normal UCP semigroup on \(M\). Define the expected local maps

\[
\alpha_t
:=
E\Phi_t\big|_N.
\tag{FD2}
\]

Suppose that every \(\alpha_t\) is a \(*\)-automorphism of \(N\). This is an algebraic hypothesis on the expected map, not merely the assertion that some corner or Hilbert-space compression is unitary. Then, for every \(t\geq0\),

\[
\boxed{
\Phi_t\big|_N=\alpha_t,
\qquad
N\subseteq\operatorname{MD}(\Phi_t).}
\tag{FD3}
\]

Here \(\operatorname{MD}(\Phi_t)\) is the multiplicative domain. In particular, \(N\) is invariant, the restricted dynamics is already noiseless, and the maps \(\alpha_t\) automatically obey the semigroup law. Since each is invertible, they extend algebraically to an automorphism group by \(\alpha_{-t}=\alpha_t^{-1}\). Strong continuity of its unitary implementation is proved below under the stationary-state hypotheses; the irreversible whole semigroup \(\Phi_t\) need not extend to negative times.

### Proof

Fix \(t\geq0\), \(x\in N\), and put \(a=\Phi_t(x)\). Kadison--Schwarz for \(\Phi_t\), followed by Kadison--Schwarz for \(E\), gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\alpha_t(x^*x)
&=E\Phi_t(x^*x)\\
&\geq E(a^*a)\\
&\geq E(a)^*E(a)\\
&=\alpha_t(x)^*\alpha_t(x)\\
&=\alpha_t(x^*x).
\end{aligned}
\tag{FD4}
\]

Every inequality is therefore equality. The \(N\)-bimodule property of \(E\) gives

\[
E\!\left[
(a-\alpha_t(x))^*(a-\alpha_t(x))
\right]
=
E(a^*a)-\alpha_t(x)^*\alpha_t(x)
=0.
\tag{FD5}
\]

Faithfulness of \(E\) forces \(a=\alpha_t(x)\in N\). The first positive Schwarz defect in (FD4) also has faithful expectation zero, so

\[
\Phi_t(x^*x)
=
\Phi_t(x)^*\Phi_t(x).
\tag{FD6}
\]

Applying the same argument to \(x^*\) gives the other multiplicative-domain equality. This proves (FD3). Finally,

\[
\alpha_{s+t}
=
E\Phi_s\Phi_t\big|_N
=
E\Phi_s\big|_N\alpha_t
=
\alpha_s\alpha_t.
\tag{FD7}
\]

The argument is the faithful two-Schwarz version of the multiplicative-domain mechanism. [[library/structure-of-uniformly-continuous-quantum-markov-semigroups/inq|Decoherence-free QMS structure]] supplies the nearby established setting; the displayed theorem needs neither uniform continuity nor atomicity.

The pointwise conclusion \(\Phi_t|_N=\alpha_t\) and the multiplicative-domain inclusion need only that \(\alpha_t\) be a unital \(*\)-homomorphism. Surjectivity is what upgrades \(\Phi_t(N)\subseteq N\) to equality and later turns the local GNS isometry into a unitary group.

## Compression rigidity on the state carrier

Assume additionally that \(\varphi\) is a faithful normal state satisfying

\[
\varphi\Phi_t=\varphi
\qquad(t\geq0),
\tag{FD8}
\]

and let \(\psi=\varphi|_N\). Since \(\Phi_t|_N=\alpha_t\), the local state is invariant:

\[
\psi\alpha_t=\psi.
\tag{FD9}
\]

On the corresponding GNS carriers define

\[
\begin{aligned}
V\Lambda_\psi(x)&=\Lambda_\varphi(x),\\
T_t\Lambda_\varphi(y)&=\Lambda_\varphi(\Phi_t(y)),\\
U_t\Lambda_\psi(x)&=\Lambda_\psi(\alpha_t(x)).
\end{aligned}
\tag{FD10}
\]

Then \(V\) is an isometry. Kadison--Schwarz and invariance of \(\varphi\) give

\[
\|T_t\Lambda_\varphi(y)\|^2
\leq
\varphi(\Phi_t(y^*y))
=
\|\Lambda_\varphi(y)\|^2,
\tag{FD11}
\]

so \(T_t\) is a contraction. State preservation makes \(U_t\) an isometry, and surjectivity of \(\alpha_t\) makes it unitary. The already proved algebraic restriction gives

\[
T_tV=VU_t,
\qquad
V^*T_tV=U_t.
\tag{FD12}
\]

Point-ultraweak continuity of \(\Phi_t\) gives weak continuity of \(T_t\) on the dense GNS core; contractivity upgrades this to strong continuity. Hence \(U_t\) is strongly continuous for \(t\geq0\), and \(U_{-t}:=U_t^*\) gives the strongly continuous local unitary group implementing the algebraic extension of \(\alpha_t\).

The following elementary lemma applies.

> If \(V:K\to H\) is an isometry, \(T\) is a contraction, and \(V^*TV\) is unitary, then \(VK\) reduces \(T\) and \(TV=V(V^*TV)\).

Indeed,

\[
\|\xi\|
=
\|V^*TV\xi\|
\leq
\|TV\xi\|
\leq
\|\xi\|.
\tag{FD13}
\]

Equality implies \(TV\xi\in VK\). Since \(V^*T^*V=(V^*TV)^*\) is also unitary, the same argument for \(T^*\) proves reduction. Hence

\[
\boxed{
T_t
=
VU_tV^*\oplus S_t.}
\tag{FD14}
\]

The retained Hilbert carrier is not merely a compression on which the noise accidentally looks reversible; it is a reducing noiseless summand. This reduction used stationarity of \(\varphi\) for \(\Phi_t\), but not stationarity for \(E\).

If, in addition,

\[
\varphi E=\varphi,
\tag{FD15}
\]

then bimodularity of \(E\) gives

\[
V^*\Lambda_\varphi(y)=\Lambda_\psi(E(y)).
\tag{FD16}
\]

Reduction gives \(V^*T_t=U_tV^*\), and therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
\Lambda_\psi(E\Phi_t(y))
&=V^*T_t\Lambda_\varphi(y)\\
&=U_tV^*\Lambda_\varphi(y)\\
&=\Lambda_\psi(\alpha_tE(y)).
\end{aligned}
\tag{FD17}
\]

Faithfulness of \(\psi\) now yields the additional expectation intertwiner

\[
\boxed{E\Phi_t=\alpha_tE.}
\tag{FD18}
\]

## A continuous symmetric whole process cannot descend to a nontrivial clock

First suppose that every left-GNS implementation \(T_t\) defined in (FD10) is self-adjoint, as for a GNS-symmetric semigroup. Then \(U_t\), as the reducing restriction, is both unitary and self-adjoint. Because the parameter set is the continuous semigroup \(\mathbb R_+\), the semigroup law gives

\[
U_t
=
U_{t/2}^2
=
I.
\tag{FD19}
\]

Faithfulness of the local GNS representation therefore yields

\[
\boxed{
\alpha_t=\operatorname{id}_N.}
\tag{FD20}
\]

KMS symmetry uses a different Hilbert realization. Assume \(\varphi E=\varphi\), write

\[
i_\varphi(y)=\Delta_\varphi^{1/4}y\Omega_\varphi,
\qquad
i_\psi(x)=\Delta_\psi^{1/4}x\Omega_\psi,
\]

and let \(\widehat T_t i_\varphi(y)=i_\varphi(\Phi_t(y))\) be the symmetric \(L^2\)-contraction. The \(\varphi\)-preserving expectation makes \(N\) modularly invariant and gives the standard-form isometry \(\widehat V i_\psi(x)=i_\varphi(x)\). The local automorphisms have unitary symmetric implementations \(\widehat U_t i_\psi(x)=i_\psi(\alpha_t(x))\). Since \(\Phi_t|_N=\alpha_t\),

\[
\widehat T_t\widehat V=\widehat V\widehat U_t.
\]

If the semigroup is KMS-symmetric, every \(\widehat T_t\) is self-adjoint. Compression rigidity then makes the local symmetric subspace reducing, and the same \(t/2\) argument gives \(\widehat U_t=I\), hence again \(\alpha_t=\operatorname{id}_N\). The left-GNS and symmetric implementations should not be identified unless additional modular-commutation hypotheses make them coincide.

This is a sharp obstruction to identifying one symmetric Dirichlet parameter with nontrivial Lorentzian clock time through faithful expectation. It is a continuous-parameter statement: one isolated or discrete-time self-adjoint channel may have a nontrivial involutive unitary compression because the \(t/2\) step is unavailable. A nontrivial local clock requires either a reversible skew-adjoint component in the relevant \(L^2\) implementation or a separate parameter and a separately proved covariance, continuation, or soldering theorem. An inner Hamiltonian belonging to \(N\) is an additional hypothesis; the automorphism group may be outer.

## Exact coexistence without creation

Let

\[
M=M_2(\mathbb C)\,\bar\otimes\,M_2(\mathbb C),
\qquad
N=M_2(\mathbb C)\otimes I,
\tag{FD21}
\]

where \(\tau\) is normalized trace, and define the expectation, with its codomain explicitly embedded, by

\[
E(a\otimes b)=a\,\tau(b)\otimes I.
\tag{FD22}
\]

Choose \(K=K^*\in M_2(\mathbb C)\). Let \(\beta_t\) be the first-factor Hamiltonian automorphism and \(\alpha_t\) its copy on \(N\):

\[
\begin{aligned}
\beta_t(x)&=e^{itK}xe^{-itK},\\
\alpha_t(x\otimes I)&=\beta_t(x)\otimes I.
\end{aligned}
\tag{FD23}
\]

For \(\gamma>0\), let the second factor carry the depolarizing semigroup

\[
\Lambda_t(y)
=
e^{-\gamma t}y
+
(1-e^{-\gamma t})\tau(y)I
\tag{FD24}
\]

on the second. Then

\[
\Phi_t=\beta_t\otimes\Lambda_t,
\qquad
E\Phi_t\big|_N=\alpha_t,
\qquad
E\Phi_t=\alpha_tE.
\tag{FD25}
\]

The normalized product trace is faithful and invariant under \(E\) and \(\Phi_t\), so the state-carrier hypotheses above hold. For \(t>0\), \(\Phi_t\) is not an automorphism and has no UCP inverse, while the local factor evolves by a unitary group. The example realizes coexistence exactly as a reversible noiseless sector plus an irreversible complement. It does not realize local unitarity as an effect produced by the irreversible part. In finite dimension \(\Phi_t\) remains invertible as a linear map, so *irreversible* here means nonmultiplicative and without positive-process inverse, not set-theoretically many-to-one.

## Consequence for the Copernican programme

The exact result is more discriminating than the phrase “the whole is not unitary so that the local can be unitary.” Under faithful expected descent, the phrase *so that* is false if it means that noise or forgetting creates an exact automorphism. The defensible reversal is

\[
\boxed{
\text{form the retained carrier first;}
\quad
\text{then identify its reversible stabilizer or noiseless sector}.}
\tag{FD26}
\]

This leaves three viable physical architectures:

1. a nonfaithful formation arrow creates the carrier, after which an independently reconstructed automorphism group acts on it;
2. a nonsymmetric whole process already contains the local unitary group as a reducing noiseless component; or
3. a symmetric Euclidean or Markov semigroup supplies a positive distinction form, while Osterwalder--Schrader reconstruction or another independent theorem supplies Lorentzian unitary clock evolution from a separately identified generator.

For the mass-gap problem, the third architecture is presently the cleanest. The symmetric process may prove coercivity, but its parameter is not thereby clock time. The missing solder must compare its positive form or bounded transfer defect with the physical Hamiltonian on the same complete neutral carrier. [[conservation-of-causal-charge/unitarity-and-ontological-time|The ontological-time ledger]] keeps that process, the nonfaithful formation map, and factual record order distinct.
