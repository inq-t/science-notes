# Opposed Boundary Responses and a Local Action

Two opposed harmonic boundary relations supply a phase carrier that neither relation supplies alone. Their summed positive response and Green pairing determine a complex structure; rotating their common normal-response rate gives a reversible clock. Its exact state action becomes the canonical wave action, up to an endpoint term. When the squared boundary-response operator is a local Laplacian, this returns a local spacetime action rather than only a Hilbert-space variational formula.

## One boundary graph is not a phase space

Let \(A\) be a positive injective self-adjoint operator on a real Hilbert space \(\mathcal H_{\mathbb R}\). In the geometric example, \(A\) is the Dirichlet-to-Neumann operator: a boundary value \(q\) induces normal response \(Aq\). Its inverse is the Neumann-to-Dirichlet map on the appropriate dense domain.

Begin on a common spectral core where \(A\) and its inverse powers are defined, and form pairs \(u=(q,p)\). These two entries have different roles: boundary value and normal response. The Green pairing is

\[
\sigma((q,p),(q',p'))=(q,p')-(p,q').
\tag{CR1}
\]

For opposed sides, with one common normal convention, the response graphs are

\[
\mathcal L_\pm=\{(v,\pm Av)\}.
\tag{CR2}
\]

Self-adjointness of \(A\) makes each graph isotropic for (CR1). In particular, treating the data of one decaying harmonic solution \(p=Aq\) as two independent phase variables would be an error: the Green pairing vanishes on that graph.

On the common core the two graphs together span the pair carrier:

\[
(q,p)=(v_+,Av_+)+(v_-,-Av_-),
\qquad
v_\pm=\frac12(q\pm A^{-1}p).
\tag{CR3}
\]

Their cross-pairing is nondegenerate, with

\[
\sigma(u,u')
=2\big[(v_-,Av'_+)-(v_+,Av'_-)\big].
\tag{CR4}
\]

This supplies the meaning of the doubling. It relates two opposed response charts; it is not a declaration that one real coordinate is secretly two coordinates.

## The whole response fixes the realized norm

If the positive cost of one boundary value \(v\) is \((v,Av)\), the two sides give

\[
\boxed{
\mu_A(q,p)
=(v_+,Av_+)+(v_-,Av_-)
=\frac12\big[(q,Aq)+(p,A^{-1}p)\big].}
\tag{CR5}
\]

Let \(\mu_A(u,v)\) denote its real polarization. Complete the initial pair space in this norm. On the dense core, define

\[
J_A(q,p)=(-A^{-1}p,Aq).
\tag{CR6}
\]

Then

\[
J_A^2=-1,\qquad
\mu_A(J_Au,J_Av)=\mu_A(u,v),\qquad
2\mu_A(J_Au,v)=\sigma(u,v).
\tag{CR7}
\]

Thus \(J_A\) extends boundedly to the response completion, even when \(A^{-1}\) is unbounded in the original \(L^2\) norm. The complex structure is fixed by the supplied positive and antisymmetric forms, not fitted afterward.

With scalar \(i\) acting as \(J_A\), the Hermitian form is

\[
h_A(u,v)=\mu_A(u,v)+\frac i2\sigma(u,v).
\tag{CR8}
\]

An explicit isometry into the usual complexification of \(\mathcal H_{\mathbb R}\) is

\[
\kappa_A(q,p)=
\frac{A^{1/2}q+iA^{-1/2}p}{\sqrt2}.
\tag{CR9}
\]

Its range is dense, and its extension is onto: spectral cutoffs bounded away from zero approximate both real and imaginary parts of any complex vector. Direct calculation gives
\(\langle\kappa_Au,\kappa_Av\rangle=h_A(u,v)\) and
\(\kappa_AJ_A=i\kappa_A\).
The pairing (CR1) extends continuously through this identity when its entries no longer have separate \(L^2\) representatives.

## Normal response and clock rotation remain different arrows

On the two response graphs, common-normal continuation has formal generator

\[
N_A(q,p)=(p,A^2q).
\tag{CR10}
\]

It has rates \(+A\) and \(-A\) on \(\mathcal L_+\) and \(\mathcal L_-\). When \(A\) is unbounded, its growing part prevents \(e^{\tau N_A}\) from being bounded on the complete pair space for \(\tau>0\); common-normal continuation then needs suitable analytic domains. For bounded \(A\), this is a bounded-operator semigroup but is not contractive. Simultaneous inward smoothing from both sides is instead generated by \(-\operatorname{diag}(A,A)\).

The proposed clock realization rotates the same response rate using the complex structure (CR6):

\[
K_A=\operatorname{diag}(A,A),\qquad
B_A=-J_AK_A,\qquad
B_A(q,p)=(p,-A^2q).
\tag{CR11}
\]

Under (CR9),

\[
\kappa_AB_A=-iA\kappa_A,\qquad
U_t=e^{tB_A}
=\kappa_A^{-1}e^{-itA}\kappa_A.
\tag{CR12}
\]

This defines a strongly continuous unitary clock on the response completion, with generator domain transported from \(\operatorname{Dom}A\). In pair coordinates,

\[
U_t(q,p)=
\left(
\cos(tA)q+A^{-1}\sin(tA)p,\,
-A\sin(tA)q+\cos(tA)p
\right)
\tag{CR13}
\]

on the core and then by continuity.

The rule (CR11) is an explicit realization prescription. It does not prove that every directed process must choose this clock. Its nontrivial return is that the norm, complex structure, generator and action are now constrained by the same \(A\) and opposed Green data; no independently selected Hamiltonian has been placed on an unrelated quotient.

## The state action becomes a canonical local action

Take smooth core-valued paths with endpoint-fixed variations. The [[algebra/quotient-clock-and-stationary-action|exact state action]] of \(\psi=\kappa_A(q,p)\) is

\[
S_{\rm state}
=\int dt\left[
\frac12\big((p,\dot q)-(q,\dot p)\big)
-\frac12\big((p,p)+(q,A^2q)\big)
\right].
\tag{CR14}
\]

Indeed its kinetic term is \(-\operatorname{Im}\langle\psi,\dot\psi\rangle\), and

\[
\langle\psi,A\psi\rangle
=\frac12\big((p,p)+(q,A^2q)\big).
\tag{CR15}
\]

Adding the endpoint term \(\frac12[(q,p)]_{t_0}^{t_1}\) gives

\[
S_{\rm can}
=\int dt\left[
(p,\dot q)-\frac12(p,p)-\frac12(q,A^2q)
\right].
\tag{CR16}
\]

Variation yields \(\dot q=p\) and \(\dot p=-A^2q\), exactly (CR11). Eliminating \(p\), with its algebraic equation and the corresponding endpoint convention, gives

\[
S[q]=\frac12\int dt
\left[(\dot q,\dot q)-(q,A^2q)\right].
\tag{CR17}
\]

For the geometric choice

\[
\mathcal H_{\mathbb R}=L^2(\mathbb R^3,\mathbb R),
\qquad A^2=-\Delta+m^2,
\tag{CR18}
\]

spatial integration by parts turns (CR17) into

\[
\boxed{
S[q]=\frac12\int_{\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^3}
\left[(\partial_tq)^2-|\nabla q|^2-m^2q^2\right]\,dt\,d^3x.}
\tag{CR19}
\]

This is a local four-dimensional scalar action returned from the boundary prescription. The three-dimensional metric, the response \(A\), and the normal-to-clock realization were inputs; a physical duration or a necessary three-dimensional arena has not been derived.

Most importantly, locality follows here from the square \(A^2\), not from the mere positivity or lower edge of \(A\). A nonlocal \(A\) can have a local square; a positive gapped \(A\) need not.

## Local observables from the same pair carrier

For (CR18), smooth compactly supported real pairs have finite response norm in three spatial dimensions, including \(m=0\). The possible infrared factor \(A^{-1}\) is locally integrable in the quadratic form at zero momentum. Constants are not \(L^2(\mathbb R^3)\) zero modes.

Choose the bosonic Weyl/Fock realization on the complex pair completion:

\[
W(u)W(v)=e^{-i\sigma(u,v)/2}W(u+v),
\qquad
\langle\Omega,W(u)\Omega\rangle=e^{-\mu_A(u)/2}.
\tag{CR20}
\]

The [[directed-analytic-realization/local-weyl-realization|coherent-vector construction]] proves these relations with \(u\mapsto\kappa_Au\). Equal-time disjoint supports commute because (CR1) is local. The clock (CR13) obeys the wave equation with propagation speed at most one when (CR18) holds, so evolved local algebras commute at spacelike separation. Its Fock implementer is \(\Gamma(e^{-itA})\).

This is a free scalar realization, not an interacting gauge theory. CCR quantization and the Gaussian vacuum are still explicit choices. [[directed-analytic-realization/three-dimensional-boundary-test|The half-space and capped-slab tests]] distinguish its genuinely local return from a boundary construction that is gapped but violates finite-speed locality.
