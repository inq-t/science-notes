# Claim and Failure Contract

The programme treats failure conditions as part of a claim's meaning. Every derivation must preserve mathematical type, carrier, tangent, normalization, locality, and logical status; a mismatch should identify which bridge failed and what neighboring theory the return value supports.

## The six sameness tests

An equality between two physical response quantities is meaningful only after answering:

1. **Same mathematical type and order?** Are both objects scalars, measures, bilinear forms, charges, or actions of the same variance and algebraic order? A Casimir, quadratic capacity, and linear charge are not made comparable by sharing a numerical value.
2. **Same carrier?** Are the states, measures, and geometric data defined on one cut or related by a specified transport?
3. **Same tangent?** Do both quadratic forms evaluate the same physical deformation, not merely vectors with equal numerical norm?
4. **Same normalization?** Are the generator, scale coordinate, trace, and extensive channel factor fixed independently?
5. **Same prescription?** Are regulator, subtraction, renormalization, and entropy conventions compatible?
6. **Same scope?** Is the claim local, integrated over one cut, constant along a path, universal over a class, or evaluated at one crossing?

A phrase such as “both capacities are defined on the same horizon” does not settle the other five tests.

Dimensional agreement is only a necessary syntactic check. It is not a seventh sameness test and cannot replace the first six: an inverse time may be a decay rate, boost rate, modular parameter rate, sampling-clock rate, or phase rate. Constants such as \(c\), \(\hbar\), and \(k_B\) can convert their quantity lines while leaving their carriers and concepts inequivalent. Every proposed equality must therefore type the map that performs the conversion, not infer identity from matching units.

## Six recurrent scope separations

### Normalized versus extensive

$$
g^{\mathrm{bin}}_{\theta\theta}
=\operatorname{sech}^2\theta
$$

fixes the normalized response of one reduced channel. The physical quantity

$$
G^{\perp}_{NN}
=C_\perp(N)\nu^2
\operatorname{sech}^2(\nu x)
$$

also contains channel multiplicity, renormalization, and physical tangent normalization. A unit binary peak does not fix the amplitude.

### Local versus integrated

$$
\chi_N(p)
\not\equiv
\overline\chi_{\Sigma,N}
:=\frac{G^{\perp}_{NN}[\Sigma,N]}{A_\Sigma}
$$

unless constant density or an averaging theorem is supplied. Equality at one integrated cosmological cut is weaker than a local law.

### Coincidence versus all-history

A relative-entropy Hessian controls neighboring states at coincidence. It does not derive a finite-distance free energy, an all-history \(\operatorname{sech}^2\) density, or a covariant stress tensor. Those require separate constitutive and dynamical input.

### Imported versus derived

Local GR and QFT can be imported as tested fibers while the programme studies global comparison and facthood. Imported local physics is neither a failure nor an emergence theorem. A recovery burden applies only to sectors the programme claims to reconstruct.

### Hamiltonian edge versus invariant-mass edge

The Clay statement calls for a gap in the physical Hamiltonian above its zero-energy space. Its invariant-mass interpretation is available only in a positive-energy Poincare-covariant representation whose strongly commuting translation generators obey the forward-cone spectrum condition. With signature \((+---)\) and \(P^\mu=(H/c,\mathbf P)\),

$$
M
=
\frac{1}{c}(P_\mu P^\mu)^{1/2}
=
\frac{1}{c^2}(H^2-c^2|\mathbf P|^2)^{1/2}.
$$

Under those hypotheses, Lorentz invariance of the joint spectrum identifies the optimal nonvacuum Hamiltonian edge with \(c^2\) times the optimal invariant-mass edge. A gap of an auxiliary Markov or Witten generator, or a finite-volume regulator Hamiltonian, is not thereby a Poincare mass gap; it still requires the declared comparison, continuum passage, and reconstruction.

### Higgs orbit direction versus radial mode

For a regular nonzero field whose normalized values lie in one fixed orbit \(G/H\), a section of \(P\times_G(G/H)\) is equivalent, under the standard bundle hypotheses, to a global reduction of an already supplied principal \(G\)-bundle to \(H\). A local trivialization supplies only a coordinate representative of that global datum. The invariant radius \(r=\lVert\Phi\rVert\) is separate field data, and the reduction correspondence by itself constructs neither its potential and dynamics, the spacetime base, nor a pure Yang--Mills gap.

## Four meanings of variation

A universal rule can return different values without being a running coupling. Keep separate:

1. **State functional:** \(\mathfrak r[\Sigma,\omega,v]\) varies because its physical arguments vary.
2. **Horizontal cosmic flow:** \(\mathrm D\mathfrak r/\mathrm dN\) compares cuts or states along a constructed scale path.
3. **Renormalization-group running:** \(\mu\,\mathrm d\mathfrak r/\mathrm d\mu=\beta_{\mathfrak r}\) requires a renormalized operator, probe scale, and scheme.
4. **Change of universality class:** the effective response law itself changes because the carrier, dimension, phase, or gravitational action changes.

Cosmic age, Weyl scale, modular parameter, and RG scale are not interchangeable. Calling a crossing a fixed point is justified only after a beta function whose zero it is has been constructed.

- self-duality fixes a symmetric first derivative or stationary point, not the Hessian normalization;
- channel replication changes extensive capacity without changing the normalized binary algebra;
- dimensionless binary data together with \(c,\hbar,k_B\) cannot produce an area;
- binary translation is not modular-temperature rescaling of the same balanced binary state;
- a Lorentzian normal plane is not automatically a \(1+1\)-dimensional conformal thermal theory;

- a nonconstant realized character does not imply a non-homogeneous sub-observable algebra or law;

Before attempting a full FLRW wall construction:

1. compute the full-sector capacity/entropy ratio in controlled Rindler, causal-diamond, and bifurcate-horizon examples with one common scheme;
2. test whether the physical tangent is escort-directed, transverse, or a mixture;
3. determine whether a sufficient reduction can preserve its norm;

These calculations can falsify proposed routes without requiring a complete cosmology.
