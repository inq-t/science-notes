# Sources and Claim Status

The $A_2$ ternary module combines established statistical geometry, a new elementary three-state calculation, and a conditional 2026 inverse-cover construction. This ledger records which source supports which layer so that the exact finite response is not mistaken for an externally established physical realization.

## Primary sources for the inverse cover and $A_2$ structure

The public verification manuscript [[library/counterexample-to-the-jacobian-conjecture/inq|*A Counterexample to the Jacobian Conjecture*]] proves for its displayed Keller map that the inverse problem is governed by a binary cubic, that the generic degree is three, and that restriction away from the discriminant is a degree-three finite étale cover. It is a primary research manuscript dated 20 July 2026, not a peer-reviewed journal publication.

[[a2-spectral-geometry-of-jacobian-counterexample-3d/inq|The local $A_2$ spectral-geometry research note]] derives the transverse depressed-cubic normal form, $A_2$ cusp, full $S_3$ monodromy, and trace-zero endomorphism package from that input. It is version 0.1, unreviewed, and records AI-assisted development. Its symbolic receipt checks selected polynomial identities; it does not independently certify every global proof claim.

The classical ADE identification is grounded in V. I. Arnol'd's primary paper [*Normal forms for functions near degenerate critical points, the Weyl groups of $A_k$, $D_k$, $E_k$ and Lagrangian singularities*](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01077644). Classical monodromy and Gauss--Manin background is supplied by E. Brieskorn's [*Die Monodromie der isolierten Singularitäten von Hyperflächen*](https://eudml.org/doc/153994).

The module's use of three sheets is conditional on the first two 2026 sources. Its Fisher calculation itself does not require the Jacobian counterexample to be true: it is an exact statement about the displayed family in $\mathbb C^3$.

## Reproducibility of the new calculation

The finite result follows from

$$
Q=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,0),
\qquad
Z=1+2\cosh\theta,
\qquad
\Psi=\log Z-\log3.
$$

Then

$$
\Psi'=\frac{2\sinh\theta}{Z}
$$

and

$$
\Psi''
=\frac{2\cosh\theta}{Z}
-\frac{4\sinh^2\theta}{Z^2}
=\frac{2(\cosh\theta+2)}{Z^2}.
$$

No numerical fit, cosmological datum, or literature formula enters this derivation.
