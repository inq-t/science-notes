# Einstein, Weyl, and Invariant Spectra

Einstein's objection to Weyl's 1918 unification asked how reproducible rods, clocks, and atomic spectral lines could survive a geometry in which transported length calibration depended on history. The present programme extends that demand: if stable factual distinctions have characteristic response spectra, their calibration must arise from a law invariant under equivalent presentations. This extension motivates a relational account of mass; it is not a historical claim that Einstein posed the Yang–Mills mass-gap problem in these terms.

## What the historical difficulty was

In Weyl's original scale geometry, the length assigned to a transported vector can depend on its path. If atomic clocks inherit that transport law, otherwise identical atoms that reunite after different journeys can retain different intrinsic rates. Sharp, reproducible spectral lines then become difficult to explain. The concern was a residual difference in calibration after reunion, beyond an ordinary difference in elapsed proper time. Romero, Lima, and Sanomiya discuss this objection in [*One hundred years of Weyl's (unfinished) unified field theory*](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1355219818301539).

This was an objection to a particular account of scale transport and material clocks, not a proof that every gauge theory lacks reproducible spectra. The treatment of the clock matters: Hobson and Lasenby construct Weyl gauge theories in which their covariant matter prescription avoids the second clock effect. Their result does not validate every Weyl model; it shows why geometry and the systems realizing its standards must be analyzed together. [*Weyl gauge theories of gravity do not predict a second clock effect*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.06407).

The philosophical question remains productive: what makes a comparison standard reproducible when its presentation is relational? A unit need not be an unexplained absolute object if the relations transporting it are themselves sufficiently rigid.

## Gauge freedom, scale, and the invariant return

A local phase convention, a local scale convention, a change of physical units, and a dilation relating different states or theories are distinct operations. A classical scale-invariant expression does not establish exact dilation symmetry of one fully constructed quantum vacuum.

For equivalent presentations \(a,b\), a candidate normalized response operator should obey

\[
\mathscr L_b U_{ba}=U_{ba}\mathscr L_a,
\qquad U_{ba}^*U_{ba}=U_{ba}U_{ba}^*=I.
\]

This equation makes the spectrum presentation invariant. It is a proposed design condition here, not an equation attributed to Einstein. An irreversible restriction need not be unitarily equivalent; it can change which modes are visible. What must remain coherent is the rule relating the full spectrum to each restricted readout. [[mass-scale-calibration/scale-torsor-and-the-global-local-gap-invariant|The scale-torsor formulation]] separates these transformations.

If exact dilations preserve a fixed vacuum and scale every positive energy by arbitrarily small factors, a nontrivial positive gap is impossible in that representation. The proposed return therefore relates scaled realizations, or selects a scale-bearing vacuum, rather than demanding that a gapped vacuum retain that exact dilation symmetry.

## The atomic analogy and its extension

Hydrogen's stationary modes turn a configuration and its dynamics into characteristic energy differences. Atomic absorption then probes transitions selected by the coupling to radiation. The valuable strategy is to identify the domain, dynamics, and allowed comparisons that explain a spectrum together.

The analogous candidate for mass is a wavefunctional of complete comparison configurations. Its modes would characterize the ways a local distinction can remain compatible with its arena. [[factive-cosmos/harmonics-of-factivity|Harmonics of factivity]] formulates this domain-first question. “Invariant spectrum of facts” names this conjectural structure; there is not presently an independently measured universal ladder of facthood.

The glueball calculation is a particularly useful target. Athenodorou and Teper extract the low-lying pure \(SU(3)\) spectrum from lattice gauge theory and extrapolate using the string tension as the scale; conversion to GeV is a subsequent calibration. [[library/the-glueball-spectrum-of-su3-gauge-theory-in-3-plus-1-dimension/inq|Their source module]] preserves the reference. It is a calculation within Yang–Mills, not experimental proof of a cosmological mechanism or a refutation of the calculation's premises.

The robust object for a new programme to predict is first a dimensionless spectral ratio, symmetry assignment, or selection rule. The common scale can then be a separate return of the same law. An isolated value fitted to a cosmic radius would not explain the pattern.

## What returns with a difference

Mass need not begin as a property attached to a primitive thing. In a reconstructed Poincaré representation it is already an invariant of joint translations:

\[
M^2c^4=H^2-c^2\boldsymbol P^2.
\]

This distinguishes invariant mass from energy relative to a chosen time direction while preserving their established relation. [[mass-scale-calibration/joint-causal-generators-and-the-mass-casimir|Joint causal generators]] develops the structural interpretation. A deeper account should construct that invariant from the joint comparison law, rather than discard mass–energy correspondence.

The proposed answer to the historical demand is therefore: reproducible spectra can be invariants of a complete relational structure even when no isolated local presentation contains its own absolute calibration. The difficult and interesting task is to construct that structure so that its invariants cannot be retuned independently.
