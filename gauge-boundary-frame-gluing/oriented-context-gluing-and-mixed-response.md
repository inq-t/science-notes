# Oriented Context Gluing and Mixed Response

Complete observable algebras for two regions need not exhaust the physical algebra of their union: a relative frame can survive on their overlap even after both regions retain orientation. For quaternionic link contexts, the overlap stabilizer identifies this missing datum, and its first harmonic gives an exactly computable failure of joint clock closure. There is also a constructive repair: products and the same supplied shared-edge response recover all cross-pairings and alternating invariants, with an operator core on the complete gauge-invariant carrier. The repair works polynomially across rank walls; it does not select the supplied dynamics or prove a continuum gap.

**Status: [EXACT FINITE CONSTRUCTION].** The input is product Haar on finitely many \(SU(2)\) links, the ordinary endpoint gauge action and positive electric edge weights. The argument is finite-dimensional invariant and differential calculus, not a claim of a new general invariant theorem.

## The interface carries a relative stabilizer

Identify unit quaternions with \(S^3\subset\mathbb R^4\). Common left
and right endpoint gauge transformations act as \(SO(4)\), as proved
in [[algebra/oriented-gram-descent-and-invariant-coverage|oriented Gram descent]].
A context retains its complete \(SO(4)\) orbit: all pairings
\(s_{ij}=u_i\cdot u_j\) and ordered four-row determinants
\(\tau_I=\det(u_{i_1},u_{i_2},u_{i_3},u_{i_4})\).

Suppose two contexts share specified vectors spanning a rank-\(r\)
subspace \(S\). After their shared vectors are aligned, the remaining
interface group is the pointwise stabilizer
\[
K_S=\{R\in SO(4):R|_S=I\}\cong SO(4-r).
\tag{OC1}
\]
Let \(H_L,H_R\subset K_S\) fix all vectors of the respective context.
The distinct global orbits with these fixed regional orbits are
parameterized by
\[
H_L\backslash K_S/H_R.
\tag{OC2}
\]
Indeed, fix a representative of the left context; every aligned right
representative is obtained by an element of \(K_S\). Left and right
context stabilizers give exactly the two residual identifications.
This is a double-coset description, not a claim that every fibre is a
free torsor.

When both contexts have rank four, \(H_L=H_R=\{1\}\).
Three independent shared vectors then leave no relative rotation,
whereas two leave a full \(SO(2)\) angle. This is an instance of
[[inq#Faithful Wilson weights preserve the carrier, not the factorization|stabilizer-relative gluing]],
not a universal binary orientation rule.

## Three shared vectors give a singular reconstruction chart

Take contexts \((u_0,u_1,u_2,u_3)\) and \((u_0,u_1,u_2,u_4)\).
Let \(S\) now also denote the matrix with the three shared rows, and put
\[
A=SS^{\mathsf T},\quad b_i=Su_i,\quad
n=*(u_0\wedge u_1\wedge u_2),\quad \tau_i=n\cdot u_i.
\]
The sign of \(n\) is chosen so that the last equality is the ordered
four-row determinant. If \(\det A>0\), orthogonal decomposition gives
\[
u_i=S^{\mathsf T}A^{-1}b_i+\frac{\tau_i}{\det A}n,\qquad
s_{34}=b_3^{\mathsf T}A^{-1}b_4+\frac{\tau_3\tau_4}{\det A}.
\tag{OC3}
\]
The division-free identity
\[
\boxed{(\det A)s_{34}
=b_3^{\mathsf T}\operatorname{adj}(A)b_4+\tau_3\tau_4}
\tag{OC4}
\]
holds on all configurations by polynomial continuity. At rank at
most two it supplies no value: both determinants vanish and
\(\operatorname{adj}(A)b_i=0\), since \(b_i\in\operatorname{Ran}A\).

There is no continuous reconstruction of \(s_{34}\) from just these
regional invariants across that wall. With orthonormal \(e_0,\ldots,e_3\),
take
\[
u_0=e_0,\quad u_1=e_1,\quad
u_2=\sqrt{1-\varepsilon^2}\,e_0+\varepsilon e_2,\quad
u_3=e_3,\quad u_4=\pm e_3.
\tag{OC5}
\]
Here \(\det A=\varepsilon^2\), all regional Gram data are identical
for the two signs, and \((\tau_3,\tau_4)=(\varepsilon,\pm\varepsilon)\).
The two lists of regional invariants approach the same limit while
\(s_{34}=\pm1\). Thus reconstruction has no rank-uniform Lipschitz
bound. This does not contradict almost-everywhere coverage on the
rank-three overlap stratum, and a singular example alone is not an
\(L^2\) nonclosure theorem.

## Two shared vectors hide a full angle on a generic stratum

Now take six independent Haar links and the contexts
\[
L=(0,1,2,3),\qquad R=(0,1,4,5).
\]
Let \(\mathscr C\) be the sigma-algebra generated by their **complete**
oriented orbit data, and let \(P_{\mathscr C}\) be Haar conditional
expectation on the full physical Hilbert space. Almost surely the
shared pair has rank two and each context has rank four. Therefore
the missing gluing fibre is \(SO(2)\), not merely an exceptional
rank-wall point.

For a concrete family, fix \(u_0=e_0,u_1=e_1,u_2=e_2,u_3=e_3\) and
\[
u_4=\cos\theta\,e_2+\sin\theta\,e_3,\qquad
u_5=-\sin\theta\,e_2+\cos\theta\,e_3.
\tag{OC6}
\]
Both regional Gram matrices are the identity and both determinants
are \(+1\), but \(s_{24}=\cos\theta\).

For the full Haar law, write
\[
A=(s_{ab})_{a,b=0,1},\quad b_i=(s_{0i},s_{1i})^{\mathsf T},
\quad r_i=P_{S^\perp}u_i,\quad \rho_i=|r_i|.
\]
Independent rotations of the two residual context frames preserve
product Haar. Conditional on their complete regional invariants,
the relative angle is uniform Haar on \(SO(2)\). Consequently
\[
\begin{aligned}
P_{\mathscr C}s_{24}&=b_2^{\mathsf T}A^{-1}b_4,\\
\operatorname{Var}(s_{24}\mid\mathscr C)
&=\tfrac12\rho_2^2\rho_4^2>0\quad\text{almost surely}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{OC7}
\]
The first angular harmonics are paired opposite \(SO(2)\) charges:
a function of the relative angle is invariant under a common
rotation, but not under independent rotations. This is precisely
the distinction between
[[inq#The whole is assembled from dual boundary charges|diagonal charge pairing]]
and separate regional closure.

## The missing angle changes the actual response

Supply the positive self-adjoint electric operator
\[
H_0=\sum_{e=0}^5\alpha_eD_{Q,e},\qquad
\alpha_e>0,\quad Q=-2\operatorname{Tr}.
\tag{OC8}
\]
Its physical domain and form domain are the invariant \(H^2\) and
\(H^1\) spaces on the compact raw product. On real smooth functions,
define its mixed response by
\[
\Gamma_\alpha(f,g)
=\frac{fH_0g+gH_0f-H_0(fg)}2
=\frac14\sum_e\alpha_e
\langle\nabla_e^{\rm round}f,\nabla_e^{\rm round}g\rangle.
\tag{OC9}
\]
Thus it operates on a pair of actual observables and returns a
function on the same configuration carrier.

For \(F=s_{02}\) and \(G=s_{04}\), only edge zero is shared:
\[
\Gamma_\alpha(F,G)=\frac{\alpha_0}{4}
(s_{24}-s_{02}s_{04}),\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(\Gamma_\alpha(F,G)\mid\mathscr C)
=\frac{\alpha_0^2}{32}\rho_2^2\rho_4^2.
\tag{OC10}
\]
The complete invariant carrier of either region individually reduces
\(H_0\): its coordinate subspace and gauge-invariant subspace are
both preserved by the raw edge heat law. Their join need not reduce it.
Indeed \(H_0F=\tfrac34(\alpha_0+\alpha_2)F\), and similarly for \(G\),
so
\[
(I-P_{\mathscr C})H_0(FG)
=-\frac{\alpha_0}{2}\,r_2\cdot r_4.
\tag{OC11}
\]
Conditional on the shared span, independent uniform \(S^3\) vectors
have \(\mathbb E\rho_i^2=1/2\). Their residual inner-product square
has expectation \(1/8\). Hence
\[
\|(I-P_{\mathscr C})H_0(FG)\|^2=\frac{\alpha_0^2}{32},
\qquad \|FG\|^2=\frac1{16},\qquad \mathbb E(FG)=0.
\tag{OC12}
\]
The centered unit vector \(f=4FG\) therefore has the exact
short-time memory coefficient
\[
\boxed{\lim_{t\downarrow0}
\frac{\langle f,(C_{2t}-C_t^2)f\rangle}{t^2}
=\frac{\alpha_0^2}{2},\qquad
C_t=P_{\mathscr C}e^{-tH_0}|_{L^2(\mathscr C)}.}
\tag{OC13}
\]
The identity follows from
\(C_{2t}-C_t^2=P_{\mathscr C}e^{-tH_0}(I-P_{\mathscr C})
e^{-tH_0}|_{L^2(\mathscr C)}\) and \(f\in D(H_0)\).
It needs no bounded off-diagonal block or fitted eigenvalue.

## Products and the shared response recover the whole algebra

Let \(\mathscr A\) be the smallest unital complex \(*\)-algebra of raw polynomial
restrictions containing the regional invariant polynomials and stable
under \(H_0\). Existence is immediate by intersecting all such algebras;
the full invariant polynomial algebra is one. The construction is also
obtained by repeated products, linear combinations and \(H_0\).
Equation (OC9) makes \(\mathscr A\) stable under \(\Gamma_\alpha\).

For every missing cross pair \(i\in\{2,3\}, j\in\{4,5\}\),
\[
\boxed{s_{ij}
=s_{0i}s_{0j}+\frac4{\alpha_0}
\Gamma_\alpha(s_{0i},s_{0j}).}
\tag{OC14}
\]
Thus the same shared-edge response supplies all entries of the full
Gram data without choosing a separate relative angle. For an ordered
four-tuple \(I\), \(a\in I\) and \(j\notin I\), cofactor differentiation
also gives
\[
\Gamma_\alpha(\tau_I,s_{aj})
=\frac{\alpha_a}{4}
\left(\tau_{I:a\mapsto j}-\tau_I s_{aj}\right).
\tag{OC15}
\]
The replacement keeps the row position; its sign is not discarded.
Starting with \(\tau_{0123}\), successive row replacements yield
every four-row determinant. Only the supplied positive edge weights
are divided by, never a determinant or a rank-dependent quantity.

All pairings and all four-row determinants separate the \(SO(4)\)
orbits at every rank. Equal Gram matrices give an \(O(4)\) isometry.
Below rank four its orientation can be corrected on the unused
orthogonal complement. At rank four a nonzero ordered determinant
forces that isometry to have determinant \(+1\). Stone--Weierstrass
therefore proves that the uniform closure of \(\mathscr A\) is the
complete continuous physical observable algebra.

There is an operator-core conclusion as well. Let \(\mathscr V_N\)
be \(\mathscr A\) intersected with restrictions of raw polynomials of
total degree at most \(N\). These spaces are finite-dimensional,
nested and \(H_0\)-invariant: a spherical Laplacian does not increase
raw polynomial degree. Their union is \(\mathscr A\), dense in
physical \(L^2\), and every member is raw-smooth. Their orthogonal
projections reduce \(H_0\) and converge strongly to the identity.
The [[algebra/oriented-gram-descent-and-invariant-coverage#Polynomial coefficients fix the inherited realization|finite-degree projection argument]]
then gives
\[
\boxed{\mathscr A\text{ is an operator core and a form core of }
H_0|_{\mathcal H_{\rm phys}}.}
\tag{OC16}
\]
No assertion about generators of all invariant polynomials, smooth
division on a singular quotient, or an arbitrary wall boundary
condition is needed.

## A bounded interaction preserves the core, not the Haar memory

Let \(H=H_0+V\), where \(V\) is a real smooth gauge-invariant
multiplication potential on this same compact finite carrier.
It is bounded, so \(D(H)=D(H_0)\) with equivalent graph norms.
If \(\mathscr B\) is the unital complex \(*\)-algebra of smooth
invariants generated by the regional polynomials and action of \(H\),
then
\[
H1=V\in\mathscr B,\qquad
H_0f=Hf-fH1\in\mathscr B\quad(f\in\mathscr B).
\tag{OC17}
\]
Thus \(\mathscr B\) contains the electric core \(\mathscr A\)
constructed above. Both are cores for \(H\); the bounded potential
does not change this finite-domain conclusion.

## What must be retained by the proposed primitive law

Regional scalar closure, even with complete orientation and autonomous
regional clocks, is not stable under composition. The missing datum is
an interface action, whose invariant contraction appears in the
mixed response. Equations (OC14)--(OC16) show that retaining the actual
shared response and multiplication repairs this finite example all the
way to a core of the whole operator, rather than only adding a witness
observable by hand.

This is not a derivation of that response from the regional clocks.
The raw metric, product Haar state, identification of the shared edge
derivations and positive weights are supplied inputs. The repair uses
the whole shared-edge law; it does not infer it from two separately
closed descriptions. A new master object must constrain this
interface response together with the state and multiplication, then
control its behavior through the required field limits. Without that
selecting law the construction passes fixed-input consistency, not
the stronger test against encoding arbitrary desired dynamics.
