# Trace-Free Silence at the Crossing

At the CST density maximum, separate conservation gives $w_X=-1$, so the response stress is instantaneously metric-proportional and contributes nothing to the trace-free Einstein--tractor transport equation. This is an exact conditional crossing identity, not a derivation of the response amplitude or of the global vacuum sector.

For a perfect fluid in four spacetime dimensions,

$$
T_{ab}=(\rho+p)u_au_b+p g_{ab},
$$

and hence

$$
\boxed{
T^\circ_{ab}
=(\rho+p)
\left(u_au_b+\frac14g_{ab}\right).}
$$

Thus a positive-density perfect-fluid sector is invisible to the trace-free equation exactly when $\rho+p=0$, or $w=-1$.

For the separately conserved CST response,

$$
\rho_X'=-3(\rho_X+p_X).
$$

The binary profile has $\rho_X'(N_c)=0$, so

$$
\boxed{
p_{X,c}=-\rho_{X,c},
\qquad
(T^X_{ab})^\circ\big|_{N_c}=0.}
$$

In the language of [[causal-scale-theory/scale-tractor|scale-tractor transport]], the response creates no trace-free transport defect at that instant. The total tractor need not be parallel, because matter, radiation, anisotropic sectors, and gradients can still contribute.

## Norm and derivative occupy different slots

If $\rho_X=C_cG^\perp_{NN}$ with fixed $C_c$, conservation gives

$$
\rho_X+p_X
=-\frac{C_c}{3}
\frac{\mathrm dG^\perp_{NN}}{\mathrm dN}.
$$

The BKM norm sets the homogeneous energy density, while its scale derivative sets the trace-free perfect-fluid source combination. This is a useful vertical/horizontal distinction; it is not evidence that the tractor norm equals the BKM norm.

On the flat, zero-residual, no-extra-component unit branch, $\mathfrak R_c=1$ also gives equal response and ordinary energy density at the crossing. The resulting one-to-one **energy-budget** split between a trace-free-silent response and a trace-free-active ordinary complement is a conditional interpretation. It does not equate their tensor norms and cannot be used to derive $\mathfrak R_c=1$.
