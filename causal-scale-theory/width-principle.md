# The Unit-Width Principle

The unit-width principle proposes that the fundamental balanced wall channel advances by one canonically normalized binary rapidity per Weyl e-fold. It selects $\nu=1$; it is neither a consequence of affine soldering nor a coordinate convention once the binary generator is normalized by $Q^2=\mathbf1$.

The general affine law is

$$
\theta=\varrho_\perp x,
\qquad
\nu:=|\varrho_\perp|.
$$

The proposed principle is

$$
\boxed{\nu=1.}
$$

Its meaning is local and differential:

$$
\left|\frac{\mathrm d\theta}{\mathrm dN}\right|=1.
$$

It does not say that a finite one-e-fold interval has unit BKM distance, because the BKM metric varies along the path.

## Why it is a physical statement

A general reparameterization of $\theta$ changes the numerical slope. The unit law becomes meaningful only after fixing:

- the normalized generator $Q^2=\mathbf1$;
- the balanced exponential coordinate conjugate to $Q$;
- the logarithmic scale coordinate $N$;
- the physical identification of that binary channel.

With those conventions fixed independently, a measured or microscopically calculated $\nu\ne1$ would falsify this principle rather than merely rename the coordinate.

## What would upgrade it

An upgrade requires a theorem or construction that selects a primitive character of the relevant scale-transport group in the canonical binary normalization. Real conformal weights alone do not do this, and the existence of two null normals does not turn the normal plane into a two-dimensional CFT.

Until such a result exists, $\nu$ remains free in [[causal-scale-theory/generalized-background]] and the unit value is evaluated only in [[unit-branch]].
