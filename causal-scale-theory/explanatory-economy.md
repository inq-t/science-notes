# Explanatory Economy

A rigid formula is not automatically an explanation. Causal Scale Theory must distinguish four kinds of economy: fewer fitted numbers, fewer freely chosen functions, fewer independent structural choices, and a deeper economy in which one independently constructed reason necessitates several otherwise separate facts. The first three make a theory sharper; only the fourth establishes the kind of novelty the programme seeks.

## Four economies

**Numerical economy** counts tunable constants after ordinary units, measured inputs, and initial data have been declared. Fixing $\nu$ and $\mathfrak R_c$ instead of fitting them is numerically economical, but a fixed value is not explained merely because it is called a principle.

**Functional economy** asks whether an arbitrary history, kernel, potential, or equation-of-state function has been supplied by hand. The response

$$
\rho_X(N)
=\frac{\mathfrak R_c}{2}\rho_{\mathrm{crit},c}
\operatorname{sech}^2\!\bigl(\nu(N-N_c)\bigr)
$$

is more falsifiable than a freely reconstructed $w(N)$ because its whole shape follows once a few data are fixed. That is a real gain even before the shape has a fundamental derivation.

**Structural economy** counts choices that do not look like fit parameters: the algebra and state family, cross-scale transport, binary reduction, extensive normalization, constitutive map, background sector, residual vacuum sector, boundary conditions, and root branch. Such choices can carry as much freedom as a coefficient or function.

**Explanatory economy** asks whether the same independently justified construction makes several facts necessary together. A model gains this economy when changing the construction to save one target would also spoil other derived relations. It is strongest when the construction was fixed before, and without reference to, the observations it later predicts.

These economies are not interchangeable. A theory can have no fitted constants and no free functions while remaining structurally underdetermined. Conversely, a phenomenological function can be useful and highly falsifiable before its deeper reason is known.

## The musical-chairs test

Removing freedom from one line of the equations does not reduce the total explanatory burden if the same freedom reappears elsewhere. The test is to list every independent choice before and after a proposed derivation.

- Replacing an arbitrary $w(N)$ by a chosen state family whose metric was selected because it produces the desired pulse moves freedom from the equation of state to the state space.
- Fixing $\nu=1$ while leaving the generator normalization or scale soldering free moves freedom from a number to a representation choice.
- Obtaining a coasting future only after selecting the zero-residual sector moves freedom from late-time dynamics to a boundary condition.
- Dating the crossing through flatness while silently choosing one root moves freedom from an epoch parameter to a branch prior.

If the total ledger of independent choices has not shrunk, the freedom has changed chairs. This does not make the resulting model worthless; it locates the work that still has to become explanatory.

## Why a fixed function can remain ad hoc

`Fixed` means that alternatives are excluded inside the stated model. `Non-ad-hoc` means that the exclusion follows from reasons not chosen to reproduce the target. A stipulated $\operatorname{sech}^2$ history is therefore functionally economical and predictively restrictive, yet it can remain ad hoc if the binary channel, the horizontal path, or the source map was selected with that history already in view.

The exact identity

$$
\eta^2+G^{\mathrm{BKM}}_{\theta\theta}=1
$$

does explain why a balanced normalized binary family has a complementary polarization and susceptibility. It does not by itself establish that the cosmic wall is such a family, that $N$ is affinely soldered to $\theta$, or that the susceptibility gravitates with the proposed normalization. Those are the load-bearing edges displayed in [[causal-scale-theory/closure-stack|the closure stack]].

## The novelty test

A genuinely novel CST prediction requires an **independent construction**. The wall algebra, scale-indexed states, cross-fiber transport, generator normalization, extensive channel measure, and covariant response must be specified without using the late-time FLRW pulse, its crossing date, or the desired future as construction data. The target history should then be eliminated from the premises and returned as an output.

The strongest test has three parts:

1. construct the microscopic and covariant objects from principles that have content outside the target fit;
2. prove that the response shape, normalization, conservation properties, and branch restrictions follow, with no equivalent family of choices left free;
3. compare the resulting relations with observations that were not used to select the construction.

At present CST has genuine functional economy and a conditional form of numerical economy. Its claim to deeper explanatory economy remains a research target owned by [[wall-construction-interface/entry|the wall construction]], [[causal-scale-theory/conjectures/covariant-response-sector|the covariant response conjecture]], and the independent tests in [[causal-scale-theory/observables|the observables programme]].
