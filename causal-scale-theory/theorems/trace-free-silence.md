# Stationary Density and Trace-Free Silence

A separately conserved perfect-fluid density that is stationary with respect to logarithmic expansion has \(p=-\rho\) at that instant and is therefore invisible to the four-dimensional trace-free Einstein equation. Applied to the rigid CST pulse, this gives trace-free silence at the crossing without fixing its amplitude or the global vacuum sector.

For a perfect fluid in four spacetime dimensions,

$$
T_{ab}=(\rho+p)u_au_b+pg_{ab}.
$$

Its trace-free part is

$$
\boxed{
T^\circ_{ab}
=(\rho+p)
\left(u_au_b+\frac14g_{ab}\right).}
$$

Suppose the fluid is separately conserved on an expanding homogeneous background:

$$
\rho'=-3(\rho+p).
$$

At any stationary point \(N_*\) of the density,

$$
\rho'(N_*)=0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
p(N_*)=-\rho(N_*),
$$

and therefore

$$
\boxed{T^\circ_{ab}\big|_{N_*}=0.}
$$

For the positive rigid CST response, \(N_*=N_c\) is its unique maximum, so

$$
p_{X,c}=-\rho_{X,c},
\qquad
(T^X_{ab})^\circ\big|_{N_c}=0.
$$

If \(\rho_X=C_cG^\perp_{NN}\) with constant \(C_c\), conservation also gives

$$
\rho_X+p_X
=-\frac{C_c}{3}
\frac{\mathrm dG^\perp_{NN}}{\mathrm dN}.
$$

Thus the cut-integrated norm controls the homogeneous density while its scale derivative controls the perfect-fluid trace-free source combination. This is not an equality between the BKM norm and a tractor norm.

Ordinary matter, radiation, gradients, and anisotropic sectors can remain trace-free active at the same cut. Nor does the theorem select the residual scalar channel described in [[causal-scale-theory/vacuum-residual-sector|the residual-sector note]].
