# The Generalized Homogeneous Background

The generalized CST background is the ordered composition of a two-parameter response law, a branch-valued present-flatness closure, and a zero-residual expansion history. It carries width $\nu$ and peak capacity $\mathfrak R_c$ as independent quantities; the values $(1,1)$ are selected only in the unit branch.

The construction assumes spatially flat $3+1$-dimensional GR--FLRW, declared matter and radiation abundances, separate conservation of the response, and a residual-sector choice. These are background premises added after [[causal-scale-theory/closure-stack|the information-geometric and constitutive closure]].

First, [[response-law|the generalized response law]] gives

$$
\rho_X(x)
=\frac{\mathfrak R_c}{2}\rho_{\mathrm{crit},c}
\operatorname{sech}^2(\nu x),
\qquad
w_X(x)
=-1+\frac{2\nu}{3}\tanh(\nu x),
$$

together with the exact conditional shape relation

$$
9(1+w_X)^2+6w_X'=4\nu^2.
$$

The width fixes transition shape; the amplitude fixes the response fraction at the crossing. Neither determines the other.

Second, [[flatness-branches|present flatness]] solves for possible crossing dates. The result is an implicit, sometimes multivalued equation, so $x_c$ is branch data rather than another fundamental constant. The tail change at $\nu=2$ is not an amplitude-independent existence bound.

Third, a selected root determines [[future-asymptotics|the expansion history and future class]]. In the zero-residual sector, $\rho_X\sim a^{-2\nu}$, so the future ranges from power-law acceleration through coasting to matter domination as $\nu$ increases. A nonzero residual changes that classification independently of the localized pulse.

[[unit-branch]] evaluates the two unit principles and the inherited abundance benchmark. [[observables]] states which parts of this background can be tested now and which require a covariant perturbation sector.

The generalized background is therefore a **[CONDITIONAL OUTPUT]**. It is more rigid than a free function $w(z)$, but it is not a microscopic derivation merely because its consequences are algebraically fixed after closure.
