# Gauge-Cycle Innovation Filtration

On a finite graph, gauge invariance does not obstruct a complete orthogonal filtration of the physical slice carrier. Conditional expectation onto any selected edge set commutes with the vertex-gauge action; ordering a spanning tree first makes every tree-stage physical innovation vanish, and each remaining chord introduces a closed relational degree of freedom. Tree gauge identifies the resulting filtration with joint cycle holonomies modulo one residual simultaneous conjugation. For the pure product transfer this filtration diagonalizes in spin-network support and recovers the exact girth edge; for the interacting Wilson vacuum it remains complete but becomes nondiagonal. The Yang--Mills analytic target is therefore no longer to construct a gauge-compatible filtration, but to prove that its diagonal attenuation plus its interaction-induced off-diagonal influence has a subunit norm with the correct continuum rate.

**Status: [EXACT] for the gauge-covariant conditional expectations, forest no-information lemma, complete cycle filtration, tree-gauge presentation, spin-network support decomposition, and pure-kinetic calibration; [EXACT UNDER THE FINITE WILSON TRANSFER HYPOTHESES] for the physical two-slice innovation bound and logarithmic energy implication; [OPEN] for the volume-, sector-, and continuum-uniform interacting estimate.**

## Conditional expectation respects the physical carrier

Let \(\Gamma=(V,E)\) be a finite connected oriented graph, let \(G\) be a
compact second-countable group, and put

$$
\Omega_\Gamma=G^E,
\qquad
\mathcal G_\Gamma=G^V.
\tag{GC1}
$$

The vertex-gauge action is

$$
(g\cdot U)_e
=
g_{s(e)}U_eg_{t(e)}^{-1}.
\tag{GC2}
$$

Let \(\nu\) be any \(\mathcal G_\Gamma\)-invariant Borel probability measure
on \(\Omega_\Gamma\). The physical configuration carrier is

$$
\mathcal H_{\mathrm{GI}}(\nu)
:=
L^2(\Omega_\Gamma,\nu)^{\mathcal G_\Gamma}.
\tag{GC3}
$$

For \(A\subseteq E\), let \(\mathscr F_A\) be the sigma-algebra generated by
the links \(\{U_e:e\in A\}\), and let

$$
E_Af:=\mathbb E_\nu[f\mid\mathscr F_A].
\tag{GC4}
$$

The sigma-algebra \(\mathscr F_A\) is stable under every gauge
transformation: fixed left and right multiplication of a selected link does
not introduce an unselected link. Since the gauge action preserves \(\nu\),
conditional expectation is equivariant,

$$
U_gE_A=E_AU_g,
\qquad
(U_gf)(U):=f(g^{-1}\cdot U).
\tag{GC5}
$$

Consequently \(E_A\) preserves \(\mathcal H_{\mathrm{GI}}(\nu)\), and its
restriction is the orthogonal projection onto

$$
\mathcal H_A^{\mathrm{GI}}
:=
L^2(\mathscr F_A,\nu)\cap\mathcal H_{\mathrm{GI}}(\nu).
\tag{GC6}
$$

This observation removes a false dilemma. One need not choose between raw
coordinate conditional expectations and the gauge-invariant carrier. The
selected-coordinate algebra is gauge **stable**, even though its individual
coordinate functions are not gauge invariant, and equivariance makes its
expectation preserve the fixed subspace.

## A forest contains no closed physical distinction

If the selected subgraph \((V,A)\) is a forest and every incident vertex is
gauged, then the gauge group acts transitively on its link assignments. A
leaf-stripping gauge transformation sets every selected tree link to the
identity. Hence every gauge-invariant function depending only on \(A\) is
constant:

$$
\boxed{
A\text{ a forest}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\mathcal H_A^{\mathrm{GI}}=\mathbb C1,
\qquad
E_A|_{\mathcal H_{\mathrm{GI}}}=P_0.}
\tag{GC7}
$$

Here \(P_0f=\nu(f)\mathbf1\). This statement uses the full vertex gauge
group. Ungauged boundary vertices, charged endpoints, matter fields, fixed
links, or prescribed boundary flux can leave nonconstant forest data; such
data must be included in the initial retained algebra rather than silently
discarded.

Choose a spanning tree \(\mathcal T\subset E\), order its edges first, and
then order the chords

$$
E\setminus\mathcal T
=
\{c_1,\ldots,c_r\},
\qquad
r=|E|-|V|+1=b_1(\Gamma).
\tag{GC8}
$$

Put

$$
A_j
:=
\mathcal T\cup\{c_1,\ldots,c_j\},
\qquad
F_j:=E_{A_j}|_{\mathcal H_{\mathrm{GI}}},
\quad 0\leq j\leq r,
\tag{GC9}
$$

where \(F_0=P_0\) by (GC7), and define

$$
D_j:=F_j-F_{j-1}.
\tag{GC10}
$$

Nested conditional expectations obey \(F_iF_j=F_{\min(i,j)}\). Therefore
the \(D_j\) are pairwise orthogonal projections and

$$
\boxed{
I-P_0=\sum_{j=1}^rD_j,
\qquad
\|f-P_0f\|_2^2=\sum_{j=1}^r\|D_jf\|_2^2.}
\tag{GC11}
$$

This is a complete physical innovation resolution for every invariant
finite-volume state, including an interacting Wilson vacuum law. The tree
edges are not small physical facts that happen to cancel; before a cycle
closes they are pure presentation data. The first possible nonconstant
innovation occurs at a closure event.

## Tree gauge displays what the filtration retains

Choose a root \(o\in V\) and let

$$
\mathcal G_o:=\{g\in G^V:g_o=1\}.
\tag{GC12}
$$

The based group acts freely. Setting every tree link to the identity leaves
one holonomy \(h_j\in G\) for the fundamental loop closed by each chord
\(c_j\). The remaining gauge transformation at the root acts by simultaneous
conjugation,

$$
(h_1,\ldots,h_r)
\longmapsto
(gh_1g^{-1},\ldots,gh_rg^{-1}).
\tag{GC13}
$$

Thus the physical measurable carrier has the presentation

$$
G^E/G^V
\simeq
G^r/\!\!/\operatorname{Ad}G,
\qquad
\mathcal H_{\mathrm{GI}}(\nu)
\simeq
L^2(G^r,\bar\nu)^{\operatorname{Ad}G},
\tag{GC14}
$$

where \(\bar\nu\) is the pushforward of \(\nu\). For product Haar measure,
the rooted-tree change of variables is measure preserving, so
\(\bar\nu=\mu_H^r\). This is the finite-graph reduction described in
[[library/deformation-quantization-and-homological-reduction-of-a-lattice-gauge-model/inq|Pflaum--Rudolph--Schmidt]].

Under (GC14), \(F_j\) is conditional expectation onto **all** simultaneous-
conjugation-invariant functions of \((h_1,\ldots,h_j)\). If

$$
\mathrm d\bar\nu(h)=w(h)\,\mathrm d\mu_H^r(h),
\qquad w>0,
\tag{GC15}
$$

then explicitly

$$
(F_jf)(h_{\leq j})
=
\frac{
\displaystyle\int_{G^{r-j}}
f(h_{\leq j},h_{>j})w(h_{\leq j},h_{>j})\,
\mathrm d\mu_H^{r-j}(h_{>j})
}{
\displaystyle\int_{G^{r-j}}
w(h_{\leq j},h_{>j})\,
\mathrm d\mu_H^{r-j}(h_{>j})
}.
\tag{GC16}
$$

Invariance of \(f,w\), and Haar measure makes the result invariant under
simultaneous conjugation. Stabilizers make the orbit space stratified, but
they do not obstruct the quotient sigma-algebras or these orthogonal
expectations.

The state on a prefix is necessarily the induced marginal
\((\pi_j)_*\bar\nu\). It is not generally the Wilson measure obtained by
writing the same bare plaquette formula on the smaller graph. Integrating
out the complement generates effective interactions, often nonlocal ones.
Replacing the induced marginal by an intrinsic smaller-lattice state would
therefore replace the whole/part relation by a different model unless a
projective-consistency or renormalization theorem proves their agreement.

The word **joint** is essential. For nonabelian \(G\),

$$
L^2(G^r)^{\operatorname{Ad}G}
\neq
\bigotimes_{j=1}^rL^2(G)^{\operatorname{Ad}G}.
\tag{GC17}
$$

For example, \(\operatorname{Tr}(h_1h_2)\) contains the relative orientation
of the two holonomies and is not determined by their separate conjugacy
classes. A list of one-loop characters can therefore miss relational
information. The prefix filtration does not: such a distinction appears in
the first innovation whose prefix contains every holonomy on which it
depends.

## The actual Wilson pair has a complete physical matrix

Let a finite, vacuum-normalized, gauge-covariant transfer be represented by
its ground-state transform \(P_T\) on \(L^2(\nu)\), and let

$$
\mathrm d\mathsf J_T(U,V)
=
\nu(\mathrm dU)P_T(U,\mathrm dV)
\tag{GC18}
$$

be its stationary two-slice law. Under the finite Wilson sandwich
\(T=M_aKM_a\), this is the exact Perron-dressed pair law

$$
\mathrm d\mathsf J_T(U,V)
=
\lambda_0^{-1}b(U)k(U,V)b(V)
\,\mathrm d\mu_H(U)\mathrm d\mu_H(V),
\qquad b=a\psi.
\tag{GC19}
$$

Assume first that the only fixed physical vector is the vacuum line. Choose
cycle filtrations on the two endpoints, not necessarily using the same tree
or order, and define the exact block coefficients

$$
c_{ij}
:=
\|D_i^XP_TD_j^Y\|,
\qquad
C=(c_{ij}).
\tag{GC20}
$$

Equivalently,

$$
c_{ij}
=
\sup_{
\substack{u\in\operatorname{Ran}D_i^X,\,
v\in\operatorname{Ran}D_j^Y\\
\|u\|=\|v\|=1}}
|\mathbb E_{\mathsf J_T}[\overline{u(X)}v(Y)]|.
\tag{GC21}
$$

The innovation-matrix theorem in
[[two-slice-innovation-geometry/inq|Two-Slice Innovation Geometry]] now
applies on the physical carrier without a missing gauge step:

$$
\boxed{
\rho_{\mathrm{GI}}(P_T)
:=
\|P_T|_{\mathcal H_{\mathrm{GI}}\ominus\mathbb C1}\|
\leq
\|C\|_{2\to2}.}
\tag{GC22}
$$

If \(P_T\) is Hilbert positive and

$$
P_T=e^{-\ell(H_T-E_0)/(\hbar c)},
\tag{GC23}
$$

then any independently proved \(\|C\|\leq q<1\) gives

$$
\boxed{
H_T-E_0
\geq
\frac{\hbar c}{\ell}[-\log q](I-P_0).}
\tag{GC24}
$$

If a boundary condition or topology leaves a common conserved algebra, begin
the filtration at its expectation \(R\), replace \(P_0\) by \(R\), and state
the estimate sector by sector. An unreduced conserved direction makes the
maximal correlation one and cannot be removed by nomenclature.

## The pure kinetic member calibrates the construction

Take product Haar measure and a product of central Hilbert-positive link
convolutions,

$$
K=\bigotimes_{e\in E}K_e.
\tag{GC25}
$$

In a spin-network basis, \(K\) acts on a label
\(\boldsymbol\pi=(\pi_e)\) by the scalar

$$
p_{\boldsymbol\pi}
=
\prod_ep_{e,\pi_e},
\qquad
p_{e,\mathbf1}=1,
\quad 0\leq p_{e,\pi}<1\ (\pi\neq\mathbf1).
\tag{GC26}
$$

[[library/spin-network-states-in-gauge-theory/inq|Baez's fixed-graph
Peter--Weyl theorem]] gives a complete orthonormal physical basis. Haar
expectation over omitted links kills every basis vector with a nontrivial
label on one of those links. Hence \(D_j\) is the closed span of physical
spin networks whose last nontrivially labelled edge in the chosen order is
\(c_j\). Every \(D_j\) reduces \(K\), and therefore

$$
D_iKD_j=0\quad(i\neq j),
\qquad
\|D_jKD_j\|
=
\sup_{
\substack{\boldsymbol\pi\text{ admissible}\\
\max\operatorname{supp}\boldsymbol\pi=c_j}}
\prod_ep_{e,\pi_e}.
\tag{GC27}
$$

Thus the innovation bound is exact in this member:

$$
\|K|_{\mathcal H_{\mathrm{GI}}\ominus\mathbb C1}\|
=
\|C\|_2.
\tag{GC28}
$$

For identical \(SU(2)\) Wilson link kernels, put

$$
r_x:=\frac{I_2(x)}{I_1(x)}.
\tag{GC29}
$$

The nontrivial representation coefficients are bounded above by \(r_x\).
Gauge closure forces nontrivial spin-network support to contain a cycle, and
a fundamental Wilson character on a shortest cycle saturates the result.
Consequently

$$
\boxed{
\rho_{\mathrm{GI}}(K)=r_x^{g(\Gamma)},}
\tag{GC30}
$$

where \(g(\Gamma)\) is graph girth. This recovers the bounded
girth--Wilson edge in
[[contemporary-puzzles/yang-mills-mass-gap/finite-spacing-transfer-and-bounded-flux-solder|Finite-Spacing Transfer]] and proves that the filtration sees the exact
physical contraction in the noninteracting calibration case.

## Interaction is an off-diagonal influence budget

For the magnetic Wilson sandwich, two changes occur on the same physical
carrier:

1. the vacuum density \(\psi^2\) changes the orthogonal expectations
   \(F_j\); and
2. the Doob transfer \(P_T\) need not preserve the resulting spin-network
   innovation shells.

The Perron dressing has therefore not disappeared. It has been localized in
the entries (GC20), including correlations between different closure
innovations. Write

$$
r_{a,L}:=\max_i c_{ii},
\qquad
R_{a,L}:=\max_i\sum_{j\neq i}c_{ij},
\qquad
S_{a,L}:=\max_j\sum_{i\neq j}c_{ij}.
\tag{GC31}
$$

The Schur estimate gives the concrete certificate

$$
\boxed{
\rho_{\mathrm{GI}}(P_T)
\leq
r_{a,L}+\sqrt{R_{a,L}S_{a,L}}.}
\tag{GC32}
$$

This splits the missing theorem into two action-derived statements:

- **persistence:** each closure innovation loses a controlled amount across
  the slab, so \(r_{a,L}<1\) at the required calibrated rate;
- **influence:** the total magnetic/vacuum mixing between distinct closure
  innovations is summable and does not consume that diagonal margin.

For a fixed physical slab thickness \(\ell_*>0\), it is enough to prove

$$
\sup_{a,L,\mathsf s}
\left[r_{a,L}^{(\mathsf s)}+
\sqrt{R_{a,L}^{(\mathsf s)}S_{a,L}^{(\mathsf s)}}\right]
\leq q_*<1
\tag{GC33}
$$

after the regulator is fine enough, uniformly over the admitted boundary or
flux sectors \(\mathsf s\). For adjacent temporal slices, the finite-rate
condition is instead

$$
\boxed{
\liminf_{a\downarrow0}\inf_{L,\mathsf s}
\frac{\hbar c}{a_{\tau,a}\Lambda_{\mathrm{YM}}^{(\mathsf s)}}
\left[-\log\!\left(
r_{a,L}^{(\mathsf s)}+
\sqrt{R_{a,L}^{(\mathsf s)}S_{a,L}^{(\mathsf s)}}
\right)\right]
>0.}
\tag{GC34}
$$

The expression inside the logarithm must first be proved below one. A fixed
positive adjacent-slice deficit would represent an energy diverging like
\(a_\tau^{-1}\), not an ordinary finite continuum mass.

Different roots, trees, and chord orders give different matrices but
unitarily equivalent complete physical carriers. The true contraction
\(\rho_{\mathrm{GI}}\) is frame independent; the matrix norm is a certificate
whose sharpness can be optimized over those choices. A favorable tree is an
analysis frame, not a preferred physical origin.

## What has and has not been reversed

The theorem gives a precise Copernican statement:

$$
\text{open link presentations}
\xrightarrow{\text{gauge quotient}}
\text{closed relational distinctions}
\xrightarrow{\text{two-slice transport}}
\text{attenuation rate}.
\tag{GC35}
$$

Gauge symmetry is the grammar under which link presentations describe the
same relation. It does not itself create the gap. Torsor frames and tree
gauge choose coordinates; they do not forget. Conditional expectation is
the noninvertible restriction, and the relative two-slice transport tests
whether every nonvacuum closed distinction fades at a uniform rate.

This is a rigorous sense in which closure precedes a physical excitation:
the tree carries no gauge-invariant distinction, while a chord can create
one. It does **not** prove that knots select three dimensions, that cycles
alone generate mass, or that \(SU(3)\) has been derived. Graph cycles occur
in every ambient dimension, and the gauge group remains an input. Nor does
the probability representation assert stochastic ontology: it is the
positive two-slice relation associated with the transfer.

The remaining hard work is analytic and continuum-sensitive. Maximal-tree
holonomies can be macroscopically long, while strict subgraph-invariant
algebras omit open boundary edge modes. [[gauge-boundary-frame-gluing/inq|The
finite Gauss-gluing theorem]] now supplies the exact carrier correction:

$$
\mathcal H_{\mathrm{GI}}
\cong
\left(
\mathcal H_A^{\mathrm{ext}}
\widehat\otimes
\mathcal H_B^{\mathrm{ext}}
\right)^{G^\partial}
\cong
\widehat\bigoplus_\lambda
M_{A,\lambda}\widehat\otimes M_{B,\lambda^*}.
\tag{GC36}
$$

A cycle crossing a cut is assembled from two charged open paths, not from
two separately closed regional loops. Averaging the boundary group on each
region first keeps only \(\lambda=\mathbf1\) and can erase that global
innovation. The local refinement must therefore resolve boundary-charge
sectors before Gauss gluing and construct physical innovation projections
only afterward. [[vacuum-aligned-innovation-completion/inq|The
vacuum-aligned completion theorem]] now performs that construction exactly:
equivariant regional innovations commute with compact-group isotypic
projections, their dual-charge products give a complete physical resolution
after diagonal gluing, and the correlation half-density transports it to the
interacting carrier. Because \(\sqrt r\) can occupy several of those blocks,
the centered carrier contains an additional canonical balance sector
\[
\overline{\operatorname{span}}\{E_{ij\lambda}\sqrt r\}
\ominus\mathbb C\sqrt r .
\]
In the two-link \(\mathbb Z_2\) diagnostic this balance line is the entire
nonvacuum carrier while every internally centered charge block is zero.
Thus the remaining analytic matrix must contain its row and column;
quantitative control of the complete correlated transfer matrix is still
open.

The finite theorem also lives in the commutative link-configuration algebra.
It does not automatically give conditional expectations on the complete
noncommutative link-plus-electric observable algebra or on a continuum
Type-III net. For a faithful normal state, Takesaki modular invariance is the
gate for a state-preserving von Neumann-algebra expectation. Ordinary nested
vacuum local algebras generally fail more sharply: Reeh--Schlieder cyclicity
and separation force any vacuum-preserving expectation under the usual
hypotheses to be the identity inclusion. The safe continuum route is to
transport the finite Euclidean/interface Hilbert projections through an OS
limit and then separately prove their algebraic, completely positive, and
tail-complete realization.
