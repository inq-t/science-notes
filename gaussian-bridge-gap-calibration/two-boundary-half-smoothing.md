# Two-Boundary Half-Smoothing

Gaussian and independent bit-flip bridges admit a complete two-ended prediction bound by one-ended propagation at half the distance. This controls discarded high-frequency observables without assuming a lower spectral gap. It also explains why comparing both predictors at the same distance can fail badly even when the complete bridge remains uniformly gapped.

## A complete operator sandwich

Write \(P_\ell=e^{-\ell L}\), where \(L\ge0\) is the inverse-length Euclidean generator. For one Ornstein--Uhlenbeck mode with frequency \(\omega>0\), put
\[
\alpha=e^{-\omega\ell},\qquad
\beta=\frac{2\alpha^2}{1+\alpha^2}.
\tag{HS1}
\]
The one-end transfer has eigenvalue \(\alpha^d\) on Hermite degree \(d\), while \(S_\ell=K_\ell^*K_\ell\) has eigenvalue \(\beta^d\). An independent symmetric bit flip has the same one-bit eigenvalues; its products are indexed by subsets rather than arbitrary Hermite degrees.

Since
\[
\alpha^2\le\beta\le\alpha,\qquad
2\alpha\le1+\alpha^2,
\]
diagonalization proves
\[
\boxed{P_{2\ell}\le S_\ell\le P_\ell,\qquad
I-P_\ell\le B_\ell\le I-P_{2\ell}.}
\tag{HS2}
\]
For finitely many independent modes with frequencies \(\omega_i>0\), multiply these inequalities on the complete tensor Hermite/Walsh basis. They also extend to a countable product on its cylinder-dense \(L^2\) carrier, when that stationary product process is supplied. No strictly positive \(\inf_i\omega_i\) is needed for the comparison; without it the complete gap may be zero.

Thus
\[
\boxed{\|K_\ell Q\|^2\le\|P_{\ell/2}Q\|^2}
\tag{HS3}
\]
for every orthogonal projection \(Q\). Equivalently, moving both endpoints from distance \(\ell\) to \(2\ell\) gives \(\|K_{2\ell}f\|\le\|P_\ell f\|\). The clock or propagation parameter has not been freely renormalized: the bridge's physical separation has changed.

For \(\Lambda>0\), let \(Q_\Lambda=\mathbf1_{[\Lambda,\infty)}(L)\) and define the genuine spectral-tail prediction cost \(\delta_\Lambda(\ell):=\|K_\ell Q_\Lambda\|^2\). Then
\[
\boxed{\delta_\Lambda(\ell)\le e^{-\ell\Lambda}.}
\tag{HS4}
\]
This is an upper-tail estimate, not a lower bound on the smallest positive eigenvalue. If \(\Lambda\) is a physical energy cutoff, replace \(\ell\Lambda\) by \(\ell\Lambda/(\hbar c)\). A spectral projection is not automatically a conditional expectation onto a retained observable algebra.

## Why the same-distance estimate is too strong

With equal one-mode damping \(\alpha\), degree-\(d\) parity or Hermite observables satisfy
\[
\frac{\|K_\ell f_d\|^2}{\|P_\ell f_d\|^2}
=\left(\frac{2}{1+\alpha^2}\right)^d.
\tag{HS5}
\]
The ratio is unbounded in degree. Yet the complete centered product bridge floor is \(1-\beta>0\), independent of the number of equal-frequency coordinates. Failure of a same-distance prediction comparison is not failure of tensorized coercivity.

The issue is not restricted to non-algebraic cutoffs. On a \(d\)-bit cube, retain every configuration separately except merge the all-plus and all-minus configurations. This is a deterministic statistic. Its discarded projection is rank one, generated by their normalized signed difference. If \(x=\alpha^2\), then
\[
\|P_\ell Q\|^2=\frac{(1+x)^d-(1-x)^d}{2^d},\qquad
\|K_\ell Q\|^2=\frac{(1+\beta)^d-(1-\beta)^d}{2^d}.
\tag{HS6}
\]
Its Walsh expansion has equal squared coefficients \(2^{1-d}\) on the odd subsets and zero on even subsets, which proves (HS6). The ratio grows asymptotically as
\[
\left(\frac{1+3x}{(1+x)^2}\right)^d,
\]
whose base exceeds one for \(0<x<1\). This intentionally nongeometric readout defeats a uniform same-distance constant even for a genuine retained sigma algebra. It does not contradict the exact zero-loss implication or the density-qualified smoothing certificate in [[bridge-score-fusion-geometry/two-boundary-multiplication-and-predictive-tails|the multiplication theorem]].

## The interacting target

A useful extension would prove
\[
S_\ell\le C_\ell P_{\theta\ell}
\]
with \(0<\theta<2\), independently controlled \(C_\ell\), and sufficient uniformity to make high-frequency prediction small. The resulting bound is \(C_\ell e^{-\theta\ell\Lambda}\); its logarithm must tend to minus infinity, so degeneration of \(\theta\) must also be controlled. Gaussian and bit-flip carriers give \((C_\ell,\theta)=(1,1)\). Their product/Hermite structure is substantive; a general Markov bridge does not inherit it.

Combined with a separately proved retained-response floor, a small upper tail can close the [[bridge-data-augmentation-solder/predictive-sufficient-interfaces|additive response margin]]. It cannot substitute for that low-sector theorem.
