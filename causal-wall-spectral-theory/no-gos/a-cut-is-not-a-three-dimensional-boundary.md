# A Horizon Cut Is Not a Three-Dimensional Boundary Theory

A codimension-two horizon cut in a four-dimensional spacetime is two-dimensional, whereas the Euclidean stress response used by holographic cosmology lives on a three-dimensional carrier. They cannot be identified directly; a CWST realization must construct a history, thickening, conformal boundary, or other functor that changes the carrier and its measure.

## Dimension and carrier

The shared wall interface uses a causal region \(D_N\), its horizon or boundary \(\mathcal H_N\), and a selected codimension-two cut \(\Sigma_N\). In \(3+1\) spacetime dimensions,

$$
\dim\Sigma_N=2.
$$

The stress-tensor decomposition and Fourier precision used by [[vendor/holographic-cosmology/entry|holographic cosmology]] are three-dimensional:

$$
\langle\!\langle T_{ij}(q)T_{kl}(-q)\rangle\!\rangle,
\qquad i,j=1,2,3,
$$

and

$$
\frac12\int\frac{\mathrm d^3k}{(2\pi)^3}
\mathcal K_\zeta(k)|\zeta_{\mathbf k}|^2.
$$

Therefore

$$
\boxed{\Sigma_N\ne\text{the three-dimensional Euclidean QFT carrier}}
$$

as a matter of dimension and type. This is **[NO-GO]** for direct identity, not for every possible representation.

## Possible bridges

A completed construction could instead use:

- the three-dimensional history or worldvolume swept out by a family of cuts;
- a spatial Cauchy hypersurface associated with the observer region;
- a thickened screen with a controlled normal direction;
- an asymptotic conformal boundary in a domain-wall/cosmology realization; or
- a boundary-to-bulk or restriction/extension functor whose induced response is three-dimensional.

Each option changes more than a label. It must transport the algebra, state, tangent, measure, locality properties, and response kernel, and it must explain the extra length dimension that separates an areal \(L^{-2}\) modulus from an \(L^{-3}\) Fourier precision.

## Consequence

The phrase “the wall stress tensor” is under-typed until the carrier has been chosen. A two-dimensional cut stress, a three-dimensional Euclidean stress tensor, a four-dimensional matter trace, and a modular generator on a horizon algebra are different objects.
