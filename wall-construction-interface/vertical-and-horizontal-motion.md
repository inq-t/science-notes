# Vertical Modular Flow and Horizontal State Change

The modular automorphism group of one fixed state and displacement through a scale-indexed family of states are different kinds of motion. The first is vertical within one fiber; the second requires a constructed comparison between fibers before it has a tangent or a distinguishability norm.

For a von Neumann algebra \(\mathcal A\) in standard form with faithful normal state \(\omega\), Tomita--Takesaki theory supplies

$$
\sigma_s^\omega(A)
=\Delta_\omega^{is}A\Delta_\omega^{-is},
\qquad
A\in\mathcal A.
$$

This automorphism group fixes both \(\mathcal A\) and \(\omega\). It moves observables relative to the selected state and is therefore vertical motion in the state-bundle picture.

The horizontal programme instead seeks

$$
N\longmapsto
(\Sigma_N,\mathcal A_N,\omega_N).
$$

Even if every fiber has its own modular automorphism group, the modular parameter \(s\) is not thereby the scale coordinate \(N\). If the algebras differ, the formal expression \(\partial_N\omega_N\) attempts to subtract states with different domains. Relative entropy and relative modular data likewise require one common carrier or a declared transport.

The missing datum is supplied schematically by maps such as

$$
\mathcal T_{N_2N_1}:
(\mathcal A_{N_1},\omega_{N_1})
\longrightarrow
(\mathcal A_{N_2},\omega_{N_2}),
$$

with a composition law, covariance properties, and controlled holonomy. [[cross-fiber-transport|Cross-fiber transport and state selection]] owns the possible constructions and their failure modes.

Only after transport can one define a physical horizontal tangent

$$
v_N=D^{\mathrm{hor}}\Phi(\partial_N)
$$

and ask for its BKM norm. Faithfulness, differentiability, the physical quotient, and finiteness or renormalization remain part of that construction.

## Distinct parameters

The following labels have different carriers:

$$
\text{proper time }t,
\quad
\text{conformal time }\eta,
\quad
\text{scale age }N,
\quad
\text{modular parameter }s,
\quad
\text{binary coordinate }\theta,
\quad
\text{signed horizon rapidity }\widehat\zeta_A.
$$

Relations among them require explicit maps. The conditional affine theorem in [[basic-concepts/soldering/affine-scale-state|affine scale--state comparison]] concerns \(N\mapsto\theta\); [[conformal-scale-geometry/horizon-allocation|horizon allocation]] defines \(N\mapsto\widehat\zeta_A\) from FLRW kinematics. Neither identifies the two targets.
