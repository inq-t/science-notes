# Kinetic Hessian Bootstrap and Uniform Conditional Response

The supplied electric geometry can control the unknown Wilson vacuum's full logarithmic Hessian, rather than assuming that Hessian has a favorable sign. A forced Killing-score equation closes a scalar Riccati bound at sufficiently small local magnetic coupling. The bound holds throughout the actual Haar preparation, survives its ground-state limit, and controls conditional score covariance for every disjoint raw-link cut without a volume or boundary-area factor.

**Status: [EXACT] for the finite compact-product Hamiltonians and explicit small-coupling regime below.** The graph, metric and Hamiltonian are supplied. This is a self-contained strong-electric-coupling estimate using standard Bochner and comparison arguments, not a novelty claim or a four-dimensional continuum construction. Its additional content relative to the [[algebra/partial-bochner-and-ground-state-score|first-score bound]] is full Hessian and pointwise conditional-response control.

## The local force norm, not the extensive potential

Let \(E\) be a finite nonempty set of raw links and \(\mathcal P\) a collection of
oriented elementary squares, each containing four distinct links. On
\(M=SU(2)^E\), with normalized product Haar measure and
\(Q=-2\operatorname{Tr}\), supply
\[
H=-\kappa\Delta+V,\qquad
\Delta=\sum_{e\in E}\Delta_e,\qquad
V=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P}\lambda_p(1-q_p),\qquad
q_p=\tfrac12\operatorname{Tr}\operatorname{Hol}_p,\quad \kappa>0.
\tag{KH1}
\]
The couplings may be real; the usual Wilson case has
\(\lambda_p=\lambda\ge0\). Define the local absolute strength
\[
\ell=\max_{e\in E}\sum_{p\ni e}|\lambda_p|,
\qquad \mu=\ell/\kappa.
\tag{KH2}
\]
For a finite open three-dimensional cubic box, each link belongs to at
most four squares, so \(\ell\le4\lambda\). Repeated link occurrences
or longer interaction loops require a different incidence bound.

For fixed \(\xi=(\xi_e)\in\mathfrak{su}(2)^E\), let \(X_\xi\) be the
Killing field generated by \(U_e\mapsto e^{s\xi_e}U_e\).
Its length is the constant \(|\xi|^2=\sum_e|\xi_e|_Q^2\), it commutes
with \(\Delta\), and \(\nabla_{X_\xi}X_\xi=0\).
These statements use the product bi-invariant metric.

A \(Q\)-unit Lie algebra matrix has matrix operator norm \(1/2\).
Differentiating a half-trace twice inserts two such matrices, including
when a link occurs with inverse orientation. Therefore
\[
|Y_\eta X_\xi q_p|
\le \frac14
\left(\sum_{e\in p}|\xi_e|\right)
\left(\sum_{f\in p}|\eta_f|\right).
\tag{KH3}
\]
The same-link ordered derivative obeys the same bound. Weighted
Cauchy--Schwarz and the four-link support give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_p|\lambda_p|\left(\sum_{e\in p}|\xi_e|\right)^2
&\le4\sum_e\left(\sum_{p\ni e}|\lambda_p|\right)|\xi_e|^2
\le4\ell|\xi|^2,\\
|Y_\eta X_\xi V|&\le\ell|\xi||\eta|,
\qquad
\sup_{|\xi|=1}\|\nabla(X_\xi V)\|_\infty\le\ell.
\end{aligned}
\tag{KH4}
\]
This is an ordered Killing-derivative estimate. No equality between
that derivative matrix and the covariant Hessian is assumed.

## A forced derivative equation closes its own curvature bound

Use the actual positive Haar preparation and its normalized density:
\[
\Psi_t=e^{-tH}1,\qquad u_t=\log\Psi_t,\qquad
\rho_t=\Psi_t^2/\|\Psi_t\|_2^2,\qquad t\ge0.
\tag{KH5}
\]
Compact ellipticity and positivity improvement give smooth
\(\Psi_t>0\); all spatial derivatives below are classical on every
finite time interval. Additive scalar terms in \(u_t\) do not affect
the argument. The logarithmic heat equation is
\[
\partial_tu_t=\kappa\Delta u_t+\kappa|\nabla u_t|^2-V.
\tag{KH6}
\]
Set
\[
L_t=\kappa(\Delta+2\nabla u_t\cdot\nabla),\qquad
r_{\xi,t}=X_\xi u_t.
\]
Because \(X_\xi\) is Killing and commutes with \(\Delta\),
\[
(\partial_t-L_t)r_{\xi,t}=-X_\xi V,\qquad r_{\xi,0}=0.
\tag{KH7}
\]
The Killing property accounts for differentiation of the metric
gradient pairing. Treating frame coefficients as constant in a
noninvariant coordinate chart would not justify (KH7).

Define a scalar envelope over both configuration and fixed field:
\[
m(t)=\sup_{\substack{x\in M\\|\xi|=1}}
|\nabla r_{\xi,t}(x)|.
\tag{KH8}
\]
At any point and any unit tangent \(v\), choose the fixed Killing field
with \(X_\xi(x)=v\). Its integral curves are geodesics, so
\[
|\operatorname{Hess}u_t(v,v)|
=|X_\xi X_\xi u_t(x)|
\le|\nabla r_{\xi,t}(x)|\le m(t).
\tag{KH9}
\]
Thus \(m\) bounds the absolute operator norm of the symmetric covariant
Hessian, although it is itself defined using an ordered derivative.

The product Ricci tensor is \(\operatorname{Ric}=\tfrac12g\).
For \(r=r_{\xi,t}\), forced Bochner gives exactly
\[
\begin{aligned}
(\partial_t-L_t)|\nabla r|^2
={}&-2\kappa|\operatorname{Hess}r|^2\\
&-2\kappa(\operatorname{Ric}-2\operatorname{Hess}u_t)
       (\nabla r,\nabla r)
-2\langle\nabla r,\nabla X_\xi V\rangle.
\end{aligned}
\tag{KH10}
\]
No time derivative of the drift is needed: the metric is fixed.
Apply the compact maximum principle over \(x\) and the unit sphere of
\(\xi\), then use (KH4) and (KH9). The upper Dini derivative obeys
\[
\boxed{
D^+m(t)\le-\frac{\kappa}{2}m(t)+2\kappa m(t)^2+\ell,
\qquad m(0)=0.
}
\tag{KH11}
\]
One may regularize the norm at zero before taking the limit. For
positive \(m\), this is simply (KH10) divided by \(2m\) at a maximum.
The parameter sphere is compact for each finite graph; it contributes
no factor counting its dimension.

If \(\mu\le1/32\), define
\[
\delta=\frac{1-\sqrt{1-32\mu}}8,\qquad
c=\frac12-2\delta
=\frac{1+\sqrt{1-32\mu}}4.
\tag{KH12}
\]
The scalar equation on the right of (KH11), starting at zero, stays
below its smaller nonnegative stationary root \(\delta\).
Scalar comparison therefore proves
\[
\boxed{
\|\operatorname{Hess}u_t\|_{\mathrm{op},\infty}\le\delta\le\frac18,
\qquad
\operatorname{Ric}-2\operatorname{Hess}u_t\ge cg,
\qquad c\ge\frac14,
\quad t\ge0.
}
\tag{KH13}
\]
At the critical endpoint \(\mu=1/32\), the comparison solution is
\[
m_{\rm cmp}(t)=\frac18\,\frac{\kappa t}{4+\kappa t},
\]
so it cannot reach the double root at finite time. When \(\ell=0\),
the preparation is constant and \(m=0\). This proves the endpoint
cases without assuming strict smallness.

This is not a curvature assumption recycled as a proof. The unknown
Hessian is bounded by \(m\), Bochner closes the scalar inequality for
that same \(m\), and the initial Haar state supplies \(m(0)=0\).
Neither an interacting gap nor an inverse interacting generator enters.

## Conditional covariance is now a bounded operator

Fix any raw partition \(E=A\sqcup B\), keeping all link coordinates
in each region. For a fixed retained configuration \(a\), the actual
conditional law on the hidden product is
\[
d\rho_t(b\mid a)=
\frac{\Psi_t(a,b)^2\,dU_B(b)}
{\int\Psi_t(a,b')^2\,dU_B(b')}.
\tag{KH14}
\]
Its weighted Ricci tensor is the \(B\)-restriction of that in (KH13);
the conditional normalization depends only on \(a\). Consequently
\[
\operatorname{Var}_{\rho_t(\cdot\mid a)}F
\le c^{-1}\int|\nabla_BF|^2\,d\rho_t(b\mid a).
\tag{KH15}
\]
For completeness, this follows by integrating Bochner for the
conditional generator \(\Delta_B+2\nabla_Bu_t\cdot\nabla_B\):
an eigenfunction with positive eigenvalue \(\gamma\) of its negative
satisfies \(\gamma^2\|F\|^2\ge c\gamma\|F\|^2\), so
\(\gamma\ge c\). Compact ellipticity supplies the spectral
decomposition; no uniform spectral estimate was assumed.

Let \(s_{A,t}=\nabla_Au_t\), and define the actual conditional
half-density score covariance
\[
M_{A,t}(a)=\operatorname{Cov}_{\rho_t}(s_{A,t}\mid a).
\]
For a fixed \(v\in T_aSU(2)^A\), apply (KH15) to
\(r_v(b)=\langle v,s_{A,t}(a,b)\rangle\).
Product geometry gives
\(\nabla_Br_v=\operatorname{Hess}_{BA}u_t\,v\);
there is no derivative of \(v\) in a hidden direction. By (KH13),
\[
\boxed{
0\le M_{A,t}(a)\le\frac{\delta^2}{c}I_{T_aSU(2)^A}
\quad\text{for every }a,t,A,B.
}
\tag{KH16}
\]
The bound is independent of both regional volumes and the number of
crossing squares. It is an upper bound, not a positive lower floor
for conditional information. It need not be sharp when the cut has
few or no crossing interactions.

In the notation of the
[[boundary-interaction-and-conditional-score-budget#The exact defect is differentiation against forgetting|conditional differentiation identity]],
(KH16) gives
\[
\|\mathcal C_{A,t}\|^2\le4\delta^2/c,\qquad
\mathcal C_{A,t}=\nabla_AP_{A,t}-P_{A,t}\nabla_A.
\tag{KH17}
\]
This is an operator norm, unlike a bound on the integrated trace of
\(M_{A,t}\). Smooth positive densities give smooth conditional
integration. The conditional differentiation identity and (KH17)
extend \(P_{A,t}\) to the \(H^1\) form domain.

## Physical return at the ground state

At a fixed preparation time, \(K_t=-L_t\) is the symmetric reversible
operator in \(L^2(\rho_t)\), with form
\(\kappa\int|\nabla f|^2\,d\rho_t\). It obeys a Poincare bound
\(\kappa c\), but it is not the complete conjugate of the original
Hamiltonian:
\[
\Psi_t^{-1}H\Psi_t=K_t-\partial_tu_t
\tag{KH18}
\]
as an operator on test functions, with the last term acting by
multiplication. Before stationarity, that nonconstant term must not
be silently omitted from a claim about the original physical clock.

For each fixed finite graph, compact ellipticity gives a unique
positive ground vector \(\psi\), and the rescaled preparation converges
to it smoothly. This finite-volume convergence needs no
volume-uniform rate. Passing (KH13)--(KH16) to the limit gives the
same bounds for \(u_\infty=\log\psi\) and \(\rho=\psi^2\).
Now the returned generator is exactly
\[
K_\infty=\psi^{-1}(H-E_0)\psi
=-\kappa(\Delta+2\nabla\log\psi\cdot\nabla),
\qquad
H-E_0\ge\kappa c
\quad\text{on the ground-state complement}.
\tag{KH19}
\]
The operators have the inherited compact elliptic \(H^2\) domains
and \(H^1\) form domains. Gauge invariance of the unique positive
ground vector allows restriction of the bound to the physical
gauge-invariant carrier without changing the vacuum.

For the complete raw-region readout, let \(P_A\) be conditional
expectation under this actual ground law, \(Q_A=I-P_A\), and
\[
a_A[f]=\kappa\int|\nabla_Af|^2\,d\rho_A,\qquad
B_Af=Q_AK_\infty f
=-2\kappa\langle s_A-P_As_A,\nabla_Af\rangle.
\]
Its principal kinetic symbol is retained, so its hidden part is first
order. Equations (KH16) and the exact covariance identity imply
\[
\boxed{
\|B_Af\|_{L^2(\rho)}^2
\le\frac{4\kappa\delta^2}{c}\,a_A[f],
\qquad
\frac{4\kappa\delta^2}{c}\le\frac{\kappa}{4}.
}
\tag{KH20}
\]
Initially this holds for smooth regional functions; it extends
continuously to the full regional form domain. Restricting to retained
gauge-invariant functions preserves the bound. At finite time the same
calculation holds for \(Q_{A,t}K_tf\), not automatically for the
conjugated original operator with its additional term in (KH18).

This does not license an arbitrary join of incomplete scalar loop
readouts. When a join forgets a nonbasic principal covariance,
[[local-score-bounds-and-the-order-of-hidden-response|its hidden response can be second order]],
and (KH20) is not the relevant type of bound.

For open three-dimensional boxes the sufficient condition is
\(\lambda/\kappa\le1/128\). It stays in the strong-electric-coupling,
fixed-spatial-regulator regime already owned by
[[strong-coupling-gap-and-continuum-crossover/hamiltonian-product-vacuum-stability|product-vacuum stability]]
and the
[[strong-coupling-gap-and-continuum-crossover/inq|strong-coupling programme]].
The new route here supplies explicit local regularity and conditional
response estimates for the actual state; it does not select the
Hamiltonian, prove a spatial continuum limit, or identify a physical
mass independently of the supplied clock.

Finally, (KH13) bounds the total Hessian. It does not by itself prove
the absolute connected-support summability defined in
[[connected-preparation-and-local-normalization|connected preparation]]:
large terms in a connected expansion could cancel before this norm
is taken. [[kinetic-smoothing-and-connected-fourier-control|The kinetic Fourier estimate]]
proves that stronger summability and distant-interaction stability
in its own smaller sufficient regime. Continuation beyond these
smallness conditions remains a separate analytic obligation.
