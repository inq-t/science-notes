# Indiscernibility and the Noether Gap

The identity of indiscernibles gives an observational quotient: presentations that no allowed observable distinguishes have one accessible image. It does not by itself give a conserved current. Noether conservation additionally requires a continuous action, a dynamical or variational structure, and invariance under that action. This gap separates a sound Leibnizian foundation from an unjustified conservation claim.

## Observational quotient

Let \(X\) be a set of presentations, and let \(\mathcal O\) be a family of accessible maps

$$
f:X\longrightarrow Y_f.
$$

Define

$$
x\sim_{\mathcal O}y
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
f(x)=f(y)
\text{ for every }f\in\mathcal O.
$$

Then \(\sim_{\mathcal O}\) is an equivalence relation. Every accessible map factors uniquely through the quotient projection

$$
\pi:X\longrightarrow X/{\sim_{\mathcal O}}.
$$

Indeed, define \(\bar f([x])=f(x)\). This is well defined by the definition of \(\sim_{\mathcal O}\), and \(f=\bar f\circ\pi\). The quotient is therefore the coarsest set through which all declared observables factor.

This is one exact mathematical form of the principle that an undetectable difference has no accessible physical meaning. It remains relative to the chosen family \(\mathcal O\); enlarging the observables can refine the quotient.

## Why the groupoid may be prior to the orbit set

Suppose a group \(G\) acts on \(X\) and its orbits are the observational equivalence classes. The orbit set \(X/G\) records which presentations are equivalent, but it forgets stabilizers and the arrows that identify them. The action groupoid

$$
G\times X\rightrightarrows X
$$

retains that data. More general observer changes, local contexts, or causal cuts may require a groupoid not generated by one global group; gluing such local symmetry data can require the structures described in [[basic-concepts/groupoids/entry|groupoids]], [[basic-concepts/descent/entry|descent]], and [[basic-concepts/stacks/entry|stacks]].

This matters for facthood. A coarse quotient can erase precisely the isotropy and transition data needed to compare different local pointings.

## The Noether gap

The observational quotient supplies none of the following by itself:

- a continuous one-parameter action;
- a phase space, symplectic form, or action functional;
- an invariant Hamiltonian or Lagrangian;
- a moment map or current;
- a temporal or scale evolution; or
- a boundary-flux prescription.

Therefore

$$
\boxed{
\text{identity of indiscernibles}
\Longrightarrow
\text{observational quotient},
}
$$

A presentation groupoid requires specified arrows whose orbits refine or realize that equivalence relation; a stack additionally requires local gluing and descent data.

but not, without additional hypotheses,

$$
\text{identity of indiscernibles}
\Longrightarrow
\text{Noether conservation}.
$$

[[diagonal-charge-balance]] states the additional Hamiltonian hypotheses under which separately represented quantities really do become parts of one conserved charge.

## Four different consequences of symmetry

The word *symmetry* can lead to different mathematical outputs.

1. A continuous global variational symmetry can produce a Noether current or charge.
2. A gauge symmetry generally produces a Noether identity or constraint; physical charges often live at boundaries.
3. A discrete symmetry such as \(Q\mapsto-Q\) can produce a grading, Casimir, selection rule, or topological sector, but no ordinary infinitesimal current.
4. A one-sided semigroup can encode irreversible accessibility or record preservation without possessing inverses.

The causal-scale programme contains candidates of all four kinds. Positive scale ratios form \(\mathbb R_+\), their logarithms form the additive line, and normal reflection extends the line to

$$
\mathbb R\rtimes\mathbb Z_2.
$$

Half-sided modular inclusion instead supplies an affine dilation--translation structure. These are related candidates, not automatically the same group. A physical theory must construct the homomorphisms or common action that connect them.

## Philosophical consequence

Not every description bottoms out in a group. A torsor may retain relative differences without a preferred origin; a groupoid may retain local equivalences without one global action; a semigroup may retain composition without reversibility. The identity of indiscernibles points toward this family of relational structures. Which one governs causal facthood is a construction problem, not a matter of terminology.
