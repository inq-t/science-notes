# A Balanced-Fisher Grain for Yang–Mills

A concrete Yang--Mills-specific grain can be proposed without importing the cosmological \(46\,\mathrm{MeV}\) value: follow a declared, covariantly transported score of the full blocked Wilson law under logarithmic change of resolution, and select the scale at which its midpoint-score response is divided equally between the part recoverable from both slab boundaries and the irreducible midpoint residue. The fixed-carrier decomposition is exact; the RG connection, existence, uniqueness, scheme covariance, continuum stability, and block-to-slab synchronization are open. Even if the selector succeeds, it tests one chosen scale tangent and does not replace the separate uniform bound on every nonvacuum direction.

**Status: [EXACT] for finite-lattice DQM along a specified smooth action path, the fixed-channel conditional-expectation Fisher decomposition, the dominated moving-channel correction, and the Markov-channel one-vector no-gap counterexample; [CONSTRUCTION PRINCIPLE] for the balanced selector; [OPEN] for a natural transported RG score, a scheme-covariant continuum root, and the complete Yang--Mills response floor.**

## A scale score requires a common carrier

Fix a reflection-positive pure Yang--Mills theory label

$$
\mathfrak t=(\mathfrak g,G,\mathcal S,\theta=0),
$$

including its compact simple gauge group or global form and claimed vacuum sector. Let \(r\) denote only regulator data: lattice and volume, spacings, the coupling point on a declared renormalized trajectory, boundary placement, and admissible discretization or blocking scheme. The fixed label \(\mathfrak t\) is suppressed below. Write

$$
N=\log\frac{\ell}{a_r}
\tag{BF1}
$$

for logarithmic blocking depth. Measures at different \(N\) generally live on different configuration spaces, so \(\partial_N\) is meaningless until the blocking maps or another covariant transport place them on one declared comparison carrier.

Assume such a transport has been constructed and let \(\nu_{r,N}\) be the transported, gauge-invariant midpoint marginal of the **full nonlinear blocked Wilson law**. Suppose it is differentiable in quadratic mean relative to an \(N\)-independent dominating measure \(m_r\), with density \(p_{r,N}\). Define its centered DQM score \(s_{r,N}\in L^2(\nu_{r,N})\) by

$$
\partial_N\sqrt{p_{r,N}}
=
\frac12s_{r,N}\sqrt{p_{r,N}}
\quad\text{in }L^2(m_r).
\tag{BF2}
$$

If the densities are positive on common support, pointwise \(C^1\) in \(N\), and dominated strongly enough to interchange differentiation and integration, then

$$
s_{r,N}=\partial_N\log p_{r,N}
\quad\nu_{r,N}\text{-a.e.}
\tag{BF2a}
$$

Normalization gives

$$
\mathbb E_{\nu_{r,N}}[s_{r,N}]=0.
\tag{BF3}
$$

If

$$
p_{r,N}
=
\mathcal Z_{r,N}^{-1}
e^{-S^{\mathrm{mid}}_{r,N}},
\tag{BF4}
$$

where all scale-dependent Jacobians have been included in the effective midpoint action, then

$$
s_{r,N}
=
-\partial_NS^{\mathrm{mid}}_{r,N}
+
\mathbb E_{\nu_{r,N}}
\!\left[\partial_NS^{\mathrm{mid}}_{r,N}\right].
\tag{BF5}
$$

The score is a state tangent generated by change of resolution. It is not the beta function, the Hamiltonian, or a spacetime velocity, although a completed RG construction may relate those objects.

On a finite lattice this regularity is not the difficult step once a path has been declared. The configuration space \(G^E\) is compact. If \(S^{\mathrm{mid}}_{r,N}\) and \(\partial_NS^{\mathrm{mid}}_{r,N}\) are jointly continuous along a fixed-carrier action path, dominated differentiation gives differentiability in quadratic mean and

$$
s_{r,N}
=
-\partial_NS^{\mathrm{mid}}_{r,N}
+
\mathbb E_{r,N}[\partial_NS^{\mathrm{mid}}_{r,N}],
\qquad
\mathcal I_{r,N}
=
\operatorname{Var}_{r,N}(\partial_NS^{\mathrm{mid}}_{r,N}).
\tag{BF5a}
$$

The theorem validates a specified smooth finite-regulator path. It does not derive a continuous path from discrete block steps or make its tangent canonical.

There is now a precise candidate interpretation for this tangent. Put \(A_{r,N}=\partial_NS^{\mathrm{mid}}_{r,N}\). Then \(s_{r,N}=-(A_{r,N}-\mathbb E A_{r,N})\): it is the centered effective-action scale insertion. If a natural RG connection and continuum Ward identity carry \(A_{r,N}\) to a smeared renormalized stress-tensor trace, the balanced-Fisher construction is a conditional decomposition of **trace susceptibility**. [[global-local-response-reconstruction/trace-source-two-moment-solder|The trace-source two-moment solder]] shows how the thermal mean and vacuum covariance of this same direction could reconnect cosmology to glueball spectroscopy without identifying them. That Ward identification, including its contact and action-curvature terms, is open.

## The moving-channel and RG-connection firewall

Suppose the conditional boundary channel also depends on \(N\), with density \(c_{r,N}(z\mid y)\) relative to an \(N\)-independent dominating measure. Assume conditional DQM with enough joint domination to differentiate the marginal law. Define its conditional DQM score by

$$
\partial_N\sqrt{c_{r,N}(\,\cdot\mid y)}
=
\frac12u_{r,N}(y,\cdot)
\sqrt{c_{r,N}(\,\cdot\mid y)}
\quad\text{in conditional }L^2.
\tag{BF5b}
$$

If the conditional densities are positive on common support, pointwise \(C^1\), and dominated strongly enough, then

$$
u_{r,N}(y,z)
=
\partial_N\log c_{r,N}(z\mid y)
\quad\text{a.e.}
\tag{BF5b'}
$$

Differentiation of the joint law gives the actual boundary-marginal score

$$
\boxed{
s^{\partial}_{r,N}(z)
=
\mathbb E_{r,N}\!\left[
s_{r,N}(Y)+u_{r,N}(Y,Z)
\mid Z=z
\right].}
\tag{BF5c}
$$

Therefore \(K_{r,N}s_{r,N}\) is the boundary score only when the channel is \(N\)-independent, or when the conditional projection of \(u_{r,N}\) vanishes. In general it remains the exact conditional prediction of the **midpoint component** of scale change, but it is not the whole observed Fisher score. The channel score \(u_{r,N}\) is not the RG connection used to identify midpoint carriers at different \(N\). [[scale-score-connection/inq|The scale-score connection]] gives the bundle-covariant construction.

The selector below deliberately uses the **frozen-channel tangent experiment**: at each \(N\), perturb the midpoint law in the instantaneous score direction \(s_{r,N}\) while holding the already constructed channel \(K_{r,N}\) fixed. Equations (BF7)--(BF11) are the exact Fisher decomposition of that auxiliary perturbation. They do not claim to be the Fisher decomposition of the actual moving boundary law in (BF5c). A different moving-experiment selector would have to use the joint score \(s+u\) on the joint carrier and redefine all three ledgers.

This is not a technical nicety. Let \(X_N\sim\mathcal N(0,e^{2N})\). On the original carrier its scale score is

$$
s_N(x)=-1+e^{-2N}x^2,
\qquad
\mathcal I_N=2.
\tag{BF5d}
$$

The \(N\)-dependent bijection \(Y=e^{-N}X_N\) transports the family to the constant law \(\mathcal N(0,1)\), whose score and Fisher information both vanish. Fisher information is covariant under a fixed sample-space isomorphism, not under an arbitrary moving identification. [[library/exact-scheme-independence/inq|Exact-RG kernels act as field connections]], and [[library/the-classically-perfect-fixed-point-action-for-su3-gauge-theory/inq|exact \(SU(3)\) blockings already contain nonunique transformations and free parameters]]. Exact blocking therefore supplies a scheme-labelled family, not a preferred derivative.

## The exact complementary ledgers

At the scale \(N\), let \(Z_{r,N}\) denote the jointly framed data on the two slab boundaries and define

$$
K_{r,N}f
:=
\mathbb E[f(Y_{r,N})\mid Z_{r,N}],
\qquad
B_{r,N}
:=
I-K_{r,N}^*K_{r,N}
\tag{BF6}
$$

on the centered midpoint carrier \(\mathcal H_{r,N}:=L^2_0(\nu_{r,N})\). Let \(Q_{r,N}\) be the orthogonal projection, in that \(N\)-dependent inner product, onto the declared **complete nonvacuum physical midpoint carrier**, and require \(s_{r,N}\in\operatorname{Ran}Q_{r,N}\). Projecting an arbitrary score afterward would define a different auxiliary tangent and would not preserve the DQM identities (BF2)--(BF5). A fixed projection on one comparison Hilbert space would require additional unitary connection maps and is not supplied by a common dominating measure. Showing that \(Q_{r,N}\) really covers the complete Osterwalder--Schrader vacuum complement is a separate realization obligation. [[nonlinear-whole-law-surface-response/inq|Nonlinear whole-law surface response]] gives \(B_{r,N}\) for the finite joint law.

The scale score has three dimensionless midpoint-score ledgers:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal I_{\mathrm{tot}}(r,N)
&:=
\|s_{r,N}\|^2,\\
\mathcal I_{\mathrm{rec}}(r,N)
&:=
\|K_{r,N}s_{r,N}\|^2,\\
\mathcal I_{\mathrm{res}}(r,N)
&:=
\langle s_{r,N},B_{r,N}s_{r,N}\rangle.
\end{aligned}
\tag{BF7}
$$

Orthogonal conditional expectation gives the exact Pythagorean identity

$$
\boxed{
\mathcal I_{\mathrm{tot}}
=
\mathcal I_{\mathrm{rec}}
+
\mathcal I_{\mathrm{res}}.}
\tag{BF8}
$$

The first term is the infinitesimal distinguishability of neighboring midpoint presentations. The second is the part of that midpoint tangent predictable from both boundaries. The third is the average conditional variance left in the midpoint. All three refer to the same state tangent and Fisher metric; no equation of units is being treated as an equation of concepts. When (BF5c) has a nonzero channel-score term, these remain an exact decomposition of the declared midpoint tangent but are not the complete Fisher decomposition of the moving boundary experiment.

Where \(\mathcal I_{\mathrm{tot}}>0\), the residual fraction

$$
\zeta_{r}(N)
:=
\frac{\mathcal I_{\mathrm{res}}(r,N)}
{\mathcal I_{\mathrm{tot}}(r,N)}
=
1-
\frac{\mathcal I_{\mathrm{rec}}(r,N)}
{\mathcal I_{\mathrm{tot}}(r,N)}
\tag{BF9}
$$

lies in \([0,1]\). With carrier and channel held fixed, it is unchanged by a regular smooth reparameterization of the scale coordinate—a local diffeomorphism with nonzero derivative—because the score and both quadratic ledgers acquire the same squared chain-rule factor. This scalar-coordinate covariance is weaker than covariance under a scale-dependent field redefinition or change of blocking scheme.

## The balanced selector

The proposed scale is a transverse solution of

$$
\boxed{
\zeta_r(N_{*,r})=\frac12,}
\tag{BF10}
$$

equivalently

$$
\boxed{
\mathcal I_{\mathrm{rec}}(r,N_{*,r})
=
\mathcal I_{\mathrm{res}}(r,N_{*,r})
=
\frac12\mathcal I_{\mathrm{tot}}(r,N_{*,r}).}
\tag{BF11}
$$

The numeral \(1/2\) is not fitted to a mass or inserted as a conventional flow threshold. It is selected by exchange of the two complementary terms in (BF8). That balance is still a proposed physical principle; Pythagoras alone does not say that nature must cross it.

Define

$$
\ell_{*,r}
:=
a_r e^{N_{*,r}}.
\tag{BF12}
$$

This is a legitimate object-relative grain only if:

1. the transported score is independent of arbitrary blocking coordinates and gauge representatives;
2. the boundary construction at \(N\) has a derived physical half-thickness \(\ell=a_r e^N\), rather than an asserted identification of RG depth with slab time;
3. \(\zeta_r\) is continuous and has one physically selected transverse crossing;
4. the root is stable under harmless changes of blocking scheme; and
5. \(\ell_{*,r}\to\ell_{\mathrm{YM}}\in(0,\infty)\) as volume and regulator are removed.

Failure of existence or uniqueness is a physical failure of this selector, not an invitation to choose a preferred root by hand.

If \(\zeta_r\) crosses from below to above \(1/2\), the root has a precise candidate meaning for “mass engagement”: beyond it, change of resolution is represented more strongly by irreducible midpoint response than by what the two boundaries can recover. This is a statement about an RG/slab family, not yet an epoch in cosmic history. Identifying it with an early-cosmos event would require a separate synchronization and fossil-transport theorem.

Equations (BF1)--(BF12) use only the scale ratio \(\ell/a_r\); no absolute metre, second, \(\hbar\), \(c\), \(G\), or \(k_B\) selects the balance point. Dimensional constants enter only when the selected scale class is presented in a recovered physical unit.

## Exact Gaussian calibration

[[gaussian-bridge-gap-calibration/inq|Gaussian bridge calibration]] shows explicitly that the method returns a dimensionless number appropriate to the chosen tangent, not a universal number. This is a two-parameter calibration of (BF7)--(BF11): an auxiliary parameter tilts the midpoint law with score \(h_k\), while the independent collar length \(\ell\) changes the frozen boundary channel. The stationary variance-one midpoint law itself has zero \(\ell\)-score, so \(h_k\neq\partial_\ell\log p_\ell\). For a Gaussian mode with \(x=\omega\ell\), let \(h_k\) be its normalized \(k\)-th Hermite distinction, with \(k\in\mathbb N_{\geq1}\). The two-boundary conditional transport has

$$
\frac{\|K_\ell h_k\|^2}{\|h_k\|^2}
=
q^{2k},
\qquad
q^2=1-\tanh x.
\tag{BF12a}
$$

Hence its residual fraction is

$$
\zeta_k(x)
=
1-\bigl(1-\tanh x\bigr)^k.
\tag{BF12b}
$$

The balanced root is exactly

$$
\boxed{
x_{*,k}
=
\operatorname{artanh}
\!\left(1-2^{-1/k}\right).}
\tag{BF12c}
$$

Thus

$$
x_{*,1}
=
\frac12\log3
\approx0.549306,
\qquad
x_{*,2}
=
\operatorname{artanh}
\!\left(1-\frac1{\sqrt2}\right)
\approx0.301728.
\tag{BF12d}
$$

A Gaussian mean-shift score lies in the first chaos, while a log-variance scale score is proportional to the second. Even this elementary model therefore gives different grains for different typed questions on the same underlying process. In a field with modes accumulating at \(\omega=0\), one selected score can still satisfy (BF10) while the worst-mode bridge floor vanishes; the calibration reinforces rather than removes the complete-carrier firewall.

## One-scale covariance

If the limiting pure theory has one scheme-labelled transmutation scale \(\Lambda_{\mathrm{YM}}^{(\mathsf s)}\), measured here in energy units, scale covariance requires the limiting balance function to depend on

$$
x
:=
\frac{\ell\Lambda_{\mathrm{YM}}^{(\mathsf s)}}{\hbar c}
\tag{BF13}
$$

and on the fixed dimensionless theory label \(\mathfrak t\). For the probability/Fisher construction in this note, \(\theta=0\): at real nonzero Yang--Mills \(\theta\), the Euclidean weight is generally complex and does not define the probability law used in (BF2)--(BF9). Such an extension needs a separate positive-state or noncommutative response construction. A unique root \(x_{*,\mathfrak t}^{(\mathsf s)}>0\) gives

$$
\boxed{
\ell_{\mathrm{YM}}
=
x_{*,\mathfrak t}^{(\mathsf s)}
\frac{\hbar c}{\Lambda_{\mathrm{YM}}^{(\mathsf s)}},
\qquad
E_{\mathrm{YM}}^{\mathrm{ref}}
=
\frac{\Lambda_{\mathrm{YM}}^{(\mathsf s)}}
{x_{*,\mathfrak t}^{(\mathsf s)}}.}
\tag{BF14}
$$

A change of renormalization convention rescales \(\Lambda_{\mathrm{YM}}^{(\mathsf s)}\). If \(\Lambda_{\mathrm{YM}}^{(\mathsf s')}=C_{\mathsf s'\mathsf s}\Lambda_{\mathrm{YM}}^{(\mathsf s)}\), naturality requires \(x_*^{(\mathsf s')}=C_{\mathsf s'\mathsf s}x_*^{(\mathsf s)}\), so that the selected physical \(\ell_{\mathrm{YM}}\) is unchanged. Without that scheme-covariance theorem, (BF14) is only a convention-labelled reference scale.

Dimensional transmutation supplies the native Yang--Mills scale family. The balanced Fisher equation selects a distinguished member relative to that family. It cannot determine an absolute MeV value without one external dimensional calibration or a specified renormalized coupling at a physical scale.

## Exact firewall: one balanced tangent is not a gap

The selector deliberately uses one declared tangent. It therefore cannot imply coercivity on its orthogonal complement. [[distinction-grain-spectrum/inq|The distinction-grain spectrum]] identifies the stronger statement: the grains of **all** normalized nonvacuum directions must have a finite uniform ceiling.

There is a counterexample inside the declared Markov-channel class. Let \(X_n\) be independent unbiased signs. Independently choose signs \(W_n\) with \(\mathbb E[W_n]=q_n\), and observe

$$
Z_n=X_nW_n,
\qquad
q_1^2=\frac12,
\qquad
q_n^2=1-\frac1n\quad(n\geq2).
\tag{BF15}
$$

Take the nonnegative square roots \(q_n\geq0\).

On \(\mathcal H=L^2(\{-1,1\}^{\mathbb N},\mu)\), using the common uniform product law for \(X\) and \(Z\), let \(\chi_F(X)=\prod_{i\in F}X_i\) be a nonconstant Walsh character and let \(Kf=\mathbb E[f(X)\mid Z]\). Then

$$
K\chi_F^X
=
\left(\prod_{i\in F}q_i\right)\chi_F^Z.
\tag{BF16}
$$

For \(s=\chi_{\{1\}}\) and \(B=I-K^*K\), both the recoverable and residual ledgers equal one half:

$$
\|Ks\|^2
=
\langle s,Bs\rangle
=
\frac12\|s\|^2.
\tag{BF17}
$$

Nevertheless, if \(Q\) projects onto all nonconstant Walsh directions,

$$
\inf_{\substack{f\in Q\mathcal H\\\|f\|=1}}
\langle f,Bf\rangle=0,
\qquad
\|KQ\|=1.
\tag{BF17a}
$$

Thus

$$
\boxed{
\zeta_r(N_*)=\frac12
\quad\not\Longrightarrow\quad
Q_{r,N_*}B_{r,N_*}Q_{r,N_*}\geq\kappa Q_{r,N_*}
\text{ for any }\kappa>0.}
\tag{BF18}
$$

For the fixed theory label \(\mathfrak t\), the Clay-relevant theorem remains the independent complete-carrier estimate, uniform only over the regulator family \(r\):

$$
\boxed{
\sup_r
\|K_{r,N_{*,r}}Q_{r,N_{*,r}}\|
\leq
\rho_*<1.}
\tag{BF19}
$$

If \(Q_{r,N_{*,r}}\) is identified with \(I-P_{0,r}\) on the complete regulated transfer carrier, reduces the positive transfer operator, and survives as the complete OS vacuum complement, then (BF19), fixed-thickness convergence, and the QFT recovery contract let the collared-surface theorem give

$$
\Delta_E
\geq
\frac{\hbar c}{\ell_{\mathrm{YM}}}
\log(\rho_*^{-1}).
\tag{BF20}
$$

The selector supplies \(\ell_{\mathrm{YM}}\); the uniform angle supplies \(\rho_*\). They may be computed from the same family \(K_{r,N}\), but the Walsh example proves the required logical non-implication: the one-vector balance does not imply the operator-norm bound, and (BF10) does not assume (BF19). This separation is what keeps (BF20) from being a disguised definition of the gap.

## Relation to other scale conventions

A [[library/properties-and-uses-of-the-wilson-flow-in-lattice-qcd/inq|Wilson-flow reference scale]] such as \(t_0\) is noncircular but uses a prospectively chosen numerical threshold. The balanced-Fisher proposal replaces such a threshold by equality of complementary same-carrier ledgers. It is not automatically more physical; it becomes so only if the RG score, boundary split, and unique root are derived naturally.

Core trace capacity and finite-index entropy can normalize logarithmic depth or supply a discrete whole ledger. They cannot replace (BF19): index controls a supremal forgetting capacity, while the mass gap is an infimal response cost. The faithful fixed-point inclusion of a continuous \(SU(N)\) action is also infinite-index under the standard minimal-action hypotheses. [[global-local-response-reconstruction/realization-ledger|The realization ledger]] records these no-gos.

The cosmological common-count grain is a different application of the same methodological pattern. Its carrier and ledgers concern horizon and background-radiation structure. Equation (BF11) concerns a Yang--Mills scale tangent of the blocked nonlinear Wilson law. A common numerical answer would be surprising data demanding a cross-carrier theorem, not a premise of this construction.

## Stopping condition

This selector becomes an internally constructed Yang--Mills yardstick only after one proves:

1. a common-carrier continuous DQM family and a declared gauge-covariant RG connection;
2. an explicit frozen-channel interpretation of (BF7)--(BF11), kept distinct from the actual moving boundary score (BF5c);
3. the full nonlinear two-boundary law at every transported scale;
4. a derived block-to-slab synchronization;
5. covariance of the root under inequivalent admissible exact blockings and scale-dependent field redefinitions;
6. existence, uniqueness, transversality, and a nonzero finite continuum limit for \(\ell_{*,r}\); and
7. a transfer-compatible realization identifying \(Q_{r,N_{*,r}}\) with the complete regulated gauge-invariant vacuum complement, preserving that identification in the OS continuum limit; and
8. the separate complete-carrier response bound (BF19).

The first six clauses construct the grain. The seventh prevents a proper selected subspace from masquerading as the physical carrier; the eighth turns the grain-bearing response into a mass-gap lower bound. If an admissible blocking, interpolation, or moving carrier map can move or erase the root, the word *canonical* must be abandoned; the scheme-labelled, object-relative diagnostic may still remain useful.
