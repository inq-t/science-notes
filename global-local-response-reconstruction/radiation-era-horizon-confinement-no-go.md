# Radiation-Era Horizon Confinement Is Parametrically Too Weak

In a standard radiation-dominated Einstein--FLRW return, the Hubble energy \(\hbar H\) is smaller than the thermal energy \(k_BT\) by the Planck-suppressed ratio \(k_BT/E_{\mathrm{Pl}}\). Near a QCD-scale thermal transition, the Hubble radius is therefore enormously larger than the thermal or strong-interaction correlation length. This rules out the literal causal patch as an ordinary resonant box whose lowest mode directly produces the Yang--Mills gap; it leaves open a more radical whole-law role in state selection, dimensionless matching, or fossil transport.

## The scale-separation identity

Define the effective radiation energy-density count \(g_{*\rho}(T)\) by

$$
\epsilon(T)
:=
\frac{\pi^2}{30}
g_{*\rho}(T)
\frac{(k_BT)^4}{\hbar^3c^3}.
\tag{HC1}
$$

For a spatially flat Einstein--FLRW solution dominated by that energy density,

$$
H^2
=
\frac{8\pi G}{3c^2}\epsilon.
\tag{HC2}
$$

Introduce the Planck energy only as a unit conversion,

$$
E_{\mathrm{Pl}}
:=
\sqrt{\frac{\hbar c^5}{G}}.
\tag{HC3}
$$

Equations (HC1)--(HC3) give the dimensionless identity

$$
\boxed{
\chi_H(T)
:=
\frac{\hbar H}{k_BT}
=
\sqrt{\frac{4\pi^3}{45}g_{*\rho}(T)}
\frac{k_BT}{E_{\mathrm{Pl}}}.}
\tag{HC4}
$$

Equivalently, with the Hubble length \(R_H=c/H\) and thermal length \(\lambda_T=\hbar c/(k_BT)\),

$$
\boxed{
\frac{R_H}{\lambda_T}
=
\chi_H(T)^{-1}.}
\tag{HC5}
$$

Whenever \(k_BT\ll E_{\mathrm{Pl}}/\sqrt{4\pi^3g_{*\rho}/45}\), this hierarchy is large. At a QCD-scale temperature with the relevant moderate effective count, \(k_BT/E_{\mathrm{Pl}}\) is of order \(10^{-20}\); the square-root factor in (HC4) changes that only by an order of magnitude. Thus \(\hbar H\) is roughly nineteen orders of magnitude below \(k_BT\), subject to the chosen thermal state and effective count. The precise decimal is not the point. The parametric dependence is.
