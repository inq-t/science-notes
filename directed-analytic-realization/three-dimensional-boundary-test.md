# Three-Dimensional Boundary Response: Local Action and the Cap Test

Opposed harmonic half-spaces return a local scalar wave action and commuting spacetime observables through their common boundary response. A finite Dirichlet cap changes that response and opens a clock gap, but the induced clock then propagates instantaneously and has no uniform mass-like invariant. This exact comparison tests the proposed boundary origin of scale: confinement must preserve the right local dynamics, not merely produce a lowest eigenvalue.

## The uncapped whole-to-boundary law

Begin with a supplied flat three-dimensional boundary and a normal preparation coordinate \(\tau>0\). For real boundary value \(q\), solve

\[
(-\partial_\tau^2-\Delta_x)u=0,\qquad
u(0,x)=q(x),
\tag{BT1}
\]

using the decaying Fourier solution

\[
\widehat u(\tau,k)=e^{-\tau|k|}\widehat q(k).
\tag{BT2}
\]

Use the unitary Fourier transform. The outward normal response and bulk quadratic cost are

\[
Aq=-\partial_\tau u|_0,\qquad A=\sqrt{-\Delta},
\qquad
\int_0^\infty\!\!\int_{\mathbb R^3}
\left(|\partial_\tau u|^2+|\nabla_xu|^2\right)\,dx\,d\tau
=(q,Aq).
\tag{BT3}
\]

The inverse response maps prescribed Neumann data \(p\) to boundary value \(A^{-1}p\), with cost \((p,A^{-1}p)\). These are maps between value and normal-response types, not two names for the same measurement.

The same response also has a compression origin. [[algebra/three-dimensional-weighted-compression-response|The three-dimensional weighted trace theorem]] proves

\[
\|H_fA^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2
=\frac{r}{8\pi^2}(f,Af)
\tag{BT3a}
\]

for scalar Schwartz \(f\), with \(H_f\) the leak across the flat Clifford polarization on \(r\) complex spinor components. The operator first converts normal sources into values, then compresses multiplication. Its ordinary unweighted trace diverges; the source type is essential. This supplies the even response, but its scalar odd trace vanishes. It does not by itself supply the Green pairing below.

Two opposed copies, referred to one common normal, give the graphs
\(p=\pm Aq\). [[algebra/cauchy-response-and-local-action|The opposed-Cauchy theorem]]
derives their pair norm, Green form, complex structure and clock:

\[
\mu_A(q,p)=\frac12[(q,Aq)+(p,A^{-1}p)],
\quad
\sigma((q,p),(q',p'))=(q,p')-(p,q'),
\quad
B_A(q,p)=(p,-A^2q).
\tag{BT4}
\]

The normal coordinate \(\tau\) is not declared physical time. Clock evolution is a different realization of the paired response. In particular, this construction does not turn the same decaying semigroup into a unitary by renaming its parameter.

## The returned local action is genuinely four-dimensional

Since \(A^2=-\Delta\), the action returned by the same state-space construction is

\[
S[q]=\frac12\int dt\,d^3x
\left[(\partial_tq)^2-|\nabla q|^2\right].
\tag{BT5}
\]

Its equation \(\partial_t^2q-\Delta q=0\) has finite propagation speed one. One direct proof integrates the nonnegative density
\(\frac12[(\partial_tq)^2+|\nabla q|^2]\)
over the exterior of an expanding ball: the outgoing flux is bounded by that density, so initially vanishing exterior data remain zero.

With the stated bosonic realization, Weyl algebras generated by pairs supported in spatial regions commute at equal time. Finite propagation extends this to spacelike-separated evolutions. The positive Fock clock is \(d\Gamma(|k|)\), and its vacuum is the unique zero-particle sector: \(|k|\) has no zero eigenvector in \(L^2(\mathbb R^3)\), though its spectral infimum is zero.

The construction therefore now has a spatially local action, a Hilbert representation, a vacuum, a positive clock and commuting spacetime observables in three spatial dimensions. It remains a supplied-flat-boundary, free scalar example. No interacting Yang–Mills law or necessary dimension has been produced.

On one-particle vectors the commuting translations have

\[
K=|k|,\qquad
P_j=k_j,\qquad K^2-\sum_jP_j^2=0.
\tag{BT6}
\]

Smooth wave packets concentrated near \(k=0\) have arbitrarily small normalized clock energy. The massless result follows from the chosen homogeneous response; it is not repaired by calling this response a causal grain.

## A finite cap really does open a clock edge

Replace the half-space by a slab \(0<\tau<L\) and impose a remote Dirichlet condition \(u(L,x)=0\). This is a new boundary law, not a conclusion forced by the uncapped geometry. Its solution is

\[
\widehat u(\tau,k)
=\frac{\sinh((L-\tau)|k|)}{\sinh(L|k|)}
\widehat q(k).
\tag{BT7}
\]

At zero momentum use the continuous limit \(1-\tau/L\). The Dirichlet-to-Neumann multiplier becomes

\[
A_L(k)=|k|\coth(L|k|),\qquad A_L(0)=L^{-1}.
\tag{BT8}
\]

Because \(z\coth z\geq1\), its clock has
\(\inf\sigma(A_L)=L^{-1}\). The infimum need not be an \(L^2\) eigenvalue.
The same paired-response and Fock prescriptions now have a positive gap above the unique vacuum on infinite \(\mathbb R^3\).

This is a genuine boundary-induced clock gap, not a finite spatial-box cutoff. It is also not yet an acceptable relativistic mass gap.

## The invariant test fails before any numerical calibration

For the supplied spatial translations,

\[
A_L(k)^2-|k|^2
=|k|^2\operatorname{csch}^2(L|k|).
\tag{BT9}
\]

This mass-Casimir candidate is positive but tends to zero at large \(|k|\). Hence no positive lower bound survives for it, despite the Hamiltonian edge \(L^{-1}\). Boost invariance has not been constructed, and this dispersion supplies no Lorentz-covariant mass-shell interpretation.

The lesson is not that a mass is a clock frequency in different units. A physical mass bound is a statement about a joint translation invariant; a lower edge of one selected clock can be insufficient. This is the same operator distinction emphasized by the workspace's [[contemporary-puzzles/yang-mills-mass-gap/joint-causal-generators-and-the-mass-casimir|Casimir comparison]].

## The local-commutation test also fails

Write

\[
A_L^2=-\Delta+b_L(-i\nabla),\qquad
b_L(k)=|k|^2\operatorname{csch}^2(L|k|),
\quad b_L(0)=L^{-2}.
\tag{BT10}
\]

The radial function \(b_L\) is smooth at zero and decays with all derivatives at infinity, so its convolution kernel

\[
g_L(x)=(2\pi)^{-3}\int e^{ik\cdot x}b_L(k)\,dk
\tag{BT11}
\]

is continuous. Since \(b_L\) is strictly positive,
\(g_L(0)>0\), and \(g_L\) remains positive in some neighborhood of zero.
Choose disjoint nonnegative smooth functions \(q,q'\) with all support differences in this neighborhood. Their local Laplacian pairing vanishes but

\[
(A_L^2q,q')>0.
\tag{BT12}
\]

For \(u=(q,0)\), \(v=(q',0)\), the paired clock obeys

\[
\left.\frac{d}{dt}
\sigma(e^{tB_{A_L}}u,v)\right|_{t=0}
=(A_L^2q,q')>0.
\tag{BT13}
\]

Consequently the Weyl commutator is nonzero for sufficiently small positive times, including times smaller than the distance between the supports. This proves failure of finite-speed locality for this capped clock. A bounded group velocity would not cure this exact support failure.

The cap has therefore passed the clock-edge test and failed two distinct physical-return tests. It is not evidence that all boundary mechanisms fail, or that locality and mass are incompatible.

## The remaining signature is more specific

A response

\[
A_m=\sqrt{-\Delta+m^2}
\tag{BT14}
\]

would pass these free-field locality and joint-invariant tests: \(A_m^2\) is local and \(A_m^2-|k|^2=m^2\). But it is the response of
\((-\partial_\tau^2-\Delta+m^2)u=0\), not the unchanged harmonic law (BT1). Supplying \(m\) there would insert precisely the unexplained scale.

The next construction must return a response whose squared local dynamics contains an independently selected positive obstruction, rather than merely confining the normal direction. It must also construct interactions, gauge-invariant observables and the required continuum theory. The capped counterexample makes that target sharper; it does not replace it with a request for another numerical mass fit.

[[algebra/clifford-mass-plane-and-selection|The Clifford mass-plane calculation]] gives a complementary operator signature: a local first-order square root can acquire such a constant term through an anticommuting skew residue. On its minimal real carrier, the allowed residues form a continuous plane whose normalized directions are rotated by symmetries of the original data. That geometry permits a mass term but selects neither its nonzero magnitude nor its direction. This is a scoped selection test, not an assertion that all physical masses must arise that way.
