# Gauge Source Action Transport

Compatibility of source comparisons has two different mathematical tests. A joint path law can exclude proposed actions by making their transformed laws singular. A source isomorphism can instead carry an already chosen action, likelihood and response into a new presentation. The first restricts an action family; the second preserves it. Neither alone selects an interaction or a physical clock.

For loop paths sharing one positive-speed heat reference, [[gauge-source-action-transport/joint-path-law-and-the-shared-boundary-action|the joint covariance theorem]] forces their right controls to agree within the stated deterministic finite-energy two-sided family. In a compact connected semisimple group, incompatible controls change an observable quadratic-variation trace and give mutually singular joint laws. Compatible controls have an explicit finite-entropy source lift and remain compatible when the reference edge changes. Separate marginal admissibility misses this shared-reference condition. Bounded positive interaction tilts preserve the classification but do not choose their potential.

[[gauge-source-action-transport/source-action-transport-through-ordered-cuts|Transport through ordered cuts]] retains the complete paired paths and derives the transformed actions from an invertible cut map. These actions depend on the preceding pieces. The likelihood identity transports quadratic-mean scores and the completed Fisher metric; transporting the observable derivatives then preserves the dual response and its own cylinder-closure generator. This does not make a source semigroup descend through a many-to-one endpoint readout.

The two-bigon test separates faithful transport from rebuilding a comparison: fresh independent edge actions give the same interacting state but a different response at first order. Transporting only a normalized endpoint bridge also changes the experiment, because its likelihood has already discarded retained-path scores. Thus a compatible state assignment or an unweighted response identity is insufficient to identify the interacting comparison law.
