# Nilpotency and the Wall

Nilpotency marks loss of semisimplicity in several nearby mathematical registers, but those registers are not one object. An $\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb R)$ unipotent, a degeneration's monodromy logarithm, a nonreduced $A_2$ local algebra, and the positive-energy translation in a half-sided modular inclusion require different representations and need not share a nilpotent operator. The exact payoff is sharper than the former identification: $*$-representations by bounded operators kill self-adjoint nilpotent thickness, one nonidentity operator cannot be both unitary and unipotent in the same Hilbert representation, and the real $A_2$ strata have the fiberwise completion types $\mathbb C^3$, $\mathbb C^2$, and $\mathbb C$. Those types are not transition maps; a separate stabilizer average supplies actual completely positive maps. Nilpotency can therefore seed a nonfaithful boundary quotient, but not literally be identified with the wall's unitary operator.
