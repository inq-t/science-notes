# The Rank-One Trichotomy and Its Representation Firewall

The elliptic--parabolic--hyperbolic classification of $\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb R)$ is the rotation--null-rotation--boost classification of $\mathrm{Spin}(2,1)$. A nontrivial unipotent in the defining representation has a nilpotent logarithm. That exact statement does not identify a degeneration monodromy, a half-sided modular translation, and a cosmological wall: parabolicity is representation-sensitive, and a nontrivial unitary operator cannot be unipotent.

## The exact statement

**[EXACT]** For $g\in\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb R)$, $g\ne\pm\mathbf1$: $|\operatorname{tr}g|<2$ is elliptic; $|\operatorname{tr}g|>2$ is hyperbolic; and $|\operatorname{tr}g|=2$ is parabolic, with the sign distinguishing a unipotent from the negative of one. In the trace-$2$ case, $g=\mathbf1+N$ after conjugacy and $N^2=0$ in the defining representation. Under $\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb R)\cong\mathrm{Spin}(2,1)$ the three classes correspond to rotations, boosts, and null rotations. The slogan “nilpotency is the algebraic signature of null” is valid only with this group and representation declared.

## The wall instance

**[STANDARD]** A half-sided modular inclusion supplies a positive-energy representation of the two-parameter affine group: modular dilations and translations obey

$$
\Delta^{it}\,U(a)\,\Delta^{-it}=U(e^{-2\pi t}a).
$$

The affine translation subgroup is parabolic when embedded in the rank-one Möbius group. Its Hilbert-space representative $U(a)$ is unitary and is not thereby algebraically unipotent in that representation; its self-adjoint generator $P$ is not thereby nilpotent. A parabolic group element may have a nontrivial unitary image under a representation-changing functor, so the obstruction forbids literal operator identification, not unitary representation of the abstract group. What the theorem supplies is positive spectrum and a one-sided invariant subalgebra, not nilpotent holonomy. The physical gate remains whether the programme's wall family realizes this affine pair.

## The reading

**[PROPOSED COMPARISON, not an identification]** In the $(3,4,\infty)$ family of [[s6-deep-read|the manuscript]], the cusp monodromy is $T_0=\mathbf1+N$ with $N^2=0$ in its rank-four lattice representation. In the HSMI theorem, the affine group contains a parabolic translation subgroup. These facts share a conjugacy-class motif but live on different carriers. [[algebra/a2-positive-completion|The positive-completion theorem]] gives the valid bridge: nilpotent thickness may be retained in an algebraic degeneration while a positive realization forgets it. It does not require cross-fiber transport itself to be nilpotent.

## Boundary

The group classification and HSMI affine relation are exact or standard in their own registers. Their comparison does not supply a state, ledger, factual arrow, or physical wall. A successful bridge must name a representation-changing functor and survive the no-unitary-unipotent lemma; [[construction-bridges|the corrected bridge ledger]] owns that target.
