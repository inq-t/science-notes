# Prepared Vacuum Fisher Comparison

A supplied physical vacuum can be lifted into a retained heat-path experiment whose Fisher response is quantitatively comparable with its physical ground-state dynamics. The response still depends on how that experiment is prepared. Rebuilding independent sources after redundant subdivision dilutes its correction; transporting the original source preserves the correction and its reference preparation. Neither procedure derives the vacuum or supplies a new physical gap.

The [[prepared-vacuum-fisher-comparison/physical-vacuum-lift-and-fisher-comparison|physical-vacuum lift]] starts from the actual finite-box \(SU(2)\) Hamiltonian and its ground state. Gauge invariance removes the heat-prior contribution to the averaged endpoint Hessian, leaving a positive vacuum Fisher correction. After an explicit clock calibration, the returned form lies between controlled multiples of the physical form, with a strict defect for a nonconstant vacuum. Independent vertex gauge symmetry and local kinetic estimates make the comparison uniform over open three-dimensional boxes at fixed coupling ratio. The adjoint-charged score estimate does not establish a gap for neutral physical excitations.

[[prepared-vacuum-fisher-comparison/prepared-vacuum-subdivision-and-the-recovered-clock|Independent preparation after subdivision]] keeps the complete physical theory fixed while replacing each edge by a chain with fresh factor controls. The source Fisher correction becomes smaller under uniform subdivision. The calibrated forms converge in relative form norm, and their operators converge in norm resolvent, to the already supplied physical ground-state clock. For a nonconstant vacuum, exact subdivision naturality would contradict the original strict correction. This is a redundant-presentation limit on one carrier, not a four-dimensional Yang--Mills continuum construction.

[[prepared-vacuum-fisher-comparison/coherent-source-subdivision|Coherent source subdivision]] instead imports the full path cut and action-transport theorem. Added Haar frames produce the same fine physical vacuum, and transporting the completed comparison preserves the original response exactly. Four-edge and eight-edge preparations can consequently retain different responses on the same sixteen-edge state and physical Hamiltonian. Faithful transport and fresh preparation answer different questions; neither makes the source response intrinsic to the static vacuum alone.
