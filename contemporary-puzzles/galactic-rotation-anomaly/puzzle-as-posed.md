# The Puzzle as Contemporary Physics Poses It

Four distinct problems travel under the name "the dark matter problem in galaxies," and their logical forms differ: a missing quantity, an unexplained regularity, a numerical coincidence, and a scope boundary. The statements below are the standard ones, sourced to the literature and not to this workspace; where a number is conventional or contested, that is said.

## P1 — The missing-quantity problem

Rotation velocities of disk galaxies do not fall off Keplerian outside the visible disk; they stay approximately flat to the last measured point (optical curves: Rubin and Ford, Astrophys. J. **159**, 379 (1970); extended HI curves: Bosma 1981; Rubin, Ford, and Thonnard 1980). Newtonian dynamics sourced by the observed baryons underpredicts the outer accelerations by factors of a few to tens. The standard resolution adds a nonbaryonic halo; the cosmological inventory independently requires $\Omega_ch^2\simeq0.120$ (Planck 2018, Astron. Astrophys. **641**, A6 (2020)).

## P2 — The regularity problem

The anomaly is not scattered; it is a function. Across 153 galaxies and 2693 points spanning the SPARC sample, the observed centripetal acceleration tracks the baryonic one through a single curve,

$$
g_{\rm obs}
=\frac{g_{\rm bar}}{1-e^{-\sqrt{g_{\rm bar}/g_\dagger}}},
\qquad
g_\dagger=(1.20\pm0.02\pm0.24)\times10^{-10}\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}},
$$

with remarkably small scatter — the radial acceleration relation (McGaugh, Lelli, and Schombert, Phys. Rev. Lett. **117**, 201101 (2016); the second error is systematic). The same scale governs the baryonic Tully–Fisher relation (Lelli, McGaugh, and Schombert 2016). Milgrom postulated exactly such a one-scale modification in 1983 (Astrophys. J. **270**, 365; 371; 384): dynamics deviates from Newton below a universal acceleration $a_0$, not below a length or mass. In halo language the regularity must emerge from feedback-shaped halos; reproducing its tightness and its one-scale character is an acknowledged challenge (the "diversity" and RAR-tightness discussions; Famaey and McGaugh, Living Rev. Relativity **15**, 10 (2012)).

## P3 — The coincidence problem

The measured scale sits at the cosmological acceleration:

$$
\frac{cH_0}{2\pi}=1.04\times10^{-10}\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}
\quad\text{vs}\quad
g_\dagger=1.20\times10^{-10}\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}},
$$

within fifteen percent (and $c^2\sqrt{\Lambda/3}/2\pi=0.86\times10^{-10}$, within forty percent). Milgrom noted $a_0\sim cH_0$ from the beginning, and in 1999 proposed reading it through the Unruh temperature of accelerated observers against the cosmological vacuum (Phys. Lett. A **253**, 273 (1999)). The $2\pi$ is a convention choice among order-one factors; it predates the precision RAR measurement. Why a galactic dynamical scale should know the Hubble rate is unexplained in both the halo and the modified-dynamics languages.

## P4 — The scope problem

The one-scale regularity fails outside disks. Rich clusters retain a factor-of-two missing mass even under MOND (Sanders 1999; Famaey–McGaugh 2012 review the residual), the Bullet Cluster shows lensing mass displaced from the baryonic plasma (Clowe et al., Astrophys. J. Lett. **648**, L109 (2006)), and the CMB acoustic structure requires a nonbaryonic component in the relativistic cosmology (Planck 2018). Conversely, particle dark matter has not been directly detected, and wide-binary tests at the $10^{-10}$ m/s² scale are currently contested in both directions (Chae 2023 reporting a boost; Banik et al. 2024 reporting Newtonian behaviour). Any account must therefore explain why the one-scale phenomenology is exact in disks and absent or diluted at cluster and cosmological scales — or accept two components.

## The shape of the demand

P1 asks for a quantity, P2 for a law, P3 for a reason, P4 for a boundary. The halo answer is strong on P1 and P4 and must work for P2 and P3; the modified-dynamics answer is strong on P2 and P3 and fails P4 as stated. The habit of demanding one answer to all four is itself a choice, and the modules beside this note question it.
