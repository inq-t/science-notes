# Augmentation Descent and the Neutral-Sector Firewall

The canonical way to package all nontrivial triangle-group characters is the augmentation representation of the finite deck group: remove the undifferentiated sheet average from its regular representation and retain every relative sheet distinction. When the cusp image generates the deck group, this representation has no cusp-invariant vector, so the associated smooth twisted Laplacian has compact resolvent and a positive bottom without choosing one character. The same construction also exposes the decisive Yang--Mills firewall: an equivariant operator reduces into invariant and augmentation blocks, so coercivity of the forgotten global distinctions supplies no lower bound on the neutral invariant block. A physical mass gap follows only from an independently constructed analysis map that detects every vacuum-complement direction and compares this descent form with the physical energy form.

## The canonical finite distinction carrier

Let \(A\) be a finite group and let \(\lambda\) be its left regular representation on

\[
\ell^2(A).
\tag{AD1}
\]

The group average

\[
P_A
:=
\frac1{|A|}\sum_{a\in A}\lambda(a)
\tag{AD2}
\]

is the orthogonal projection onto the constant line. Its orthogonal complement

\[
I_A
:=
(1-P_A)\ell^2(A)
=
\left\{\xi\in\ell^2(A):\sum_{a\in A}\xi(a)=0\right\}
\tag{AD3}
\]

is the complex augmentation representation. No character or preferred orientation has been selected: \(I_A\) contains every distinction among sheets after their common average has been removed.

Let \(\Gamma\) be a cofinite Fuchsian group with one cusp generated by \(c\), and let

\[
q:\Gamma\twoheadrightarrow A,
\qquad
d:=q(c).
\tag{AD4}
\]

The representation \(\rho_A:=\lambda\circ q|_{I_A}\) defines a flat unitary bundle \(E_{I_A}\) over \(\Gamma\backslash\mathfrak H\). The associated Laplacian has the same local differential expression as the scalar hyperbolic Laplacian; only its global equivariance domain has changed.

## Exact fixed-space theorem

The cyclic subgroup \(\langle d\rangle\) acts on \(A\) by left translation. Its orbits are the left cosets of \(\langle d\rangle\), so

\[
\dim \ell^2(A)^{\langle d\rangle}
=
[A:\langle d\rangle].
\tag{AD5}
\]

The constant line is one of these invariant directions. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\dim I_A^{\langle d\rangle}
=
[A:\langle d\rangle]-1.}
\tag{AD6}
\]

Consequently

\[
I_A^{\langle d\rangle}=0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\langle d\rangle=A.
\tag{AD7}
\]

Thus the regular augmentation carrier removes every cusp zero channel exactly when the cusp image acts transitively on the sheets. This criterion is stronger than saying merely that the deck group or its action is discrete.

For

\[
\Gamma=\Delta^+(3,4,\infty),
\qquad
A=\Gamma_{\mathrm{ab}}\cong C_{12},
\tag{AD8}
\]

the cusp class generates \(C_{12}\). Hence the cusp-invariant subspace of \(I_{C_{12}}\) is zero. Applying the cusp estimate in [[triangle-character-cusp-coercivity]] gives the Friedrichs Laplacian

\[
\Delta_{\mathrm{aug}}
\quad\text{on}\quad
L^2(\Gamma\backslash\mathfrak H,E_{I_{C_{12}}})
\tag{AD9}
\]

compact resolvent and zero kernel. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\Delta_{\mathrm{aug}}\geq\delta_{\mathrm{aug}}I,
\qquad
\delta_{\mathrm{aug}}>0.}
\tag{AD10}
\]

Fourier transform on \(C_{12}\) identifies this operator with the direct sum of the eleven nontrivial line-character Laplacians, so \(\delta_{\mathrm{aug}}=\min_{\chi\ne1}\lambda_0(\Delta_\chi)\). The augmentation presentation is canonical; it does not improve the presently qualitative value of that minimum.

## What is discrete and what is continuous

The local operator, metric, sections, and derivatives in (AD9) remain continuous. The discrete object is the finite comparison law among global lifts. The sheet average \(P_A\) is the undifferentiated whole; \(I_A\) is the carrier of relative distinctions. In this exact sense, distinction presupposes an ambiguity of possible lifts and is represented by departure from their common average.

Orientation reversal sends the cusp generator to \(d^{-1}\). The inversion permutation of \(A\) unitarily conjugates \(\lambda(d)\) to \(\lambda(d^{-1})\), so \(\Delta_{\mathrm{aug}}\) is orientation-even. This carrier packages positive distinction but cannot itself select chirality.

The complex-\(S^6\) defect still supplies the line-character evaluation

\[
\chi_\ell(c)=e^{2\pi ip/12}.
\tag{AD11}
\]

It chooses one Fourier component of the augmentation carrier. The whole augmentation representation, however, comes from the canonical abelian cover and does not depend on choosing \(p=-1\). This separates the unit-cokernel topology, one selected phase, and the orientation-even positive carrier.

## Exact neutral-sector no-go

Let a finite group \(A\) act unitarily on a Hilbert space \(\widetilde{\mathcal H}\), let \(P_A\) be its averaging projection, and suppose a nonnegative self-adjoint operator \(L\) commutes with that action. Then \(P_A\) strongly commutes with \(L\), and

\[
\widetilde{\mathcal H}
=
\widetilde{\mathcal H}^{A}\oplus\widetilde{\mathcal H}_{\mathrm{aug}},
\qquad
L=L_{\mathrm{inv}}\oplus L_{\mathrm{aug}}.
\tag{AD12}
\]

A lower bound \(L_{\mathrm{aug}}\geq\delta I\) says nothing about \(L_{\mathrm{inv}}\). Indeed, for any nonnegative \(B\) on the invariant block and any \(C\geq\delta I\) on the augmentation block,

\[
L_{B,C}:=B\oplus C
\tag{AD13}
\]

is still equivariant and has the same augmentation floor. The invariant block may have zero modes or an arbitrarily small positive edge.

The quotient formulation is even sharper. Fiber averaging \(E_A=P_A\) forgets the augmentation directions. For every invariant \(f\),

\[
\inf_{\substack{F\in\widetilde{\mathcal H}\\P_AF=f}}
\langle(1-P_A)F,L_{\mathrm{aug}}(1-P_A)F\rangle
=0,
\tag{AD14}
\]

because the invariant lift \(F=f\) has no augmentation component. Positive stiffness in the forgotten vertical carrier does not descend as positive stiffness on the retained quotient.

This is the same structural no-go encountered for conditional relative-entropy loss and positive-operator shorting: forgetting can reveal or remove a costly transverse direction, but it cannot by itself manufacture a tangential response on the data that remain.

## The vacuum-sector dichotomy

There are two inequivalent ways to read (AD10).

1. If the vacuum lies in the trivial deck sector and \(I_A\) labels other sectors, then \(\delta_{\mathrm{aug}}\) is a charged-sector threshold. A theorem must still show that the physical Yang--Mills vacuum complement is covered by those sectors. In ordinary AQFT, gauge-invariant glueballs belong to the vacuum representation; assigning them an unobservable deck superselection charge would obstruct rather than explain their creation by local observables.

2. If one twisted augmentation carrier is itself declared to be the complete physical Hilbert space, its lowest eigenvector is the ground state. After vacuum-energy subtraction, the relevant gap is the separation between the ground eigenspace and the next eigenvalue, not the positivity of \(\lambda_0(\Delta_{\mathrm{aug}})\). Compact resolvent guarantees such a separation on one fixed geometry after the ground projection is removed, but the cusp estimate does not give a useful uniform bound on that spacing.

Thus a positive bottom and a mass gap above a physical vacuum are different typed statements.

## The only viable transfer theorem

Let \(\mathcal H_{\mathrm{phys}}\) be a reconstructed physical carrier with vacuum projection \(P_0\) and closed energy form \(\mathfrak h_{\mathrm{YM}}\). A descent explanation must construct, without using the spectrum of \(H_{\mathrm{YM}}\), a closed analysis map

\[
J:\operatorname{Dom}\mathfrak h_{\mathrm{YM}}\cap(1-P_0)\mathcal H_{\mathrm{phys}}
\longrightarrow
\operatorname{Dom}q_{\mathrm{aug}}
\tag{AD15}
\]

and constants \(b,\kappa>0\) such that

\[
\|J\Psi\|^2\geq b\|\Psi\|^2,
\qquad
\mathfrak h_{\mathrm{YM}}[\Psi]\geq\kappa\,q_{\mathrm{aug}}[J\Psi].
\tag{AD16}
\]

Then (AD10) gives the exact implication

\[
\boxed{
\mathfrak h_{\mathrm{YM}}[\Psi]
\geq
\kappa\delta_{\mathrm{aug}}b\,\|\Psi\|^2.}
\tag{AD17}
\]

The map \(J\) cannot be the raw deck difference on an invariant physical carrier, because that vanishes identically. It must instead be a covariant derivative of local presentation data, a curvature/holonomy response, or an OS-interface analysis map whose kernel is exactly the vacuum. This identifies the mathematical role of a cocycle: not a hidden Hamiltonian and not a sector label, but a first-order comparison map from retained physical variation to globally constrained descent variation.

For Yang--Mills, \(b\), \(\kappa\), and the normalization of \(q_{\mathrm{aug}}\) must be controlled uniformly in volume and regulator removal. Only after OS/Poincare reconstruction and an independent length or energy calibration may the product in (AD17) be called a mass gap.

## Consequence for the current programme

The triangle theorem supplies a canonical positive **target carrier**, but not the physical analysis map. The exceptional slice-groupoid branch supplies local stabilizer torsors, comparison links, a faithful holonomy response, and an exact Wilson action on the ordinary finite-lattice carrier. A comparison between these constructions would have to send augmentation distinctions of global lifts to gauge-invariant connection or interface derivatives, rather than identify \(C_{12}\) with \(SU(3)\) or assign glueballs a new global charge.

That comparison is now the smallest live Copernican theorem target:

\[
\boxed{
\text{global presentation torsor}
\xrightarrow{\text{comparison cocycle}}
\text{physical gauge-invariant derivative}
\xrightarrow{\text{OS/Poincare solder}}
\text{mass Casimir}.}
\tag{AD18}
\]

It must fail if its analysis map has a nonvacuum kernel, if it acts only on charged superselection sectors, if its constants collapse in the continuum limit, or if its dimensional scale is fitted from the desired glueball mass.
