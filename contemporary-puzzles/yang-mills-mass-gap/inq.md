# Yang–Mills Existence and the Mass Gap

The Yang--Mills mass-gap problem asks for a nontrivial continuum quantum theory whose complete physical vacuum complement begins at positive invariant mass. The popular question “how do massless gluons acquire mass?” contains a category error because it demands that a gauge-invariant spectral property of the reconstructed observable carrier appear as a mass term or pole of a gauge-dependent field. The carrier-first reversal now has two exact abstract arrows: a local observable can be pulled into the whole response to obtain a safe Dirichlet restriction, while a local presentation can be assigned the least response among all forgotten whole representatives. Pointed CP composition adds a third exact distinction: the minimal composite correspondence is only the cyclic part of the fused history correspondence, so endpoint descent is a coisometry with a genuine fusion residue. A middle-slice insertion now gives a canonical history-sensitive map into that residue whose Gramian is bounded above by the physical transfer defect; reverse-prediction innovations realize the full defect exactly. The regulator-uniform bridge lower frame, Type-III lift, continuum reconstruction, and Poincare realization required by the Clay problem remain open.

The most concrete geometric bridge is the stationary past--future construction: the Friedrichs cosine of disjoint history cuts is the transfer contraction across their slab, its supported positive return recovers the transfer Hamiltonian by a logarithm per calibrated thickness, and a gap is equivalent to a finite uniform Euclidean-dwell ceiling. The reciprocal angle defect is exactly the optimal two-block variance-factorization constant, and a complete endpoint-innovation matrix now supplies a genuine local-to-global bound for that cosine. With vacuum energy normalized to zero, let \(P_0:=E_H(\{0\})\) be the zero-energy spectral projection; it is rank one only after vacuum uniqueness. If \(R(\ell)=-\log\|e^{-\ell H/(\hbar c)}(I-P_0)\|\), then \(m_{\mathrm{gap}}=(\hbar/c)\,\mathrm dR/\mathrm d\ell=(\hbar/c^2)(\mathrm d/\mathrm d\tau)R(c\tau)\) only after Poincare reconstruction. Here \(R\) is dimensionless attenuation depth, not yet a number of facts; a factification reading additionally needs a record-producing process and a noncircular same-carrier solder.

The cleanest exact pre-QFT model of the proposed Copernican mechanism is now the triangle-character theorem. On the hyperbolic orbifold of \(\Delta^+(3,4,\infty)\), the local Casimir remains an ordinary smooth differential operator, but discrete global equivariance restricts its domain. The complex-\(S^6\) winding defect has the phase shadow \(\chi(c)=e^{2\pi ip/12}\); every nontrivial such character removes the invariant cusp Fourier channel, so the twisted Laplacian has compact resolvent, no zero mode, and a positive lowest eigenvalue. This proves that a globally discrete gluing law can open a coercive sector without discretizing the local continuum. It also proves a useful direct-lift obstruction: every $SU(3)$ cusp holonomy fixes at least a rank-two Cartan subspace in the adjoint representation, so ordinary color holonomy alone cannot remove all gluonic zero channels by this mechanism. The automorphic carrier is still two-dimensional, the neutral sector and continuum limit remain untreated, and no physical scale or same-carrier comparison has been derived.

[[augmentation-descent-and-the-neutral-sector-firewall]] removes the arbitrary choice of one phase by taking the canonical augmentation representation \(I_{C_{12}}=\mathbf1^\perp\subset\ell^2(C_{12})\). It contains all eleven relative sheet modes and no cusp-invariant vector, so its twisted Laplacian has one positive floor. But this stronger packaging yields an exact neutral-sector no-go: every deck-equivariant operator splits into invariant and augmentation blocks, and fiber averaging kills the augmentation carrier, so its coercivity imposes no bound on invariant observables. If the nontrivial characters label separate sectors, their positive bottoms do not cover neutral glueballs; if one twisted sector is the whole carrier, its lowest eigenvalue is vacuum energy and the mass gap is the next spacing. The missing cocycle must therefore be a spectrum-independent analysis map from every physical vacuum-complement direction into the constrained descent carrier, with a uniform energy-form comparison—not a raw quotient, global charge, or modular Hamiltonian.

[[global-discreteness-kazhdan-rigidity-and-the-gap]] sharpens what the local-continuous/global-discrete rhyme can actually mean. For a flat-bundle Laplacian, topology identifies the parallel kernel, whereas a gap is the quantitative closed-range condition for the covariant derivative. Kazhdan property \((T)\) is exactly the representation-uniform version for a discrete group comparison Laplacian. But \(\Delta^+(3,4,\infty)\cong C_3*C_4\) has a finite-index free subgroup \(F_6\) and therefore lacks property \((T)\); an explicit irreducible two-dimensional family has cusp eigenvalues approaching one. The eleven \(C_{12}\) characters remain an exact finite coercive family, but their separation does not extend to all descent representations. Thus discreteness is the grammar, while quantitative rigidity on the actual carrier is the gap-bearing predicate.

[[triangle-presentation-descent-on-the-neutral-adjoint-carrier]] supplies the first finite exact way around the direct charge mismatch. For the irreducible two-dimensional triangle presentation, pass from \(V\) to the neutralized operator carrier \(V\otimes\bar V\simeq\operatorname{End}(V)\), then average conjugation over the \(C_3\) and \(C_4\) generators. The two trace-preserving expectations have common range \(\mathbb CI\), and their summed restriction-loss Hessian has sharp relative floor \(1-|\cos2\theta|\) on every traceless finite-algebra density tangent. The same ordered pair has the reversal-odd commutator spectrum \(\{0,\pm\sin4\theta/4\}\), so handedness and stiffness arise from one relative presentation without being identical. This is a finite algebraic prototype of the global-presentation-to-local-entropy arrow, not its physical completion: its affine mixed-state paths are not localized inner-unitary QFT paths. One three-dimensional matrix frame also cannot cover an infinite-dimensional Yang--Mills form core; the full conditional target therefore requires an \(\ell^2\)-family with uniform floor \(\kappa_{\mathrm{pres}}>0\), a natural complex-linear analysis map, and one genuine localized restriction loss whose positive Hermitian extension dominates the entire summed frame on a complex energy-form core. With those hypotheses and the isotropic whole-to-part yardstick, Longo's comparison would give \(\Delta_E\geq b_J\kappa_{\mathrm{pres}}(\gamma s_*)^{-1/3}E_{A,c}\iota_c^{-2/3}\). The discrete relations alone are insufficient because the one-fibre floor tends to zero as the presentations align.

[[twisted-holonomy-and-the-neutral-hessian]] tests the same idea inside gauge theory. A center twist charges lines rather than point-local glueball operators; on a twisted finite torus it gives a neutral opposite-flux threshold \(4\pi/(NL)\), which vanishes as \(L\to\infty\). The stronger positive precedent is center-stabilized Yang--Mills on \(\mathbb R^3\times S^1\): holonomy gaps charged \(W\)-modes but leaves Cartan photons perturbatively massless, while monopole-instantons generate a cosine potential whose Hessian is positive on the physical neutral modes after the diagonal \(U(1)\) direction is removed. Global descent organizes the carrier and makes the residue calculable; the dynamical neutral Hessian, not the twist alone, bears the gap. Deformation removal and the return to ordinary \(\mathbb R^4\) remain unproved.

[[localized-relative-entropy-and-the-energy-solder]] supplies a newly located exact bridge. Longo's finite-width theorem bounds regional vacuum relative entropy by \(2\pi R/(\hbar c)\) times physical Hamiltonian energy. Differentiating a localized local-unitary path under an explicit energy-form regularity condition gives the real inequality \(q_B\leq(2\pi R/\hbar c)\mathfrak h\); a restriction-loss Hessian lies below \(q_B\). To infer a Hamiltonian gap, that real Hessian must have a positive Hermitian extension to a complex \(H^{1/2}\)-form core with the same upper bound and an independently derived lower frame bound. Those conditions imply \(\Delta_E\geq\hbar c\,\kappa_B/(2\pi R)\). The real same-path energy solder is established; the complexification, global-to-regional domination, and uniform neutral coercivity remain the central construction.

## The argument in order

**1. The problem is an existence theorem, not a puzzle.** The Clay statement asks for a nontrivial quantum Yang--Mills theory on \(\mathbb R^4\), for any compact simple gauge group, satisfying axioms at least as strong as the standard Wightman framework, with a spectral gap \(\Delta>0\) above the vacuum. Numerical evidence is strong: lattice calculations place the pure-\(\mathrm{SU}(3)\) \(0^{++}\) glueball near \(1.65\)–\(1.73\,\mathrm{GeV}\) after a scale convention is chosen, and compute low-lying dimensionless ratios to a few percent. What is missing is a construction and proof. [[puzzle-as-posed]] separates existence, gap, nontriviality, and numerical evidence.

As of September 2026, Clay still lists the problem as unsolved. [[library/reflection-positive-construction-of-four-dimensional-su-n-yang-mills-theory-with-mass-gap-and-confinement/inq|Faizal and Shabir (2026)]] present a published claimed reflection-positive \(SU(N)\) construction with a multiscale continuum gap. It is useful because its theorem architecture names the right bridge—fixed-scale clustering, summably controlled interlacing, OS reconstruction, and ultraviolet identification—but it is not treated here as an accepted solution. A primary-source audit finds that its gap transport needs total error smaller than the starting gap rather than mere summability, its final proof uses the opposite operator inequality from the one stated, and its strong/weak trajectory comparison invokes an \(O(g_k^2)\sim O(1/k)\) bound as summable while presuming entry into a shared controlled domain.

**2. A gap is not a failure of continuum mathematics.** The statement \(E_H((0,\Delta))=0\) is expressible without strain on continuous \(\mathbb R^4\). A free massive field already supplies an elementary continuum example: the vacuum is isolated, a one-particle shell occurs at positive mass, and multiparticle spectrum lies above it. ### Fixed-direction and reciprocal-generator theorem

Let \(U\) be a strongly continuous unitary representation of the connected Poincare group with translation spectral measure \(E_{(H/c,\mathbf P)}\) supported in the closed forward cone. Define

$$
P_0:=E_{(H/c,\mathbf P)}(\{0\}),
\qquad
\mathcal C:=H^2-c^2|\mathbf P|^2,
$$

and, for one fixed unit vector \(\mathbf n\),

$$
P_\pm(\mathbf n):=H\pm c\,\mathbf P\!\cdot\!\mathbf n,
\qquad
Q_{\mathbf n}:=P_+(\mathbf n)P_-(\mathbf n).
$$

The translation generators strongly commute, so these are nonnegative self-adjoint joint-functional-calculus operators. The projection \(P_0\) is rank one only under the additional unique-vacuum hypothesis. For every \(\Delta>0\), the spectral operator inequalities, equivalently the closed-form inequalities on their natural form domains, satisfy

$$
\boxed{
Q_{\mathbf n}\geq\Delta^2(I-P_0)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
H\geq\Delta(I-P_0)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\mathcal C\geq\Delta^2(I-P_0).}
$$

Here \(Q_{\mathbf n}^{1/2}\leq H\), the implication from the Hamiltonian floor to the Casimir floor uses Lorentz invariance of the joint spectral support, and

$$
Q_{\mathbf n}
=
\mathcal C+c^2\bigl(|\mathbf P|^2-(\mathbf P\!\cdot\!\mathbf n)^2\bigr).
$$

Thus one fixed physical direction suffices after full Poincare reconstruction. A common floor on any countable dense \(D\subset S^2\) gives the stronger direct pointwise tomography

$$
\mathcal C(p)=\inf_{\mathbf n\in D}Q_{\mathbf n}(p),
$$

but that angular family is not required merely to infer the gap.

The reciprocal normal form has additional kernel and covariance hypotheses. Let \(P_\pm\) be positive strongly commuting self-adjoint operators, restrict to their joint-positive carrier

$$
\mathcal H_+^{\mathrm{joint}}
:=
E_{P_+,P_-}\bigl((0,\infty)^2\bigr)\mathcal H,
$$

and suppose a unitary group \(B_s\) obeys \(B_sP_\pm B_s^*=e^{\pm s}P_\pm\). Joint Borel functional calculus on this carrier defines

$$
M:=(P_+P_-)^{1/2},
\qquad
\Theta:=\frac12(\log P_+-\log P_-),
$$

and gives

$$
P_\pm=Me^{\pm\Theta},
\qquad
\frac{P_++P_-}{2}=M\cosh\Theta,
\qquad
B_s\Theta B_s^*=\Theta+sI,
\qquad
B_sMB_s^*=M.
$$

In \(3+1\) dimensions this \(M\) is invariant under the declared reciprocal boost but, for a fixed axis, is not the full Lorentz Casimir because it still contains transverse momentum.

The same-sign firewall likewise requires a shared action. If positive strongly commuting \(P_1,P_2\) obey

$$
\Delta_M^{it}P_j\Delta_M^{-it}=e^{-2\pi t}P_j,
\qquad j=1,2,
$$

for the same modular group, then their joint-calculus product scales with weight \(e^{-4\pi t}\). On every common reducing sector invariant under that group, the restricted product is either zero or, if nonzero, has spectrum \([0,\infty)\); hence it has no positive floor. Merely having two same-sign HSMIs does not supply the shared modular action or strong commutation needed for this conclusion.
