# Triangle-Character Cusp Coercivity

The orientation-preserving triangle group \(\Delta^+(3,4,\infty)\) gives an exact model in which a continuous local Casimir becomes coercive because of discrete global descent. Every nontrivial unitary character of the group has nontrivial holonomy around its unique cusp, which removes the zero Fourier channel, makes the twisted hyperbolic Laplacian have compact resolvent, and leaves a strictly positive lowest eigenvalue. The signed winding defect in the complex-\(S^6\) construction has the exact phase shadow \(\chi(c)=e^{2\pi i p/12}\), so \(p=-1\) selects one of these coercive sectors. This is a real descent-to-gap theorem on an automorphic carrier, but not yet the Yang--Mills mass gap: the operator is two-dimensional, the positive number is uncalibrated, and no map to the physical Yang--Mills carrier has been constructed.

## One Lie group, two geometric real forms

Let

\[
\mathfrak H=\{x+iy:y>0\},
\qquad
\mathrm ds^2=\frac{\mathrm dx^2+\mathrm dy^2}{y^2}.
\tag{TC1}
\]

Then

\[
\mathfrak H\cong PSL_2(\mathbb R)/PSO(2)
\tag{TC2}
\]

is the Riemannian hyperbolic plane, often called Euclidean \(AdS_2\) after a curvature radius is supplied. Lorentzian \(AdS_2\) instead has the local homogeneous presentation \(PSL_2(\mathbb R)/SO_0(1,1)\). The ambient group and its quadratic Casimir are shared, but the stabilizer, signature, causal structure, Hilbert carrier, and self-adjoint domain are different.

The complex-\(S^6\) construction uses the Fuchsian action on \(\mathfrak H\). Its exact spectral analogue therefore begins with a positive elliptic operator in Riemannian signature, not with a Lorentzian spacetime Hamiltonian.

## The unitary automorphic carrier

Put

\[
\Gamma
:=
\Delta^+(3,4,\infty)
=
\langle a,b\mid a^3=b^4=1\rangle,
\qquad
c=(ab)^{-1}.
\tag{TC3}
\]

The quotient \(Y=\Gamma\backslash\mathfrak H\) is a finite-area hyperbolic orbifold with elliptic points of orders three and four and one cusp generated by \(c\). For a finite-dimensional unitary representation \(\chi:\Gamma\to U(V)\), define

\[
\mathcal H_\chi
=
L^2(Y,E_\chi)
\cong
\left\{
f:\mathfrak H\to V:
f(\gamma z)=\chi(\gamma)f(z),
\ \int_F\|f\|^2\frac{\mathrm dx\,\mathrm dy}{y^2}<\infty
\right\}.
\tag{TC4}
\]

On smooth compactly supported orbifold sections, the energy form is

\[
q_\chi[f]
=
\int_F
\left(
\|\partial_xf\|^2+\|\partial_yf\|^2
\right)\mathrm dx\,\mathrm dy.
\tag{TC5}
\]

Its Friedrichs operator is the positive self-adjoint twisted Laplacian

\[
\Delta_\chi
=
-y^2(\partial_x^2+\partial_y^2)
\tag{TC6}
\]

in local coordinates. On right-\(PSO(2)\)-invariant functions on \(\Gamma\backslash PSL_2(\mathbb R)\), this is the quadratic Casimir up to the chosen sign and normalization.

Unitarity is essential to this canonical positive construction. The actual \(GL_4(\mathbb Z)\) cusp monodromy in the torus family is a nonidentity unipotent \(I+N\), \(N^2=0\). Such an operator cannot preserve a positive-definite Hermitian form: a unitary unipotent is diagonalizable and hence must be the identity. The positive line-character twist constructed below is therefore a separate unitary shadow of the winding data, not the original rank-four monodromy relabelled.

## Cusp holonomy removes the escape channel

Choose a cusp coordinate in which

\[
c:z\longmapsto z+w,
\qquad w>0.
\tag{TC7}
\]

For a unitary line character with

\[
\chi(c)=e^{2\pi i\alpha},
\tag{TC8}
\]

equivariance gives the shifted Fourier expansion

\[
f(x,y)
=
\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}
f_n(y)e^{2\pi i(n+\alpha)x/w}.
\tag{TC9}
\]

Let \(d_\alpha=\operatorname{dist}(\alpha,\mathbb Z)\). On the tail \(C_R=\{0\leq x<w,\ y\geq R\}\), Parseval and the circle Poincare inequality give

\[
\begin{aligned}
q_\chi[f;C_R]
&\geq
\left(\frac{2\pi d_\alpha}{w}\right)^2
\int_{C_R}|f|^2\,\mathrm dx\,\mathrm dy\\
&\geq
\left(\frac{2\pi d_\alpha R}{w}\right)^2
\|f\|_{L^2(C_R,y^{-2}\mathrm dx\mathrm dy)}^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{TC10}
\]

If \(d_\alpha>0\), every form-bounded sequence has uniformly vanishing \(L^2\) mass in the tail as \(R\to\infty\). Rellich compactness on the truncated compact orbifold then makes

\[
\operatorname{Dom}(q_\chi)
\hookrightarrow
\mathcal H_\chi
\tag{TC11}
\]

compact. Hence \(\Delta_\chi\) has compact resolvent.

If \(\chi(c)=1\), the \(n=0\) Fourier channel survives. It is the Eisenstein scattering channel responsible for continuous spectrum. This does not by itself make the fixed finite-area scalar quotient gapless above its constant zero mode; it means that its resolvent is not compact. On \(\mathfrak H\) itself, the continuous spectrum starts at \(1/4\), but there is no normalizable zero-energy vacuum relative to which that number would be a mass gap.

## Triangle-character cusp-coercivity theorem

The abelianization and its character group are

\[
\Gamma_{\mathrm{ab}}
\cong C_3\times C_4
\cong C_{12},
\qquad
\widehat{\Gamma_{\mathrm{ab}}}
\cong\mu_3\times\mu_4.
\tag{TC12}
\]

Write

\[
\chi_{r,s}(a)=e^{2\pi ir/3},
\qquad
\chi_{r,s}(b)=e^{2\pi is/4}.
\tag{TC13}
\]

Then

\[
\chi_{r,s}(c)
=
\exp\!\left[-2\pi i
\left(\frac r3+\frac s4\right)\right].
\tag{TC14}
\]

Because \(\mu_3\cap\mu_4=\{1\}\),

\[
\chi_{r,s}(c)=1
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\chi_{r,s}=1.
\tag{TC15}
\]

Therefore all eleven nontrivial characters remove the cusp zero mode. For each such \(\chi\), equations (TC10)--(TC11) give compact resolvent. If \(\Delta_\chi f=0\), then \(q_\chi[f]=0\), so \(f\) is parallel; equivariance would require its constant value to be fixed by the nontrivial line character, hence \(f=0\). Thus

\[
\ker\Delta_\chi=0,
\qquad
\lambda_0(\Delta_\chi)>0.
\tag{TC16}
\]

Since the character set is finite,

\[
\boxed{
\delta_{\mathrm{aut}}
:=
\min_{\chi\ne1}\lambda_0(\Delta_\chi)
>0.}
\tag{TC17}
\]

This last statement is qualitative. Every nonzero twelfth-root phase has \(d_\alpha\geq1/12\), so the cusp estimate is uniform:

\[
q_\chi[f;C_R]
\geq
\left(\frac{\pi R}{6w}\right)^2
\|f\|_{L^2(C_R)}^2.
\tag{TC18}
\]

But an eigenfunction may concentrate in the compact core. A numerical lower bound for (TC17) still requires a quantitative core Poincare/trace estimate or a certified spectral computation.

## The unit defect has an exact phase shadow

The complex-\(S^6\) gluing uses winding integers with

\[
p=12\ell_0-4\ell_1-3\ell_2.
\tag{TC19}
\]

The same finite data define a unitary character of (TC3):

\[
\chi_\ell(a)=e^{2\pi i\ell_1/3},
\qquad
\chi_\ell(b)=e^{2\pi i\ell_2/4}.
\tag{TC20}
\]

Its cusp phase is exactly

\[
\boxed{
\chi_\ell(c)
=
e^{2\pi ip/12}.}
\tag{TC21}
\]

Indeed, the term \(12\ell_0\) vanishes in the exponential and the remaining terms reproduce (TC14). For the selected data

\[
(\ell_0,\ell_1,\ell_2)=(0,1,-1),
\qquad
p=-1,
\tag{TC22}
\]

the cusp holonomy is a primitive twelfth root. Thus the same signed descent data that make the integer relation defect a unit also select a cusp-regular automorphic sector, and (TC16) gives it a positive Casimir floor.

The converse fails in several exact ways:

- the automorphic operator sees \(p\bmod12\), whereas the topology uses \(p\in\mathbb Z\);
- every \(p\not\equiv0\pmod{12}\) removes the cusp channel, not only \(|p|=1\);
- the same-sign alternative has \(p=-7\), also a primitive cusp phase and hence also a coercive twisted sector; and
- replacing \(p\) by \(-p\) conjugates the character, while complex conjugation antiunitarily intertwines the two Laplacians, so their spectra agree.

Consequently this mechanism neither explains why the sphere topology needs a unit defect nor distinguishes a physical handedness. Its exact return is

\[
|p|=1
\Longrightarrow
\chi_\ell(c)\ne1
\Longrightarrow
\Delta_{\chi_\ell}\geq
\lambda_0(\Delta_{\chi_\ell})I>0,
\tag{TC23}
\]

after the unitary automorphic carrier and Friedrichs operator have been supplied.

## The commutator cover displays whole and sectors together

Let \(K=[\Gamma,\Gamma]\). The canonical cover

\[
K\backslash\mathfrak H
\longrightarrow
\Gamma\backslash\mathfrak H
\tag{TC24}
\]

has deck group \(C_{12}\). It is a once-punctured genus-three surface. Indeed, \(K\) is torsion-free; the cusp element has order twelve in the deck group, so the cover has one cusp; and

\[
\chi_{\mathrm{orb}}(Y)
=
\frac13+\frac14-1
=-\frac5{12}.
\tag{TC25}
\]

The degree-twelve cover therefore has Euler characteristic \(-5\), and \(2-2g-1=-5\) gives \(g=3\).

Deck Fourier decomposition yields

\[
L^2(K\backslash\mathfrak H)
\cong
\bigoplus_{\chi\in\widehat{C_{12}}}
L^2(Y,E_\chi).
\tag{TC26}
\]

The same local Casimir acts in every summand. Only the trivial deck sector contains the cusp zero Fourier mode and Eisenstein continuum; each of the eleven nontrivial sectors has compact resolvent and positive bottom. Thus one continuous whole decomposes under finite global comparison data into one scattering sector and eleven confined sectors.

This is closer to a **charged-sector mass floor** than to a complete vacuum gap. The whole cover retains the trivial sector; if it is proposed as the physical carrier, the entire vacuum complement must still be bounded, including the nonconstant trivial-sector spectrum. On this one fixed finite-area cover that lower edge is positive, but this is a fixed-geometry fact and not uniform control of a growing-volume family.

There is a further vacuum-energy distinction. If a nontrivial character labels a separate sector while the vacuum remains in the trivial sector, \(\lambda_0(\Delta_\chi)\) is a sector threshold but coverage of neutral vacuum excitations is unproved. If \(\mathcal H_\chi\) itself is declared to be the full physical carrier, its lowest eigenstate is the ground state; after subtracting that ground energy, the relevant gap is the next spectral spacing rather than the positivity of \(\lambda_0(\Delta_\chi)\). [[augmentation-descent-and-the-neutral-sector-firewall]] packages all eleven characters canonically and proves why their augmentation gap does not descend automatically to the invariant quotient.

## Filling is not the mechanism

Conformally adding the cusp point does not fill the hyperbolic metric. With \(q=e^{2\pi iz/w}\),

\[
\mathrm ds^2
=
\frac{|\mathrm dq|^2}
{|q|^2(\log|q|)^2},
\tag{TC27}
\]

so the added point remains at infinite hyperbolic distance. The toric cusp filling in the complex-threefold construction fills the **fiber over the cusp**, not this base metric and not the domain of \(\Delta_\chi\). It cannot be used as an automatic spectral boundary condition.

One could truncate the cusp or replace its metric by a smooth metric on a compact filling, but that changes the operator and introduces boundary or metric data. The theorem above needs neither move: nontrivial unitary cusp holonomy already supplies confinement on the complete cusp.

## The direct color-adjoint lift has an invariant-channel obstruction

The line-character theorem cannot be relabelled as an $SU(3)$ gluon theorem. Let

\[
\varphi:\Gamma\longrightarrow SU(3)
\tag{TC27a}
\]

be any homomorphism and twist the Laplacian by $\operatorname{Ad}\circ\varphi$. Put $g=\varphi(c)$. Every element of a compact connected Lie group lies in a maximal torus, so $g\in T$ for some $T\subset SU(3)$. Consequently

\[
\mathfrak t_{\mathbb C}
\subseteq
\ker\!\left(\operatorname{Ad}(g)-I\right),
\qquad
\dim_{\mathbb C}\mathfrak t_{\mathbb C}=2.
\tag{TC27b}
\]

Thus an adjoint-valued cusp section always retains at least two unshifted Fourier channels. If $g$ is central, the entire adjoint representation is fixed. A nontrivial color holonomy therefore cannot invoke the zero-channel-removal proof of compact resolvent above. This does not rule out a positive bottom for one fixed adjoint-twisted quotient; it rules out transporting **this particular confinement mechanism** by identifying the line character with ordinary color holonomy.

Tensoring the adjoint carrier by an additional descent line changes the cusp action to $\chi(c)\operatorname{Ad}(g)$ and can remove fixed vectors if $\chi(c)^{-1}$ is absent from the adjoint spectrum. But that line is extra global data: a physical construction must explain why it accompanies every nonvacuum gauge-invariant direction and why the vacuum remains untwisted. Equivalently, the descent operator may have to act after the gauge quotient rather than on the gauge-dependent gluon representation. This is a precise form of the Copernican requirement that the observed stabilizer need not be the structure which produces its gap.

## What would turn the template into mass

If the dimensionless curvature-one metric is rescaled by a length \(L_*\), then a Laplace eigenvalue \(\lambda\) has inverse-length-square value \(\lambda/L_*^2\). A relativistic conversion would be

\[
m_*c^2
=
\frac{\hbar c}{L_*}\sqrt{\lambda}.
\tag{TC28}
\]

This is precisely “a dimensionless number from geometry times a dimensional yardstick,” but it remains only dimensional conversion until the operator types are soldered. Here \(\lambda\) must already be an invariant excitation threshold relative to the declared physical vacuum; inserting the raw lowest eigenvalue of an independently twisted sector would confuse sector energy or vacuum energy with a mass gap. A Yang--Mills use must construct:

1. a natural automorphic/descent carrier inside, or mapping to, the gauge-invariant OS Hilbert space;
2. an intertwiner or closed-form comparison from \(\Delta_\chi\) to the reconstructed transfer Hamiltonian or Poincare mass Casimir;
3. coverage of every physical nonvacuum direction, including the neutral sector;
4. lower-bound constants uniform in spatial volume, regulator spacing, and the asymptotically free continuum trajectory; and
5. an independently selected \(L_*\), rather than fitting it from the desired glueball mass.

The exact conceptual chain is therefore

\[
\boxed{
\text{local differential Casimir}
+\text{global unitary descent}
+\text{no invariant escape channel}
\Longrightarrow
\text{coercivity}.}
\tag{TC29}
\]

This is a rigorous realization of the proposed local-continuum/global-discrete rhyme.
