# Exceptional Normal Holonomy and the Residual Gauge Form

Selecting the exceptional oriented flag derives the residual group \(H=S(U(2)\times U(3))\), but the flag's intrinsic four-dimensional normal is not a faithful \(H\)-module and cannot see surviving color or \(U(1)\) holonomy. The larger, exact normal quotient of the flag's full defining-data space is different: it is a faithful 149-dimensional real \(H\)-representation. Local lifts of the flag orbit form \(H\)-torsors, so compatible comparisons between lifts produce the ordinary gauge-transformation law and normal-bundle holonomy without postulating \(H\) first. Its character supplies a gauge-invariant, reflection-positive plaquette response with trace-index ratio \(69:71:24\); pulled back to the derived color \(SU(3)\), that response is exactly the fundamental Wilson action after the fixed coupling conversion \(\beta_W=144\beta\). The color trace metric has a further common-carrier explanation: after adding 43 trivial color directions, the normal is eight copies of the octonionic Jordan-frame tangent. This is an exact finite-regulator recovery of the standard color action from the deeper flag geometry, not merely a continuum resemblance. It does not select the color-only member, fix its bare coupling, or prove interacting-vacuum coercivity and a continuum mass gap.

## The intrinsic normal gives a no-go

Let

$$
H:=\operatorname{Stab}_{F_4}(\ell,w)
\cong
\frac{SU(2)\times U(1)\times SU(3)_{\mathrm c}}{\mathbb Z_6}
\tag{NH1}
$$

be the stabilizer constructed in [[order-three-orientation-and-the-exceptional-stabilizer]]. On the reduced oriented-flag bundle, the idempotency Hessian has a four-dimensional normal

$$
(N_{\mathrm{int}})_{\mathbb C}
\cong
(\mathbf3,\mathbf1)_0\oplus(\mathbf1,\mathbf1)_0.
\tag{NH2}
$$

Only the \(SU(2)\) factor acts nontrivially. If \(\sigma_{\mathrm{int}}\) denotes this normal representation, then

$$
SU(3)_{\mathrm c}\times U(1)
\subseteq\ker\sigma_{\mathrm{int}}.
\tag{NH3}
$$

Parallel transport in this intrinsic normal bundle is therefore blind to two factors of the very stabilizer that the flag derived. The internal idempotency gap cannot be repurposed as the residual gauge gap.

This is an exact answer to the question “what does the operator operate on?” The intrinsic Hessian operates on deformations away from rank-two idempotency. A connection valued in the stabilizer preserves the idempotent and need not excite those directions at all.

## The full defining-data normal is faithful

There is a larger natural carrier before the subalgebra and idempotency constraints are imposed. At the standard continuous flag \(f=(\ell,B)\), with \(B\cong\mathfrak h_3(\mathbb C)\subset J=\mathfrak h_3(\mathbb O)\), the tangent to

$$
J\times\operatorname{Gr}_9(J)
\tag{NH4}
$$

is

$$
V_f
=J\oplus\operatorname{Hom}(B,B^\perp),
\qquad
\dim_{\mathbb R}V_f=27+9\cdot18=189.
\tag{NH5}
$$

The exact rank calculation in [[jordan-idempotency-and-the-stabilizer-gap]] proves that the linearized natural flag constraints have kernel exactly

$$
\ker D_{\mathrm{flag},f}
=T_f(F_4\cdot f),
\qquad
\dim_{\mathbb R}\ker D_{\mathrm{flag},f}=40.
\tag{NH6}
$$

The canonical representation-theoretic normal is therefore the quotient

$$
\boxed{
N_{\mathrm{def}}
:=V_f/T_f(F_4\cdot f),
\qquad
\dim_{\mathbb R}N_{\mathrm{def}}=149.}
\tag{NH7}
$$

An \(H\)-invariant metric identifies this quotient with an orthogonal complement, but the quotient itself should not be confused with the 874-dimensional redundant constraint codomain. Constraint-row weights alter \(D^*D\); they do not alter the quotient representation (NH7).

Write an irreducible \(H\)-multiplet as

(d_2,d_3)_q,
\tag{NH8}

where \(d_2\) and \(d_3\) are the \(SU(2)\) and \(SU(3)_{\mathrm c}\) representations and the integral charge is generated by

\oplus(\mathbf1,\mathbf3)_2
\oplus(\mathbf2,\bar{\mathbf3})_1
\oplus(\mathbf1,\bar{\mathbf3})_{-2}.
\tag{NH11}

Using

$$
(V_f)_{\mathbb C}
=(B_{\mathbb C}\oplus B^\perp_{\mathbb C})
\oplus B_{\mathbb C}^*\otimes B^\perp_{\mathbb C}

and the [[library/group-theory-for-unified-model-building/inq|standard \(F_4\downarrow SU(3)\times SU(3)\) branching]], the complexified orbit tangent is

\end{aligned}
\tag{NH12a}

The first line is tangent to the \(\mathbb CP^2\) fibre; the second is tangent to the \(F_4/C_{F_4}(w)\) base. Subtracting (NH12a) from (NH12) gives

$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}

={}&2(\mathbf1,\mathbf1)_0\oplus(\mathbf3,\mathbf1)_0\\
&\oplus4(\mathbf2,\mathbf3)_{-1}

\oplus(\mathbf3,\mathbf3)_{-4}

\end{aligned}}
\tag{NH13}
$$

Here \(\mathrm{c.c.}\) applies to the six colored summands. The uncolored part has dimension five, and each colored half has dimension seventy-two, giving \(5+72+72=149\).

The conjugate pairing is a firewall. \(N_{\mathrm{def}}\) is a real orthogonal, hence vectorlike, response representation. It does not by itself produce the chiral fermion content of the Standard Model. The orientation \(w\) supplies a preferred complex structure on the underlying exceptional complement, but turning one complex polarization into a physical graded fermion carrier would require another construction and the usual local and global anomaly tests. Simply deleting the conjugate half would not be a derivation.

Let

\sigma_N:H\longrightarrow O(N_{\mathrm{def}})
\tag{NH14}

be this real representation. Every Lie-algebra ideal acts nontrivially in (NH13), and the invariant trace form below is positive on each ideal, so the infinitesimal representation is faithful. Its remaining kernel can only be central. For

the two contained multiplets \((\mathbf2,\mathbf3)_{-1}\) and \((\mathbf1,\mathbf3)_2\) force

Their six solutions are exactly the \(\mathbb Z_6\) already divided out in (NH1). Hence

$$
\boxed{\ker\sigma_N=\{1_H\}.}
\tag{NH17}

Unlike the intrinsic normal, the full defining-data normal detects every residual gauge factor.

## A derived relative trace metric

Normalize nonabelian generators by

$$
\operatorname{tr}_{\mathbf2}(T_aT_b)
=\operatorname{tr}_{\mathbf3}(t_at_b)
=\frac12\delta_{ab},
\tag{NH18}
$$

and normalize the abelian generator as

$$
Q_{\mathrm n}:=\frac{Q}{2\sqrt3},
\qquad
\operatorname{tr}_{\mathbf3}(Q_{\mathrm n}^2)=\frac12.
\tag{NH19}
$$

Summing the indices of (NH13) gives

$$
I_{SU(2)}(N_{\mathrm{def}})=71,
\qquad
I_{SU(3)}(N_{\mathrm{def}})=24,
\qquad
I_{U(1)}(N_{\mathrm{def}})=69.
\tag{NH20}
$$

Thus the Hilbert--Schmidt form induced by the normal representation has the factor ratio

$$
\boxed{
I_{U(1)}:I_{SU(2)}:I_{SU(3)}
=69:71:24.}
\tag{NH21}
$$

This is a dimensionless representation-theoretic number, not yet a prediction of measured gauge couplings. It becomes a bare coupling relation only if the full normal trace is independently justified as the microscopic action, and renormalization would still evolve the factors differently.

The full finite-group character does not split into a sum of three independent factor characters: the colored multiplets in (NH13) carry \(SU(2)\), \(U(1)\), and \(SU(3)\) labels simultaneously. Thus \(Q_N\) contains mixed finite-plaquette interactions on \(H\), even though its quadratic expansion at the identity is block diagonal with the three indices in (NH20). The exact fundamental-Wilson identity proved below is special to the color pullback; (NH21) alone is not an exact Standard Model lattice action.

The number also depends on a substantive carrier choice. The reduced intrinsic normal gives no such faithful metric, whereas the full defining-data quotient does. The deeper theory must explain why deviations of both \(\ell\) and the subalgebra plane \(B\) are physically available response directions; otherwise (NH21) is a mathematically natural candidate rather than a forced law.

## Gauge variables are comparisons of local flag lifts

The appearance of an \(H\)-connection need not be a second postulate. Let

$$
\mathcal F_{\mathrm{or}}:=F_4/H
\tag{NH21a}
$$

be the orbit of the reference oriented flag \(f_*=(\ell,w)\). The projection

$$
F_4\longrightarrow\mathcal F_{\mathrm{or}}
\tag{NH21b}
$$

is a principal \(H\)-bundle. Its fibre over a flag is an \(H\)-torsor: local presentations of the same flag differ by a unique element of the stabilizer, but the fibre has no preferred identity presentation.

Let \(\Gamma\) be an oriented incidence graph. Assign a flag \(f_v\in\mathcal F_{\mathrm{or}}\) to each vertex and a compatible comparison \(G_e\in F_4\) to every edge \(e:s(e)\to t(e)\), meaning

$$
G_e f_{s(e)}=f_{t(e)}.
\tag{NH21c}
$$

Choose local lifts \(g_v\in F_4\) with \(f_v=g_vf_*\). Then

$$
U_e:=g_{t(e)}^{-1}G_eg_{s(e)}\in H.
\tag{NH21d}
$$

Changing only the presentations, \(g_v\mapsto g_vk_v\) with \(k_v\in H\), gives

$$
U_e\longmapsto k_{t(e)}^{-1}U_ek_{s(e)}.
\tag{NH21e}
$$

For a closed path based at \(v\), the ordered product therefore transforms by conjugation with \(k_v\). This is precisely the lattice gauge law, but its order of explanation has been reversed: the oriented flag comes first, \(H\) is its stabilizer, and an \(H\)-valued link variable is the coordinate of a comparison arrow after local lifts have been chosen.

For fixed lifts the correspondence is bijective, since

$$
G_e=g_{t(e)}U_eg_{s(e)}^{-1}.
\tag{NH21g}
$$

Equations (NH21d), (NH21e), and (NH21g) therefore give a lift-independent equivalence between the groupoid of flag-compatible comparison data and the usual groupoid of \(H\)-connections and vertex gauge transformations on \(\Gamma\). The group law is not being inferred from measured link variables; it is the automorphism law of the fibre of presentations.

The defining-data normals form the associated bundle

$$
\mathcal N_{\mathrm{def}}
:=F_4\times_HN_{\mathrm{def}}
\longrightarrow\mathcal F_{\mathrm{or}}.
\tag{NH21f}
$$

In the chosen lifts, transport of this normal fibre along \(e\) is represented by \(\sigma_N(U_e)\). Its product around a plaquette is therefore an endomorphism of one normal fibre, defined up to conjugation. No spacetime metric or particle mass has entered this construction; \(\Gamma\) supplies only incidence and composition. A probability law for the comparisons, a clock transfer, and a metric realization remain additional data.

## Normal holonomy gives a Wilson response

Extend \(\Gamma\) to a finite connected oriented two-complex \(\Lambda\), and let \(U_p\) be the ordered holonomy around plaquette \(p\). The full normal representation defines

$$
\mathscr J_N(U)_p
:=\sigma_N(U_p)-I_N
\tag{NH22}
$$

and the dimensionless form

$$
\boxed{
Q_N(U)
:=\sum_p
\lVert\sigma_N(U_p)-I_N\rVert_{\mathrm{HS}}^2.}
\tag{NH23}
$$

This is the reversal tactic in operational form. The operator acts on the transport of the flag's defining normal data around a closed incidence relation. Curvature is inferred from failure of that transport to return the normal fibre to itself.

Under a vertex gauge transformation, each based plaquette holonomy changes by conjugation, so (NH23) is gauge invariant. Faithfulness gives

$$
Q_N(U)=0
\iff
U_p=1_H\quad\text{for every }p.
\tag{NH24}
$$

On a simply connected complex with fixed boundary trivialization this is equivalent to \(U\) being pure gauge. On a general connected complex, the zero set modulo gauge is

$$
\operatorname{Hom}(\pi_1(\Lambda),H)/H.
\tag{NH25}
$$

For \(U_e=\exp(tA_e)\) near the trivial connection,

$$
Q_N(U)
=t^2\sum_p
\left\lVert
\mathrm d\sigma_N\bigl((\mathrm dA)_p\bigr)
\right\rVert_{\mathrm{HS}}^2
+O(t^3).
\tag{NH26}
$$

If the relevant relative first cohomology vanishes, the Hessian kernel consists exactly of infinitesimal vertex-gauge directions. At fixed finite regulator, its quotient therefore has a positive edge. This is a curvature stiffness, not yet the quantum Hamiltonian mass gap.

Because \(\sigma_N\) is real orthogonal,

$$
\lVert\sigma_N(h)-I\rVert_{\mathrm{HS}}^2
=2\bigl(149-\chi_N(h)\bigr).
\tag{NH27}
$$

Hence the plaquette weight is, up to an irrelevant constant,

$$
\exp\!\left[-\frac\beta2
\lVert\sigma_N(h)-I\rVert_{\mathrm{HS}}^2\right]
=e^{-149\beta}e^{\beta\chi_N(h)}.
\tag{NH28}
$$

Its expansion

$$
e^{\beta\chi_N(h)}
=\sum_{n\geq0}\frac{\beta^n}{n!}
\chi_{N^{\otimes n}}(h)
\tag{NH29}
$$

has nonnegative character coefficients. It therefore fits the standard finite-lattice reflection-positive Wilson route represented by [[library/gauge-field-theories-on-a-lattice/inq|Osterwalder and Seiler]] and [[library/construction-of-a-selfadjoint-strictly-positive-transfer-matrix-for-euclidean-lattice-gauge-theories/inq|Luscher]].

## The color pullback is exactly Wilson's regulator

The color factor is not chosen from two indistinguishable ideals. At the Lie-algebra level,

$$
\mathfrak h
=\mathfrak{su}(2)\oplus\mathfrak u(1)\oplus\mathfrak{su}(3),
\qquad
[\mathfrak h,\mathfrak h]
=\mathfrak{su}(2)\oplus\mathfrak{su}(3).
$$

The eight-dimensional simple ideal is unique and therefore invariant under every Lie-algebra automorphism. Its connected subgroup is the structurally distinguished color factor. This identifies a factor of the stabilizer; it does not select a color-only physical theory.

That factor does more than share the same quadratic continuum form. Restricting (NH13) along the distinguished inclusion

$$
\iota_{\mathrm c}:SU(3)_{\mathrm c}\hookrightarrow H
\tag{NH29a}
$$

and forgetting the \(SU(2)\) multiplicity labels gives

$$
\left.(N_{\mathrm{def}})_{\mathbb C}\right|_{SU(3)_{\mathrm c}}
=5\,\mathbf1\oplus24\,\mathbf3\oplus24\,\bar{\mathbf3}.
\tag{NH29b}
$$

Because \(N_{\mathrm{def}}\) is real, its real character is the character of this complexification. Hence, for every \(V\in SU(3)\),

$$
\boxed{
\chi_N(\iota_{\mathrm c}V)
=5+24\operatorname{tr}_{\mathbf3}V
+24\overline{\operatorname{tr}_{\mathbf3}V}
=5+48\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{tr}_{\mathbf3}V.}
\tag{NH29c}
$$

Substitution into (NH27) is exact, with no small-plaquette expansion:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\left\lVert\sigma_N(\iota_{\mathrm c}V)-I\right\rVert_{\mathrm{HS}}^2
&=96\left(3-\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{tr}_{\mathbf3}V\right)\\
&=288\left(1-\frac13\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{tr}_{\mathbf3}V\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{NH29d}
$$

[[faithful-and-adjoint-holonomy-response|Faithful versus adjoint response]] decomposes this exact color function as \(288a+144|1-\operatorname{tr}V/3|^2\), where \(a\) is the identity-Hessian-normalized adjoint defect. The extra summand is already part of (NH29d), not a new stiffness; it retains center information invisible to the adjoint probe.

Let

$$
Q_W(V):=\sum_p\left(1-\frac13
\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{tr}_{\mathbf3}V_p\right)
\tag{NH29e}
$$

be the normalized plaquette action introduced by [[library/confinement-of-quarks/inq|Wilson]], using the same convention of counting each unoriented plaquette once in both sums. On the color configuration space, the action-level pullback is the commutative recovery square

$$
\begin{array}{ccc}
SU(3)^{E(\Lambda)}&\xrightarrow{\ \iota_{\mathrm c}^{E(\Lambda)}\ }&H^{E(\Lambda)}\\[3pt]
\big\downarrow {Q_W}&&\big\downarrow {Q_N}\\[3pt]
\mathbb R_{\geq0}&\xrightarrow{\ \times288\ }&\mathbb R_{\geq0}.
\end{array}
\tag{NH29f}
$$

Therefore

$$
\frac\beta2Q_N\!\left(\iota_{\mathrm c}^{E}V\right)
=\beta_WQ_W(V),
\qquad
\boxed{\beta_W=144\beta.}
\tag{NH29g}
$$

Equipped with product \(SU(3)\) Haar measure, the normalized finite-lattice Gibbs law on the left is exactly the ordinary fundamental Wilson law at \(\beta_W\). Its gauge-invariant correlators, reflection-positive Osterwalder--Schrader form, and transfer matrix are consequently the standard Wilson ones at every lattice spacing and finite volume. In the common convention \(\beta_W=6/g_0^2\), the conversion is \(\beta=1/(24g_0^2)\), exposing the same color index \(24\) found in (NH20).

This is stronger than a universality argument or a naive continuum expansion. It is also narrower than emergence. Pullback along \(\iota_{\mathrm c}\) defines a color-only member with its own Haar measure; it is not conditioning the full \(H\) measure on a measure-zero subgroup, and no dynamics here selects that member. Exact equality of the regulators transfers the unsolved continuum existence and mass-gap problem unchanged rather than solving it.

## The normal metric fixes a kinematic Haar floor

The same representation fixes the electric metric convention before a dimensional kinetic coefficient is supplied. For anti-Hermitian color generators \(X_a=it_a\) with \(\operatorname{tr}_{\mathbf3}(t_at_b)=\delta_{ab}/2\), put

$$
b_N(X,Y)
:=\operatorname{Tr}_{N_{\mathrm{def}}}
\bigl(\mathrm d\sigma_N(X)^*\mathrm d\sigma_N(Y)\bigr).
\tag{NH29h}
$$

The color index in (NH20) and \(-B_{\mathfrak{su}(3)}(X_a,X_b)=3\delta_{ab}\) give

$$
\boxed{b_N=8\bigl(-B_{\mathfrak{su}(3)}\bigr).}
\tag{NH29i}
$$

The coefficient now has an exact exceptional-geometric origin. If \(T_{\mathrm{fr}}=T(F_4/\operatorname{Spin}(8))\) is the ordered Jordan-frame tangent, then the same color subgroup sees

$$
T_{\mathrm{fr}}\cong\mathbb R^6\oplus3(\mathbb C^3)_{\mathbb R},
\qquad
N_{\mathrm{def}}\cong\mathbb R^5\oplus24(\mathbb C^3)_{\mathbb R},
\tag{NH29i-a}
$$

and therefore

$$
\boxed{
N_{\mathrm{def}}\oplus\mathbb R^{43}
\cong
8T_{\mathrm{fr}}
}
\qquad
\text{as real }SU(3)_{\mathrm c}\text{-modules}.
\tag{NH29i-b}
$$

Trivial summands do not contribute to the differentiated character, while \(b_{T_{\mathrm{fr}}}=-B_{\mathfrak{su}(3)}\), so (NH29i) is the trace-form shadow of (NH29i-b). [[octonionic-clifford-completion-of-the-color-normal]] proves the stable identity and the sharper Clifford statement: the selected complement \(W=\mathbb C_u^\perp\) acts on \(\mathbb O\) as \(\mathrm{Cl}_{0,6}\), and \(N_{\mathrm{def}}\oplus\mathbb R^{43}\cong24\mathbb O\). It also keeps the categorical firewall: this same-degree multiplication by eight is not Bott's invertible degree-eight shift, and a Morita class alone cannot fix a trace normalization.

Laplacian eigenvalues scale inversely with the metric. The fundamental matrix coefficients have lowest nonzero eigenvalue \(4/9\) in the negative-Killing convention, so in the normal metric

$$
\lambda_{SU(3)}^{(N)}
=\frac18\frac49
=\frac1{18}.
\tag{NH29j}
$$

The finite-graph girth--Casimir theorem in [[gauge-descent-flux-fisher-coercivity]] therefore gives the exact unweighted gauge-invariant Haar constant

$$
\lambda_{\Gamma,\mathrm{Haar}}^{\mathrm{GI},N}
=\frac{g(\Gamma)}{18}.
\tag{NH29k}
$$

For an ordinary square-plaquette graph with no shorter cycle this is \(2/9\), independent of total graph volume. It is the first dimensionless lower-frame number fixed by the exceptional normal geometry on the actual color configuration carrier. It is not an interacting mass gap. Passing from Haar measure to the vacuum law \(\psi_0^2\mu_{\mathrm{Haar}}\) is the unsolved coercivity step, and rescaling the metric rescales the electric kinetic coefficient reciprocally. Only their physically normalized product can have energy units.

Equivalently, on the gauge-invariant Haar carrier the pure electric operator built from this metric obeys the exact form inequality

$$
\kappa_NK_{\Gamma,N}
\geq
\kappa_N\frac{g(\Gamma)}{18}(1-P_0),
\tag{NH29l}
$$

and its square-girth gap is \(2\kappa_N/9\). This is the solvable strong-coupling anchor: it acts on wavefunctions of flag-preserving comparison holonomies, not on the normal fibre itself. Adding the Wilson magnetic potential changes the ground-state weight, while taking the asymptotically free continuum limit changes both \(\kappa_N\) and the tuned coupling. Transporting a positive physical product through those changes is the actual gap problem.

## What this buys, and what it does not

The chain is now longer than a stabilizer coincidence:

$$
\boxed{
(\ell,w)
\longrightarrow F_4/H\text{ with }H\text{-torsor fibres}
\longrightarrow H\text{-holonomy}
\longrightarrow N_{\mathrm{def}}
\longrightarrow\chi_N
\longrightarrow Q_N
\xrightarrow{\ SU(3)_{\mathrm c}\text{ pullback}\ }
Q_W.}
\tag{NH30}
$$

It derives a gauge group, the gauge-coordinate law, a faithful probe, a relative invariant metric, a sharp unweighted Haar floor, and a reflection-positive finite-lattice action from one exceptional flag architecture. Its color pullback then recovers the ordinary Wilson regulator exactly. In the heliocentric analogy, this is a common construction behind several appearances rather than an independent fit to each symmetry factor.

[[exceptional-wilson-same-carrier-factorization]] sharpens the carrier statement. The normal representation is the finite response probe, not the Yang--Mills state space. Its Hilbert--Schmidt contraction produces \(Q_N\), its trace produces \(b_N\), and both act on the standard gauge-invariant carrier. On that common carrier,

$$
H_N(\kappa_N,\lambda_N)
=
H_W\!\left(\frac{\kappa_N}{8},288\lambda_N\right).
\tag{NH30a}
$$

Thus the holonomy branch already has an exact finite-regulator carrier bridge. The separate field-valued map into normal fibres is required only if the idempotency Hessian itself is proposed as the gap form.

It is not yet the desired mass-gap mechanism:

1. \(H\) is not simple and contains an abelian factor. The color pullback gives the Clay-relevant \(SU(3)\) regulator exactly, but choosing the color-only member is another declared step rather than a dynamical selection theorem.
2. At finite lattice spacing, a positive Hessian modulo gauge is automatic once relative cohomology vanishes; it says nothing uniform about large volume or continuum removal.
3. The magnetic plaquette multiplier cannot itself be gapped on a fixed vacuum complement: gauge-invariant wavefunctions may concentrate arbitrarily near the flat locus where \(Q_N=0\). It must be joined to the electric kinetic form and the interacting vacuum measure.
4. Reflection positivity constructs a possible Hilbert carrier and transfer operator; it does not by itself prove a vacuum-complement contraction.
5. The normal representation fixes the conversion \(\beta_W=144\beta\), not \(\beta\) itself, a dimensional scale, or the renormalized coupling.

For the finite Kogut--Susskind carrier, the relevant stopping condition remains the weighted Poincare inequality in [[gauge-descent-flux-fisher-coercivity]]. If \(\nu_r=\psi_{0,r}^2\mu_{\mathrm{Haar}}\) is the interacting gauge-invariant vacuum law and \(\mathcal E_r\) is its closed electric form, one needs

$$
\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_r}(F)
\leq
\lambda_r^{-1}\mathcal E_r(F,F)
\tag{NH31}
$$

Let \(\kappa_{N,r}\) be the electric kinetic coefficient in energy units and let \(\widehat\Lambda_{\mathrm{YM},r}^{(\mathsf s)}\) be an independently defined running representative of the scheme-labelled Yang--Mills scale. The gap-specific stopping condition is

$$
\boxed{
\gamma_N
:=\liminf_{\substack{a\to0\\L\to\infty}}
\frac{\kappa_{N,r}\lambda_r}
{\widehat\Lambda_{\mathrm{YM},r}^{(\mathsf s)}}
>0,}
\tag{NH32}
$$

along the tuned trajectory \(\beta_{W,r}=144\beta_r\). Generalized Mosco convergence of the physical forms, convergence of the complete vacuum projections, and a nontrivial OS/Poincare reconstruction would then give

$$
H_{\mathrm{YM}}
\geq
\gamma_N\Lambda_{\mathrm{YM}}^{(\mathsf s)}(1-P_\Omega).
\tag{NH33}
$$

This is a conditional passage theorem, not a proof of (NH32). The exceptional construction fixes the regulator, metric normalization, and exact strong-coupling anchor; it does not yet control the interacting vacuum through the weak-bare-coupling continuum trajectory. Equivalently, the normalization-free relative coercivity

$$
\Theta_r
:=
\frac{\lambda_r}{g(\Gamma_r)/18}
\tag{NH33a}
$$

equals one on the Haar anchor but has no proved positive uniform lower limit for the interacting vacuum. The geometric factor \(8\) cancels against the matched kinetic coefficient and cannot supply that missing control. A finite normal Hessian cannot substitute for the vacuum theorem.
