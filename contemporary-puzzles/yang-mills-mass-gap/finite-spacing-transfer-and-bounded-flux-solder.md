# Finite-Spacing Transfer and the Bounded Flux Solder

At nonzero Wilson temporal spacing, the transfer Hamiltonian is not the Kogut--Susskind flux Laplacian plus plaquette potential, and it cannot in general dominate that unbounded local gradient form on the full carrier. The obstruction is already exact for one \(SU(2)\) Wilson convolution: logarithmic eigenvalues grow like \(j\log j\), while the invariant Laplacian grows like \(j^2\). Its bounded defect nevertheless has an exact action-derived edge. On a pure product-Wilson graph, gauge closure raises that edge from one nontrivial link to a shortest closed spin network, and its temporal-continuum rate reproduces the electric girth--Casimir floor. The interacting magnetic sandwich and its vacuum density remain the unsolved comparison.

**Status: [EXACT] for the finite-transfer ground-state transform, the one-step energy inequality, the \(SU(2)\) Wilson-kernel counterexample, the pure product-Wilson bounded edge, its gauge-invariant girth refinement, and the bounded-solder implication; [ESTABLISHED LIMIT PRECEDENT] for extraction of the Kogut--Susskind Hamiltonian from an anisotropic temporal-continuum limit; [OPEN] for a regulator-uniform interacting bounded-defect comparison along four-dimensional Yang--Mills continuum removal.**

## Two Hamiltonians on one slice carrier

Let

\[
\mathcal H_\Sigma
=
L^2(G^{E_\Sigma},\mu_{\mathrm H})^{\mathcal G_\Sigma}
\tag{FB1}
\]

be a finite gauge-invariant time-zero carrier. Under the usual finite Wilson hypotheses, [[library/construction-of-a-selfadjoint-strictly-positive-transfer-matrix-for-euclidean-lattice-gauge-theories/inq|Luscher's construction]] gives a bounded self-adjoint strictly positive transfer operator \(T\). If its maximal eigenvalue \(\lambda_0\) is simple, normalize

\[
\widetilde T=\lambda_0^{-1}T,
\qquad
H_T-E_0
=
-\frac{\hbar c}{a_\tau}\log\widetilde T.
\tag{FB2}
\]

This is the exact finite-\(a_\tau\) transfer Hamiltonian. It should not be silently replaced by the Kogut--Susskind Hamiltonian

\[
H_{\mathrm{KS}}
=
\kappa_{a_s}D_{\mathrm H}+V_{\mathrm{plaq}},
\qquad
D_{\mathrm H}
=
-\sum_{e,A}(X_e^A)^2.
\tag{FB3}
\]

For a positive Kogut--Susskind ground state \(\psi_{\mathrm{KS}}\), integration by parts gives the exact ground-state identity

\[
\left\langle
\psi_{\mathrm{KS}}f,
(H_{\mathrm{KS}}-E_{\mathrm{KS}})\psi_{\mathrm{KS}}f
\right\rangle
=
\kappa_{a_s}
\sum_{e,A}\int|X_e^Af|^2\psi_{\mathrm{KS}}^2\,\mathrm d\mu_{\mathrm H}.
\tag{FB4}
\]

That cancellation uses the second-order Laplace-plus-potential structure of (FB3). [[library/gauge-fixing-the-transfer-matrix-and-confinement-on-a-lattice/inq|Creutz]] extracts this Hamiltonian by taking the timelike spacing to zero. [[library/su-n-gauge-theory-couplings-on-asymmetric-lattices/inq|Karsch]] further shows why the temporal limit needs separately renormalized spatial and temporal couplings. Neither source licenses (FB4) for \(-\log T\) at fixed isotropic spacing.

## The exact one-step form

Let \(\psi_T>0\) be the normalized maximal eigenvector of \(T\), put

\[
\mathrm d\nu_T
=
\psi_T^2\,\mathrm d\mu_{\mathrm H},
\qquad
U_Tf=\psi_Tf,
\tag{FB5}
\]

and define the ground-state-transformed transfer

\[
P_T
:=
U_T^{-1}\widetilde T U_T.
\tag{FB6}
\]

When the transfer kernel is nonnegative, \(P_T\) is a reversible Markov operator on \(L^2(\nu_T)\). Its bounded one-step Dirichlet form is

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal E_T^{\mathrm{step}}[f]
&=
\langle f,(I-P_T)f\rangle_{\nu_T}\\
&=
\frac1{2\lambda_0}
\iint
|f(U)-f(V)|^2
\psi_T(U)K_T(U,V)\psi_T(V)
\,\mathrm dU\,\mathrm dV.
\end{aligned}
\tag{FB7}
\]

The spectral inequality \(-\log x\geq1-x\) on \(0<x\leq1\) yields

\[
\boxed{
\left\langle
U_Tf,(H_T-E_0)U_Tf
\right\rangle
\geq
\frac{\hbar c}{a_\tau}
\mathcal E_T^{\mathrm{step}}[f].}
\tag{FB8}
\]

This is exact and uses no excited eigenvalue. It does not say that the semigroup generated by \(-\log P_T\) is Markov at every fractional step; one-step Markovity is weaker than infinite divisibility.

## The fixed-spacing unbounded-solder no-go

The proposed full-carrier comparison

\[
-\log P_T
\geq
\epsilon D_{\mathrm H},
\qquad
\epsilon>0,
\tag{FB9}
\]

does not follow from Wilson transfer structure and is already false for its pure one-link kinetic member. Equations (17) and (20)--(23) of [[library/construction-of-a-selfadjoint-strictly-positive-transfer-matrix-for-euclidean-lattice-gauge-theories/inq|Luscher's transfer construction]] factor the gauge kernel into one-link Wilson factors and expand such a factor in positive group characters. The following \(SU(2)\) Bessel evaluation is our derived witness, not a theorem stated in that source. Take the class-function carrier \(L^2(SU(2))^{\operatorname{Ad}}\), normalized Haar class measure

\[
\mathrm d\mu_{\mathrm{cl}}(\theta)
=
\frac{2}{\pi}\sin^2\theta\,\mathrm d\theta,
\qquad
0\leq\theta\leq\pi,
\tag{FB9a}
\]

and the central Wilson factor

\[
k_x(\theta)=e^{x\cos\theta},
\qquad x>0.
\tag{FB10}
\]

The irreducible characters

\[
\chi_j(\theta)
=
\frac{\sin((2j+1)\theta)}{\sin\theta},
\qquad
j\in\tfrac12\mathbb N_0,
\tag{FB11}
\]

are orthonormal in \(L^2(\mu_{\mathrm{cl}})\). Define

\[
(C_xf)(g)
=
\int_{SU(2)}k_x(gh^{-1})f(h)\,\mathrm dh,
\qquad
P_x
=
\frac{C_x}{C_x\mathbf1}.
\tag{FB11a}
\]

Schur orthogonality and the Weyl integral formula give, with \(d_j=2j+1\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
\int_{SU(2)}k_x(g)\chi_j(g)\,\mathrm dg
&=
\frac{2}{\pi}
\int_0^\pi
e^{x\cos\theta}\sin\theta\sin(d_j\theta)\,\mathrm d\theta\\
&=
I_{2j}(x)-I_{2j+2}(x)
=
\frac{2d_j}{x}I_{2j+1}(x).
\end{aligned}
\tag{FB11b}
\]

Thus \(C_x\) has scalar \(2I_{2j+1}(x)/x\) in the spin-\(j\) sector, while \(C_x\mathbf1=2I_1(x)/x\). Because the kernel is central, the same scalar acts on every matrix coefficient in the full spin-\(j\) Peter--Weyl block; the character calculation is not a restriction of the spectrum to class functions. The normalized convolution and the invariant Laplacian, in the convention where the spin-\(j\) Casimir is \(j(j+1)\), therefore obey

\[
P_x\chi_j
=
p_j\chi_j,
\qquad
p_j
=
\frac{I_{2j+1}(x)}{I_1(x)},
\qquad
D_{\mathrm H}\chi_j
=
j(j+1)\chi_j.
\tag{FB12}
\]

At fixed \(x>0\),

\[
I_n(x)
\sim
\frac{(x/2)^n}{n!},
\qquad
-\log p_j
\sim
2j\log j.
\tag{FB13}
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
\frac{-\log p_j}{j(j+1)}
\longrightarrow0.}
\tag{FB14}
\]

No positive \(\epsilon\) can make (FB9) hold. More precisely,

\[
\begin{aligned}
D(D_{\mathrm H}^{1/2})
&=
\left\{(c_j):\sum_j j(j+1)|c_j|^2<\infty\right\},\\
D((-\log P_x)^{1/2})
&=
\left\{(c_j):\sum_j(-\log p_j)|c_j|^2<\infty\right\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{FB14a}
\]

The quadratic-form domains point in the opposite direction:

\[
D(D_{\mathrm H}^{1/2})
\subsetneq
D((-\log P_x)^{1/2}).
\tag{FB15}
\]

For example, coefficients with \(|c_j|^2\asymp j^{-3}\) give finite \(\sum_j(-\log p_j)|c_j|^2\) but divergent \(\sum_j j(j+1)|c_j|^2\). This witness blocks an unbounded comparison claimed solely from finite Wilson transfer structure. An interacting full-lattice comparison would need genuinely additional input; it cannot be treated as the automatic OS solder.

For each fixed representation, the large-\(x\) asymptotic

\[
-\log\frac{I_{2j+1}(x)}{I_1(x)}
=
\frac{2j(j+1)}{x}+O(x^{-2})
\tag{FB15a}
\]

does recover Casimir scaling. The convergence is not uniform as \(j\to\infty\), so taking the temporal-continuum limit and taking the ultraviolet representation label to infinity do not commute. That is precisely why a form-domain theorem, not a termwise expansion, is required.

## The pure product-Wilson bounded edge

The failure of unbounded domination does not make the pure kinetic transfer gap mysterious. [[library/on-an-inequality-for-modified-bessel-functions/inq|Soni's inequality]], also recorded in [NIST DLMF section 10.37](https://dlmf.nist.gov/10.37), gives strict order monotonicity of \(I_\nu(x)\) at fixed \(x>0\). Hence

\[
1=p_0>p_{1/2}>p_1>\cdots>0,
\qquad
p_{1/2}=\frac{I_2(x)}{I_1(x)}.
\tag{FB15b}
\]

Let \(\Pi_{\mathbf1}\) be the projection onto constants. Full Peter--Weyl diagonalization immediately gives the sharp one-link inequality

\[
\boxed{
I-P_x
\geq
\gamma_x(I-\Pi_{\mathbf1}),
\qquad
\gamma_x
:=
1-\frac{I_2(x)}{I_1(x)}
>0.}
\tag{FB15c}
\]

[[library/inequalities-for-modified-bessel-functions/inq|Näsell's stronger ratio inequality]] with \(\nu=1\) gives the elementary certificate

\[
\frac{I_2(x)}{I_1(x)}
<
\frac{x}{x+1},
\qquad
\boxed{\gamma_x>\frac1{x+1}.}
\tag{FB15c1}
\]

Thus even the weaker coefficient \(1/(x+1)\) is an analytic function of the bare action parameter and does not require numerical spectral inspection.

Since \(0\leq I-e^{-\tau D_{\mathrm H}}\leq I-\Pi_{\mathbf1}\) for every fixed \(\tau>0\), this also proves the noncircular bounded solder

\[
I-P_x
\geq
\gamma_x\bigl(I-e^{-\tau D_{\mathrm H}}\bigr).
\tag{FB15d}
\]

The coefficient is computed analytically from the bare one-link Wilson kernel rather than fitted from the interacting transfer spectrum. Inequality (FB15d) is deliberately not advertised as optimal for finite \(\tau\); (FB15c) is the sharp spectral-edge statement.

Now let \(\Gamma\) be a finite connected graph or multigraph containing a cycle. Assume independent \(SU(2)\)-valued link coordinates with product Haar measure, gauge the full vertex group, and impose no matter fields, fixed links, flatness constraints, or global holonomy constraints. Let \(g(\Gamma)\) be the multigraph girth, counting a self-loop as length one and a parallel pair as length two, and set

\[
P_{x,\Gamma}
:=
\bigotimes_{e\in E_\Gamma}P_x,
\qquad
D_\Gamma
:=
\sum_{e\in E_\Gamma}D_{{\mathrm H},e}.
\tag{FB15e}
\]

Both operators preserve \(L^2(SU(2)^{E_\Gamma})^{\mathcal G_\Gamma}\). A spin-network block with edge labels \((j_e)\) has transfer eigenvalue \(\prod_ep_{j_e}\). Every nonconstant gauge-invariant block has nontrivial support containing a cycle, hence at least \(g(\Gamma)\) nontrivial edges, where \(g(\Gamma)\) is graph girth. Strict Bessel monotonicity gives

\[
\prod_ep_{j_e}
\leq
p_{1/2}^{\,g(\Gamma)}.
\tag{FB15f}
\]

The fundamental Wilson character on a shortest cycle attains equality. If \(P_0\) denotes the constant projection on the gauge-invariant carrier, then

\[
\boxed{
\left\|
P_{x,\Gamma}(I-P_0)
\right\|
=
\left(\frac{I_2(x)}{I_1(x)}\right)^{g(\Gamma)},}
\tag{FB15g}
\]

and therefore

\[
\boxed{
I-P_{x,\Gamma}
\geq
\gamma_{x,\Gamma}(I-P_0)
\geq
\gamma_{x,\Gamma}
\bigl(I-e^{-\tau D_\Gamma}\bigr),}
\qquad
\gamma_{x,\Gamma}
:=
1-
\left(\frac{I_2(x)}{I_1(x)}\right)^{g(\Gamma)}.
\tag{FB15h}
\]

The first inequality in (FB15h) is sharp. A fully gauged tree has only constant invariant functions and hence no nonvacuum edge to estimate. Ungauged boundary vertices, charged endpoints, matter, fixed link or holonomy data, flatness constraints, and other restrictions can change the admissible spin-network supports and invalidate (FB15g). A Bessel-free lower certificate is

\[
\gamma_{x,\Gamma}
>
1-
\left(\frac{x}{x+1}\right)^{g(\Gamma)}.
\tag{FB15h1}
\]

The corresponding pure kinetic logarithmic edge is exactly

\[
\delta_{x,\Gamma}
:=
\inf\sigma\!\left(
-\log P_{x,\Gamma}\big|_{(I-P_0)\mathcal H_\Gamma^{\mathrm{GI}}}
\right)
=
g(\Gamma)\log\frac{I_1(x)}{I_2(x)}.
\tag{FB15i}
\]

This is the finite-step counterpart of the [[gauge-descent-flux-fisher-coercivity#gauge-invariance-sharpens-the-constant-to-girth-times-casimir|girth--Casimir theorem]]. The linked theorem defaults to the metric \(-B_{\mathfrak{su}(2)}\), for which the fundamental Casimir is \(3/8\). Here \(D_{\mathrm H}|_{V_j\otimes V_j^*}=j(j+1)I\) is the standard lattice-generator convention, twice that Laplacian, so its fundamental floor is \(3/4\). This explicit rescaling prevents the two normalizations from being mixed. Indeed,

\[
\frac{I_2(x)}{I_1(x)}
=
1-\frac{3}{2x}+\frac{3}{8x^2}+O(x^{-3}),
\qquad
\delta_{x,\Gamma}
=
\frac{3g(\Gamma)}{2x}+O(x^{-2}).
\tag{FB15j}
\]

In the standard tree-level anisotropic \(SU(2)\) Wilson convention in \(3+1\) dimensions, a timelike plaquette has weight

\[
\exp\!\left[
\frac{\beta_t}{2}\operatorname{ReTr}(UU'^{-1})
\right],
\qquad
\beta_t
=
\frac{4a_s}{g_0^2a_\tau},
\tag{FB15k}
\]

Here \(\operatorname{Tr}\) is the unnormalized trace in the two-dimensional fundamental representation, so \(\operatorname{ReTr}U=2\cos\theta\) and \(x=\beta_t\). At fixed \(a_s\) and \(g_0\), the pure-kinetic logarithmic rate then satisfies, as \(a_\tau\downarrow0\),

\[
\frac{\hbar c}{a_\tau}\delta_{x,\Gamma}
\longrightarrow
\frac{g_0^2\hbar c}{2a_s}
\left(\frac{3g(\Gamma)}4\right).
\tag{FB15l}
\]

The right side is exactly the Kogut--Susskind electric coefficient times the \(SU(2)\) gauge-invariant Haar floor \(g(\Gamma)\lambda_{SU(2)}\), with \(\lambda_{SU(2)}=3/4\) in the present lattice convention. This is a normalization check and a controlled tree-level temporal-continuum benchmark, not a physical glueball mass: beyond tree level the spatial and temporal couplings and the renormalized anisotropy require separate tuning; the full Wilson transfer is a spatial-plaquette multiplier/kinetic-kernel/multiplier sandwich; its ground state is not Haar; and the interacting operator is not diagonal in these product spin-network blocks.

[[contemporary-puzzles/yang-mills-mass-gap/receipts/wilson_bounded_solder_receipt.py|The finite receipt]] checks the Bessel ordering on representative sectors, the elementary certificates, the square-girth product edge, and convergence of the pure-kinetic logarithmic rate to the electric Casimir normalization. Its [[contemporary-puzzles/yang-mills-mass-gap/receipts/wilson-bounded-solder-receipt-output.txt|stored output]] records the passing run. It verifies arithmetic, not the Peter--Weyl proof, the interacting transfer comparison, or a continuum mass gap.

## The full finite Wilson sandwich has an explicit global bound

The magnetic terms do not erase the kinetic result, but they change its carrier. For pure Wilson gauge theory in temporal gauge, Luscher's factorization has the schematic exact form

\[
T
=
\text{scalar}\times M_a K M_a,
\qquad
K
=
\bigotimes_{e\in E_\Gamma}P_x,
\tag{FB15m}
\]

where \(K\) is the normalized positive Haar-reversible product Markov kernel and \(a>0\) is the gauge-invariant half-slice spatial-plaquette Boltzmann multiplier. The scalar disappears after normalizing by the maximal transfer eigenvalue. Let

\[
T\psi=\lambda_0\psi,
\qquad
\int\psi^2\,\mathrm d\mu_{\mathrm H}=1,
\qquad
\rho:=\psi^2,
\tag{FB15n}
\]

where the Perron vector \(\psi\) is chosen positive and gauge invariant, and assume the finite-volume essential bounds

\[
0<a_-\leq a\leq a_+,
\qquad
0<\rho_-\leq\rho\leq\rho_+.
\tag{FB15o}
\]

The stationary-edge representation in [[markov-edge-measure-solder/inq]] gives the right grammar, but the reference kernel \(K\) is stationary for product Haar measure while the Doob transform \(P_T\) is stationary for \(\nu=\rho\mu_{\mathrm H}\). The comparison therefore needs the following direct calculation rather than an application of the same-stationary-law theorem. After removing the irrelevant positive scalar from \(T\), the ground-state edge identity is

\[
\lambda_0\mathcal E_{P_T}(f)
=
\frac12\iint
k(U,V)a(U)\psi(U)a(V)\psi(V)
|f(U)-f(V)|^2
\,\mathrm d\mu_{\mathrm H}(U)\mathrm d\mu_{\mathrm H}(V).
\tag{FB15o1}
\]

Because \(\lambda_0=\|M_aKM_a\|\leq a_+^2\), (FB15o) implies

\[
\mathcal E_{P_T}(f)
\geq
\left(\frac{a_-}{a_+}\right)^2\rho_-\mathcal E_K(f).
\tag{FB15o2}
\]

Moreover,

\[
\operatorname{Var}_{\nu}(f)
=\inf_c\int|f-c|^2\rho\,\mathrm d\mu_{\mathrm H}
\leq
\rho_+\operatorname{Var}_{\mu_{\mathrm H}}(f).
\tag{FB15o3}
\]

Applying the Haar spectral gap of \(K|_{\mathrm{GI}}\) to (FB15o2) and then using (FB15o3) gives

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{gap}(P_T)
\geq
\operatorname{gap}(K|_{\mathrm{GI}})
\left(\frac{a_-}{a_+}\right)^2
\frac{\rho_-}{\rho_+}.}
\tag{FB15p}
\]

For the \(SU(2)\) product Wilson kernel on a graph satisfying the hypotheses above,

\[
\operatorname{gap}(K|_{\mathrm{GI}})
=
\gamma_{x,\Gamma}
=
1-
\left(\frac{I_2(x)}{I_1(x)}\right)^{g(\Gamma)}.
\tag{FB15q}
\]

This is already an exact finite-volume interacting transfer bound. It is noncircular if the vacuum-density ratio is estimated without using the desired transfer gap, but the displayed global-extrema certificate is not thereby uniform in volume.

There is also a completely action-level, and correspondingly cruder, certificate. If the normalized product kernel obeys

\[
0<k_-\leq k(U,V)\leq k_+,
\tag{FB15r}
\]

then the Perron eigen-equation implies

\[
\frac{\sup\psi}{\inf\psi}
\leq
\frac{a_+}{a_-}\frac{k_+}{k_-}.
\tag{FB15s}
\]

Substitution into (FB15p) yields

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{gap}(P_T)
\geq
\operatorname{gap}(K|_{\mathrm{GI}})
\left(\frac{a_-}{a_+}\right)^4
\left(\frac{k_-}{k_+}\right)^2.}
\tag{FB15t}
\]

The reason this exact estimate does not approach the Clay problem is visible in its factors. The oscillation of the spatial plaquette multiplier grows with the number of spatial plaquettes. For a product kinetic kernel,

\[
\frac{k_-}{k_+}
=
\left(
\frac{k_{1,-}}{k_{1,+}}
\right)^{|E_\Gamma|};
\tag{FB15u}
\]

for the \(SU(2)\) factor \(e^{x\cos\theta}\), the one-link ratio is \(e^{-2x}\). On a regular lattice family with \(|E_\Gamma|\) and the number of spatial plaquettes proportional to volume and with fixed \(x\), the kernel factor alone makes (FB15t) deteriorate exponentially in spatial volume even though the exact pure kinetic edge (FB15g) does not. In infinite volume the interacting vacuum need not have any globally bounded density relative to product Haar measure.

This locates the remaining interaction problem more sharply. A local or block-conditional comparison is the viable next route: it should cover every admissible boundary-flux sector and remain uniform after gauge-covariant blocking to a fixed physical scale. The calculation does not prove that every possible global comparison must fail; it proves that this global minimum-to-maximum Holley--Stroock/Harnack certificate cannot supply the desired thermodynamic stopping quantity. [[two-scale-rg-descent-and-the-crossover-lemma]] records the summable-RG version of the obligation, while [[gauge-descent-flux-fisher-coercivity]] records the conditional Poincare/Dobrushin route.

[[gauge-cycle-innovation-filtration/inq|Gauge-Cycle Innovation Filtration]]
now supplies the complementary exact localization. Subgraph conditional
expectations preserve the physical invariant carrier; after a spanning tree,
the chord innovations resolve every closed gauge distinction. They diagonalize
this pure kinetic member and recover (FB15g), while the full magnetic sandwich
appears as a matrix of diagonal persistence and off-diagonal vacuum influence.
Thus the remaining problem is a uniform estimate on that actual
Perron-dressed matrix, not the construction of gauge-compatible coordinates.

## The bounded-solder theorem

Let \(D_{\mathrm{cyl}}\geq0\) be the self-adjoint generator of an independently constructed cylinder Dirichlet form on the same \(L^2(\nu_T)\) carrier. Let \(P_0\) be the common fixed-space projection, and suppose

\[
D_{\mathrm{cyl}}
\geq
\lambda_{\mathrm{cyl}}(I-P_0),
\qquad
\lambda_{\mathrm{cyl}}>0.
\tag{FB16}
\]

Choose \(\tau>0\) with reciprocal units to \(D_{\mathrm{cyl}}\), so that \(\tau D_{\mathrm{cyl}}\) is dimensionless. In the dimensionless invariant-link normalization used for the cylinder form, \(D_{\mathrm{cyl}}\), \(\lambda_{\mathrm{cyl}}\), and \(\tau\) are all dimensionless; \(\tau\) is a smoothing parameter, not Euclidean clock time. The bounded response

\[
B_\tau(D_{\mathrm{cyl}})
:=
I-e^{-\tau D_{\mathrm{cyl}}}
\tag{FB17}
\]

has the exact lower edge

\[
B_\tau(D_{\mathrm{cyl}})
\geq
\left(1-e^{-\tau\lambda_{\mathrm{cyl}}}\right)(I-P_0).
\tag{FB18}
\]

Assume the same-carrier bounded comparison

\[
\boxed{
I-P_T
\geq
\eta
B_\tau(D_{\mathrm{cyl}}),
\qquad
\eta>0.}
\tag{FB19}
\]

Put

\[
q
:=
\eta
\left(1-e^{-\tau\lambda_{\mathrm{cyl}}}\right).
\tag{FB19a}
\]

Then (FB18)--(FB19) imply \(P_T\leq(1-q)I\) on the vacuum complement. For \(0<q<1\), the exact logarithmic transfer relation gives

\[
\boxed{
H_T-E_0
\geq
\frac{\hbar c}{a_\tau}
\log\!\bigl((1-q)^{-1}\bigr)(I-P_0)
\geq
\frac{\hbar c}{a_\tau}q(I-P_0).}
\tag{FB20}
\]

Strict positivity of the finite Wilson transfer forces \(q<1\) whenever the vacuum complement is nonzero. All high-frequency quantities in (FB19) are bounded, so the mismatch in (FB14) has disappeared without imposing a representation cutoff. The theorem is noncircular only when \(\eta\) is proved from the transfer kernel, Euclidean action, or independently normalized RG comparison rather than defined from the unknown transfer edge. [[markov-edge-measure-solder/inq|Stationary edge-measure domination]] is a concrete sufficient certificate and is stable under tensor product, gauge restriction, and deterministic one-step blocking. The exponential is the safest primary bounded transform here because both sides of (FB19) are defects of self-adjoint Markov contractions and have the same fixed space. The resolvent transform \(\tau D_{\mathrm{cyl}}(I+\tau D_{\mathrm{cyl}})^{-1}\) is equally domain-safe but lacks that direct one-step interpretation.

The one-stage theorem is stronger than necessary. Since $P_T$ is positive,
$I-P_T^2\geq I-P_T$, so (FB19) also supplies the stage analysis

\[
L=\sqrt\eta\left(I-e^{-\tau D_{\mathrm{cyl}}}\right)^{1/2}.
\]

[[transported-response-observability-solder/inq|The transported-response
theorem]] pulls such analyses back through an ordered fixed-slab product.
It can force joint contraction even when each individual cylinder response
has additional zero modes, provided differently oriented stages form a
uniform lower frame on the complete initial vacuum complement. This does
not weaken the need for an action-derived $\eta$; it weakens only the
unnecessary demand that every one-step response be individually coercive.

[[directed-isometric-residue-completion/inq|The residue-completion theorem]]
then packages the Wilson survivor and all transported bounded defects into an
isometric chronological archive. That archive is an exact norm completion,
not yet a physical record or an alternative to the fixed-slab lower-frame
estimate.

For a regulator family, put

\[
q_{r,L}
:=
\eta_{r,L}
\left(1-e^{-\tau_r\lambda_{\mathrm{cyl},r,L}}\right).
\tag{FB20a}
\]

The invariant stopping condition is

\[
\boxed{
\liminf_{r}
\inf_L
\frac{\hbar c}{a_{\tau,r}\Lambda_{\mathrm{YM}}^{(\mathsf s)}}
\log\!\bigl((1-q_{r,L})^{-1}\bigr)
>0.}
\tag{FB21}
\]

A finite nonzero continuum energy requires \(-\log(1-q_{r,L})\) to be of order \(a_{\tau,r}\), equivalently \(q_{r,L}=O(a_{\tau,r})\) in the finite-rate regime. Specifically, the lower bound \(m_*\Lambda_{\mathrm{YM}}^{(\mathsf s)}\) follows from

\[
q_{r,L}
\geq
1-
\exp\!\left[
-\frac{a_{\tau,r}\Lambda_{\mathrm{YM}}^{(\mathsf s)}}{\hbar c}
\,m_*
\right]
\tag{FB22}
\]

uniformly in \(L\). The right side equals \(a_{\tau,r}\Lambda_{\mathrm{YM}}^{(\mathsf s)}m_*/(\hbar c)+O(a_{\tau,r}^2)\). A fixed positive raw one-step floor would instead correspond to an energy diverging as \(a_{\tau,r}^{-1}\). If a genuine temporal-continuum theorem identifies a dimensionless cylinder generator with a Kogut--Susskind kinetic term \(\kappa_{a_s,r}D_{\mathrm{cyl}}\), its natural small-step matching is

\[
\tau_r
=
\frac{a_{\tau,r}\kappa_{a_s,r}}{\hbar c}
+o(a_{\tau,r}),
\tag{FB24}
\]

with \(\eta_{r,L}\) tending to a positive normalized comparison coefficient. More generally the needed order may be distributed between \(\eta_{r,L}\) and \(\tau_r\), but both must be fixed from nonspectral regulator data before the transfer edge is inspected.

## Why the heat-kernel action is structurally cleaner

For a temporal group heat kernel, the representation coefficient is exactly \(e^{-\tau C_R}\). Its pure kinetic transfer logarithm is therefore exactly proportional to the invariant Laplacian at finite step. [[library/the-action-of-su-n-lattice-gauge-theory-in-terms-of-the-heat-kernel-on-the-group-manifold/inq|Menotti and Onofri]] introduced this action precisely as an alternative with a closer Hamiltonian relation.

This does not finish the problem. Magnetic multiplication does not commute with the kinetic heat semigroup, finite-step factorization introduces additional comparison terms, and universality plus continuum coercivity still have to be proved. But it reveals a serious regulator-design option: choose a transfer kernel whose global representation weights already have the desired local Casimir calculus, rather than demand an impossible full-carrier unbounded domination from the Wilson exponential.

## Copernican reading

At finite spacing, the primitive physical-time datum is the two-slice jump form (FB7). It operates on the difference between two complete slice presentations. The smooth flux Laplacian is a tangent generator on one slice. Their relationship is not equality of concepts; it is the bounded comparison (FB19), followed—if the limit exists—by rescaled form convergence to a local continuous-time generator.

This is the precise global-discrete/local-smooth pattern. A finite transition compares entire neighboring configurations through a bounded kernel. Local differential QFT appears only after the temporal and ultraviolet limits have been controlled. The mass gap is the surviving uniform logarithmic rate on the nonvacuum carrier, not a pixel size and not a high-representation cutoff.
