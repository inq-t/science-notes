# Soft Gaussian Gauge Blocking

Normalized Gaussian readouts of gauge classes preserve a Maxwell comparison through arbitrarily many blocking steps. Their accumulated noise is controlled by a geometric series in physical block widths, and the full observation inequality gives a uniform lower bound for each reverse conditional precision. This norm theorem is an exact Gaussian ultraviolet result; a separate holomorphic-symbol argument supplies spatial localization. Neither theorem establishes a nonlinear Yang--Mills bound or a physical mass gap.

## The carrier and the normalized law

Use the periodic cubical geometry and compatible averages of [[endpoint-averages-and-quadratic-ultraviolet-control|the endpoint-average theorem]]. Fix blocking factor \(L>1\) integer, dimension \(d\ge2\), physical meshes \(b_j=L^ja\), and enough blocks at every tested scale for its two-block patches.

Let

$$
H_j=\ker(d_{b_j}^{(0)})^*\cap
\{\text{constant one-forms}\}^{\perp},
\qquad
K_j=(d_{b_j}^{(1)})^*d_{b_j}^{(1)}|_{H_j}>0.
\tag{SG1}
$$

Here \(d^{(0)}\) is the scalar gradient and \(d^{(1)}\) the curl; their adjoints use the physical lattice metrics. The real inner product is \(b_j^d\) times the sum of bondwise Lie-algebra coefficient pairings. Thus \(H_j\) represents the gauge quotient with harmonic modes removed. Without that last removal, the starting massless Gaussian is not normalized.

Let \(Q_j:H_j\to H_{j+1}\) be volume averaging followed by the orthogonal coarse gauge projection. The endpoint-average theorem gives

$$
\|Q_j\|\le1,\qquad
Q_j^*K_{j+1}Q_j\le K_j,\qquad
K_j\le\frac{4d}{b_j^2}I.
\tag{SG2}
$$

The final bound follows from the periodic transverse symbol
\(4b_j^{-2}\sum_\mu\sin^2(p_\mu/2)\), with dimensionless lattice momentum \(p\). The average preserves spatial means and takes gradients to gradients. Intermediate gauge projections therefore do not change the induced quotient composition.

Start with \(X_0\sim\mathcal N(0,K_0^{-1})\) and set

$$
X_{j+1}=Q_jX_j+\xi_{j+1},\qquad
\xi_{j+1}\sim\mathcal N(0,\eta b_{j+1}^2I_{H_{j+1}}),
\quad \eta>0,
\tag{SG3}
$$

with independent noises, also independent of \(X_0\). This is a normalized kernel, so the enlarged law leaves the initial marginal exactly unchanged.

The covariance in (SG3) uses the **physical quotient metric**. In raw bond coordinates its covariance is \(\eta b_{j+1}^{2-d}P_{H_{j+1}}\), not independent per-bond variance \(\eta b_{j+1}^2\). It can be generated by independent raw noise with the former variance followed by projection. The quotient projection need not be spatially local.

## A bound that does not lose a factor at each step

Write \(T_{i:k}=Q_{k-1}\cdots Q_i\), \(T_{k:k}=I\). Exact Gaussian integration gives

$$
C_k:=\operatorname{Cov}(X_k)
=T_{0:k}K_0^{-1}T_{0:k}^*
+\eta\sum_{i=1}^k b_i^2T_{i:k}T_{i:k}^*.
\tag{SG4}
$$

The additive covariance recursion has a gauge-blocking precedent in [[library/the-classically-perfect-fixed-point-action-for-su3-gauge-theory/inq|DeGrand et al.]], equation (20). The block geometry and physical-metric estimates here are specified separately; their fixed-point calculation is not being cited as proof of (SG5).

For these regular averages, [[accumulated-readout-noise|the entire noise sum has an exact nearest-neighbor unprojected covariance]]. Its projection onto \(H_k\) need not be local. That result also gives the full inherited-Maxwell alias formula and the correct transverse inverse, isolating the remaining spatial estimate.

For completeness, energy contraction implies the inverse comparison: from
\(T^*K_kT\le K_0\), the operator \(K_k^{1/2}TK_0^{-1/2}\) is a contraction, hence \(TK_0^{-1}T^*\le K_k^{-1}\). Norm contraction separately bounds every noise term. Therefore

$$
C_k\le K_k^{-1}+
\frac{\eta b_k^2}{1-L^{-2}}I
\le\Gamma K_k^{-1},
\qquad
\Gamma=1+\frac{4d\eta}{1-L^{-2}}.
\tag{SG5}
$$

Inverting the positive matrices yields

$$
\boxed{P_k:=C_k^{-1}\ge\Gamma^{-1}K_k.}
\tag{SG6}
$$

The constant is independent of starting cutoff, volume, and completed depth. A stepwise comparison that repeatedly multiplies by a loss factor would miss this bound: noise injected far upstream has physical amplitude proportional to the square of its smaller block width. Unrolling the law before estimating retains that information.

## The reverse conditional response

At fixed coarse field \(X_{k+1}=B\), the Gaussian law of \(X_k=A\) has precision

$$
R_k=P_k+\frac1{\eta b_{k+1}^2}Q_k^*Q_k.
\tag{SG7}
$$

This operator acts on variations of the hidden **configuration**, not on clock-time states. Its inverse is independent of the conditioned value \(B\); that independence is specific to this Gaussian law.

Let \(c=c_{L,d}>0\) be the finite-patch constant from the endpoint-average theorem. Its full observation inequality says

$$
c\|A\|_{b_k}^2
\le\frac{2d}{L^2}b_{k+1}^2
\langle A,K_kA\rangle_{b_k}
+L^{-d}\|Q_kA\|_{b_{k+1}}^2.
\tag{SG8}
$$

Combining (SG6)--(SG8) gives

$$
\boxed{
R_k\ge\frac{c_{\rm soft}}{b_{k+1}^2}I,\qquad
c_{\rm soft}=
\min\!\left\{
\frac{cL^2}{2d\Gamma},
\frac{cL^d}{\eta}
\right\}>0.}
\tag{SG9}
$$

Thus the reverse conditional covariance has operator norm at most \(b_{k+1}^2/c_{\rm soft}\), uniformly in depth and volume. Gaussian Poincare then bounds conditional variance by this constant times the expected squared hidden gradient. Neither assertion implies exponential off-diagonal decay in a declared spatial metric.

[[local-completion-of-soft-gauge-conditioning|A local partial-divergence completion]] proves first-step exponential covariance and coarse-source response. [[multilevel-local-gauge-completion|The whole-chain enlargement]] supplies the actual terminal Schur precision, although its unweighted full-stack norm loses coercivity with depth. [[uniform-gaussian-conditional-locality|The accumulated locality theorem]] avoids that norm: exact gradient compatibility cancels the principal inverse pole, a common holomorphic strip controls the coefficients, and weighted terminal inversion yields depth-uniform spatial decay. The comparison (SG6) alone would not provide that result.

The observation term is essential. In an abstract two-dimensional example with \(0<\varepsilon\le1\), take

$$
K_f=\operatorname{diag}(1,\varepsilon^2),\quad
K_c=1,\quad Q(x,y)=\varepsilon y.
\tag{SG10}
$$

Both contractions in (SG2) hold, and \(\ker Q\) has unit stiffness. Yet \(K_f+\lambda Q^*Q=\operatorname{diag}(1,(1+\lambda)\varepsilon^2)\) has no uniform floor as \(\varepsilon\to0\). A hard-fiber estimate alone cannot justify soft conditioning; (SG8) controls the nearly invisible directions too.

## The compact gauge kernel returns this model only in a specified limit

[[compact-gauge-kernel-tangent-response|The compact-kernel calculation]] derives (SG3), at fixed finite lattice and bounded fine-field inputs, from a normalized \(SU(r)\) readout in near-identity scaling. In four dimensions its blocking coefficient \(k/\varepsilon^2\) gives \(\eta=1/k\) after the field and physical-volume normalizations are included.

That local limit is not uniform control of the full compact Wilson law, all large-field regions, or an unbounded number of blocking steps. A Gaussian approximation of a mode Hessian at fixed finite kernel concentration is not the same normalized law. Its apparent extra scatter stiffness must not be silently added to the original fine action.

Finally, this chain starts with massless Maxwell covariance. Every original correlation survives in the joint law. The uniform reverse floors describe ultraviolet conditional fluctuations; the retained infrared law and the [[inq|covariance-residue source and terminal estimates]] still decide whether a physical gap follows.
