# A Register of Globality

The word *global* names several non-equivalent mathematical properties. Radical Copernicanism becomes precise only after each use declares whether it concerns an object, section, valuation, state, trivialization, invariant, charge, completion, or observer; failure of one of these does not imply failure of the others.

## Distinct meanings

| Term | Strict question |
|---|---|
| **global object** | Does compatible descent produce an object over the whole base? |
| **global section** | Does a projection, sheaf, or bundle admit a section over the whole base? |
| **global trivialization** | Is the object globally isomorphic to a product or constant family? |
| **global valuation** | Is there one value assignment compatible with every declared context? |
| **global state** | Is there a normalized positive functional on the declared global algebra? |
| **global weight** | Is there a positive extended-valued functional with the declared normality, faithfulness, or semifiniteness properties? |
| **global invariant class** | Is some isomorphism, Morita, outer-action, or cohomology class defined independently of presentation? |
| **global charge** | Does a symmetry and its boundary/integrability data define a charge for the whole system? |
| **global spacetime completion** | Do local observations determine a unique maximal or otherwise selected spacetime? |
| **global observer** | Is there a physical perspective whose accessible algebra contains every declared fact? |

These questions have different source and target categories. The same answer word—*yes*, *no*, or *obstructed*—does not make their mathematical content interchangeable.

## Important non-implications

Effective descent does not imply a global trivialization or preferred section. A global object can possess nontrivial monodromy. Conversely, the absence of a global section can be the defining property of a perfectly good global presheaf.

The Kochen--Specker obstruction to a context-independent valuation therefore does not imply that there is no global algebra, no global state, no cosmos, or no sufficient reason. It forbids one specific kind of classical pointing. Likewise, type III does not imply the absence of normal states or faithful normal semifinite weights; a $\sigma$-finite type-III algebra admits a faithful normal state.

A reconstructed global gauge or field algebra does not by itself select a unique global spacetime completion. A global charge requires more than a global symmetry expression: boundary conditions, admissible generators, and integrability enter the construction.

## Whole and local registers

The phrase **whole register** should name the carrier on which cosmos-wide compatibility or closure is posed. The phrase **local fact register** should name the carrier on which a contextual readout and obtained value are represented. Neither phrase should imply that its object is a spatial whole or part before geometry has been realized.

This register is a **[DEFINITION PROPOSAL]** for an eventual refinement of [[algebra/local-global-individuation|Local--Global Individuation]], with [[sufficient-reason/facticity-and-pointing|Facticity and Pointing]] retaining ownership of factual selection. Its main use is negative but productive: it blocks arguments of the form

$$
\text{no global }X
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\text{no global }Y
$$

until a theorem relating $X$ and $Y$ has been supplied.

**Status.** Most distinctions in the table are **[STANDARD]**. “Whole register,” “local fact register,” and “global observer” are programme-level **[DEFINITION PROPOSALS]**. Any equivalence between two rows is an **[OPEN CONSTRUCTION]** unless a specialist theorem supplies it.
