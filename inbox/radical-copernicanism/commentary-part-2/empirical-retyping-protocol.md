# From Ontological Retyping to an Empirical Signature

A philosophical retyping becomes empirically meaningful only after a forward map carries its mathematical invariants into observables with declared nuisance parameters and comparison models. The programme may seek cosmic acceleration without a new dark-energy field. It must eventually address distances, expansion history, growth, lensing, and perturbations, while each present test states which sectors are predicted, imported, or not yet executable.

## What must be constructed

For any proposed global-to-local explanation, require the chain

$$
\text{ontological carrier}
\xrightarrow{F}
\text{effective geometry or source}
\xrightarrow{\mathcal O}
\text{observable distribution}
\xrightarrow{\mathcal L}
\text{data likelihood}.
$$

The programme must construct or explicitly assume:

- the realization map $F$ and its normalization;
- the effective background and perturbation equations;
- the observable map $\mathcal O$ for the actual survey quantities;
- nuisance, calibration, selection, and covariance models;
- a declared parameter measure or prior when Bayesian evidence is used; and
- a versioned numerical implementation with limiting-case checks.

Only after this chain is fixed does the theory possess a measurable signature rather than an evocative shape.

## What may then be measured

A rigid predicted point or lower-dimensional locus should be tested as a constrained model. Under an ambient absolutely continuous posterior with no atomic or singular component, the probability of an exact point—and ordinarily of a lower-dimensional locus—is zero. A separately specified constrained model may nevertheless have nonzero posterior model probability. Ambient posterior density at a point is coordinate-dependent.

Suitable comparisons include:

- the likelihood evaluated under the fully specified constrained model;
- a likelihood-ratio statistic against a declared alternative;
- a Bayes factor with a separately declared prior on the constrained model, or a pushforward prior from its own parameterization;
- a posterior predictive check; or
- a preregistered covariance-weighted discrepancy from the predicted curve.

A generic CPL posterior is not a substitute for directly fitting the CST forward model. A local tangent in $(w_0,w_a)$ is a posterior consistency diagnostic after the direct fit, not the primary prediction.

## The useful “Mercury moment” lesson

The anomaly taxonomy from the first commentary is worth preserving:

- a **Michelson--Morley anomaly** removes an expected effect;
- a **Mercury anomaly** is a rigid residual explained by a deeper structure;
- an **eclipse test** checks a striking new consequence away from the original fit.

The programme should seek all three kinds, but label them separately. For CST-B2, any unit-branch prediction is parameter-free only after $\nu=1$, the matching law, crossing selection, matter input, residual sector, and nuisance treatment have been fixed independently. It is not a prediction of CST in general.

The immediate empirical value of radical Copernicanism is methodological: it motivates testing whether acceleration is a consequence of the observation/realization map rather than an additional field. The explanation succeeds only if the resulting forward predictions survive the same observational standards as the field models they would replace.
