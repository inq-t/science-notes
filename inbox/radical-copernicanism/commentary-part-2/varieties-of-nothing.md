# Varieties of Nothing

“Nothing” is philosophically productive only when it marks a declared absence or indiscernibility, not when it collapses zero, emptiness, trace, vacuum, kernel, fibre, torsor, gauge orbit, missing section, and absence of fact into one object. The programme should preserve these null structures precisely because their differences may carry the ontology being sought.

## A null-structure register

| Mathematical expression | What is absent | What may remain |
|---|---|---|
| zero element or zero morphism | a value or map contribution | the ambient object and its operations |
| empty initial object | elements of a set-like carrier | its universal mapping property |
| terminal object | nothing by itself; in some process categories it represents discarding | a unique map from every object |
| kernel | distinctions visible after a specified map | internal structure on the killed directions |
| fibre of a restriction map | a unique antecedent | a convex subset of an affine hyperplane of compatible antecedents |
| quotient or gauge orbit | a privileged representative | stabilizers, isotropy, and orbit structure |
| unpointed torsor | a preferred origin | a free transitive group action |
| no global section | a compatible global pointing | a well-defined global sheaf, bundle, or presheaf |
| vacuum state | excitations relative to chosen dynamics | correlations, entanglement, and modular structure |
| tracial state | modular bias for a finite normalized trace | nontrivial algebraic observables |
| semifinite trace on a $\mathrm{II}_\infty$ factor | finite normalization on the identity | a trace finite on a sufficiently large ideal |
| type-III algebra | a nonzero normal semifinite trace | abundant normal states and weights |
| absence of fact | an obtained value and record | an observable law or probability assignment |

These rows are not metaphors for one another without explicit maps.

## Three recurring corrections

First, a trace is not generally unique. A $\mathrm{II}_1$ factor has a unique normalized trace, whereas a $\mathrm{II}_\infty$ factor has a semifinite trace but no tracial state normalized on the identity.

Second, a fibre of the restriction map on states is not automatically a torsor. A torsor requires a specified group action that is free and transitive. A many-to-one restriction merely supplies a family of antecedents.

Third, the absence of a conditional expectation or operator-valued weight does not prove that “the past is a torsor.” It proves that one proposed canonical retraction or comparison is unavailable under the stated hypotheses.

For an inclusion $N\subset M$, the relative commutant

$$
N'\cap M=\{x\in M:xn=nx\text{ for every }n\in N\}
$$

records elements of $M$ commuting with $N$. It is neither the kernel nor a fibre of the restriction map on states.

## The useful philosophical residue

For an inclusion $N\subset M$ and restriction $r:S(M)\to S(N)$, one precise relational analogue is the indistinguishability class

$$
[\omega]_N
:=\{\varphi\in S(M):\varphi|_N=\omega|_N\}.
$$

Equivalently, a declared readout $\mathcal O:X\to Y$ induces the kernel-pair relation $x\sim_{\mathcal O}y$ exactly when $\mathcal O(x)=\mathcal O(y)$. This should be called a kernel only in an additive or pointed setting where an actual zero object and linear map have been supplied. It is relational nothingness, not nonbeing. What is indiscernible to one local fact register may still possess stabilizer, cocycle, correlation, or obstruction data in the whole register.
