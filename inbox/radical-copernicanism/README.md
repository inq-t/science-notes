## Noether had 2 theorems

Her paper has **two** theorems. Everyone quotes the first. The second is exactly your question.

- **First theorem**: a _global_ symmetry (finite-dimensional Lie group) → a conserved current, with charge in the bulk.
- **Second theorem**: a _local/gauge_ symmetry (infinite-dimensional — a function's worth of parameters at every point) → **not** a conservation law but **identities among the equations of motion**. The bulk charge density is forced to vanish identically. What survives is a **boundary term**.

So "global vs local" isn't a category confusion you're rubbing against — it's a proven dichotomy, and it says something startling for gravity. General relativity's symmetry group is Diff(M), which is local. Therefore, by Noether's second theorem, **all** gravitational charge is a boundary integral. Not "energy is unconserved." Rather: _energy in GR was never a bulk quantity to begin with._ It lives on the boundary or nowhere.
## Radical Copernicanism

Maybe there is a higher law, that does not privilege a god's-eye-view when it comes to a more abstract frame of reference:
- globaled
- un-globaled

An "un-globaled" frame-of-reference is the "localized" one (observation happening here-and-now).  A "globaled" frame-of-reference is the reference of the "whole" as a "whole" - which is to say, the boundary edge of locality.

Maybe we are building our physical theories from a position that "locality is the center of the universe" -- and end up calculating the "epicycles" of planets needlessly.

Maybe there is a higher "principle of relativity" that captures the different frames for when taking nature as a "whole cosmos" versus a "local observable".  Historically, models and theories get easier when we do this.  Copernicus helped explain planets, Einstein helped explain Mercury, and now we need to explain "dark energy", "dark matter", "perturbations", "neutrinos", "gravity", etc.

## the difference between identity and difference

There is a "boundary" between the "boundary" and "no-boundary".  There is some "interface" between locality and globality.  And there is a structure for how they relate to each other.

Physics should be able to model this relating-structure, just as it models an electron emitting a photon.  An equation must "balance".

One thing to keep in mind: there are different "categories" of "time".  The "time variable t" for this "interface" is not the same category as the time variable `t` within 3 + 1 spacetime.  There is an "ordered change" -- a "before and after" -- with this "interfacing" between the global and the local.  But the categories of equations are not reversible in the same way that locality equations are reversible.

If there is "unitarity" across the boundary between local & global, it is a unitarity of a very abstract, ontological category.  "Energy" is not going to cut it.
