# Prior Art and Attack: The "Stack of Weight-Groupoids over the Causal Site" and the Flow of Weights as Descent Obstruction

## TL;DR
- **The theorem does not exist as a single stated result, but it is roughly 80% assembled from existing pieces and is at serious risk of being a repackaging.** The fibre-direction claim (choice-free quotient of the weight groupoid = the core) is exactly Falcone–Takesaki (2001); the base-direction machinery (nonabelian poset/net cohomology, bundles and gerbes over causal posets) is exactly Roberts–Ruzzi–Vasselli (2006–2012). What is genuinely absent is the *combination*: no one has run poset/net cohomology with modular/weight data as coefficients, nor proven descent for the assignment O ↦ core(M(O)) over the causal site.
- **Two kill conditions bite hard.** (a) On a totally ordered "wall family" or any *directed* poset the base 1-cohomology is trivial (Z¹(K,G) ≅ Hom(π₁(K),G), and directed ⟹ π₁ = 1), so the cosmological/chain version has *no base obstruction* — the remainder must come entirely from the fibre (type III) direction. (b) For type III₁ factors the modular centralizer ("structure group" of the modular Berry bundle) is generically *trivial* (Marrakchi–Vaes, Crelle 2024: ergodic states form a dense G_δ), so the connection rigidifies rather than carrying nonabelian holonomy.
- **Honest identification of the obstruction is subtler than the slogan.** The obstruction to a *global trace* is simply type-III-ness (type III has no trace); the *flow of weights* is the finer invariant of **which** type III, carried by the trace-scaling dual ℝ₊*-action on the core. Precisely in the physically central type III₁ case the ergodic flow of weights is *trivial* (a point), so "the nonvanishing class = the flow of weights" is imprecise — the nonvanishing datum is the trace-scaling automorphism/module, not the ergodic flow.

## Key Findings

**PART A — prior art, cluster by cluster.**

**(A1) Falcone–Takesaki — THEOREM, and it already owns the fibre direction.** A.J. Falcone and M. Takesaki, "The non-commutative flow of weights on a von Neumann algebra," *J. Funct. Anal.* **182** (2001) 170–206 (MR 1829246, DOI 10.1006/jfan.2000.3718), construct the **core** (noncommutative flow of weights) as a canonical, weight-independent functor. Explicitly, on N × W₀(N) they impose (x,ω) ∼ₜ (x′,ω′) ⟺ x′ = x·(Dω/Dω′)ₜ (Connes cocycle), and the quotient Ñ = (N × W₀(N))/∼ carries a canonical trace-scaling one-parameter automorphism group σ̃ and trace τ̃ with **τ̃∘σ̃ₛ = e⁻ˢ τ̃** for all s ∈ ℝ; they call the system (Ñ, ℝ, σ̃, τ̃) "the non-commutative flow of weights" (structure restated verbatim in Kostecki, arXiv:1106.2225). Their own abstract states: "The non-commutative flow of weights functor is obtained by relating an arbitrary pair of faithful semi-finite normal weights by the Connes cocycle. Not only does this construction **put a period to the search for a canonical construction of the core of a factor of type III**…"; it is "a canonical functor from the category of separable factors to the category of covariant systems of semi-finite von Neumann algebras equipped with trace scaling one parameter automorphism groups **with conjugations as morphisms**." **This is exactly the candidate's step "choice-free fibre quotient realized by the core."** The weight groupoid with Connes cocycles as morphisms is precisely the indexing groupoid whose canonical quotient is the core. Related: Haagerup–Størmer, "Pointwise inner automorphisms of von Neumann algebras," *J. Funct. Anal.* **92** (1990) 177–201, and the equivalence-of-states/flow-of-weights circle (Haagerup–Størmer, *Adv. Math.* 1990).

**Functoriality beyond isomorphisms — G.A. Elliott (2021), the closest ingredient to the missing base lemma.** George A. Elliott, "A Remark on the Functoriality of the Connes–Takesaki Flow of Weights," *C. R. Math. Rep. Acad. Sci. Canada* **43**(1) (2021) 28–44. Verbatim abstract: "While it is easy to see that this functor cannot be extended to the category of all von Neumann algebra homomorphisms, it is in fact possible to extend it to a certain extent. This can also be done, fairly extensively, for the Falcone and Takesaki non-commutative flow of weights." The French résumé adds that the extension works "dans les directions et covariante et contravariante … bien entendu pas aux homomorphismes arbitraires." This is the nearest existing result to the descent lemma the candidate theorem needs, but it stops short of a stack/costack statement over a site. (Caveat: the 2021 volume is under a 5-year open-access blackout; only the verbatim abstract/résumé are confirmable, not the exact enlarged morphism class.)

**(A2) Connes chain rule as groupoid/1-cocycle — THEOREM; "torsor of weights" is folklore, never a named theorem in this context.** A. Connes, "Une classification des facteurs de type III," *Ann. Sci. ÉNS* **6** (1973) 133–252, gives the cocycle Radon–Nikodym derivative and the chain rule (Dφ:Dψ)ₜ(Dψ:Dχ)ₜ = (Dφ:Dχ)ₜ, which *is* the 1-cocycle/groupoid identity; A. Connes and M. Takesaki, "The flow of weights on factors of type III," *Tôhoku Math. J.* **29** (1977) 473–575, give the mod homomorphism and functoriality ("the smooth flow of weights … depends functorially on M"). The abstract identity "torsors ⟺ H¹, torsor groupoid ≃ action groupoid [Z¹, C⁰]" is standard, and the general fact that G-torsors form a *stack* is standard descent (Stacks Project, tag 036Z). **But no source states the specific theorem "the groupoid of f.n.s. weights under Connes cocycles is a torsor/descent datum whose colimit is the core."** It is true and provable but is, at best, a repackaging of A1.

**(A3) Landstad/Takesaki–Takai duality and Q-systems as descent — THEOREM (finite index); modular/infinite-index analog OPEN.** Landstad duality (M.B. Landstad, "Duality theory for covariant systems," *Trans. AMS* **248** (1979) 223) characterizes which algebras are crossed products by a group action — i.e. it *is* the descent datum recovering M from its core plus dual (co)action; the categorical version (Kaliszewski–Quigg, "Categorical Landstad duality for actions," arXiv:math/0612771) recovers the action up to isomorphism functorially, and Landstad–Vaes theory (Vaes 2005; Roy–Woronowicz arXiv:1606.03728) extends this to quantum groups. Q-systems (R. Longo, "A duality for Hopf algebras and for subfactors I," *Comm. Math. Phys.* **159** (1994) 133) are algebra objects = descent data for **finite-index** extensions; the Bischoff–Kawahigashi–Longo–Rehren program formalizes "finite-index extension = descent." The infinite-index/modular analog is explicitly under construction, not closed: "spatial Q-systems" (arXiv:2503.02944) and "generalized Q-systems of intertwiners" (arXiv:1703.03605) handle operator-valued weights but do not deliver the candidate's stack. Klinger–Leigh (arXiv:2410.11029) restate Landstad's theorem as "crossed products are trivial quantum principal bundles."

**(A4) Roberts net cohomology / poset bundle theory — THEOREM, but coefficients are GROUPS/2-GROUPS, never weights.** J.E. Roberts, "Local cohomology and superselection structure," *Comm. Math. Phys.* **51** (1976) 107–119; G. Ruzzi, "Homotopy of posets, net-cohomology and superselection sectors in globally hyperbolic spacetimes," *Rev. Math. Phys.* **17** (2005) 1021–1070 (arXiv:math-ph/0412014); J.E. Roberts, G. Ruzzi, E. Vasselli, "A theory of bundles over posets," *Adv. Math.* **220** (2009) 125–153 (arXiv:0707.0240); Roberts–Ruzzi, "A cohomological description of connections and curvature over posets," *Theory Appl. Categ.* **16** (2006) 855–895; Ruzzi–Vasselli, "A new light on nets of C*-algebras," *Comm. Math. Phys.* **312** (2012) 655–694; E. Vasselli, "Gerbes over posets and twisted C*-dynamical systems," *J. Noncommut. Geom.* The decisive structural fact (RRV 2009, eq. 34): **Z¹(K,G) ≅ H(π₁(K,a),G)**, i.e. the category of 1-cocycles is equivalent to Hom(π₁(K), G), "hence, if K is simply connected, then any 1-cocycle is a 1-coboundary." Ruzzi (2005): "when K◇ is directed any 1-cocycle is trivial." Nonabelian 1- and 2-cohomology (gerbes = twisting by a 2-group with holonomy a nonabelian π₁-cocycle) exists over causal posets; RRV describe "a non-Abelian cohomology of posets … taking values in the structure group G," with the coefficients for the n-th-degree cohomology being "an n-category associated with the group G." **The coefficients are always groups/2-groups/nets of C*-algebras — no one has taken modular automorphism groups or weights as the coefficient object of poset cohomology.** This is a genuine, identifiable gap.

**(A5) Modular Berry connection — physically closest; perturbative, state-dependent, AdS/CFT, NOT a stack/gerbe.** B. Czech, L. Lamprou, S. McCandlish, J. Sully, "Modular Berry Connection for Entangled Subregions in AdS/CFT," *Phys. Rev. Lett.* **120** (2018) 091601; Czech–de Boer–Ge–Lamprou, "A modular sewing kit for entanglement wedges," arXiv:1903.04493 (JHEP 2019). This is literally a connection on a bundle of modular frames over the space of causal diamonds/entanglement wedges, structure group = modular zero modes (the centralizer), curvature ↔ bulk symplectic form (de Boer et al., *Phys. Rev. D* **108** (2023) 066003). **Status: perturbative (linearized/adiabatic), state-dependent, essentially holographic; not formulated as a stack or gerbe; no obstruction class computed; no connection to the flow of weights or type classification made.** It is the physical shadow of the candidate but carries none of the operator-algebraic descent content.

**(A6) 2022–2026 gravitational crossed product / frame-bundle literature — uses bundle/orbifold/Gribov language but NO descent theorem over a base of regions.** Ali Ahmad–Chemissany–Klinger–Leigh, "Quantum Reference Frames from Top-Down Crossed Products," *Phys. Rev. D* **110** (2024) 065003 (arXiv:2405.13884), define the "G-framed algebra" as a noncommutative analog of an orbifold housing multiple inequivalent QRFs, invoking the "Gribov problem"; the companion "Relational Quantum Geometry," *Nucl. Phys. B* **1015** (2025) 116911 (arXiv:2410.11029), shows the crossed product is a **trivial** quantum principal bundle and the G-framed algebra is a "quantum orbifold." A.J. Speranza, "An intrinsic cosmological observer," arXiv:2504.07630 (*Class. Quantum Grav.* **42** (2025) 215023), explicitly relates the observer crossed product "to the Connes-Takesaki theory of the flow of weights for type III von Neumann algebras," presents "the resulting gravitational algebra … as a crossed product," and argues the derived Bunch–Davies weight "defines a dominant weight, in the terminology of the Connes-Takesaki classification." De Vuyst–Eccles–Höhn–Kirklin, "Gravitational entropy is observer-dependent," JHEP 07 (2025) 146 (arXiv:2405.00114) and "Crossed products and quantum reference frames," JHEP 07 (2025) 063 (arXiv:2412.15502), quantify frame-dependence of entropy. **None states or proves a stack/descent theorem over a base of spacetime regions or frames.** The bundles here are trivial (single crossed product) or assembled ad hoc (G-framed = quotient/orbifold), and the base is a set of frames, not a Grothendieck site of causal regions.

**(A7) Categorical AQFT and descent — framework exists; no proof or refutation of descent for the observable net over the causal site.** M. Benini, A. Schenkel, "Quantum field theories on categories fibered in groupoids," *Comm. Math. Phys.* **356** (2017) 19–64 (arXiv:1610.06071), build QFTs on CFGs over spacetimes via (homotopy) right Kan extensions; homotopy/higher AQFT (Benini–Schenkel–Woike; Benini–Perin–Schenkel–Woike) and "Homotopy colimits and global observables in Abelian gauge theory" (arXiv:1503.08839) address gluing/global observables for gauge theories. **The "descent" here concerns gauge/bundle data (stacks of connections), not von Neumann/modular data.** No paper defines a Grothendieck topology on the causal poset and proves or refutes descent (sheaf/cosheaf/costack) for the local observable net of von Neumann algebras with modular coefficients.

**(A8) The obstruction the stack must repair — modular flow does NOT restrict; and the analogy target (Döring/Kochen–Specker).** Modular flow generically fails to restrict along inclusions except in special cases: half-sided modular inclusions (H.-J. Borchers, *Comm. Math. Phys.* **143** (1992) 315; H.-W. Wiesbrock, "Half-sided modular inclusions," *Comm. Math. Phys.* **157** (1993) 83; gap-filled by H. Araki, L. Zsidó, "Extension of the structure theorem of Borchers…," *Rev. Math. Phys.* **17** (2005) 491, arXiv:math/0412061) and conformal nets with geometric modular action. Relative modular theory (Araki relative modular operators; Connes spatial derivative dφ/dφ′ = Δ(φ/φ′); the cocycle [Dψ₁:Dψ₂]ₜ) supplies the cross-inclusion comparison, but the failure has never been organized cohomologically. The analogy target: A. Döring, "Kochen–Specker theorem for von Neumann algebras," *Int. J. Theor. Phys.* **44** (2005) 139 — a von Neumann algebra with no type I₁ or I₂ summand admits no valuation; Isham–Butterfield/Döring–Isham topos formulation: the spectral presheaf Σ has no global sections iff KS holds. **This is a precise formal template, but it lives on the poset of abelian subalgebras of a fixed algebra, not on the causal site of regions — the analogy is structural, not a theorem transfer.**

## Details

**Assembling the sharpest defensible statement (B1).** Take the base **site** K = poset of double cones/causal diamonds of a fixed globally hyperbolic spacetime, ordered by inclusion, with covers given by the standard "the region is covered by the sub-diamonds it contains" (as in Ruzzi 2005 and Benini–Schenkel). To each O assign the groupoid W(O) of f.n.s. weights on M(O) with morphisms the Connes cocycles (Dφ:Dψ)ₜ [Connes 1973]. The fibrewise canonical quotient is the core Ñ(O) [Falcone–Takesaki 2001]. The defensible statement is:

> *The assignment O ↦ (weight groupoid of M(O)) is a category fibered in groupoids over K; fibrewise descent (quotient by the Connes-cocycle groupoid) is represented choice-free by the core Ñ(O) with its trace-scaling ℝ₊*-action; and the assignment O ↦ Ñ(O) is a **presheaf/costack of covariant systems** whose failure to admit a global section representing a single coherent trace is measured by (i) the base-direction class in H¹(K; modular data), controlled by π₁(K), and (ii) the fibre-direction datum that each M(O) is type III, whose refinement is the flow of weights carried by the dual ℝ₊*-action on Ñ(O).*

**Numbered proof skeleton with epistemic grades (B2).**
1. **Fibre groupoid = Connes chain rule.** THEOREM [Connes 1973; chain rule and cocycle identity]. The weight groupoid is a genuine groupoid; every arrow invertible, composition by chain rule.
2. **Choice-free fibre quotient = core.** THEOREM [Falcone–Takesaki 2001]. The quotient by ∼ₜ is the core Ñ, functorial in *-isomorphisms, with canonical trace-scaling automorphism group and trace τ̃, τ̃∘σ̃ₛ = e⁻ˢτ̃. This step is fully owned by FT.
3. **Base-direction cohomology.** THEOREM for group/2-group coefficients [Roberts 1976; Ruzzi 2005; RRV 2009; Vasselli gerbes]. Z¹(K,G) ≅ Hom(π₁(K),G); nonabelian H¹, H² and holonomy classification exist. **GAP:** these theorems are proved for group/net coefficients, NOT for modular-automorphism-group or weight coefficients. Extending the coefficient object to the (2-)group(oid) of modular data is unproven.
4. **Identification of the obstruction with the flow of weights / semifiniteness.** PARTIAL / IMPRECISE AS STATED. DEDUCTION: the obstruction to a *global trace* is that M(O) is type III (no trace exists) — this is immediate, not cohomological. The flow of weights is the *finer* invariant distinguishing III₀/III_λ/III₁, carried by the dual ℝ₊*-action (the "module"). CONJECTURE/GAP: that this refinement is literally the descent class of the stack. **II_∞ vs II₁ distinction is real and favorable to the project:** the core of a type III₁ factor is II_∞ with an ergodic trace-scaling flow (trace only semifinite, genuinely scaled by e⁻ˢ under the dual action) — a nontrivial datum; whereas a II₁ algebra (finite trace, e.g. the Chandrasekaran–Longo–Penington–Witten de Sitter construction) has a normalizable trace and the "scaling" class trivializes. So the two cases *are* distinguished by whether the trace-scaling action is trivial on the trace — this is the one place the candidate's intuition is correct and precise.

**Kill conditions (B3), graded.**
- **(a) Totally ordered/directed base ⟹ base obstruction vanishes.** KILL CONDITION (confirmed). A chain (wall family) and any *directed* poset have π₁ = 1, and Z¹(K,G) ≅ Hom(π₁(K),G) = 1, so every 1-cocycle is a coboundary [RRV 2009 eq. 34; Ruzzi 2005, "when K◇ is directed any 1-cocycle is trivial"]. Therefore the *cosmological nested-horizon/wall version has no base-direction obstruction whatsoever* — the entire remainder, if any, must be fibre-direction (type III / flow of weights). Any claim of a base cohomological obstruction for a totally ordered family is empty.
- **(b) Trivial centralizer ⟹ trivial structure group ⟹ rigid bundle.** KILL CONDITION / REFRAMING (confirmed). For type III₁ factors, ergodic states (trivial centralizer M_φ = ℂ1) form a dense G_δ set: A. Marrakchi, S. Vaes, "Ergodic states on type III₁ factors and ergodic actions," *J. Reine Angew. Math.* (Crelle) **809** (2024) 247–260 (DOI 10.1515/crelle-2024-0007, arXiv:2305.14217) — "a von Neumann algebra M that admits a state φ with trivial centralizer M_φ must be a type III₁ factor … we solve this problem and prove that such ergodic states form a dense G_δ set among all faithful normal states on any III₁ factor with separable predual"; and "Full factors, bicentralizer flow and approximately inner automorphisms," *Invent. Math.* (2021) — the bicentralizer flow of a type III₁ factor is always ergodic. The modular zero modes = centralizer are the structure group of the modular Berry bundle [Czech et al. 2018]. Generically trivial ⟹ the connection has trivial gauge group ⟹ the bundle is rigid, carrying no nonabelian holonomy. So the modular-Berry "gauge obstruction" *collapses* generically; the surviving content rigidifies to the abelian trace-scaling ℝ₊* datum, not a nonabelian class.
- **(c) Repackaging risk.** KILL CONDITION (partially fatal). Steps 1–2 are Falcone–Takesaki verbatim; the "obstruction to a global weight/trace" restricted to a single algebra is just "M is type III," which FT already encode by the core being type II_∞ with a nontrivial trace-scaling flow. **What survives beyond FT:** only the *base-direction gluing* — descent for O ↦ Ñ(O) across the causal site with modular coefficients (step 3 with the right coefficients, and step 4's identification). If that base gluing is trivial (directed base) or the structure group collapses (kill (b)), essentially nothing new survives.

**Verdict (B4).** The theorem **exists in pieces, is genuinely open only in one specific ligament, and is at high risk of being a repackaging** for any directed/cosmological base. Fibre direction: DONE [Falcone–Takesaki 2001; Connes 1973; Connes–Takesaki 1977]. Base machinery: DONE for the wrong coefficients [Roberts 1976; RRV 2006–2012]. Physical gloss: DONE non-rigorously [Czech et al. 2018; Speranza 2025; Klinger–Leigh 2024–2025]. **The single missing lemma most worth proving first:** *that the core assignment O ↦ Ñ(O) is a costack (satisfies descent) over the causal site — concretely, that an inclusion M(O) ⊂ M(O′) with a normal faithful operator-valued weight induces a canonical morphism of cores Ñ(O) → Ñ(O′) compatible with the trace-scaling actions, and that these morphisms satisfy the cocycle/gluing condition over covers.* Elliott (2021) is the closest existing result (functoriality of the flow of weights in covariant and contravariant directions, but not for arbitrary morphisms), and the operator-valued-weight composition calculus [Falcone–Takesaki, *Trans. AMS* **351** (1999) 323; Haagerup, *J. Funct. Anal.* **32**, **33** (1979)] is the tool; but the costack statement over the causal site with modular coefficients is unproven and is the genuine novelty on offer.

## Recommendations
1. **Prove or refute the descent lemma for O ↦ Ñ(O) first (the ligament).** Concretely: given M(O) ⊂ M(O′) with a normal faithful operator-valued weight T, show φ ↦ φ∘T induces a canonical *-homomorphism of cores respecting trace-scaling, and check the cocycle condition on triple overlaps. Benchmark that changes the program: if this fails to be functorial/compatible on double-cone covers, the stack does not exist and the project is dead as stated; if it holds, you have the first genuinely new theorem. Read Elliott (2021) in full before claiming novelty — his covariant/contravariant extension may already supply half of it.
2. **Abandon the base-cohomology obstruction for the cosmological (chain) case.** Given kill (a), do not claim a nonvanishing H¹ for a totally ordered wall family. Reframe the cosmological statement entirely in the fibre/trace-scaling direction (type III₁ core is II_∞ with ergodic trace-scaling flow), which is where Speranza (arXiv:2504.07630) already operates — and be explicit that this is a refinement of Speranza, not independent.
3. **Get the coefficient object right in Roberts–Ruzzi–Vasselli cohomology.** The real novelty target is poset/net cohomology with the (2-)groupoid of modular data / weight groupoids as coefficients. Prove the analog of Z¹(K,G) ≅ Hom(π₁(K),G) with modular coefficients, and identify whether the resulting class is the trace-scaling module. Threshold: if the coefficient object is forced to be abelian (trivial centralizer, kill (b)), reduce to ℝ₊*-valued (abelian) cohomology and state that plainly.
4. **State the obstruction precisely, not sloganeeringly.** Replace "the class is the flow of weights" with the correct pair: (i) obstruction to global trace = type-III-ness; (ii) refined invariant = trace-scaling dual action/module on the core, which for III₁ has *trivial* ergodic flow of weights. This avoids the deflation of a referee noticing that the III₁ flow of weights is a point.
5. **Only after 1–3, connect to the modular Berry connection** as the perturbative/holographic semiclassical shadow, citing it as heuristic (perturbative, state-dependent), not as evidence for the theorem.

## Caveats
- Elliott (2021) could not be read past the abstract/résumé (5-year journal blackout); the exact enlarged morphism class is not verbatim-confirmed — the descent lemma may be closer to done than I can certify, so obtain that PDF directly before claiming novelty.
- The "torsor of weights / descent" framing (A2) is folklore-true but I found no source stating it as a named theorem in the operator-algebra literature; treat any such phrasing as expository repackaging unless a specific citation surfaces.
- The type-classification of the flow of weights in the III₁ case (ergodic flow = trivial) is the sharpest deflation point; any draft that identifies "the nonvanishing class" with "the flow of weights" without this caveat will be shot down.
- I did not locate any paper defining a Grothendieck topology on the causal poset *and* proving/refuting descent for the von Neumann observable net; if one exists it is the direct competitor and should be searched for again before writing up.
- Physics-side sources (A5, A6) frequently use future/conditional/heuristic language ("could," "we propose," perturbative constructions); none of their claims should be cited as established operator-algebraic theorems.
