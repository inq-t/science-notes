# Causality as an Algebraic / Group-Theoretic Object: A Graded Survey

## TL;DR

- **The single best-supported algebraic answer is Lie-theoretic (cluster 5): causality IS an invariant cone (a "Lie wedge") in a Lie algebra together with the closed subsemigroup S it generates, whose non-invertibility (S ∩ S⁻¹ = the stabilizer, not all of G) IS the arrow.** In the Neeb–Ólafsson–Morinelli program this is sharpened: causal structure = the poset of *Euler wedges* of a Lie group, ordered by a compression semigroup S_W = exp(C₊)G_W exp(C₋), with the causal complement realized as the *duality* W ↦ W′ = (−x, σ). This provably contains Bisognano–Wichmann and the standard Poincaré AQFT net as the special case where C is the forward light cone (THEOREM).
- **Three registers give three non-identical objects.** Operator-algebraic causality = commutant-as-causal-complement plus modular-positive inclusion order; Lie-theoretic causality = cone/semigroup with non-invertible direction; categorical causality = terminality of the monoidal unit (unique discarding). They are *partially* linked by theorems (Borchers/Wiesbrock positivity ↔ the ax+b semigroup ↔ Lie-wedge cone), but no theorem states all three are equivalent. Indefinite causal order (process matrices) is an explicit counterexample regime to the definiteness the first two assume.
- **For your causal-wall chain, the object that degenerates gracefully is the semigroup+modular-positivity pair, not the lattice.** A totally ordered nested family of horizon algebras is exactly a chain of half-sided modular inclusions (one-dimensional Borchers triples); there the commutant/orthomodular-lattice structure trivializes (no spacelike complements to speak of) but the Borchers–Wiesbrock positive translation semigroup and the direction-fixing spectrum condition survive intact and *are* the causal content.

## Key Findings

1. **"Causal complement = commutant" is an axiom, not a theorem.** Einstein causality (spacelike-separated algebras commute) is the Haag–Kastler locality *axiom*; Haag duality M(O′) = M(O)′ is a *supplementary* property that provably fails for spontaneously broken symmetries, for gauge theories with Wilson/’t Hooft lines, and for non-simply-connected/topologically nontrivial regions. Casini's theorem (that causally complete regions form a complete, atomic orthomodular lattice under causal complementation) is a genuine THEOREM and is the cleanest statement that the causal-complement operation mirrors the projection-lattice orthocomplement.

2. **Causality can be DERIVED from a positive-energy representation (modular localization).** Brunetti–Guido–Longo build the entire local, causal net from only a positive-energy unitary representation of the Poincaré group plus the Bisognano–Wichmann prescription; positivity of energy ⟺ isotony (Borchers). This is a reconstruction theorem — causality is *output*, not input.

3. **A single modular inclusion with positivity IS a causal semigroup + time orientation.** Borchers' and Wiesbrock's theorems (corrected by Araki–Zsidó) establish a *bijection* between half-sided modular inclusions and one-dimensional Borchers triples / positive-energy representations of the ax+b group. The slogan "causal order = inclusion order + modular positivity, direction = the endomorphic (non-automorphic) character of the inclusion" is fair and theorem-backed.

4. **The strongest "causality from pure algebra" result is CGMA.** Buchholz–Dreyer–Florig–Summers show that from a state and a family of wedge-indexed von Neumann algebras, the modular conjugations J_W generate the spacetime isometry group and reconstruct the spacetime and its causal order — carried out rigorously for 4d Minkowski and 3d de Sitter, and extended (via transplantation) to Robertson–Walker spacetimes. The modular stability condition replaces the spectrum condition where there is no translation group.

5. **The Lie-theoretic cluster is the direct answer and it recovers the AQFT structure.** Euler elements + invariant cones give an abstract wedge space with an intrinsic order and duality; type III₁ factors, Bisognano–Wichmann, and the Poincaré net emerge as theorems.

6. **"Causality = self-dual cone" is real in dimension-counting and structurally (Koecher–Vinberg, Connes) but nobody has built QFT causality from it.** The forward light cone in d=4 is exactly the positive cone of the spin factor / 2×2 complex Hermitian matrices; the Lorentz group is its cone-automorphism group. This is a THEOREM, but it is an identification of the *cone*, not a construction of the QFT causal net.

7. **Categorical causality is a property of the category (terminal unit); causal order is a property of unitary factorization; indefiniteness makes the order a quantum variable.** These are frameworks with theorems inside them, not reconstruction theorems about spacetime.

## Details (in depth-priority order)

### Cluster (5): Causality as cone + semigroup in Lie theory — the group-theoretic answer proper

**Definitions first.** A *convex cone* C in a real vector space is a convex set closed under non-negative scaling. It is *pointed* if C ∩ (−C) = {0} (no lines), *generating* if C − C spans, *regular* if it is pointed and closed. Given a Lie group G with Lie algebra 𝔤, a *Lie wedge* is a closed convex cone W ⊆ 𝔤 invariant under the group e^{ad(W∩−W)} — the tangent object at the identity of a local subsemigroup. A *(closed) subsemigroup* S ⊆ G satisfies SS ⊆ S; its *unit group* is S ∩ S⁻¹. The subsemigroup induces a left-invariant preorder g ≤ h ⟺ g⁻¹h ∈ S; this is a genuine *partial order* (antisymmetric) iff S ∩ S⁻¹ = {e}, and it is *global* iff the Lie wedge L(S) is "global" (integrates to an actual, not merely local, subsemigroup).

**(a) The Hilgert–Hofmann–Lawson / Lawson synthesis.** The foundational reference is J. Hilgert, K. H. Hofmann, J. D. Lawson, *Lie Groups, Convex Cones, and Semigroups* (Oxford University Press, 1989). The key structural theorem is Lawson's: J. D. Lawson, "Ordered Manifolds, Invariant Cone Fields, and Semigroups," *Forum Math.* 1 (1989) 273–308. THEOREM (Lawson 1989): a G-invariant *cone field* (a cone C_m ⊆ T_m M assigned to each point of a homogeneous manifold M = G/H, varying equivariantly) is equivalent to the choice of a single Lie wedge in 𝔤 (by invariance one cone determines all); its integral curves define a local partial order (a "conal order"), and the extension of this local order to a *global* partial order on M is equivalent to the globality of the corresponding Lie wedge. This is the precise sense in which **"causal structure on a homogeneous space = an invariant cone field = a Lie wedge," and "future = a closed subsemigroup S with S ∩ S⁻¹ = {e}."** (Grade: THEOREM.)

**Honest deflation / kill condition.** The conal order induced by a cone field is *always* reflexive and transitive but **need not be antisymmetric and need not be closed** — antisymmetry fails precisely when M admits closed timelike (conal) curves, e.g. Gödel-type spacetimes, and the non-closedness already occurs in Minkowski space (Lawson 1989, p. 299; Neeb, *Holomorphy and Convexity in Lie Theory*, 2000, p. 470). So "the future is a subsemigroup" gives a *global partial order* only under the globality/pointedness hypotheses; where S fails to be pointed (S ∩ S⁻¹ ≠ {e}) or the wedge is non-global, one gets only a preorder. This is the Lie-theoretic image of causality pathologies. The relevant invariant-cone and Ol'shanskii-semigroup theory is due to Ol'shanskii ("Invariant cones in Lie algebras, Lie semigroups, and the holomorphic discrete series," *Funct. Anal. Appl.* 15 (1982) 275–285) and Vinberg ("Invariant cones and orderings in Lie groups," *Funct. Anal. Appl.* 14 (1980) 1–13).

**(b) Causal symmetric spaces.** J. Hilgert, G. Ólafsson, *Causal Symmetric Spaces: Geometry and Harmonic Analysis*, Perspectives in Mathematics 18 (Academic Press, 1996). DEFINITION: a symmetric space M = G/H is *causal* if it carries a G-invariant cone field, equivalently an H-invariant regular cone C in the tangent space q at the base point. The dichotomy is a THEOREM: if the cone's interior meets the compact part k one gets a *compactly causal* space (includes anti-de Sitter and its coverings), if it meets the non-compact part p one gets a *non-compactly causal* space (includes de Sitter). THEOREM (Hilgert–Ólafsson, also Faraut): for non-compactly causal (= "ordered") symmetric spaces the invariant causal order is a genuine partial order with *compact order intervals* D(x,y) = {z : x ≤ z ≤ y} (Thm. 5.3.5); the associated causal semigroup S = {g ∈ G : g·x₀ ≥ x₀} = exp(C)·H is a closed subsemigroup, and the *order compactification* provides the boundary structure. Compactly causal orientations by contrast need be neither antisymmetric nor have compact intervals — a second kill condition. There is a duality compactly causal ↔ non-compactly causal at the infinitesimal level h + q ↔ h + iq.

**(c) THE MODERN SYNTHESIS — Neeb–Ólafsson–Morinelli.** This is the cluster that most literally answers "causality in group theory," and I give it the most depth, corroborated by primary sources.

*Euler elements.* DEFINITION (Morinelli–Neeb): an element h of a finite-dimensional real Lie algebra 𝔤 is an *Euler element* if ad h is diagonalizable with Spec(ad h) ⊆ {−1, 0, +1}, hence induces a 3-grading 𝔤 = 𝔤₁(h) ⊕ 𝔤₀(h) ⊕ 𝔤₋₁(h), 𝔤_ν(h) = ker(ad h − ν). h is *symmetric* if −h ∈ Inn(𝔤)h. The modular flow is exp(t ad h); the modular involution integrates τ_h = e^{πi ad h}. This is exactly the "boost = Euler element" abstraction of Bisognano–Wichmann.

*Abstract wedge space (verified from arXiv:2010.07128 and arXiv:2508.10960).* Starting from a graded Lie group (G, ε_G) with ε_G: G → {±1} and the twisted adjoint action Ad^ε(g) := ε_G(g)Ad(g), one defines the set of abstract wedges
  𝒢 := {(x, σ) ∈ 𝔤 × G↓ : σ² = e, Ad(σ)x = x},
G-action g.(x,σ) = (Ad^ε(g)x, gσg⁻¹), and the subset of *Euler couples / Euler wedges*
  𝒢_E := {(x, σ) : x Euler, Ad(σ) = e^{πi ad x}}.
The **causal complement (duality)** is a DEFINITION, purely group-theoretic:
  W′ := (−x, σ) = σ.W,   with (W′)′ = W and (gW)′ = gW′.
Morinelli–Neeb–Ólafsson state that this "fits the relation J_V V = V′ for standard subspaces" — i.e. the modular conjugation J_V of Tomita–Takesaki theory maps the standard subspace V to its symplectic complement V′, and the abstract σ encodes exactly this. The **order** is a DEFINITION via an Ad^ε(G)-invariant pointed closed convex cone C ⊆ 𝔤: with C± := ±C ∩ 𝔤_{±1}(x) one forms the compression semigroup S_W := exp(C₊)G_W exp(C₋) and sets g₁.W ≤ g₂.W ⟺ g₂⁻¹g₁ ∈ S_W. This is precisely the Hilgert–Hofmann–Lawson cone-semigroup order transplanted to the wedge space.

*What is theorem vs. definition here (the crucial gradings):*
- The wedge complement W ↦ W′ and the order via S_W are DEFINITIONS.
- That the *standard subspaces* satisfy twisted duality H(W)′ = B·H(W′) (twisted locality) is an AXIOM of the covariant net, then VERIFIED as a theorem in the concrete second-quantization constructions.
- **Half-sided modular inclusions are recovered (THEOREM, MN21 Def. 2.11 + Lemma 2.12):** for y ∈ ±C with [x,y] = ±y, exp(y) ∈ S_W and exp(y).W ≤ W is a ±half-sided modular inclusion; every wedge inclusion factors through a + and a − half-sided one. So Borchers/Wiesbrock positivity IS the statement that the cone generators in 𝔤_{±1} lie in the invariant cone.
- **The Poincaré AQFT net is the special case (THEOREM, MN21 Example 2.10(f), eq. 2.27–2.28):** for G = ℝ^{1,d} ⋊ SO_{1,d} with C = closed forward light cone, the Euler couple (boost generator k₁, reflection j₁) corresponds to the standard right wedge W₁ = {|x₀| < x₁}, S_{(k₁,j₁)} = {g : gW₁ ⊆ W₁}, and g.(k₁,j₁) ↦ gW₁ is an *order-preserving bijection* between the abstract wedge space and Minkowski wedge domains. The Brunetti–Guido–Longo net is reproduced. The choice of cone controls the geometry: for PSL₂(ℝ), C = {0} gives 2d de Sitter (no proper inclusions) while a proper invariant cone gives the chiral circle.
- **The Euler element is forced (THEOREM, "Euler Element Theorem," Morinelli–Neeb, *Adv. Math.* 458 (2024) 109960, arXiv:2312.12182):** under a regularity condition, any geometrically-implemented modular group arising from the Bisognano–Wichmann property is generated by an Euler element; conversely Euler + BW give regularity and localizability.
- **Wedge algebras are type III₁ (THEOREM, Morinelli–Neeb, *Adv. Math.* 458 (2024) 109960, arXiv:2312.12182, Theorem 5.15):** given a connected Lie group G, when the Bisognano–Wichmann property and a suitable regularity property hold and there is a unique G-fixed (vacuum) state, the wedge algebras are factors of type III₁; the key condition is that the Euler element h be *anti-elliptic*. This matches the known AQFT result and is directly relevant to your type III₁ fibres.
- **Twisted duality / causal complements in general (THEOREM, Morinelli–Neeb–Ólafsson, "Orthogonal pairs of Euler elements I," arXiv:2508.10960, *Forum Math.* 38(3) (2026) 893–923):** causal complements of wedge regions correspond to *twisted complements* W′^α := (−x, ασ) for central α with τ_h(α) = α⁻¹; any two twisted complements are connected by a chain of complements coming from 𝔰𝔩₂(ℝ)-subalgebras; h is symmetric iff a complement lies in the positive orbit (Lemma 3.2). A pair (h,k) is *orthogonal* if e^{πi ad h}k = −k, and (h,k),(k,h) orthogonal ⟺ they generate an 𝔰𝔩₂(ℝ) (Thm. 2.6).

*Three-characterizations-coincide theorem (geometric heart).* Neeb–Ólafsson, "Wedge domains in compactly causal symmetric spaces" (arXiv:2107.13288, *IMRN* 2023) and "Wedge domains in non-compactly causal symmetric spaces" (arXiv:2205.07685, *Geom. Dedicata* 2023): the wedge domain attached to an Euler element admits three coincident descriptions — (i) the *positivity domain* of the modular vector field {m : X^M_h(m) ∈ C°_m}, (ii) a *KMS-like analytic extension* domain for the modular flow, (iii) a *polar/cone decomposition* domain. In Morinelli–Neeb–Ólafsson "Modular geodesics and wedge domains…" (*Ann. Global Anal. Geom.* 65 (2024) 9) the positivity region coincides with the *observer domain* traced by a modular geodesic, and only one conjugacy class of Euler elements has non-empty positivity region — a locality-relevant fact.

**Verdict for cluster (5):** the program goes very far toward the slogan "causal structure = the poset of Euler wedges of a Lie group with its cone-semigroup order, complement = duality W ↦ (−x, σ)," and it *provably* reproduces Bisognano–Wichmann and the AQFT nets as special cases (with new E₇-type and Jordan-spacetime models beyond the known ones). The residual gaps: full geometric Bisognano–Wichmann and spin–statistics are in the forthcoming Morinelli–Neeb–Ólafsson, "Orthogonal pairs of Euler elements II: Geometric Bisognano–Wichmann and Spin–Statistics Theorems" (arXiv:2603.26390, dated 30 March 2026), which "derive both a Bisognano–Wichmann Theorem and a Spin–Statistics Theorem for nets of standard subspaces and von Neumann algebras" — preprint; do not treat its theorem numbers as final.

### Cluster (4): Spacetime and its causal order FROM modular involutions — the CGMA program

*Definitions.* The *modular conjugation* J_W and *modular operator* Δ_W attach to (M(W), Ω) by Tomita–Takesaki theory. The **Condition of Geometric Modular Action (CGMA)** (Buchholz–Dreyer–Florig–Summers, "Geometric modular action and spacetime symmetry groups," *Rev. Math. Phys.* 12 (2000) 475–560, arXiv:math-ph/9805026): given a state ω and a family {M(W)}_{W∈𝒲} of wedge-indexed von Neumann algebras, the set 𝒥 of modular conjugations *maps the family into itself* — each J_W implements an automorphism of the collection {M(W)}. Equivalently 𝒥 ⊆ Aut(𝒱) where 𝒱 is the family (arXiv:1704.01336 formulation).

*Exact conclusions (THEOREM, BDFS 2000).* Verbatim from the abstract: "This condition is naturally associated with transformation groups of partially ordered sets and provides these groups with projective representations. Under suitable additional conditions, these groups induce groups of point transformations on these space-times, which may be interpreted as symmetry groups." The J_W generate the isometry group; the spacetime and its causal order are reconstructed. Explicitly carried out for **4-dimensional Minkowski** and **3-dimensional de Sitter**. For Minkowski, CGMA + the modular stability condition yields a continuous unitary representation of the proper Poincaré group leaving ω invariant and acting covariantly ("An Algebraic Characterization of Vacuum States in Minkowski Space," parts I–III).

*Modular stability condition (the spectrum-condition replacement).* DEFINITION (BDFS §5.1): "the modular unitaries are contained in the group generated by the modular involutions, i.e. Δ_W^{it} ∈ 𝒥 for all t, W." THEOREM (BDFS Thm. 5.1.2): in Minkowski, CGMA + modular stability imply the *relativistic spectrum condition* (positivity of energy) — remarkably, since neither hypothesis contains any input about the arrow of time, yet every state satisfying them breaks time-reflection symmetry. Because the condition is phrased purely in terms of algebraically-determined modular objects, it is well-defined on *arbitrary* spacetimes with no translation group. There are indications connecting it to the microlocal spectrum condition and, in de Sitter, to the geodesic KMS condition of Borchers–Buchholz.

*Which spacetimes.* Minkowski (4d) and de Sitter (3d) fully worked; **Robertson–Walker**: Buchholz–Mund–Summers, "Transplantation of Local Nets and Geometric Modular Action on Robertson–Walker Space-Times" (Fields Inst. Commun. 30 (2001) 65–81; arXiv:hep-th/0011237) establish existence of nets and states satisfying CGMA + modular stability on certain FRW spacetimes via a *transplantation* technique, showing 𝒥 can be much larger than the (possibly trivial) isometry group — this is the direct hook for your FRW causal-wall program. Anti-de Sitter also treated (Buchholz–Florig–Summers). "On Deriving Space-Time From Quantum Observables and States" (arXiv:hep-th/0304179) makes the reconstruction explicit.

*How strong is it really?* This is genuine derivation of causality/isometry from algebra + state, but the honest caveats: (i) the *choice* of the index set 𝒲 of wedges (a partially ordered set with a specified structure) is an input carrying causal information; (ii) the full point-transformation conclusion needs continuity/cohomological inputs (resolved for Poincaré in part III via reflection maps); (iii) beyond maximally symmetric spacetimes the reconstruction of the symmetry group from FRW nets was left as a program rather than a finished theorem. So: strongest known "causality from pure algebra," but not input-free.

### Cluster (3): Causal order from modular inclusions and positivity

*Borchers' theorem* (H.-J. Borchers, "The CPT-theorem in two-dimensional theories of local observables," *Comm. Math. Phys.* 143 (1992) 315–332), verbatim: "Let ℳ be a von Neumann algebra with cyclic and separating vector Ω, and let U(a) be a continuous unitary representation of ℝ with positive generator and Ω as fixed point. If these unitaries induce for positive arguments endomorphisms of ℳ then the modular group acts as dilatations on the group of unitaries." Concretely, for a one-dimensional Borchers triple (M, T, Ω) with T of *positive generator*,
  Δ^{it} T(x) Δ^{−it} = T(e^{−2πt}x),  J T(x) J = T(−x).
Thus spectral positivity forces the modular group to act as a boost-like dilation on the translations, and *fixes the direction* of causal translation. Grade: THEOREM.

*Wiesbrock's theorem* (H.-W. Wiesbrock, "Half-sided modular inclusions of von Neumann algebras," *Comm. Math. Phys.* 157 (1993) 83–92; erratum 184 (1997) 683–685; corrected and completed by H. Araki, L. Zsidó, "Extension of the structure theorem of Borchers and its application to half-sided modular inclusions," *Rev. Math. Phys.* 17 (2005) 491): a *standard half-sided modular inclusion* N ⊆ M (Δ_M^{−it} N ⊆ N for t ≥ 0, common cyclic separating Ω) is *equivalent* to a one-dimensional Borchers triple / positive-energy representation of the ax+b group. Explicitly (Araki–Zsidó Thm. 2.1, one-particle form): U(1 − e^{−2πt}) := Δ_K^{it} Δ_H^{−it} extends to a positively-generated one-parameter group making (H, U) a standard pair with K = U(1)H — a *one-to-one correspondence* between half-sided modular inclusions and standard pairs. Grade: THEOREM.

*Two/three inclusions → Möbius/conformal.* Wiesbrock and Guido–Longo–Wiesbrock: two half-sided modular inclusions in suitable relative position generate the Möbius group PSL₂(ℝ); the corresponding modular data build a full conformal net on the circle. Borchers ("Half-sided modular inclusion and the construction of the Poincaré group," *Comm. Math. Phys.* 179 (1996) 703–723) constructs the Poincaré group directly (not just its cover) from wedge modular groups. Grade: THEOREM.

*Assessing the slogan* "causal order = the partial order of algebra inclusions equipped with modular positivity; direction = the non-automorphic (endomorphic) character of the inclusion." Verdict: **fair and theorem-backed, with one precisification.** The inclusion N ⊆ M carries an order (⊆); modular positivity (Δ_M^{−it}N ⊆ N one-sidedly) is exactly what makes the translation semigroup positive and singles out a direction. The "endomorphic character" clause is correct in spirit: the compression n ↦ T(1) n T(−1) is a proper endomorphism (semigroup, not group) — this is the operator-algebraic face of "irreversibility = semigroup not group." Longo's canonical endomorphism (γ: M → N associated to a subfactor N ⊆ M via the Jones/conjugation machinery) is relevant as the finite-index refinement: it converts the inclusion into an endomorphism with a conjugate, and half-sided modular inclusions carry finite-index endomorphisms extending to the generated conformal net (Longo; Bertozzini–Conti–Longo). Grade of slogan: DEDUCTION from the cited THEOREMs.

### Cluster (1): Causality as commutativity structure (Einstein causality)

*Locality axiom.* Haag–Kastler: spacelike-separated observable algebras commute, [M(O₁), M(O₂)] = 0 for O₁ ⊆ O₂′. This is an AXIOM (microcausality / Einstein causality). Grade: AXIOM.

*Haag duality.* M(O′) = M(O)′ — the causal complement realized as the *commutant*. This is a *supplementary property* (sometimes an axiom, often a theorem for specific models, e.g. free fields via Bisognano–Wichmann): "the commutant not only contains the algebra of the causal complement (Einstein causality) but equals it." Grade: PROPERTY that is model-dependent — provable for the vacuum sector of nice theories, **false in general**.

*Where Haag duality fails (kill conditions, all sourced):*
- *Spontaneously broken symmetry:* its violation for observables localized in a simply-connected causally complete region *signals* SSB in the charge-carrying field algebra (Roberts; Buchholz). SSB does not occur without breakdown of Haag duality. Remedy: pass to the dual net O ↦ M(O′)′, at the cost of additivity.
- *Non-simply-connected / topologically nontrivial regions:* for toroidal/ring-like regions Haag duality generically fails; the mismatch M(O)′ ⊋ M(O′) is the home of generalized (higher-form / non-invertible) symmetries — Wilson loops for uncharged reps in the complement, ’t Hooft loops, etc.
- *Gauge theories:* Maxwell in a disk breaks Haag duality (Casini et al., lattice-verified); 1+1d non-abelian gauge theories violate it via nonlocal Wilson lines.

*Statistical independence hierarchy* (S. J. Summers, "On the independence of local algebras in quantum field theory," *Rev. Math. Phys.* 2 (1990) 201–247; "Subsystems and Independence in Relativistic Microscopic Physics," arXiv:0812.1517). Definitions, in increasing strength: *microcausality* (commutation) ⊂ *extended locality* (microcausality + A ∩ B = ℂ) ⊂ *C\*-independence* (Schlieder property: ab = 0 ⟹ a = 0 or b = 0) ⊂ *C\*-independence in the product sense* (A ∨ B ≅ A ⊗ B) ⊂ *split property* (a type-I factor interpolates A ⊂ N ⊂ B). Summers argues the split property is the primary notion of subsystem independence. Crucial deflation (Buchholz–Summers): **C\*-independence does not entail Einstein locality, and Einstein locality does not entail C\*-independence** — commutativity and statistical independence are logically distinct facets of "causal independence." The split property holds under nuclearity (Buchholz–Wichmann) and is what lets local regions define genuine subsystems.

*Casini's lattice theorem.* H. Casini, "The logic of causally closed spacetime subsets," *Class. Quantum Grav.* 19 (2002) 6389–6404, arXiv:gr-qc/0205013. DEFINITION: for a region S the causal complement S′ = {points not timelike-related to any point of S}; a region is *causally complete* if S″ = S. THEOREM (verbatim): "the algebra of subsets that arises from this negation operation is a complete orthomodular lattice, and thus has several of the properties characterizing the algebra of physical propositions in quantum mechanics… we show that the causal lattice is in addition atomic, find its atoms, and give necessary and sufficient conditions for irreducibility." Structurally this parallels the projection lattice of a von Neumann algebra with orthocomplement P ↦ 1 − P (equivalently the commutant on subalgebras). This is the sharpest statement that **causal complementation is an orthocomplementation** — the order-theoretic skeleton shared by causality and quantum logic. Grade: THEOREM. (Caveat, Casini's own: this orthomodular lattice is not made of open sets; one cannot simultaneously have openness and orthomodularity — a structural obstruction relevant to matching it with the net of algebras, which are attached to open regions.)

### Cluster (7): Categorical definitions — causality = terminality / discarding

*Causality axiom.* G. Chiribella, G. M. D'Ariano, P. Perinotti, "Probabilistic theories with purification," *Phys. Rev. A* 81 (2010) 062348, arXiv:0908.1583: a theory is *causal* iff there is a *unique deterministic effect* (unique "discard" map) for every system — equivalently, "no signalling from the future," the probability of a preparation is independent of the choice of future measurement. Grade: AXIOM (one of the axioms from which they reconstruct quantum theory in *Phys. Rev. A* 84 (2011) 012311).

*Terminality.* B. Coecke, "Terminality implies no-signalling …and much more than that," *New Generation Computing* (2016); Coecke–Kissinger, *Picturing Quantum Processes* (CUP 2017). DEFINITION: an object T is *terminal* if for every A there is a unique morphism A → T. THEOREM: a monoidal category is *causal* iff the monoidal unit I is terminal ("discarding is natural" / unique), and terminality ⟺ no-signalling once causal structure is made explicit. Grade: THEOREM (within the process-theory framework). Coecke–Lal, "Causal categories: relativistically interacting processes," arXiv:1010.4572, and Kissinger–Uijlen, "A categorical semantics for causal structure," build a category where a partial causal order is imposed and no-signalling is guaranteed by construction.

*Causal ORDER via unitary factorization.* J.-M. Allen, J. Barrett, D. Horsman, C. Lee, R. Spekkens, "Quantum common causes and quantum causal models," *Phys. Rev. X* 7 (2017) 031021, arXiv:1609.09487; J. Barrett, R. Lorenz, O. Oreshkov, "Quantum Causal Models," arXiv:1906.10726, and "Cyclic quantum causal models," *Nat. Commun.* 12 (2021) 885. THEOREM (quantum Reichenbach): assuming fundamentally unitary dynamics, if a channel with input A and outputs B, C is compatible with A being a *complete common cause* of B and C, then it *factorizes* in a specific way; causal relations are defined intrinsically via the factorization structure of unitaries, and every quantum causal model corresponds to a unitary circuit with matching causal structure. Grade: THEOREM (framework-internal). So in this register the causal *order* is a property of unitary/channel factorization, not of a category.

*Indefinite causal order (the counterexample regime).* O. Oreshkov, F. Costa, Č. Brukner, "Quantum correlations with no causal order," *Nat. Commun.* 3 (2012) 1092, arXiv:1105.4464. Verbatim: "we find correlations that cannot be understood in terms of definite causal order. These correlations violate a 'causal inequality' that is satisfied by all space-like and time-like correlations… in a classical limit causal order always arises, which suggests that space-time may emerge from a more fundamental structure." The *quantum switch* (Chiribella–D'Ariano–Perinotti–Valiron, *Phys. Rev. A* 88 (2013) 022318) realizes a coherent superposition of orders A∘B and B∘A: "happens-before is not merely unknown; it is undefined." Grade: THEOREM (existence of causal-inequality-violating processes) + FRAMEWORK. This is the explicit kill condition for any definition that builds definiteness of order in from the start (clusters 3, 5 assume a definite semigroup/inclusion direction).

### Cluster (6): Causality as positive cone / order structure of the algebra itself

*Koecher–Vinberg.* THEOREM (M. Koecher 1957, E. B. Vinberg 1961): there is a one-to-one correspondence between finite-dimensional formally real (Euclidean) Jordan algebras and *symmetric cones* = open convex cones that are regular, homogeneous (Aut acts transitively on the interior), and self-dual; equivalently the cones of squares {x² : x ∈ V}. The Jordan–von Neumann–Wigner classification lists them: Hermitian matrices over ℝ, ℂ, ℍ; the exceptional 3×3 octonionic algebra; and the *spin factors* ℝ^n ⊕ ℝ. THEOREM (the load-bearing identification): the spin factor's cone of squares is exactly the forward light cone {(a, t) : t ≥ ‖a‖} of Minkowski space in the corresponding dimension; for d=4, V = 2×2 complex Hermitian matrices H₂(ℂ), the cone of positive-semidefinite matrices is the closed forward light cone (det ≥ 0, trace ≥ 0), and the *algebra automorphisms / cone automorphisms* realize the (restricted) Lorentz group. So "the causal cone IS a Jordan-algebra positive cone" is literally true as a statement about the cone and its symmetry group. Grade: THEOREM.

*Connes' cone characterization.* A. Connes, "Caractérisation des espaces vectoriels ordonnés sous-jacents aux algèbres de von Neumann," *Ann. Inst. Fourier* 24 (1974) 121–155. THEOREM: the category of von Neumann algebras is equivalent to the category of self-dual, *facially homogeneous*, complex cones. Combined with the Tomita–Takesaki *natural positive cone* P^♮ = closure of {Δ^{1/4} m Ω : m ∈ M₊} (Araki, Connes, Haagerup standard form), the ordered Hilbert space cone *determines the von Neumann algebra*. Grade: THEOREM. This is the deepest sense in which "the algebra is recoverable from its order/cone structure."

*Assessment (honest deflation).* Does "causality = the self-dual cone" hold beyond dimension counting? The Koecher–Vinberg and Connes theorems are genuine and structural, not coincidental: the Lorentz group really is the cone-automorphism group of a Jordan positive cone, and the light-cone order really is the Jordan order x ≤ y ⟺ y − x ∈ C. **But:** (i) this identifies the *tangent-space / single-cone* causal order (the order on one Minkowski vector space), not the global net-theoretic causal structure of a QFT; (ii) the self-dual cone P^♮ of a type III₁ QFT wedge algebra is infinite-dimensional and is *not* a Minkowski light cone — the finite-dimensional Koecher–Vinberg picture and the Connes picture live at different levels; (iii) **no one has built QFT causality (a causal net) from the Jordan/cone identification.** The Jordan-spacetime models of Neeb–Ólafsson–Morinelli (simple Jordan algebras as spacetimes) are the closest bridge, but there causality still comes from the *Euler-element/cone-in-the-Lie-algebra* structure of cluster (5), not from the Jordan positive cone per se. Verdict: TRUE as a cone identification (THEOREM); OPEN/unrealized as a construction of QFT causality. Note also the Alfsen–Shultz program (orientations of state spaces recovering the dynamics/Jordan structure) shows *dynamics and orientation* come from order — supporting but not completing the picture.

### Cluster (2): Modular localization (the derivation from group representations)

Covered structurally under (5). The primary source is R. Brunetti, D. Guido, R. Longo, "Modular localization and Wigner particles," *Rev. Math. Phys.* 14 (2002) 759–786, arXiv:math-ph/0203021 (and independently B. Schroer's "modular localization"). *Assumed:* an irreducible positive-energy unitary (ray) representation of the proper Poincaré group on the one-particle space + the Bisognano–Wichmann relations as the *definition* of the wedge data (run in reverse). *Derived:* for each wedge W a real standard subspace H(W) via Tomita operators S_W = J_W Δ_W^{1/2}, H(W) = ker(S_W − 1); the net of local algebras by second quantization; the Reeh–Schlieder property; wedge duality; and — for continuous-spin (infinite-spin) reps — a net on spacelike cones. THEOREM (BGL): **positivity of the energy ⟺ isotony of the net** (in the spirit of Borchers) — this is the precise sense in which causal localization is *equivalent* to the spectrum condition given the group. PCT and spin–statistics follow from the modular structure (J implements PCT, the modular relation encodes statistics). Grade: THEOREM. The construction extends to conformal and de Sitter spacetimes homogeneous under the relevant group. **This is the paradigm "causality DERIVED from group theory + positivity, with no spacetime input beyond the group,"** and it is the historical seed of the Neeb–Ólafsson–Morinelli abstraction.

### Cluster (8): Order-theoretic reconstruction (brief)

K. Martin, P. Panangaden, "A domain of spacetime intervals in general relativity," *Comm. Math. Phys.* 267 (2006) 563–586 (and arXiv:gr-qc/0407094). THEOREM: a globally hyperbolic spacetime with its causality relation is a *bicontinuous poset* whose interval topology is the manifold topology; consequently the spacetime can be reconstructed from *only a countable dense set of events and the causal order* — no metric or smoothness input — because the category of globally hyperbolic posets is equivalent to a category of *interval domains* (Scott domains). This complements causal sets (Sorkin): where causal sets *postulate* a discrete order and try to recover the continuum, Martin–Panangaden *prove* that the continuum order already contains a countable dense causal skeleton that regenerates it — "spacetime emerges from something discrete" as a theorem rather than a hypothesis. Grade: THEOREM.

## Synthesis: the reader's schematic triple

- **TIME = the outer class of the modular flow.** Given (M, ω) the modular automorphism group σ_t^ω is canonical *modulo inner automorphisms*: by Connes' cocycle (Radon–Nikodym) theorem the class [σ] in Out(M) = Aut(M)/Inn(M) is state-independent. Direction is fixed by the KMS condition / spectrum positivity. Grade: THEOREM (Tomita–Takesaki + Connes).
- **TEMPERATURE = the KMS normalization / Connes cocycle rate.** The inverse temperature β is the periodicity of the KMS condition (σ analytic in the strip of width β); the Connes cocycle [Dφ:Dψ]_t measures the relative rate. Grade: THEOREM/DEFINITION.
- **CAUSALITY = an invariant cone in a Lie algebra with its compression semigroup, read through the Euler-element/modular-flow dictionary; complement = the group-theoretic duality W ↦ (−x, σ).** Grade of the schematic: THEOREM-supported at the wedge/Euler-element level (adjudication below).

**The three candidate schematics and their logical relations.**

(i) *Operator-algebraic:* commutant-as-causal-complement + modular-positive inclusion order. Causal complement = M ↦ M′ (Casini's orthocomplementation at the region level; Haag duality where it holds); before/after = half-sided modular inclusion with positive generator.

(ii) *Lie-theoretic (Neeb–Ólafsson):* an invariant cone / compression semigroup; the future = the subsemigroup S_W; direction = non-invertibility (S ∩ S⁻¹ = stabilizer, not G); complement = the duality W ↦ (−x, σ).

(iii) *Categorical/informational:* terminality of the monoidal unit (unique discard); causal order = unitary factorization; indefiniteness = the order becomes an operator-valued variable.

**Are they equivalent?** *Partially, with proven links between (i) and (ii); (iii) stands apart.*
- (i) ⟷ (ii) are **provably connected**: Borchers/Wiesbrock positivity (the modular-positive inclusion of (i)) is *exactly* the statement that the cone generators lie in 𝔤_{±1} of the invariant cone, giving the half-sided modular inclusion of (ii) (MN21 Def. 2.11/Lemma 2.12); and the Euler-wedge order recovers the AQFT commutant/inclusion structure and Bisognano–Wichmann as the Poincaré special case (MN21 Ex. 2.10(f); MN24 Euler Element Theorem, Thm. 5.15 for type III₁). So the operator-algebraic and Lie-theoretic pictures are two faces of one object — the modular flow exp(t ad h) with its positive cone. Grade of equivalence: THEOREM (on wedges/Euler elements); the *full* region-lattice of (i) is richer than the wedge-order of (ii), so equivalence is established at the wedge level, not for all causally complete regions.
- (iii) is **not known to be equivalent** to (i)/(ii). Terminality/no-signalling is a statement about a *process category*, provable from unitarity, and it captures "no signalling from the future" — but there is no theorem identifying the terminal-unit structure with the modular-positive semigroup or the invariant cone. The bridge is at best verbal ("both express irreversibility/direction"). Honest deflation: connecting (iii) to (i)/(ii) is OPEN.
- Indefinite causal order (process matrices, quantum switch) is a **counterexample** to the definiteness that (i) and (ii) build in: there the "order" is not a fixed partial order at all. Any claim that causality is *always* a semigroup/lattice fails in this regime. Grade: THEOREM (causal inequality violation) as a kill condition.

**Honest verdict — which single algebraic object most deserves the name "causality" for your causal-wall program.** The **invariant cone + its compression semigroup, read through the Euler-element/modular-flow dictionary** (candidate ii, fused with the operator-algebraic i). Reasoning: (a) it is the one object that is simultaneously a THEOREM-level match to Bisognano–Wichmann, to the Borchers/Wiesbrock positivity you have internalized, to type III₁ wedge algebras, and to crossed-product/flow-of-weights structure (the compression semigroup is the geometric shadow of the modular one-parameter group whose crossed product gives the Falcone–Takesaki core); (b) it degrades gracefully to your setting.

**Employment notes for a totally ordered chain of horizon (causal-wall) algebras.** Your base is a nested one-parameter family N_s ⊆ N_{s'} (s ≤ s') of type III₁ fibres — i.e. a *chain* of half-sided modular inclusions / one-dimensional Borchers triples. In this 1-dimensional/chain case:
- **What survives (and IS the causality):** the *semigroup direction* — the positive translation semigroup T(a), a ≥ 0, from Borchers/Wiesbrock, with S ∩ S⁻¹ = {stabilizer}; and the *modular positivity* — Δ^{it} T(a) Δ^{−it} = T(e^{−2πt}a) fixing the arrow. These are precisely the two clauses of candidate (ii)/(i) that do not require a spacelike complement. The chain is a totally ordered sub-poset of the Euler-wedge order with cone C degenerating to a ray (the future generator).
- **What trivializes:** the *lattice/complement* structure. On a totally ordered chain there are no spacelike-separated pairs, so the commutant-as-causal-complement (Haag duality) and Casini's orthomodular lattice collapse to the trivial or Boolean structure — the meet/join of a chain is just min/max. The orthocomplementation W ↦ W′ has nothing transverse to act on. So candidate (i)'s *lattice* face is vacuous here while its *inclusion-order + modular-positivity* face is exactly candidate (ii)'s surviving content.
- **Practical upshot:** model the causal content of the wall chain as a *Lie-semigroup order with modular-positive generator* (a positive Borchers triple per pair), not as an orthomodular lattice. The CGMA/modular-stability apparatus (which needs no translation group) is the right tool where your FRW background has trivial isometry — Buchholz–Mund–Summers' Robertson–Walker transplantation is the precedent, and the modular stability condition is your spectrum-condition surrogate.

**Kill conditions (where each candidate breaks), collected.**
- Candidate (i): Haag duality fails under SSB, in gauge theories (Wilson/’t Hooft lines), and for non-simply-connected regions; C\*-independence and Einstein locality are logically independent (Buchholz–Summers), so "commutativity" underdetermines "independence." Casini's lattice cannot be realized by open sets.
- Candidate (ii): the conal order is generally non-antisymmetric and non-closed (closed timelike curves; even Minkowski gives a non-closed relation); "future = subsemigroup" is a partial order only when the Lie wedge is global and S is pointed. Only one conjugacy class of Euler elements has non-empty positivity domain.
- Candidate (iii): indefinite causal order shows the framework's definite-order case is not universal; and its link to the modular/cone objects is unproven (OPEN).

Benchmark: check whether your horizon net satisfies modular stability (Δ_W^{it} ∈ 𝒥); if yes, you inherit a boost-like geometric action and an arrow without needing a translation group.

## Caveats

- **Axiom vs. theorem, kept explicit:** Einstein locality (cluster 1) is *causality assumed*; modular localization (2), modular inclusions (3), CGMA (4), and the Euler-wedge program (5) are *causality (partly) derived* — but each derivation still takes some causal information as input (the wedge index set 𝒲 in CGMA; the Bisognano–Wichmann prescription in modular localization; the invariant cone C in the Lie program). None is fully input-free; the honest claim is "causal structure is derived from a group representation + positivity + a minimal choice of wedge/cone data," not "from nothing."
- **Forthcoming results not to over-cite:** the geometric Bisognano–Wichmann and spin–statistics theorems in "Orthogonal pairs of Euler elements II" (arXiv:2603.26390, dated 30 March 2026) are preprint/forthcoming; treat their theorem numbers as provisional.
- **Level mismatch in cluster (6):** the finite-dimensional Koecher–Vinberg light-cone-as-Jordan-cone identification and the infinite-dimensional Connes natural-cone characterization are both theorems but live at different levels; do not conflate the Minkowski light cone with the type III₁ standard cone P^♮.
- **Casini lattice caveat (author's own):** the orthomodular lattice of causally complete regions is not realizable by open sets, obstructing a naive identification with the (open-region-indexed) net of algebras.
- **Conflicting emphases in the literature:** Summers treats the *split property* as the primary independence notion, whereas much of the philosophy literature defaults to microcausality; these are provably inequivalent (Buchholz–Summers), so "causal independence" is not a single concept.
- **Indefinite causal order** is a genuine counterexample regime, experimentally instantiated (quantum switch); any universal claim that causality is a semigroup or lattice is false there.
