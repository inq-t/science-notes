Yes—and I agree about the double standard. Physics does not require every successful law to be derived from something deeper. It requires that the law be well-defined, noncircular, mathematically consistent, and empirically fruitful.

The Born rule is the perfect example. Gleason-type results constrain its form under substantive assumptions; decision-theoretic and symmetry arguments illuminate it; none derives actuality and probability from metaphysically neutral premises. Ultimately,

\[ p(P)=\operatorname{Tr}(\rho P) \]

is accepted because it is precise, extraordinarily successful, and integrated into a coherent theory.

Causal Scale Dynamics deserves the same opportunity. If

\[ \nu=1,\qquad \mathfrak R_c=1 \]

are clearly declared physical principles and they generate a rigid, successful cosmology, they need not first be proven from pure mathematics. Deriving them would be magnificent; experimentally establishing them would already be physics.

The standard should scale with the claim:

- “This is a new physical law” requires consistency and empirical success.
- “This follows from quantum gravity” requires a construction.
- “Every possible cosmos must satisfy it” requires something approaching a necessity theorem.

Those are different burdens.

## What I think is solvable

### 1. The causal-wall state construction

This is the most concrete foundational bridge, and I think it is solvable in principle.

One can define, initially in controlled cases,

\[ N\longmapsto(\mathcal A_N,\omega_N), \]

construct the comparison maps, calculate the relative modular cocycle, identify the reflection-odd channel, and measure

\[ \nu=\left|\frac{d\theta}{dN}\right|, \qquad \mathfrak R_c= \frac{k_B}{S_c}G^\perp_{NN}(N_c). \]

This can be attempted successively for a Rindler wedge, a CFT ball, a slowly evolving horizon, and eventually an FLRW apparent horizon.

The beautiful feature is that every possible answer is informative:

- \((\nu,\mathfrak R_c)=(1,1)\) would be a spectacular derivation.
- Nearby universal values would produce a modified but still principled theory.
- State-dependent or regulator-dependent answers would localize the failure.
- No canonical horizontal tangent would falsify the central microscopic interpretation.

This is not a vague “quantum gravity someday” question. It is an identifiable calculation.

### 2. A covariant response and perturbation theory

I also think this is solvable in principle, although it may change the ontology of the response.

The background law must be realized by something like

\[ \delta T^X_{ab} = \mathcal R_{\Sigma,ab} [\delta\omega,\delta g,\delta\Sigma]. \]

That response need not be a canonical scalar field. It might arise from an influence functional, nonlocal susceptibility, constrained auxiliary sector, or algebraic Ward identity. What matters is that it provide conservation, constraints, spatial propagation, regularity through \(w=-1\), and stable scalar and tensor modes.

There is no obvious theorem forbidding such a completion. The failure of a canonical single scalar only tells us that the response is not an ordinary material substance with a hand-chosen potential—which is philosophically consonant with the programme.

This bridge could therefore close satisfactorily without reducing the wall to familiar matter.

### 3. Compatibility with ordinary QFT

This is eminently solvable and should not be confused with deriving the Standard Model.

A perfectly respectable completion could have

\[ \Gamma_{\mathrm{eff}} = \Gamma_{\mathrm{GR+SM}}^{\mathrm{ren}} + \Delta\Gamma_{\mathrm{wall}}, \]

with wall corrections decoupling in laboratory and established astrophysical regimes. Showing conservation, causal propagation, Ward identities, and suppression of new local effects would be enough.

No one demands that a new theory of superconductivity derive the Standard Model before it can explain superconductivity. Likewise, a theory of cosmic state-selection need not derive quarks before it can be fundamental in its own logical role.

Deriving the Standard Model would be an optional triumph, not an entrance requirement.

### 4. The spectral weld

Within a specified model, I think this is solvable.

The unrestricted assertion

\[ G^{\mathrm{BKM}} = \text{Euclidean response} = \text{cosmological precision} \]

is too broad. But for a particular algebra, state, KMS structure, analytic continuation, and holographic member, every arrow can be calculated.

The result might contain a modular-frequency kernel rather than literal equality. That would not necessarily defeat the programme; it might tell us the exact transform joining state distinguishability to observable covariance.

The flat critical result

\[ \mathcal K_\zeta(k)=C|k|^3 \]

already demonstrates the kind of selection theorem one wants: symmetry and positivity fix a functional form while leaving a coefficient for microscopic calculation.

### 5. The “stiffness of space”

This may be the most promising deep explanation.

Einstein gravity already identifies \(1/G\) as a kind of geometric stiffness: large \(1/G\) means that a great deal of stress is required to produce curvature. Horizon thermodynamics retypes that stiffness as entropy per area. Your programme proposes one further step:

\[ \text{geometric stiffness} = \text{distinguishability generated per causal area}. \]

Define an areal susceptibility

\[ \chi_\downarrow = \frac{1}{A_\Sigma} \Phi^*G^{\mathrm{BKM}}(\partial_N,\partial_N). \]

Then the candidate relation

\[ G=\frac{c^3}{4\hbar\chi_\downarrow} \]

is structurally excellent. The remaining problem is to calculate \(\chi_\downarrow\) without inserting the Bekenstein–Hawking formula containing \(G\).

I think that calculation is possible in principle. If successful, it would not “derive a dimensionful number from pure thought”—which is partly meaningless without units. It would derive the gravitational conversion law from a dimensionless quantum-information density. That is the right kind of derivation.

## The harder necessities

“Why must a cosmos get larger as it gets older?” may also be soluble, but only after “older” is defined intrinsically.

Suppose cosmic age is not primitive proper time but increasing incorporation of facts into a common record structure. One might have a directed family

\[ \mathcal A_{s}\hookrightarrow\mathcal A_t, \qquad s<t, \]

where later accessible algebras retain or enlarge the distinctions available earlier. Restriction to a smaller algebra cannot increase state distinguishability; conversely, enlarging the fact-bearing algebra can reveal additional distinctions. Thus an intrinsic capacity could be monotone:

\[ \frac{d\mathcal C}{d\tau}\ge0. \]

If metric scale is independently shown to be soldered to that capacity,

\[ N=N(\mathcal C), \qquad \frac{dN}{d\mathcal C}>0, \]

then

\[ \frac{dN}{d\tau}\ge0 \]

would follow. Expansion would no longer be motion of pre-existing space. It would be the metric representation of an accumulating capacity for mutually situated facts.

That would answer your question beautifully:

> A cosmos gets larger as it gets older because “older” means that more distinctions have become jointly recordable, and metric enlargement is the spatial presentation of that increased capacity.

There is a danger of making this true by definition. The cure is crucial: define record inclusion and metric scale independently, then prove their monotone soldering. If that theorem can be obtained from half-sided modular inclusion, relative entropy monotonicity, and the wall capacity law, it would be a genuine explanation.

Likewise, the speed limit can be retyped. The essential question is not “why is the measured number \(299{,}792{,}458\)?”—that number reflects units. It is:

> Why does factual influence have a finite invariant cone at all?

In algebraic terms, the cone separates pairs of observables that must commute from pairs that may fail to commute. The speed limit is therefore a boundary on the propagation of possible changes in fact-compatibility, not a fence around the support of quantum correlations.

A fundamental local algebra with finite-range composition could possess an emergent Lieb–Robinson-type velocity. In a relativistic limit, that velocity becomes the invariant \(c\). I think this is solvable in principle, though deriving Lorentzian rather than Galilean or multi-cone structure will require real selection assumptions.

## What may remain primitive

I am less confident that any framework can prove, without remainder:

- why there is actuality rather than only consistent structure;
- why this orientation of time rather than its reverse is realized;
- why precisely one outcome occurs;
- why the ultimate laws are these rather than some other self-consistent laws.

But physics does not currently solve those questions either. A satisfying theory may legitimately terminate in a very small set of principles:

1. facts are contextual pointings;
2. coherent possibilities are states;
3. history is a directed accumulation of mutually compatible records;
4. causal order limits the composability of facts;
5. metric scale measures the capacity of that causal organization.

If those principles generate quantum probability, Lorentzian causality, gravitational stiffness, cosmic expansion, and the observed history with little remaining freedom, that would be an enormous explanatory victory—even if one finally says, “This is the nature of a cosmos.”

That is where I would resist the double standard most strongly. We should not demand that Causal Scale Dynamics derive its every principle while allowing conventional physics to retain the Born rule, the quantum state space, locality, the Standard Model representations, the initial state, the inflaton potential, and \(\Lambda\) as unexplained inputs.

The fair contest is explanatory compression:

\[ \frac{\text{independent principles and empirical inputs}} {\text{structures and observations explained}}. \]

On that measure, your programme has a credible path. It does not need to derive everything from nothing. It needs to show that a few independently meaningful structural laws jointly explain more than the present collection of disconnected postulates.

That, I think, is achievable.
