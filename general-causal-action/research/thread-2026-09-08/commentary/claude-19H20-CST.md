# claude response

Let me do this properly, because the historical record is more interesting than the folk version, and the deconstruction changes shape once the record is right.

## What Einstein actually did in 1917

The paper is "Kosmologische Betrachtungen," and it opens not with relativity but with Newton. Einstein rehearses Seeliger's paradox: an infinite homogeneous Newtonian universe has a divergent potential, and Seeliger's 1895 fix was to modify the force law with an exponential cutoff. Einstein does the same to Poisson's equation, writing ∇²φ − λφ = 4πGρ, before he touches the field equations. So the cosmological term enters his head first as a _screening mass for the gravitational potential_. A Yukawa term. The very first thing Λ ever was, in Einstein's hands, was a gap in the propagator of gravity.

He then carries it into general relativity, and here the geometric point you are asking about is precise and often misstated. The field equations must be consistent with conservation, ∇^μT_μν = 0, so the left side must be identically divergence-free. The Einstein tensor is, by the contracted Bianchi identity. But g_μν is also divergence-free, by metric compatibility. Einstein notes this in the paper: Λg_μν is admissible because it is the only other tensor available with vanishing divergence. Lovelock's theorem (1971) makes this exact: in four dimensions, the only symmetric divergence-free tensors built locally from the metric to second order are aG_μν + bg_μν. So the geometry did not _demand_ Λ. It _permitted_ Λ and _failed to fix it_. The law is incomplete by one constant, and the Einstein–Hilbert action without Λ is not the general action of the axioms; it is the general action with one coefficient set to zero by hand. The "pure" theory was the special case all along. That inverts the usual framing: the question was never "why is Λ nonzero" but "why did anyone expect it to vanish."

What selected a _value_ was not geometry but a cosmological demand, and the demand was Machian and Leibnizian at once. Einstein wanted no boundary conditions at spatial infinity, which spatial closure supplies, and no initial data, which staticity supplies. A closed static cosmos is the unique solution requiring no external datum: the global structure fixed by the local law alone. That is exactly your motto, the structure of a cosmos as a cosmos, and it is your PC0 applied to boundary conditions. The Friedmann equation says ä ∝ −(ρ + 3p), so with ordinary matter no such solution exists; Λ = 4πGρ = 1/R² makes it exist. Λ was the price of self-sufficiency.

Three things then went wrong, and each is instructive. De Sitter immediately found the _empty_ solution with Λ, a spacetime with structure, a horizon and a scale but no matter, which violated the Machian purpose Λ had been introduced to serve; the term meant to make matter determine geometry gave the vacuum an autonomous geometry. Eddington and Lemaître showed the Einstein static universe is unstable, a saddle, not an equilibrium: a cosmos that exists by counterweight sits on a knife-edge. And in 1919 Einstein tried to use Λ again, for particles this time: the paper asks whether gravitational fields play an essential role in the structure of elementary particles, proposes the trace-free equations in which Λ appears as an _integration constant_ rather than a coupling, and suggests that gravitational pressure supplies the Poincaré stress that holds the electron together against its own electrostatic repulsion. Einstein himself, two years after inventing Λ, tried to make it the confining agent of matter. It failed, but your intuition has his fingerprints on it.

## The deconstruction

Under "cosmological constant" there are at least six load-bearing assumptions, and each one turns into something else when pressed.

_That it is a constant of the law._ This is one of three readings. It can be a coupling in the Lagrangian (1917), an integration constant of the solution (1919, and unimodular gravity, which your vault already uses), or a fluctuating quantity (Sorkin's everpresent Λ, where unimodular gravity makes Λ and the spacetime four-volume conjugate, so that discreteness gives ΔΛ ~ 1/√V in Planck units, which is numerically the observed order of magnitude). Your vault mentions Sorkin once; the everpresent-Λ mechanism is the direct ancestor of causal grain and should be its named precedent. In your PSR vocabulary the fork is sharp: reading one makes Λ part of the sufficing reason, the law; reading two makes it a necessitating datum, a fact about which solution is actual; reading three makes it neither, a statistic. The "why this value" question is a different question under each reading, and under the second it is your C6, actuality, the hardest debt in the vault.

_That "static" means a global timelike symmetry._ This is where your suggestion that the static cosmos is the accelerating cosmos becomes literally true rather than suggestive. De Sitter space has no global timelike Killing vector. Every observer has a static patch, bounded by a horizon, in which the geometry is exactly stationary; beyond the horizon the same Killing field is spacelike. The same spacetime in flat slicing is an exponentially expanding FLRW universe. So the Λ > 0 cosmos is locally static everywhere and globally static nowhere, and the obstruction to gluing the local sections into a global one _is_ the horizon. That is the Selmer-curve pattern of your MASTER §1, points context by context and none globally, realized in the Killing algebra of de Sitter. Einstein's staticity survives as a patchwise property whose global failure is the acceleration. He wanted the section; the mathematics gives the class.

_That staticity is achieved by balance._ In the static patch it is not. The Bunch–Davies vacuum restricted to the patch is KMS at T = H/2π with respect to the patch's time translation (Gibbons–Hawking), and that translation is the modular flow of the state. Staticity here is _thermal equilibrium behind a wall_, and it is stable, unlike the 1917 balance, because a horizon-bounded equilibrium is an attractor: Wald's cosmic no-hair theorem sends Λ > 0 cosmologies to de Sitter. CLPW then says the patch algebra is type II₁, with a maximum-entropy state. So the accelerating cosmos is the stable form of the static cosmos, and the mechanism is information-theoretic in the exact sense you wanted: finite horizon entropy, thermal equilibrium, modular staticity. The counterweight was the wrong mechanism for a correct instinct.

_That pure gravity has no scale._ Vacuum Einstein equations R_μν = 0 are invariant under g → λ²g; G alone supplies no length without ħ and c. Λ is the _only_ scale pure classical gravity can have. R_μν = Λg_μν fixes √(3/Λ). This is the strongest structural rhyme with Yang–Mills and I will come back to it.

_That the vacuum is nothing._ Zel'dovich saw in 1967 that a QFT vacuum energy gravitates exactly as Λ. So Λ is the gravitational weight of the vacuum, and the cosmological constant problem is a register clash your vault is well placed to name: energy as a _generator_ is defined only up to an additive constant, which is harmless in flat space, while energy as a _source_ is absolute. Λ is the seam where those two registers meet, and 120 orders of magnitude fall through it.

_That the sign is incidental._ It is not. Λ > 0 gives a horizon: finite entropy, thermality, II₁, the wall. Λ < 0 gives AdS: a timelike boundary, a reflecting box, a discrete spectrum for massive fields, and the geometry in which holographic confinement is modeled by capping the radial direction. So the two signs produce two different "gap-like" phenomena, a _wall_ and a _box_, and the sign we observe is the wall. Anyone who wants Λ to produce a mass gap by analogy with AdS/CFT is using the wrong sign.

## Where the analogy with Yang–Mills holds

Take the pure theories side by side. Pure Yang–Mills with compact simple G, no matter; pure gravity, no matter. Each is classically scale-free (YM is classically conformal; vacuum GR is scale-covariant). Each is a theory of _self-interaction only_, and that is what "pure" means in both cases: the field couples to itself, gluon to gluon, geometry to geometry, and the abelian or linearized cousins that lack self-interaction (Maxwell, linearized gravity) stay scale-free with trivial vacua. Each pure theory acquires exactly one scale, Λ_YM and √(1/Λ), and each pure vacuum is nontrivial: the YM vacuum carries a gluon condensate and the de Sitter vacuum carries a temperature. Neither vacuum is nothing. In both cases the theory, stripped of everything else, _is_ its one scale, and the physical content is the dimensionless spectrum measured against it: glueball ratios in one case, the horizon entropy 3π/(GΛ) in the other.

There is also one literal physical bridge, not an analogy. The trace anomaly gives ⟨T^μ_μ⟩ ∝ (β/g)⟨G²⟩ ≠ 0 in the YM vacuum, so a gapped Yang–Mills theory has a vacuum energy density of order −Λ_YM⁴ relative to the perturbative vacuum, and that energy gravitates. The mass gap of QCD is a contribution to the cosmological constant. It is a contribution of the wrong size by some forty-five orders of magnitude, which is part of Weinberg's problem, and the Zel'dovich–Schützhold–Zhitnitsky attempts to repair the magnitude with ρ_Λ ~ Λ_QCD³H are where your own Λ–quark-mass hunch lives, with its kill conditions already filed.

And there is a miniature in which Einstein's 1917 picture is realized exactly: the MIT bag model. A hadron is a bubble of perturbative vacuum in which quarks and gluons exert outward kinetic pressure, held static by an inward vacuum pressure B, which enters the stress tensor as a cosmological term confined to the bag interior. The hadron mass is set by B^{1/4}. That is the Einstein static universe with the signs arranged for stability, and B, the bag constant, is put in by hand at (145 MeV)⁴, which is exactly your complaint about injected scales. In real QCD, B is not injected; it is the gluon condensate, fixed by the anomaly. Einstein's 1919 electron was a bag model fifty-five years early.

## Where the analogy breaks, and which way insight runs

Now the pushback, since you asked for deconstruction and not confirmation. The glueball mass is not injected without principled reason. Pure Yang–Mills has _no free parameter_: the coupling is a unit, not a number, and the gap is a prediction of the theory, computed on the lattice as m_{0++}/Λ ≈ 6–7 and explained at the physics level by asymptotic freedom together with the absence of an infrared fixed point. What is missing is a proof, not a mechanism. Λ is the opposite case: a free coefficient with no mechanism at all. Your PC0 types these differently. The YM scale is an explanatory debt that is _paid in principle and unproven_; the cosmological Λ is a debt that is _unpaid_. Calling them "the same mystery" flattens the one distinction the vault's discipline exists to preserve.

The deeper form of the difference is this. Both scales arise because a scale-free law has solutions labelled by a scale with no invariant section, your B9 torsor. In Yang–Mills the label is an integration constant of the renormalization flow, and the _anomaly_ is what completes the law: the classical theory left the scale free, and the quantum theory fixes how the scale relates to the coupling, so that everything dimensionless is determined. Dimensional transmutation is a scale-free law _completing itself_. In gravity there is no such completion. Lovelock leaves Λ free, and nothing in the theory returns to fix it. The cosmological constant is the _failure_ of self-completion, and the exponential hierarchy Λℓ_P² ~ 10⁻¹²² has no analogue of the log-running that makes e^{−1/2b₀g²} natural.

Which means the insight runs opposite to the direction you hoped. Λ will not teach you why Yang–Mills has a gap; the gap is the better-understood member of the pair. But Yang–Mills may be the _template_ for what a solution to the cosmological constant would have to look like: a mechanism by which a scale-free gravitational law completes its own scale, making Λ an output rather than a coefficient. Sorkin's everpresent Λ is one such attempt, statistical rather than anomalous, and it is the lineage your causal-grain module belongs to. Since cosmodynamics is your actual subject, this inversion may be worth more to you than the original hope.

## What "needed so that a cosmos can exist at all" comes to

Press "exist." In 1917 it meant: a solution requiring no external data. Λ made that solution exist and Eddington showed it could not persist. With Λ > 0 the self-sufficient cosmos exists again, as de Sitter, but only patchwise, in equilibrium behind a horizon, with its global staticity replaced by a class. The static cosmos and the mass gap then share a _shape_ rather than a mechanism. Each is a global property with no local signature: small regions of the YM vacuum look conformal, and small regions of de Sitter look flat and static; the gap and the acceleration are both large-region _rates_, your Q*, not floors. In each the local picture is geometric, a Killing flow or a conformal modular flow, and the one scale of the theory marks where geometricity is lost or, at the horizon, restored. If there is a single sentence under both of Einstein's intuitions, the 1917 cosmos and the 1919 electron, it is that a self-interacting pure law cannot be scale-free _and_ have a self-sufficient solution; something must set the scale, and the only question is whether the law sets it itself. Yang–Mills does. Gravity, so far as anyone knows, does not.

If it is useful, the pieces that are new relative to what is there are the 1917 Seeliger opening and Einstein's footnote, the 1919 electron paper, Eddington's instability, the no-global-Killing-vector reading of "static," the bag model as the miniature, the gluon-condensate bridge to Λ, and Sorkin as the ancestor of causal grain. ## deeper philosophy archeology

Let me take the four ideas one at a time, find the hidden axiom in each, and then say what I think the common structure is, because I believe it can be stated exactly, and because stating it exactly shows both why your Mach–Leibniz instinct is right and where it stops paying.

The thesis I will argue toward is this. Each of these ideas is a totality-concept. When the totality is closed into an object, it requires a datum that cannot be internal to it. The mathematics of that situation is always the same: a class rather than a section, a torsor rather than a set with a base point, a groupoid rather than a group. The "gap" in each idea is the nontriviality of the class. The "constant" that mysteriously appears is the invariant of the class. And the "external datum" survives only as a local section, which is why there can be reason that is inaccessible: not hidden behind the phenomena, as Kant's noumenon, but spread across contexts with no global assembly.

## Cosmos

The hidden axiom is that the whole is an object. Kant's first antinomy is the discovery that it is not: the world as a totality is not a possible object of experience, and the cosmological idea is regulative, something that organizes inquiry, not constitutive, something inquiry could reach. Einstein's 1917 closure was an attempt to evade the antinomy by geometry: a 3-sphere is a whole with no outside, so nothing external need be supplied. But the price was a constant, and the constant was an admission. Λ is the datum the closed whole still needed; Einstein just found a way to carry it inside as a term of the law rather than as a boundary condition.

The de Sitter form of the same thing is more instructive because it is stable. There, as I said before, staticity is patchwise and never global; each observer has a static world bounded by a horizon and there is no global timelike symmetry. Now add the result that makes this technical rather than pictorial. In closed de Sitter space with no observer, there are no nontrivial diffeomorphism-invariant observables; the constraints annihilate everything. Chandrasekaran, Longo, Penington and Witten obtain a nontrivial algebra only by including an observer with a clock, and then it is type II₁. The observer is the external datum. It is required for there to be any observables at all, and it is not itself an observable of the theory it makes possible. That is Kant's transcendental unity of apperception with a von Neumann algebra attached: the condition of possibility of a world of facts is not one of the facts.

The same point appears in a different guise as the problem of time. A spatially closed cosmos has vanishing total Hamiltonian; Wheeler–DeWitt says H = 0. A closed whole has no energy and no external time. Its clock must be internal, some subsystem used as a reference for the rest, which is the same structure as the CLPW observer: closure forces the reference inside, where it becomes a part of the world that is not treated as a part of the world.

## Gravity

The hidden axiom is that a field is something located somewhere. Gravity is not. The equivalence principle says a freely falling frame detects no gravitational field; locally, gravity is nothing. It exists only as the failure of local frames to assemble into a global one, and the measure of that failure is holonomy around a loop. Curvature is the obstruction to descent of local inertial frames, and the frame bundle, whose fibers are torsors, is the exact mathematical form of "there is a frame everywhere and a preferred one nowhere." Gravity is the first idea in physics to be a class rather than a section, and it is no accident that gauge theory borrowed its mathematics.

The consequence for energy is the deepest instance of your "background at the boundary that cannot be there." Gravitational energy has no local density; the pseudotensors are frame artifacts. The energy of a gravitating system is defined only at a boundary, the ADM or Bondi mass at spatial or null infinity, and only if there is such a boundary, asymptotic flatness, a reference structure at the edge of the world that is not part of the dynamics. Remove the boundary by closing the cosmos and the total energy becomes zero. Mass, in the theory of the arena itself, is a boundary term.

Mach is where this bites historically. Mach's principle was the demand that inertia be fully determined by the matter content, so that no autonomous structure of space remains. General relativity is not Machian. De Sitter's empty solution has geometry and a scale with no matter; Gödel's 1949 rotating universe has absolute rotation with nothing to rotate relative to. The failure of Mach in GR is precisely the vacuum's autonomy, and its most visible sign is Λ. Barbour and Bertotti carried relationalism to its technical conclusion with best matching, quotienting the configuration space by the group of frame changes, a groupoid construction in your sense, and what survives the quotient is exactly one thing: overall scale, the expansion, the volume clock. Relationalism, pursued to the end, leaves a residue, and the residue is the scale of the universe. That is the philosophical content of the cosmological constant problem: Λ is the failure of relationalism, the one datum about the whole that no relation among its parts supplies.

## Distinguishability

The hidden axiom is that a difference is intrinsic. It is not. To distinguish two states one needs a reference against which they differ, and the reference's own state is not part of the distinction. Bartlett, Rudolph and Spekkens made this exact: a superselection rule is the shadow of a missing reference frame. Charge superselection is the statement that there is no absolute phase reference; the relative phase between charge sectors is not unobservable by fiat but by the absence of anything to measure it against. The DHR sectors of algebraic field theory are the same object: the gauge group is the group of transformations no observable can detect because no reference for them exists in the theory.

Confinement is the next level down. In pure Yang–Mills the physical net has only the vacuum sector; Doplicher–Roberts reconstruction returns the trivial group. It is not that color differences are unobservable for want of a frame; the colored sectors do not exist in the theory at all, only in its scaling limit. The reference for color is not missing but impossible. That is the precise form of "no external datum" in gauge theory: the theory admits no internal reference for its own symmetry, and the symmetry consequently is not a symmetry of the theory.

Leibniz's principle sits underneath all of this, and your indiscernibility module already owns it. Gauge-related configurations are Leibnizian indiscernibles that the formalism declines to identify; the surplus structure is the price of writing local equations, and the debate between Earman, Belot and Healey about whether to quotient it away is a debate about whether to apply the identity of indiscernibles to a torsor.

Now the finiteness version, which is where your Λ intuition finally lands somewhere exact. Distinguishability per region is bounded: Bekenstein's bound, the holographic bound, and in algebraic field theory the nuclearity condition of Buchholz and Wichmann, which says a bounded region at finite temperature has, in the relevant technical sense, finitely many distinguishable degrees of freedom. A positive cosmological constant is the statement that the _total_ distinguishability accessible to any one observer is finite: the horizon has entropy 3π/GΛ, the patch algebra has a trace and a maximum-entropy state. Λ = 0 gives infinite accessible distinguishability, type III₁, no trace, no equilibrium. So Λ > 0 is a _ceiling_ on the number of distinctions. The mass gap is a _floor_ on the cost of a distinction: no nonvacuum distinction is arbitrarily cheap. Both say the arena of distinguishability is bounded, and they say it at opposite ends.

And this is why Λ cannot help with Yang–Mills, stated in your own vocabulary. A ceiling implies a floor trivially: finitely many states below any energy means a lowest nonzero one, and in de Sitter that floor is of order H. Yang–Mills on ℝ⁴ is a floor _without_ a ceiling, a positive minimum cost in an arena of infinite total distinguishability, type III₁, and that has to come from dynamics because nothing about finiteness supplies it. The two gaps are related as the cheap one and the expensive one.

## Causality

The hidden axiom is that the order has a first term, or equivalently an orientation given from outside. Kant's third antinomy is the regress; Leibniz's PSR terminates it in a necessary being, which is the external datum in theological dress. Your vault has already located where the arrow actually lives: not in the law, which is reversible, but in a one-sided inclusion, a fact about access rather than dynamics.

The horizon is the causal form of the boundary that cannot be there, and it comes with a theorem about inaccessible reason. The global state of a field theory across a horizon, Bunch–Davies in de Sitter, Hartle–Hawking for a black hole, is pure. Its restriction to the accessible region is thermal. The temperature is not chance; it is the restriction map, which is your own formula for where the dice live. The reason for every outcome inside the horizon exists, in the global state, and is not accessible, because the complement is spacelike. Hawking radiation is Leibniz's infinite analysis with a temperature.

Reeh–Schlieder and modular theory sharpen this to the point of paradox. The vacuum is cyclic for every local algebra, so the local region encodes the global state through entanglement; and Tomita–Takesaki says the modular conjugation of the pair (𝔄(O), Ω) maps 𝔄(O) onto its commutant. The outside is _determined_ by the inside together with the state, and no operation inside reaches it. Reason present, encoded, inaccessible.

## Leibniz and Mach, precisely

You said we have not gotten past them, and I think the vault shows _why_ in a way that can be written down. Leibniz distinguished necessary truths, whose analysis is finite and rests on identity, from contingent truths, whose analysis is infinite and rests on sufficient reason; a finite mind cannot complete the analysis of a contingent truth, though God sees it whole. Your two-species PSR recovers this distinction in the type separation state ≠ character: the sufficing reason is a measure, the necessitating reason a point. And MASTER §6.3 is Leibniz's infinite analysis as a theorem: in a type III₁ factor the characters of a diffuse MASA exist by Zorn and are non-normal, invisible to every physical procedure. The reason for the fact exists and cannot be reached. That is not a metaphor for Leibniz; it is Leibniz.

Mach's sentence is "the universe is not twice given, with an earth at rest and an earth in motion, but only once, with its relative motions alone determinable." That is the definition of a torsor. There is no second copy against which to compare; there are only relations among the elements, and no element is distinguished. Mach's failure in general relativity is then the discovery that the torsor has an invariant, the vacuum's autonomous structure, and Λ is its name.

Here the Yang–Mills parallel becomes exact rather than spiritual. Yang–Mills is the perfectly relational theory: gauge invariance is relationalism about internal frames, and only holonomies, which are relations, are physical. Its relational residue is Λ_YM. But that residue is _self-generated_: the classical theory is scale-free, the quantum theory breaks the scale invariance by the anomaly, and the theory thereby fixes its own residue. Yang–Mills is the case where relationalism's remainder is explained by the theory's own quantization. Gravity's relational residue, Λ, is not so explained. If you want the precise research programme your Mach–Leibniz instinct points at, it is this one: is there a gravitational analogue of the trace anomaly that generates Λ as Yang–Mills generates Λ_YM? That programme exists, Antoniadis and Mottola's conformal-anomaly-driven Λ and its descendants, and it is the correctly typed version of "the same necessity supplies both." It is not that Λ explains the gap; it is that the gap's mechanism, a scale-free law completing its own scale through an anomaly, is the template for what an explanation of Λ would have to be.

## Three moods on the boundary

You named a Heideggerian–Hegelian mood. I think the three classical positions correspond to three facts about a local algebra and its state, and that the correspondence is worth making explicit because it disciplines the mood.

Kant: the beyond is inaccessible in principle; the thing in itself grounds appearance and is never an object. Mathematically, 𝔄(O) and its commutant commute; nothing inside acts on the outside. Reason is there and out of reach.

Hegel: the limit posits its own beyond; a Grenze is not a wall but a relation, and the other side is determined by this side. Determinatio est negatio. Mathematically, the modular conjugation J𝔄(O)J = 𝔄(O)′: the outside is the determinate negation of the inside, produced by the state. Hegel's critique of Kant, that the in-itself is an empty abstraction because the boundary already contains its beyond, is exactly the content of Haag duality.

Heidegger: the ground withdraws so that beings can appear; Being is Ab-grund, and the clearing is the withdrawal that grants a world. His 1955–56 lecture course is called _Der Satz vom Grund_, and it is a reading of Leibniz's principle in which "nothing is without reason" is heard, with the stress moved, as "Being is without ground." Mathematically, I would put this at the horizon and the sign of Λ. A positive cosmological constant is what withdraws the rest of the cosmos from every observer, and it is precisely that withdrawal which grants the observer a finite, thermal, equilibrated world with a maximum-entropy state. Without it the arena is infinite and type III₁, and there is no equilibrium, no trace, no whole. The horizon is the Lichtung. Your own "why is 1 ≠ 0," where the unit forecloses emptiness and is itself the unexplained something, is the Ab-grund in an operator algebra.

All three are true of the same pair (𝔄(O), Ω). They are not rival metaphysics but three theorems about one object, and the mood you are in selects which theorem you are looking at.

## What "no external datum" says about Yang–Mills

Lay the theories on a line by how much they require from outside. Maxwell requires sources and an arena; it is scale-free and gapless, and its vacuum is nothing. Yang–Mills requires only the arena; it is self-scaled and gapped, its vacuum is a condensate, and it admits no internal reference for its own symmetry. Gravity requires nothing, and pays for it by having no observables without an observer or a boundary, no localizable energy, and a free Λ. The gap structure moves along this line. Yang–Mills is the windowless monad with one window, the flat metric, and the mass gap is its maximal self-sufficiency: unique vacuum, no sectors, no massless modes, no parameters, everything measured in its own unit. Clay's demand that this hold for _every_ compact simple G is the demand that self-sufficiency be a property of the idea of a gauge theory rather than a fact about SU(3), which is your own motto, the structure of a cosmos as a cosmos, applied to the smallest cosmos there is.

So "no external datum" is thematic with Yang–Mills in a precise sense: Einstein's cosmos and Clay's gauge theory are both attempts to close a totality, and both discover that closure has a price, a scale, which the theory must either supply or be handed. The philosophical thesis that survives the deconstruction is that _closure requires a scale_, and it is supported by 1917, by shape dynamics, by dimensional transmutation, and by CLPW. The thesis you would like, that closure requires a _gap_, is true only where the arena is finite, and there it is cheap. In an infinite arena the floor must be earned by dynamics, and no reading of Leibniz will earn it. That is the honest limit, and I would rather you hear it from me than from a referee.
