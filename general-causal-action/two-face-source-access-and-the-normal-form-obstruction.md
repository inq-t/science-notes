## The discrepancy is the exterior part of the source lift

\[
\dot P_A=M_\alpha P_A+P_AM_\alpha-2P_AM_\alpha P_A.
\tag{TF1}
\]
This is the finite-source derivative proved by the projection owner; an operator-norm expansion on the whole carrier is not required.

Suppose \(S\) is real, formally skew and invariant on the polynomial-Gaussian core, with
\[
[K_0,S]=-V_1,\qquad S\Omega=u,\qquad
D(F)=\Omega^{-1}[S,M_F]\Omega .
\]
The difference between normal-form transport and actual fixed access is
\[

\]
For every regional polynomial \(F\),
\[
\boxed{B_A(\Omega F)=\Omega\,k_A(F),\qquad
k_A(F)=(I-E_A)D(F).}
\tag{TF2}
\]
Indeed \(\dot P_A(\Omega F)=(I-P_A)(uF)\), while
\([S,P_A](\Omega F)=(I-P_A)S(\Omega F)\).

Let \(\mathcal U F=\Omega F\). Both projection tangents are symmetric and off-diagonal on the core, so more generally

The star denotes the formal adjoint on polynomial inputs. Here \(D^*=-D-2M_\alpha\) in \(L^2(\mu_0)\), since
\(\mathcal U^{-1}S\mathcal U=D+M_\alpha\) is formally skew. The vacuum score does not disappear from the full adjoint; it cancels in (TF2) only because the input is regional.

## Tower and overlap transport retain ordered products

For \(A\subset B\), both the actual projections and their transported counterparts obey the tower relation. Differentiation gives
\[
\boxed{B_AP_B+P_AB_B=B_A,\qquad
B_BP_A+P_BB_A=B_A.}
\tag{TF4}
\]
These are compatibility identities for discrepancies, not statements that the discrepancies vanish.

For arbitrary, possibly overlapping \(A,B\), retain the ordered map \(P_AP_B\). Its transport discrepancy is exactly
\[

\tag{TF5}

\tag{TF6}

Thus even separate radius calibrations must retain the product's oriented channel when comparing an overlap. Tower identities alone do not supply that term or select a normal form.
