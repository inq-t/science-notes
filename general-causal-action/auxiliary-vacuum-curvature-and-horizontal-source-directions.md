# Auxiliary Vacuum Curvature and Horizontal Source Directions

The auxiliary Gaussians are uniformly confined when the frame history is fixed, but that confinement does not pass unchanged to the reflected vacuum. Spatial frame averaging and the actual Perron equation subtract two positive score covariances from its flat-coordinate Hessian. A regular gauge-horizontal direction detected by a neutral plaquette has arbitrarily negative curvature. Along the many-copy Wilson trajectory, bounded auxiliary estimators of physical sources have vanishing Euclidean gradient energy and positive limiting variance. The neutral auxiliary Poincaré floor therefore closes while the chronological gap returns positively at fixed spatial graph and Wilson coupling.

## Differentiate the selected vacuum, not a supplied density

Use the amplitude, support and Perron vector of [[reflection-sewing-and-the-auxiliary-boundary-carrier|RS1–14]]. Write each site's copies as a matrix \(X_x\in\mathbb C^{n\times\nu}\), collect them into the real Euclidean vector \(\Xi\), and use the inner product \(\operatorname{ReTr}(X^*Y)\). Put
\[
V(U,\Xi)=\Xi^TQ_{\rm sp}(U)\Xi,\qquad
m(\Xi)=\int e^{-V(U,\Xi)}dU,\qquad
d\pi(\Xi)=\varphi(\Xi)^2m(\Xi)d\Xi,
\tag{HV1}
\]
where \(Q_{\rm sp}\) denotes the underlying real self-adjoint precision, including all copies. The actual normalized vacuum satisfies
\(\widetilde{\mathsf T}\varphi=\lambda_0\varphi\), \(\varphi>0\), and \(\int\varphi^2m=1\).
The conditional slice Gaussian has
\[
\nabla_\Xi^2V=2Q_{\rm sp}(U)\ge2c_{\rm sp}I,
\qquad c_{\rm sp}=1-2(D-1)\kappa>0,\quad\kappa=rw.
\tag{HV2}
\]
This statement holds for each fixed \(U\), independently of the number of copies.

Let \(\rho_\Xi(dU)=m(\Xi)^{-1}e^{-V(U,\Xi)}dU\). The conditional-score identity in [[global-local-response-reconstruction/vacuum-boundary-gluing-and-wall-response|VB24b–c]] gives, in the fixed flat coordinates,
\[
\boxed{\displaystyle
\nabla^2[-\log m]
=2\mathbb E_{\rho_\Xi}Q_{\rm sp}
-4\operatorname{Cov}_{\rho_\Xi}(Q_{\rm sp}\Xi).}
\tag{HV3}
\]
Vector covariances here and below are real symmetric forms. Compactness of the frame integral permits these derivatives. The covariance is subtracted, rather than added as confinement.

The Perron equation supplies the other term without choosing a new vacuum. If \(g\in G^\Lambda\), let \(Y=\rho(g)\Xi'\), with the same site rotation on all copies. Define the normalized positive measure
\[
d\eta_\Xi(\Xi',g)
=\frac{e^{2\kappa\langle\Xi,Y\rangle}
\varphi(\Xi')m(\Xi')\,d\Xi'\,dg}
{\lambda_0\varphi(\Xi)}.
\tag{HV4}
\]
Its denominator is exactly the RS temporal integral. Consequently
\[
\nabla\log\varphi=2\kappa\,\mathbb E_{\eta_\Xi}Y,
\qquad
\boxed{\nabla^2\log\varphi
=4\kappa^2\operatorname{Cov}_{\eta_\Xi}(Y)\ge0.}
\tag{HV5}
\]
To justify differentiation, use \(\varphi\in L^2(m)\), \(m(\Xi')\le e^{-c_{\rm sp}\|\Xi'\|^2}\), and Cauchy–Schwarz. Every fixed polynomial moment multiplied by \(e^{c\|\Xi'\|}\) is integrable, locally uniformly in the incoming \(\Xi\). Thus the positive Laplace transform in (HV4) is smooth and log-convex. This is the RS specialization of the actual-vacuum score method in [[vacuum-aligned-innovation-completion/local-perron-oscillation-and-conditional-coercivity|local Perron response]].

For the actual vacuum potential \(W_\pi=-\log m-2\log\varphi\), the exact result is
\[
\boxed{\displaystyle
\nabla^2W_\pi
=2\mathbb E_{\rho_\Xi}Q_{\rm sp}
-4\operatorname{Cov}_{\rho_\Xi}(Q_{\rm sp}\Xi)
-8\kappa^2\operatorname{Cov}_{\eta_\Xi}(Y).}
\tag{HV6}
\]
The second covariance is generated by temporal sewing itself. It cannot be dropped by replacing \(\varphi\) with a constant or a separately supplied Gaussian.

## A regular horizontal direction has negative curvature

Take the fundamental representation of \(SU(2)\), \(\nu\ge4\), and a spatial square \(0\to1\to2\to3\to0\). It fits the hypercubic slicing for \(D\ge3\). Choose two matrices \(E,F\in\mathbb C^{2\times\nu}\) with
\[
EE^*=FF^*=I_2,\qquad EF^*=0,
\]
and positive real numbers \(a,b\). At the four sites put
\[
X_0=aE,\qquad X_1=bF,\qquad X_2=bF,\qquad X_3=aE+bF.
\tag{HV7}
\]
Every site matrix has full row rank. The site-gauge action is therefore free at this configuration.

For an edge \(x\to y\), write \(J_{xy}=X_yX_x^*\); its spatial factor is
\[
Z_{xy}=\int_{SU(2)}
e^{2\kappa\operatorname{ReTr}(UJ_{xy})}\,dU.
\]
At (HV7),
\[
J_{01}=0,\qquad
J_{12}=J_{23}=b^2I_2,\qquad J_{30}=a^2I_2.
\tag{HV8}
\]
Consider the unit direction \(H_0=F/\sqrt2\) at site \(0\), with all other variations zero. It is orthogonal to every infinitesimal gauge direction \(LX_0\), because \(FE^*=0\). Thus it is horizontal, not an infinitesimal gauge transformation.

The conditional link law at edge \(01\) is Haar. Its score in this direction is
\[
\partial_H\log(e^{2\kappa\operatorname{ReTr}(UJ_{01})})
=\sqrt2\,\kappa b\,\operatorname{ReTr}U.
\]
Haar character orthogonality gives mean zero and variance \(2\kappa^2b^2\). The exponent is linear in \(X_0\), so this is exactly \(\partial_H^2\log Z_{01}\). Every other incident edge has nonnegative second derivative of its log normalizer in the same direction, by the same linear-score covariance identity. Therefore
\[
\boxed{\displaystyle
\partial_H^2[-\log m]\le2-2\kappa^2b^2,\qquad
\partial_H^2W_\pi\le2-2\kappa^2b^2.}
\tag{HV9}
\]
The second inequality uses (HV5). It concerns the actual selected vacuum at every fixed admissible \(\kappa>0\). Sending \(b\to\infty\) makes the upper bound arbitrarily negative.

The construction also embeds in a larger spatial graph. Keep the four displayed matrices and fix arbitrary full-row-rank matrices elsewhere. Extra edges incident to site \(0\) only contribute further nonnegative log-normalizer curvature, so (HV9) persists. Nonincident edges do not enter this directional derivative.

This direction is detected by an actual neutral source. Let
\(F_\square(U)=\frac12\operatorname{ReTr}U_\square\), and let \(\Phi F_\square\) be its RS conditional return. For scalar \(J=jI_2\), set
\[
c_\kappa(j)=\frac{I_2(4\kappa j)}{I_1(4\kappa j)}>0
\qquad(j>0).
\]
The matrix character integral in [[feature-number-grading-and-compact-reconstruction|FN6–7]] gives conditional mean \(c_\kappa(j)I_2\). At edge \(01\) the mean is zero and its derivative in \(H\) is \((\kappa b/\sqrt2)I_2\). Conditional link independence then gives
\[
\boxed{\displaystyle
\partial_H(\Phi F_\square)
=\frac{\kappa b}{\sqrt2}\,
c_\kappa(b^2)^2c_\kappa(a^2)>0.}
\tag{HV10}
\]
Derivatives of the other link means multiply the zero mean on edge \(01\), so they do not enter. The curvature failure therefore touches a direction seen by a neutral frame source.

On the fixed-Wilson path \(\nu r_\nu^4\to c_4>0\), take \(b^2=c_b\nu\), \(c_b>0\). Equation (HV9) gives
\[
\partial_H^2W_{\pi_\nu}
\le2-2c_bw^2\nu r_\nu^2\longrightarrow-\infty.
\tag{HV11}
\]
This is a pointwise discriminator. The displayed configurations have neighborhoods of positive vacuum measure, but no estimate of their typicality or mass has been used.

## Bounded frame estimators have small auxiliary gradient energy

The source estimator from [[auxiliary-boundary-sufficiency-and-the-wilson-source-algebra|AS3–6]] gives a complementary averaged test. For each spatial edge use
\[
A_e(\Xi)=\frac1{\nu rw}\sum_{a=1}^{\nu}
\xi_x^{(a)}\xi_y^{(a)*}.
\]
Its real Hilbert–Schmidt Jacobian has the exact squared norm
\[
\boxed{\displaystyle
\|DA_e\|_{\rm HS,\mathbb R}^2
=\frac{2n}{(\nu rw)^2}
\sum_a\bigl(\|\xi_x^{(a)}\|^2+\|\xi_y^{(a)}\|^2\bigr).}
\tag{HV12}
\]
Indeed, varying the \(2n\) real coordinates of one complex vector gives \(2n\) copies of the squared norm of the other vector.

On every even temporal cylinder, the conditional auxiliary covariance is \((I-rP_U)^{-1}\le(1-r)^{-1}I\). Hence, uniformly in cylinder length,
\[
\mathbb E\|DA_e\|_{\rm HS,\mathbb R}^2
\le\frac{4n^2}{\nu r^2w^2(1-r)}.
\tag{HV13}
\]
Fix an invariant matrix polynomial source \(F\) supported on finitely many spatial edges. Clip each \(A_e\) to the Hilbert–Schmidt ball of radius \(\sqrt n\), and evaluate a bounded Lipschitz polynomial extension on the resulting finite product of balls. An extension may first be Haar averaged to make it invariant away from group-valued matrices. Denote the resulting bounded neutral auxiliary observable by \(G_\nu\).

Clipping is nonexpansive. The real Sobolev chain rule, or its piecewise derivative away from the clipping surface, and (HV13) give
\[
\mathbb E_{\nu,L}|\nabla_\Xi G_\nu|^2
\le\frac{C_F}{\nu r^2}
\qquad(r\le r_0<1),
\tag{HV14}
\]
where \(C_F\) depends on the fixed source, its support and the declared dimension and weights, but not on the even cylinder length \(L\). Shared endpoints merely cause several nonnegative Jacobian contributions in this upper bound.

For fixed \(\nu,r\), apply the AS trace-vacuum limit to the bounded invariant functions \(\min\{|\nabla G_\nu|^2,M\}\), then let \(M\to\infty\). It follows that the same estimate holds in the actual temporal vacuum:
\[
\boxed{\displaystyle
\int|\nabla_\Xi G_\nu|^2d\pi_\nu
\le\frac{C_F}{\nu r^2}.}
\tag{HV15}
\]
The clipped estimator is not a new Gaussian preparation. Conditional best approximation in the same cylinder, followed by its vacuum limit, also gives
\[
\boxed{\displaystyle
\|G_\nu-\Phi_\nu F\|_{L^2(\pi_\nu)}^2
\le C_F\bigl[(\nu r^2)^{-1}+r^4\bigr].}
\tag{HV16}
\]
This is the AS mean-square estimate before discarding the estimator error. Both observables are uniformly bounded for fixed \(F\), so the trace-vacuum passage for this term uses bounded conditional source integrals.

## The flat-gradient floor closes in the finite Wilson return

Fix the spatial graph and positive Wilson coupling, and take a real nonconstant invariant polynomial source \(F\), such as a plaquette trace. The actual vacuum-source return in [[cycle-moments-and-the-pure-gauge-vacuum-return|CM18]] proves
\[
\operatorname{Var}_{\pi_\nu}(\Phi_\nu F)\longrightarrow v_F>0.
\tag{HV17}
\]
Here \(v_F\) is the variance of \(F\) in the finite Wilson vacuum. This uses the cycle-moment theorem's uniform temporal cap estimate and AS's coincident-source correction; fixed-slab density convergence alone would not supply it.

On the Wilson path, (HV16) and the contraction of centering imply
\(\operatorname{Var}_{\pi_\nu}G_\nu\to v_F\). For the flat-coordinate auxiliary Poincaré quotient restricted to all gauge-invariant Sobolev observables, it follows that
\[
\boxed{\displaystyle
\lambda_{\rm grad}^{\rm neutral}(\pi_\nu)
\le
\frac{\int|\nabla_\Xi G_\nu|^2d\pi_\nu}
{\operatorname{Var}_{\pi_\nu}G_\nu}
=O((\nu r_\nu^2)^{-1})\longrightarrow0.}
\tag{HV18}
\]
The centered normalized estimators are therefore genuine low-gradient directions correlated in \(L^2\) with returned physical sources. Membership of \(G_\nu\) in the exact cyclic frame-source subspace has not been proved and is not needed for (HV18). The result concerns the larger neutral auxiliary Sobolev carrier.

The gradient form in (HV18) is not the logarithm of the RS transfer. CM15 proves convergence of the latter's gap to a positive finite Wilson transfer gap on this same fixed-graph trajectory. That positive chronological gap therefore coexists with the closing auxiliary gradient floor. Neither estimate is a spatial-volume or continuum-uniform result. Uniform confinement of the conditional Gaussians, curvature of the marginal, and contraction of chronological innovation are three distinct predicates.

## What would have to change

Equations (HV9)–(HV11) reject a global positive flat-Hessian estimate for the actual vacuum, even in a regular horizontal direction visible to a plaquette. They do not reject a quotient Bakry–Émery estimate including orbit volume, Ricci curvature and the induced connection, a conditional influence bound, or the required chronological gap. A positive rescaling of a tangent does not change the sign of the displayed Hessian. A nonlinear Gram coordinate or an amplitude-dependent metric introduces new connection, volume and comparison terms; it is not a sign repair by changing units.

Likewise, multiplying the auxiliary gradient form by a divergent copy-dependent pace would change that geometric clock and needs a separate constitutive and temporal comparison theorem. The inherited transfer and its full source law do not change merely because another gradient metric is declared.

The useful next geometric quantity is therefore a complete conditional-score or influence estimate for the actual three-slab transport, including its vacuum factors and lag cost. [[temporal-angular-cancellation-and-the-conditional-variance-ledger|Temporal angular cancellation]] separates the Haar presentation average from the remaining spatial mixture. The conjectured positive surplus in [[two-slice-innovation-geometry/oriented-innovation-and-finite-temporal-repair|OI24–28]] cannot be obtained by counting massive Gaussian conditional directions or by dropping either covariance in (HV6).
