# Anomaly Lines and the Yang–Mills Phase Test

A comparison law can fail to admit a globally compatible scalar evaluation while still admitting stable realizations in particular phases. The proposed master object should retain both the obstruction to such an evaluation and the positive response within an allowed realization. The first decisive return is a known Yang–Mills exclusion: recover the center–CP compatibility constraint from sourced defect composition. The further conjecture is that the same composition law restricts the norm that measures distinguishability, so phase compatibility and quantitative rigidity cease to be independently supplied inputs.

## Evaluation need not begin as a number

For an anomalous relative theory, an evaluation on a closed background can belong to a one-dimensional vector space supplied by a theory in one higher dimension. Choosing an ordinary scalar presentation requires additional identification data. With a unitary anomaly theory, the remaining ambiguity can be expressed as a phase. This is the established framework of [[library/relative-quantum-field-theory/inq|Freed–Teleman relative field theory]], §§1–2; general relative theories need not have one-dimensional values.

Its proposed use here is
\[
\mathcal Z_D[J;B]\in\mathcal L_{D,B},
\qquad
\mu_{21}:\mathcal L_{D_2,B_2}\otimes\mathcal L_{D_1,B_1}
\longrightarrow\mathcal L_{D_2\circ D_1,B}.
\tag{AP1}
\]
This is schematic line-level sewing after declaring the compatible boundary data; a full theory also assigns boundary carriers and contraction maps. The associativity comparisons for \(\mu\), orientation reversal and background changes are part of the law. A scalar answer is a possible presentation of this structure, rather than the initial type of every answer.

The [[scale-bearing-descent/master-object-and-compatible-realizations|master-object signature]] must therefore allow relative evaluations. The philosophical reversal is precise: what looks absolute in one presentation may arise from choosing a compatible trivialization of relational data. Some demanded choices can fail to exist. Others exist without being unique or quantitatively stable.

## A discrete return inside Yang–Mills

Use the oriented four-dimensional background convention of [[library/theta-time-reversal-and-temperature/inq|Gaiotto–Kapustin–Komargodski–Seiberg]], §§2.2–2.4, without additional spin identifications. For the \(\mathbb Z_N\) two-form background, allowed counterterms have
\[
p\in\mathbb Z/(2N),\quad
\begin{cases}
p\text{ arbitrary},&N\text{ even},\\
p\text{ even},&N\text{ odd}.
\end{cases}
\qquad
\mathrm{CP}_{0}:p\mapsto-p,
\quad
\mathrm{CP}_{\pi}:p\mapsto-p+N-1.
\tag{AP2}
\]
The properly quantized quadratic background term uses the Pontryagin square for even \(N\). Equations (2.10), (2.13) and (2.24) give the transformations. Thus CP invariance at \(\pi\) requires
\[
2p=N-1\pmod{2N}.
\tag{AP3}
\]
For even \(N\), there is no solution: the two sides have opposite parity. For odd \(N>1\), exactly one allowed even class solves (AP3); it differs from the unique allowed CP-invariant class \(p=0\) at \(\theta=0\). This is the distinction between the even-\(N\) mixed anomaly and the odd-\(N\) global inconsistency.

The anomaly excludes a symmetry-preserving trivial nondegenerate gapped vacuum. It permits CP-related gapped vacua. Absence of this anomaly at zero theta supplies no gap estimate.

The desired result is stronger than entering (AP2) as data and solving a congruence. The object must first return center defects, their intersection operation, theta transport and the allowed scalar redefinitions. Their composition must then force (AP2). Otherwise the calculation verifies an imported answer.

## Conjecture: phase-compatible selection of the norm

**Conjecture. A common sourced descent law can select both its anomaly-line sewing and its positive comparison pairing, up to declared equivalences and a common unit, through one compatibility condition. In an admissible vacuum sector this condition fixes a non-retunable comparison response; it can permit multiple symmetry-related sectors while making each quantitatively rigid.**

The minimal algebraic experiment is a family of Hermitian lines \(L_i\) over compatible boundary presentations, with
\[
K_{ij}:L_j\longrightarrow L_i,\qquad
\sum_{i,j}\langle v_i,K_{ij}v_j\rangle\ge0.
\tag{AP4}
\]
The maps must be constructed by the same sourced sewing that produces defect transport. Positivity is an operator-matrix condition on \(\bigoplus_iL_i\). Arbitrarily multiplying individual entries of a positive scalar kernel by unit phases does not preserve it. Changing line frames transports both the kernel and the vectors and preserves (AP4).

For a field-theoretic return, this Gram model must be replaced by reflection-positive sewing: specify reflection, its antilinear maps on boundary carriers and anomaly lines, and the contraction of each reflected pair. If an inflow theory is used, identify it and prove positivity of the combined pairing. An anomalous boundary partition function alone is not a positive probability law. These requirements are the mathematical content of the proposed bridge.

The resulting readouts should construct the positive product defects of [[scale-bearing-descent/variance-completed-rigidity|variance-completed descent]]. Those defects and anomaly phases have different types. The conjecture demands a constitutive relation through the common sewing law; it does not identify a phase cocycle with a Kadison variance.

Even successful phase matching leaves a second task. [[quantitative-descent/rigidity-certificates-and-soft-escape|Exact gluing can coexist with arbitrarily soft distinctions]]. The law must restrict a norm and a repair strongly enough to prevent that escape in the target sector. [[mass-scale-calibration/joint-causal-generators-and-the-mass-casimir|Joint causal generators]] supplies a compatible interpretation: individual directed rates can approach zero while an invariant of their joint realization stays positive.

## First experiment: exclude one scalar realization

Start with a finite presentation of center-defect networks for the \(SU(2)\) return. Declare the boundary operations, background cocycle data, changes of cocycle representative and reflection before choosing any kernel. Construct the quadratic background operation and theta-transport map from these rules. This finite combinatorial calculation is a test of the return map; it does not claim to derive the group or dimension.

Then ask whether one symmetry-compatible scalar trivialization extends across both zero and pi theta. The expected contradiction at pi is \(2p=1\pmod4\). Repeat with \(SU(3)\): the distinct expected result is the allowed class \(p=4\pmod6\) at pi, versus \(p=0\) at zero. This prevents a construction from mistaking all theta dependence for the same obstruction.

Only after this exclusion succeeds, solve for reflection pairings on the same generated diagrams, with the same sources and normalization. Hold their primitive data fixed and attempt to vary the induced response spectrum. A freely variable spectrum means the construction has explained phase compatibility but has not selected dynamics. A forced relation between defect transport and a repair coefficient would be meaningful new progress even before an infinite-volume theorem.

[[scale-bearing-descent/minimal-conjectures-and-decisive-returns|The minimal conjectural chain]] places this experiment before continuum recovery. The first achievement would be an impossible realization proved impossible from the proposed primitives. The next would be an allowed realization whose stability those same primitives constrain.
