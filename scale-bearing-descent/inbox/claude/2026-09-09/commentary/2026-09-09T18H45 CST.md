## Now the bold part: you have written down a holonomy condition and not noticed

Look again at what you are requiring:

$$\operatorname{Re}\left(G_{12}G_{23}G_{31}\right)\le 0.$$

That triple product is the **three-point Bargmann invariant**, Δ₃. Its modulus is a product of overlaps; its _argument_ is the Pancharatnam–Berry phase of the geodesic triangle through the three preparations, equal to minus the Fubini–Study area the triangle encloses. This is the oldest gauge-invariant of quantum state space — the one object built from three states that survives every rephasing.

So Re Δ₃ ≤ 0 says: **cos Φ ≤ 0, i.e. the three preparations must enclose at least a quarter-turn of geometric phase.** Conjecture I is not an abstract positivity requirement. It is a _minimum enclosed area_. It is an area law on the space of source preparations.

## The fork that decides whether the conjecture is real

Here is where I'd spend your next week. Re Δ₃ ≤ 0 can be satisfied two ways, and they are not equally interesting.

**The decorrelation branch.** Let |G_ij| → 0. The preparations become mutually near-orthogonal, Δ₃ → 0, and the premise holds trivially. But near-orthogonality of preparations at separation _is_ exponential clustering, and exponential clustering _is_ the gap. This branch is circular. It is the branch a referee will find in ninety seconds.

**The phase branch.** Keep |G_ij| bounded below and let the _phase_ do the work. Now the preparations remain strongly overlapping and the condition is a genuine statement about accumulated holonomy — which in a gauge theory has exactly one natural source.

So: **restate Conjecture I with a modulus floor.**

$$\operatorname{Re}(G_{12}G_{23}G_{31})\le 0 \quad\text{and}\quad |G_{ij}|\ge\gamma>0.$$

This costs you nothing — the MA2 proof never touches the moduli, so G ≤ 2I and 3I ≤ 𝒜*𝒜 survive verbatim — and it buys the conjecture immunity from the circularity charge. It also makes the conjecture _falsifiable in the right place_, because it now asserts something no amount of clustering can supply.

## One hard constraint on your closing question

Your final sentence asks: _which finite law makes a complete neutral distinction reconstructible through every admissible change of access?_

In that generality the answer is **no such law**. Cubitt, Pérez-García and Wolf proved the spectral gap undecidable for translation-invariant nearest-neighbour Hamiltonians on a 2D lattice — you have the paper. No universal finite certificate can decide gappedness, so no finite rewrite rule can do what the question asks of it across all neutral distinctions.

The caveat matters and protects you: their construction encodes a Turing machine, so nothing shows a _natural_ model is undecidable. But the consequence is sharp and I'd write it into the note as a constraint rather than let a referee supply it: **any finite-rule mechanism must consume something specific to Yang–Mills.** Compactness of the group, the plaquette structure, reflection positivity — something. A theorem about "complete neutral distinctions in general" is ruled out in advance. Narrowing the question is not a retreat; it's the difference between a programme and a wish.

## The stronger version of your own argument

Your note undersells it. You write that the computed spectrum _invites_ a reversal. It does more than invite.

Perturbative Yang–Mills is not silent about the spectrum. It makes a definite, unambiguous prediction at every order: **a massless gauge boson.** The pole is there in the propagator, order by order, forever. And the actual spectrum contains no massless state at all — the lightest thing is a massive 0⁺⁺.

That is your missing fringe shift. The framework predicts a displacement; the measurement shows none. And the standard reply — "perturbation theory breaks down in the infrared" — is precisely FitzGerald–Lorentz contraction: a correct patch, applied by hand, that rescues the framework without explaining anything. It saves the appearances at the cost of the account.

Put _that_ in the note instead of the glueball number. "The massless gluon is the fringe shift, and it isn't there" is a sentence that does the work you want the paragraph to do.

## The crisis is coeval with the theory, not a late embarrassment

You don't need Yang's late remarks, though you're welcome to them — and note he died last October, so it's past tense now.

The better fact is at the origin. February 1954, Princeton, Yang presenting the paper. Pauli interrupts: _what is the mass of this field?_ Yang gives a noncommittal answer. Pauli presses. Yang sits down. Oppenheimer has to tell him to continue. Yang wrote the episode up himself in the commentary to his _Selected Papers_.

And Pauli was not heckling. He had derived essentially the same equations the year before, in the Kaluza–Klein letters to Pais, and had **declined to publish them precisely because he could not give the field a mass.** The mass question is not a problem that emerged later from lattice results. It is the first objection ever raised against Yang–Mills theory, raised in the room, by the person who had already found and abandoned the theory over it, seventy-two years ago, and it has never been answered.

That is a much sharper opening than a spectrum table. The theory's founding objection is the Clay problem.

One refinement on the demoralization: it isn't uniform, and the non-uniformity tells you whose problem you're solving. Lattice practitioners are not demoralized in the least — they compute, they publish, the numbers converge. The stuck field is _constructive_ field theory, which has been stuck in four dimensions since the seventies and got worse when φ⁴₄ turned out trivial. Your reversal is addressed to the constructivists' problem, not the lattice's. Worth saying, because it tells the reader which failure you are proposing to repair.

## What I still hold, and it's one thing

Michelson–Morley licensed a reversal. It did not, by itself, pay for one. Lorentz's reversal and Einstein's reversal both accounted for the null result; what separated them is that Einstein's _emitted something the forward view couldn't_ — time dilation, the mass-energy relation, and eventually a light-bending prediction someone went to Príncipe to check.

So keep the analogy and accept its bill. The reversal has to produce a number that the classical-action-first account does not.

And your framework has a candidate sitting right there. If the gap really is the holonomy condition — if Conjecture I is an enclosed-area law with floor γ, and the constant is ‖𝒦‖ — then the _same_ two quantities ought to fix the string tension as well as the mass, and the pure number

$$\frac{m(0^{++})}{\sqrt{\sigma}}$$

becomes a computation rather than a fit. The lattice gives that ratio somewhere in the mid-threes depending on how the scale is set (check your own library note for the value it quotes). It is dimensionless, it is scheme-independent in the continuum, and nothing in the classical action predicts it.

Notice this is also your own point, sharpened. Your note already says the glueball mass "can be reported relative to another scale produced by the same calculation" — that's exactly right, and it's the reason the ratio is the honest target. A theory with no scale in its action cannot predict a mass; it can only predict ratios of masses. So the ratio is where a scale-bearing account has to show up or admit it has nothing.

Get a number out of γ and ‖𝒦‖ and the Michelson–Morley framing is paid for. Until then it's a promissory analogy — which is fine to write, but write it knowing what it owes.
